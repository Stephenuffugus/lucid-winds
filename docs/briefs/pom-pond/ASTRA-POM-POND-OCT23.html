2. The app in two minutes

Live: https://pom-pond.web.app, the only thing under review. Open it on a phone, portrait. A free family app for chores and kindness. No ads, nothing sold.

  • The loop: a kid does a chore (or a parent gives a Pom for kindness, helping, effort, manners, school or family) and earns a Pom. Every Pom hatches a critter into that kid's own pond. Poms also fill three buckets: small (4 Poms), medium (3), big (2) by default, each unlocking a reward the parent picks (defaults: 15 minutes of screen time, pick dinner, movie night). When the medium bucket fills, the kid chooses to save toward big or keep the medium win. The points can be renamed per family (Pom, Gem, Star).
  • Who uses it: a parent (email or Google login, sets a 4 digit parent PIN); a kid on their own device (family join code plus a 4 digit kid code, can never open the parent screen); a co parent (joins with an invite code, full login); a cheerleader, a read only relative (joins with a cheer code). Every Pom, critter and bucket change is made on the server.
  • Screens: sign in (Start, Join, Cheer tabs); a welcome wizard (family name, parent PIN, add kids); the lobby "Who's here" with member cards, gallery, Wall of Fame, weekly recap; a kid's pond view (Pom count, critter count, streak, the three buckets, My Pond with a Mix button); the chore list (4 defaults: Tidy Room, Dishes, Brush Teeth, Feed Pet; each once a day); a ring timer for a chore; the hatch reveal with confetti and a voice; the Dex (species found out of 609, shiny count, morphs, top tier, 11 milestone badges, Critter of the Day); a daily quest (do 2 chores); the parent screen behind the PIN (approve, rewards to deliver, kids, grown ups, chores, rewards by tier, activity); settings (Calm mode, Read aloud, join and invite codes, backup, reset, delete the family, send feedback); Brag Card and weekly recap images to share.
  • The pond: draggable and zoomable, shows the kid's last 28 critters, and follows the real clock (dawn, day, dusk, night). Reeds are emoji today.
  • The critters: 609 species, all drawn by code as small vector pictures from a seed, with gradient shading, 10 patterns, 8 eye styles, accessories by rarity. 4 rarities (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary; Rare and Legendary glow, Legendary wears a crown), 10 colour morphs (Classic, Golden, Azure, Rose, Jade, Ember, Violet, Aqua, Shadow, Albino), shiny ones, and 40 evolution tiers from Hatchling to Apex reached by mixing critters together.
  • Calm mode hides the collecting game (just chores and one cute critter). Read aloud speaks chores and critters for kids who cannot read yet.
  • Look today: mint and teal on cream (#E9F4F1 background, #3FA7A1 teal, #23413E ink, #FFC24B gold), fonts Fredoka and Nunito. The app icon is a painted lime frog resting in a pea pod hammock on magenta, with a moon and stars.

How to get in for the review: on pom-pond.web.app tap Start, make a family with a throwaway email, set a PIN, add one kid, do a chore, watch the hatch, open the Dex, open the parent screen with the PIN, open Settings. In a second browser, join as that kid with the join code and kid code from Settings. When you are done, delete the family in Settings (two taps and the PIN); that deletes the throwaway account too.

3. What is weak, honestly (go past these)

  • Almost nothing is painted. The pond's reeds, the member avatars and the confetti are emoji; the buckets, the Pom icon and the timer are simple vector shapes.
  • Two looks: the frog in a pea pod brand lives only in the app icon; inside, the app is mint and teal with no character at all.
  • A kid's own device lands on the shared "Who's here" lobby instead of going straight to their pond.
  • Daily reminders are built but hidden until a push key is set up.
  • Nothing on a parent's first screen says what the buckets are for.
  • It has never been tested on a real iPhone or Android phone end to end.

4. Part one: the full check and the polish list

The studio's ask is "make sure everything connects and accounts work properly, then polish". What we want from you: (a) walk every flow as a parent, a kid on their own phone, a co parent and a cheerleader, and tell us everything confusing, broken, slow or missing; (b) whether it is ready for Google Play as an app for families, and what Play's families rules would want to see first; (c) polish: UI, feel, onboarding, the moments that delight a kid; (d) one art direction that joins the frog in a pea pod brand to the inside of the app; (e) what brings a family back every day without nagging, guilt or slot machine tricks.

Rules that do not bend here: the critters stay drawn by code. There are 609 species and every critter a kid already owns must look exactly the same forever (a test enforces it), so do not redesign critters as pictures. Notes on their shading or proportions are welcome as directions. Free app, no ads, nothing ever sold to a kid. Kid screens must work for a child who cannot read (icons plus Read aloud). Calm, warm and kind beats loud.

The bar every direction must respect: portrait phone first, 412 x 915 is the main viewport (also check 360 and 375 wide); every touch target at least 48 px; no text under 0.7 rem; player facing copy never uses dashes of any kind and never uses exclamation marks; every colour choice must work for colour blind players (say how: shape, icon or pattern, never colour alone); Sky Wolf Studio, singular, is the studio name. Be specific to what you saw on the live page: name the screen and the spot.

Format, strictly: one numbered block per direction: Title / What and why (3 lines max) / Where on screen / Who it affects (parent, kid, co parent, cheerleader) / Effort S, M or L / Priority 1 to 3. Group them under these headings: Accounts and joining, Kid screens, Parent screens, Onboarding, Art and look, Retention, Google Play readiness, Bugs you saw. End the document with a json block: an array of objects with keys id, title, group, who, effort, priority, summary. No prose after the json.

5. Part two: the art pack

5.1 Style

Your call, within this: soft picture book painting (gouache or watercolour feel), rounded friendly shapes, warm light, readable on a phone at 32 to 96 px. It must sit happily beside the code drawn critters (bright, rounded, soft gradient shading): put two real critters from the app next to your samples before you commit. Grow the frog in a pea pod brand into the app: the pond, the buckets, the hatch should feel like the same world as the icon. Kid friendly but never babyish; a parent should like having it on their phone.

5.2 Rules for every file

  • PNG with a transparent background for every sprite, prop, icon and overlay. If your tool cannot make true transparency, use a flat pure magenta #FF00FF background with no magenta anywhere inside the art, and say so in the manifest; we key it out. JPG only for full bleed plates.
  • Deliver at the sizes given. Never a side over 1600 px.
  • Animations: one picture per frame, named like cheer_01.png, cheer_02.png. Every frame of a set on the same canvas size, the subject centred, the feet or base on the same line in every frame, the same scale across every set of one character. Characters face RIGHT; we mirror for left.
  • No text baked into any picture, except a logo or wordmark where the list asks for one.
  • No real trademarks, logos or known characters.
  • One light direction across the whole pack: upper left.
  • File names lowercase with underscores, exactly as listed. If you add something of your own, put it in a folder called extras/ and list it in the manifest.
