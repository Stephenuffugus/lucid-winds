WHAT LOAF IS
The virtual copy of YOUR cat. You photograph your cat, the app reads her coat from the photo, and you meet a living
3D version of her that you hang out with every day: a card with her stats, a room where she lives, toys, a laser, a
fishing pond, toe beans to press, a daily ritual. The moat is "essence capture": the owner looks at the 3D cat and
feels "that's her". The test the whole plan is built around is the Two Cat Test: put the owner's cat beside a
stranger's and the owner must pick theirs instantly, without labels.

THE HARD LINES (they bind the art too)
- Comfort first. Nothing in the room dies, gets sick, runs away or is punished. A need that runs low is a reason to
  do something, never an accusation: no alarm red, no sad cat, no "you neglected her".
- Coat, never breed. The app never names a breed. Draw cats by coat (black, tabby, calico, tuxedo...), never as a
  breed.
- Chonk never reads as body condition. A big cat is a big cat, never a health warning.
- "Days together" only counts up. No streak that breaks.
- The owner's PHOTO stays the hero of the card.
- The cat is the hero of every scene. Props are supporting cast: soft, rounded, muted, nothing sharp, nothing that
  implies harm, illness, confinement or absence.

PART 1. THE ASSESSMENT (write it first; it decides the rest)
Walk every screen in screens/ against the plan, as a cat owner opening the app for the first time and then as one
coming back on day 30. Then write:
  a. ASSESSMENT.md:
     - What already reads and should be kept.
     - What does not read, confuses, or looks unfinished, screen by screen. Be blunt: the 3D room today is a dark empty
       space with no floor, walls or furniture; the toy and ritual icons are small emoji like glyphs; there is no app
       icon or store art. Find the rest.
     - Anything that breaks a hard line visually (a colour that alarms, a prop that implies harm, a stat that reads as
       weight shaming).
     - The smoothing plan: a numbered list of changes, each with its size (S, M, L), what it depends on, and whether it
       is art (yours) or code (ours). Order it by what most raises "that's her" and "I want to come back tomorrow".
     - The three biggest risks you see to Loaf selling, and what you would do about each.
  b. STYLE-GUIDE.md (or a PDF with the same name): the look Loaf should ship with. Keep the dark "field unit"
     look, evolve it, or replace it, and say why. Palette (hex), type (Google Fonts only), icon grammar (stroke or fill,
     corner radius, the grid), illustration style for the room and the fish, how the five rarities differ, how motion
     should feel. Show it on at least the card and the room.

PART 2. THE ASSETS (in this order; send what you finish if you cannot finish all)
Formats: icons and UI marks as SVG (flat, they must read at 24 px and at 48 px), illustrations and plates as PNG at
3x with transparent backgrounds where they float, textures as tileable PNG. Names lowercase_with_underscores. A
manifest.json lists every file: file, id, kind (icon, card, plate, prop, texture, illustration, store), size, notes.

  A. Identity: the LOAF wordmark; the app icon (512 square with no transparency for the store, a 512 maskable with
     the safe zone respected, a 192); the store feature graphic 1024x500; a splash for the app's first frame.
  B. The card: a frame for each of the five rarities (each rarer one reads rarer at a glance; FOIL and MYTHIC shine);
     the card back; the six stat icons; the six ability badges; the fifteen posture silhouettes for the shelf (one
     consistent silhouette style, a neutral coat, never a breed).
  C. The room: a cozy room for the 3D cat to live in, as plates the code can map in 3D: a floor (tileable), a back
     wall, a window, each for day, dusk and night. Then the sixteen furnishings of LOAF_FURNISHINGS.md, each as a
     clean three quarter view on a transparent ground at the scale noted there. These double as the input pictures for
     the 3D model generator we use, so draw each one alone, whole, evenly lit, nothing cropped.
  D. The toys and games: the eight toy icons and the Ability and Clip buttons; the laser dot and its glint; the
     rainbow yarn ball and its trail; the nine fish of the Fish Book as illustrations (each one a card in the book,
     the Old Boot included); the pond's water surface and its ripple; toe beans in close up (pink, black, mixed) for
     the Beans toy.
  E. The daily ritual and the bond: the five ritual step icons; the three need icons with their calm low state (amber
     "you could", never red "you failed"); bond level badges 1 to 10; a "days together" motif that only counts up.

WHAT NOT TO DRAW
- The cat herself as a finished character: the 3D cat is generated from the owner's own photo. Silhouettes, poses,
  icons and reference sketches of cats are welcome; a fixed mascot cat is not.
- Any breed name, any medical or weight cue, any sad, sick, hungry or lonely cat.
- Store screenshots: we compose those from the real app.
- The dog (Biscuit): it is planned only after the cat ships. If your style guide should leave room for a second
  species, say so in one line.
