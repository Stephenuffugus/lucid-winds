# LOAF / design review 01
**Decision for Stephen and Fable.** Give an owner one photo, one correction and one good catch before asking them to file anything. Keep the field-card voice. Move most of the fieldwork behind the card. The room should be the place where an owner recognizes a habit they see at home.

## Three commercial risks
1. **A stranger in the room.** Correct coat classification can still produce a generic cat. A tuxedo vs tabby comparison is too easy. Pair same-coat strangers and repeat after a single correction. Stop at phase 1 if recognition fails.
2. **A charming purchase that gets opened once.** A new checklist every morning gives an owner little to notice. Spend the first retention experiment on the toy invitation and yesterday’s memory; compare voluntary returns and interviews before adding more currencies.
3. **A one-dollar promise the listing cannot prove.** A polished icon cannot demonstrate likeness or sustainable scan costs. Use an opt-in owner’s real photo→avatar→habit capture; test the equivalent local price with actual purchase choices. Work from net receipt per sale less actual remote scan, support and refund costs. Track repeat rescans and offline fallback. Worldwide demand and profitable pricing remain hypotheses.

## The Two Cat Test to run before release

Recruit 20 owners as a first directional gate. Include several look-alike pairs (two tuxedos, two brown tabbies, two solid black cats) and mixed household photos. Record the owner's identifying feature in their words before generating anything. Fix camera angle, pose, room light and scale. Hide the photo, name, rarity, collar, title and stats. Randomize which side holds their cat. Give “neither looks right” as a real answer.

Record first choice, seconds to choice, confidence and cue named. Keep the raw denominator. Test the first scan, then permit one owner correction and retest against a different same-coat distractor. Keep the two scores separate. A proposed pilot gate is 16/20 correct within three seconds on the first scan, with no repeated failure pattern for dark coats or white markings. A favorable small pilot justifies testing more households. If recognition depends on one accessory or palette, fix the face and texture and rerun. Include a second photo of the same cat to expose lighting-dependent changes.

## What good play looks like on a phone

| Moment | Player action | Cat response and next invitation | Device check |
|---|---|---|---|
| First touch | Stroke shoulder or tap “Pet” | Eyes turn first, cheek leans into hand, then a slow blink. Let the owner repeat immediately. | With sound and vibration off, can four of five new owners point to how she responded? |
| Laser | Drag across open floor, stop within one body length | Cat follows moving dot, crouches during pause, pins the dot. Glint opens beyond paw as she lets it go. | Compare two pause windows (e.g. 350 and 650 ms). Count intentional catches and accidental ends. Cancelled touches settle the toy. |
| Beans | Choose a visible pad, then hold | One toe splays under that finger. Holding invites a second paw and a gentle knead. | At three screen widths the pad has a 44-unit hit area. Tap-only and reduced-motion modes give readable feedback. |
| Pond | Twitch, watch the cat, release | Ear and shoulders turn toward a widening ripple. A caught fish surfaces beside the paw and becomes one illustrated entry. | Muted phone, eyes obscured, one thumb. Can players explain what tells them to release? |
| Yarn | Flick toward the cat and wait | Paw redirects ball toward the player. Trail shows its path. A second bat begins another exchange. | A slow or short flick still reaches the cat. On an interrupted session the rope never loops around her neck. |
| Box | Put it near her and give her time | A sniff, one paw over the rim, a turn, then the overflow loaf. | Walk away midway. Returning leaves the room and any earned memory in a coherent state. |

**First session.** Photo → choose subject if needed → “Does this look like her?” with a visible change/accept choice → one pet or reachable catch → optional name and card. The player can stop after meeting the cat. Put the long quiz and full shelf behind explicit invitations.

**Day 30.** Reopen to a familiar cat approaching a previously chosen toy. Show one true, dated observation (“You filed The Loaf yesterday” only if recorded). Offer another filing, play, or quiet company. Check the same path after a seven-day gap, midnight rollover and two cats swapping rooms. A skipped day keeps the last filing, learned tricks and bond intact.

**Paid pilot.** Show the real photo, avatar and interaction in one short authorized capture. Ask people outside the studio's following to decide whether to spend the proposed $0.99. Count actual offers, purchases, refunds, voluntary returns on days 1 and 7, and owners who independently say what makes this their cat. Keep gift copies separate. A million copies is a sales goal. This pack provides no sales forecast.
