THE DECK IN m2 (exact; the rules are the truth, so draw to them)
- The objective, on the menu, in How to play and in the lesson's first beat: Get through the scene without anyone
  hearing you.
- Four pads in a 2 by 2 block across the bottom of the play screen, portrait. The upper row is the HANDS (left hand,
  right hand); the lower row is the CHEEKS (left cheek, right cheek). The left hand's two fingers work the left column,
  the right hand's the right column, until a call moves a hand across.
- The cheeks: hold both to keep it in. Pressure rises all the time. A cheek let go while no cover noise plays is a
  squeak: the room hears it, a strike. Three strikes and the room knows; the witnesses turn as in m1.
- The vent is letting go. When a cover noise plays (applause, thunder, a passing bus, a sneeze, church bells, a
  passing train, a coughing fit, dropped books, a siren, an organ swell, a crying baby; your banner and its countdown,
  as in m1), lift both cheek fingers: that is the vent. Lifting in the first 40 percent of the noise is a Perfect. A
  noise lasts about two to three seconds; the fingers go back down before it ends. There is no Vent button.
- The hands: the scene calls them. A hand pad lights and says what it wants in words with an icon (Hold the hymn
  book, Shake hands, Clap), and a ring counts down the time left to touch it (a few seconds, shorter in later acts).
  Touch it in time and keep holding while a second ring fills (a few seconds more) until the call lets go. A missed
  call, too late or let go before the ring filled, is a fidget the witnesses notice: a smaller penalty than a strike
  (DECISION 2, his, may make it a strike).
- The Twister, the act ramp: calls overlap, so later acts need three and then four fingers at once, a hand call during
  a vent, and a call that moves a hand from one pad to the other. Each pad's state is its own; any mix of the states
  below can be on screen at once.
- The lesson is the first scene and cannot be lost: one beat at a time, each waiting for the player, the words on the
  pads themselves plus one big line above them, never a paragraph. Beat 1, hold both cheeks. 2, a bus comes: let go
  now (the vent, a Perfect). 3, cheeks back. 4, a hand call: Hold the hymn book. 5, a hand call while a bus comes
  (three fingers). 6, you are ready: three strikes and the room knows.
- The table card comes before the first lesson and in How to play: your illustration, one line (Set your phone flat
  on a table and use both hands; the line is DECISION 5, his), one button (Got it).
- Sizes in CSS px, delivered at 2x. The block spans the width less 8 px a side, its pads 8 px apart. A pad is about
  194 by 140 on a 412 px phone (a Pixel 9), 168 by 130 at 360, 148 by 120 at 320 (never under 120 square) and up to
  268 by 170 at the page's 560 px desktop cap. The stage gives up height before a pad does. The play screen at 412 by
  915, approximately (your plates stretch, so the exact numbers are ours to keep):

THE HARD LINES (round 1's still bind; these are the ones this round touches)
- Not gross. A composure comedy: the joke is the straight face. The cheek pads are named in words and drawn as
  anything but a body (a seat, a cushion: your call); never anatomy, never toilet imagery, never a cloud. In this game
  any rising wavy line reads as a smell, so no steam on the coffee, no smoke on the candle, no scent lines on the
  bouquet.
- A finger covers the middle of a held pad. With the phone flat on a table the finger comes from below, so the
  fingertip and the finger behind it hide about 70 CSS px of the pad's lower middle. Every state reads at the rim and
  the upper edge, around a fingertip.
- Never by colour alone: every state carries a shape or a mark as well (a lit rim, a ring, a cross, a check, a change
  of depth). Silver Screen keeps the state shapes.
- The four theatre palettes tint your raster plates through the art filter, as in round 1. Draw them once, in Matinee
  Sepia, from your round 1 tokens (paper, sage, deep, ink, accent, relief, danger).
- Formats as round 1: icons and marks as SVG (48 unit viewBox, 3 unit rounded strokes, open internal spaces, one
  currentColor, no text nodes, no images, no filters; read at 24 and 48 px); raster UI art as PNG at 2x, transparent
  where it floats; plates and illustrations as WebP or JPG; nothing over 1600 px a side; a plate under 200 KB, a
  sprite under 150 KB; names lowercase_with_underscores.
- Phone first, portrait, 360 to 430 px wide, and 320 must still work. Every target 48 px; the pads are far over it.
- Copy: spare and wry; no exclamation marks; no dashes of any kind; no text inside plates. The game sets every word:
  the calls, Hold, Let go now, the lesson's lines, the table card's line.
- No real product, brand or person, no mascot. The phone in the table card is no real model: no logo, no camera
  layout anyone could name.
- Religious objects (the hymn book, the rosary, folded hands) are drawn plainly and with respect; the joke is never on
  a faith, the mourners or the dead.
- Never rename or replace a round 1 file. Every round 2 file has a new name.
- Generated art is never called hand painted.
