WHAT FLATULENCE FIGHTER IS
A silent film comedy of composure, one HTML file, played with two thumbs in portrait. You are a person in a room where
no sound is allowed: a funeral, a job interview, a first date, live on air, silent yoga, wedding vows, a library, an
elevator, a recital, a confession, a meditation retreat, a baby's nap. Twelve scenes, played as acts with an
intertitle card between them (Act I, The Funeral, "Do not make a sound."). Pressure only ever climbs. You hold both
pads to clench it in; each hand tires as its bar fills, so you lift one to rest it and swap before it slips. When a
cover noise hits (applause, thunder, a passing bus, a sneeze, church bells, a passing train, a coughing fit, dropped
books, a siren, an organ swell, a crying baby) you tap VENT to let it out under the noise, and venting the instant the
noise starts is a perfect. Leaks bubble up on the face and you tap them to plug them. Three abilities: a fake cough
(make your own cover, charges), a sip of water (hold to drain pressure, but both hands leave the pads), a waft (fan
away a fresh strike inside a short window). A breath bar: hold your breath to slow the rise, let go before you gasp.
A posture track: you drift in your seat and lean to stay still; hit the edge and you shift (a squeak, a leak). Any
sound the room hears is a strike, and the room reacts in a line of text ("The widow turns to look."). Three strikes
and you are exposed. A whiff warning, combo callouts (COMPOSED, ICE COLD, UNTOUCHABLE, INHUMAN), a score, Sunbeams
(the arcade's currency) paid on a run.
Modes: Stage (the classic bill, on Easy, Normal or Panic), Daily (today's seeded ordeal, with a streak), Zen (no
strikes), One Cheek (one pad benched, swapping every fifteen seconds). Eighteen medals. A wardrobe of five characters
(The Everyman, The Gentleman, Grandma, The Mime, The Bulldog) and four theatre palettes (Matinee Sepia, Midnight
Show, Technicolor, Silver Screen), every one earned by play at a threshold printed on its locked card, nothing bought.
A how to play card, a tutorial, pause, stats, medals, and a game over card (Exposed, Mortified, Undone at last, or
Curtain Call when you bow out of Zen).
It lives at lucidwinds.com/satellites/flatulence-fighter/ in the Sky Wolf Studio arcade, open to players: 114 KB,
every pixel drawn by CSS and one inline SVG face, every sound synthesised. It was built in July 2026 from Stephen's
own earlier draft, which was called "Try Not To Fart" (the line on the menu is ours; see the risks).
The plan: your pack, then a Google Play listing of its own (a Trusted Web Activity of the live page, like Shutterbug),
priced like our other small games.

THE HARD LINES (they bind the art too)
- One HTML file, vanilla JavaScript, no frameworks, no build step. Art arrives as files the page loads (PNG, JPG,
  WebP, SVG) or as CSS values. Code keeps every CSS and SVG draw as a fallback behind an image loaded check.
- Phone first, portrait, 360 to 430 px wide, two thumbs; the page caps at 560 px wide on a desktop. Every target
  48 px, and the two HOLD pads are far bigger than that: they are the game.
- Every sound is synthesised in code. No sound files. Notes about sound are welcome; sounds are not.
- Not gross. This is a composure comedy: sweat, a blush, pursed lips, a face holding it in. Never toilet imagery,
  never a cloud, never anything a store reviewer could call crude. The joke is the straight face.
- No real brands, no real people, no other game's characters, no mascot. Nothing that reads as a specific product.
- Generated art is never called hand painted. No text in a plate except a wordmark and whatever a row asks for: the
  game sets every word.
- Player facing words use no dashes of any kind and no exclamation marks (the fleet's copy law). The game has a few
  today ("VENT!") and we are removing them; keep the law in any sample copy you write.
- Google Fonts only for type. Today the game uses a system serif stack (Iowan Old Style, Palatino, Georgia). If the
  look wants a 1920s title card face, name a Google Font and show it. Wordmarks arrive outlined.
- Never a side over 1600 px except the store sizes (the host resizes bigger files on the way out). Web files stay
  small: a plate under 200 KB, a sprite sheet under 150 KB.

WHAT IS WRONG TODAY (our read; find the rest)
- The player is a bald floating head on blank parchment. No shoulders, no body, no seat, no room. It reads as a
  balloon, not a person trying not to be noticed.
- All twelve scenes look the same. The only things that change are the chip's text and the intertitle. The Funeral
  and The Elevator are the same paper.
- The reaction lines print in red across the face. "The widow turns to look" and nothing turns. The room has no one
  in it, so the whole premise (people who would hear) is text.
- Every icon is an emoji: the three abilities, the cover banner, the breath bar, the medals, the wardrobe, the sound
  and haptics toggles, the pause button. On the test phone half of them are empty boxes; on real phones each is a
  different drawing.
- The intertitle is the menu card again. No silent film frame, no grain, no iris, nothing that says 1920s except the
  serif.
- The pads are two dark green tiles with LEFT HOLD and RIGHT HOLD in small caps, and VENT is a pink strip. The
  biggest controls in the game have the least character.
- The face's six expressions work (calm, nervous, strain, critical, blowout, relief, plus a flush and sweat drops),
  but they are thin SVG lines. Strain reads; nothing is funny yet.
- The wardrobe's characters are hats on the same head and the palettes are tints. The economy is sound, the art is
  not there.
- No identity: no wordmark, no app icon, no manifest. The arcade card is a cropped frame of paper and the social card
  is the fleet's generic one.
- Nothing after a run is worth showing anyone. The game over card is a table.

PART 1. THE ASSESSMENT (write it first; it decides the rest)
Walk every screen in screens/ as a thirteen year old who was sent the link by a friend, then as a player on day 30
chasing the last three medals. Then write:
  a. ASSESSMENT.md:
     - What already reads and should be kept (the two pad grip, the cover noise rhythm, the scene titles and their
       lines, the paper and sage, the medals' names?).
     - What does not read, confuses, or looks unfinished, screen by screen. Be blunt.
     - Anything that breaks a hard line (an emoji, a target under 48 px, a crude read, a brand).
     - The look: confirm Silent Reel from prior/ or argue for something else, and say why in one paragraph. Whatever
       you pick must survive the four palettes.
     - The smoothing plan: a numbered list, each with its size (S, M, L), what it depends on, and whether it is art
       (yours) or code (ours). Order by what most raises "this is a real game" and "I want to show someone".
     - Improvements worth building (code, ours), ranked by hook. Seeds: onlookers who turn on a strike; a cover noise
       that crosses the frame (the bus passes behind the window, the pram rolls by); a share card after a run
       ("Exposed at The Funeral, Act III, 47 seconds held"); a three panel comic reel of the run; more scenes; a pass
       the phone duel, who cracks first; a daily ordeal with a seed to send a friend; a silent film piano score,
       synthesised. Say which three you would ship first and why, and what each needs from the art.
     - The three biggest risks to this selling, and what you would do about each. One is ours to raise: on a store, a
       game about this reads as a kids' novelty unless the first screenshot says comedy of manners; another is the
       menu's "an homage to Try Not To Fart" line and the "Lucid Games presents" eyebrow, which must not go to a store
       (that name is Stephen's old draft, but it is also other people's apps).
     - Naming: Flatulence Fighter is the name on the arcade and Stephen likes it. Say whether it should stay for the
       store, draw its wordmark either way, and propose two alternates with a one line case each for the record.
     - The store read: what a Google Play reviewer and a parent see in the first screenshot, and what the six
       screenshots should show in what order.
  b. STYLE-GUIDE.md (or a PDF with the same name): the look the game ships with. Palette (hex, and how the four
     theatre palettes retint it), type (Google Fonts only), icon grammar (stroke or fill, how an icon reads at 24 and
     48 px, one colour so code can tint), surface rules (what a card is made of, what the pads are, what the stage
     floor is, what an intertitle is), motion (what pulses, what shakes, what never moves; the game shakes on a
     blowout and floods red on a strike today), the twelve scenes' identities, the five characters' silhouettes.
     Show it on at least the play screen (08) and the intertitle (07).

WHAT NOT TO DRAW

- Any real product, brand, person, or another game's character.
- Store screenshots: we compose those from the real game inside your frames.
- Sounds. Notes about sound (what a perfect vent should feel like, what the organ swell does) go in NOTES.md.
