HOW TO WORK (Stephen's standing order; it binds this pack)
- No tests. No checks. No validation scripts, no pixel audits, no scripts that prove your own files, no reports about
  reports. The art is the deliverable. Nobody reads a test log.
- Look at each sheet ONCE with your own eyes when it is done, fix what is wrong, move on. Never render again a thing
  that already reads.
- One assessment, written once. One contact sheet per section. One zip. No progress notes, no plans about plans.
- If something is ambiguous, pick the reading that ships and write one line about it in NOTES.md. Do not stop to ask.

WHAT DEWBALL IS
A 3D roll to grow game. You roll a sticky dew ball that picks up everything smaller than itself and grows from a
crumb to a planet. Left stick rolls the ball, right stick turns the camera, a dash button bursts forward; a hard hit
knocks your last pickups loose. Each world has a size goal and a clock, three stars, size gates that open as you
grow, movers that bump you around until you outgrow them, five named keepsakes to find, and size facts that fire as
you pass them ("you're as long as a blue whale"). Seven worlds, in order:
  1. Crumb Country, a giant picnic blanket: 4 cm to 24 cm.
  2. Toybox Peaks, a playroom in rings: 8 cm to 70 cm.
  3. Night Garden, a garden at night: 15 cm to 1.7 m.
  4. Bazaar Lane, a market town: 30 cm to 3.4 m.
  5. Starfall Bay, a beach and harbour: 60 cm to 16 m.
  6. The Whole World, a small planet you roll around: 45 cm to 22 m, three stars at about 108 m.
  7. Dream Meadow, endless, no clock, everything.
Sunbeams earned per run (the arcade's currency), cosmetic ball skins, a keepsake collection. One HTML file with
Three.js; the props are Meshy models built from Stephen's paid lane (over a thousand credits so far). Live at
https://lucidwinds.com/satellites/dewball/ : play it on a phone, in landscape (portrait works too), and take your own
captures of every world; that is the material for this pack.
What it shows today: the menu (a world row, Keepsakes, Skins, How to play, Install, Fullscreen, Sound, Motion, Invert
Y, the arcade exit), the world intro card (its name and line), the HUD (world, size, a bar to the goal, the goal, the
clock, a combo, a threat marker), the two sticks and the dash ring, the pause card, the result card (title, stars,
an unlock, the numbers, the buttons), the rotate tip, toasts. The icon is a generated ball; the social card is a
screenshot; the store line is "Roll a sticky ball through seven worlds and grow from a crumb to a planet."

THE HARD LINES
- Dewball is ours. Nothing may look like the famous console game this genre descends from: no royal father figure,
  no tiny caped prince, no rainbow cosmos, no star making, no cousins, no fanfare lettering, none of its colours or
  its type. Dewball's own world is morning dew and small things made huge: a drop of water that gathers a picnic,
  then a town, then a planet.
- The props are models from Stephen's lane. Do not draw props for the game to use. You may draw props in store and
  key art, as illustration, where no real capture goes.
- Store screenshots are real captures composed inside your frames, by us. Draw the frames, never a fake screenshot.
- Copy: spare, warm, a little wry; no exclamation marks; no dashes of any kind in anything a player reads; no text
  inside plates except the wordmark and what a row asks for.
- Formats: icons and marks as SVG (24 unit viewBox, one currentColor ink, readable at 24 and 48 px); raster UI art as
  PNG at 2x with transparent backgrounds where it floats; plates as WebP or JPG; store art at the exact sizes named,
  opaque; nothing over 1600 px a side except store sizes; names lowercase_with_underscores; a manifest.json listing
  every file (file, id, kind, size, notes) and an anchors.json with every window, band and nine slice inset.
- One light, chosen once and written down: morning, low and warm, so every world, card and frame agrees.
- Every target 48 px. Landscape is the recommended way to play; every screen still reads in portrait.

WHAT NOT TO DRAW
- Anything that could be mistaken for the other game (see THE HARD LINES).
- Props for the game, or a ball surface the game should use (the ball and its skins are code and models).
- Store screenshots, and text in plates beyond the wordmark and what a row asks for.
- Sounds (notes on what growing, a keepsake and a gate opening should sound like are welcome in NOTES.md).
- Any real product, brand or person. No mascot.
