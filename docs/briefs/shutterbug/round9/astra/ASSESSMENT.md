# Shutterbug — Round 9 assessment

The five guardians need different outlines before their attacks can feel different. This pack separates them by construction: a low moth carrying a pitched shed, a jagged ice crown around a dark core, a spreading anemone with several mouths, an open thorn knot around a seed, and a square brick kiln with a chimney. Their pieces remain aligned to the existing 80 × 64 body canvas and 16-pixel erosion grid.

I opened the requested live game and played its available sample room. The title identified itself as **build sb m2 11** and offered photo/sample play, Album and Trophies; it did not expose the brief’s Darkroom house route. I therefore did **not** reach or observe a guardian fight. The sample showed why strong contours matter: the small characters sit against a busy, pale room photograph. This assessment uses that observed room presentation and the supplied brief for guardian behaviour; it does not claim a completed house run.

The new attack props use different motion silhouettes: airborne dust, a horizontal ice cone, a rising floor tentacle, a rotating seed and a low flame. Each has a separate anticipation mark. Shove art stays a thin, hollow perimeter, so the warning does not cover the body or masquerade as a solid wall.

The ten new enemy kinds use distinct anatomy and poses rather than colour swaps. Both normal and elite sizes are supplied for each kind, with the explicitly requested idle, move, hurt and dead states. The old `jelly` key is preserved by naming the new deep-water creature `lamp_jelly`.

Room pieces communicate their motion through moving belt cleats, a shelf that loses its support, a lamp pivoted from one fixed ceiling point, and a sparse gust column. These are art and import metadata for the developers; collision, attack timing, erosion state, AI and room selection remain game-owned.

Delivery follows A–E, with native PNG strips, editable indexed source grids, a manifest and one contact sheet in each folder.
