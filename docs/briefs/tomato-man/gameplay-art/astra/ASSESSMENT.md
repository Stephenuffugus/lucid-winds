# Tomato Man gameplay art

The seed toss adds a useful choice: make the next safe foothold, pin a pursuer, or interrupt the Angry Sun. Keep those outcomes visually related through one seed and one recognisable two-leaf sprout. The art must make the landing point and temporary shade readable before surface texture matters.

I played Dawn Ribbon in the live browser, threw a seed, saw its sapling, and used movement around the awnings. The SEED control is beside DASH and currently uses a large seed-and-sprout placeholder on a slate circle. The live rules describe three seeds per level, checkpoint refills, a three-second sapling, and a two-second critter pin. The visible sprout and code-drawn seed button have less material detail than the installed puppet art.

This session had a desktop browser, not a physical phone. Phone emulation was unavailable under the browser's policy. Midday Blaze and Dunes at Dusk were locked in this session; I did not encounter their critters directly. Their behaviour and dimensions below follow the supplied gameplay brief and live rules, rather than an invented late-world playthrough. Contact sheets use a real Dawn Ribbon capture, with the proposed new art explicitly labelled as a composite.

The scorpion should read through a dark clay silhouette, pale joint highlights, open pincers and a tail that lifts before charging. Its charge must change posture as well as colour. The mirage should read as a hollow, broken orange echo with no green calyx, black limbs or orange boots rendered as solid costume pieces. Both threats stay distinguishable on the five light terrain palettes.

The sapling shade remains a radius-58 gameplay circle. The art supplies its soft visual surface, never a replacement safety mask. The interrupt burst uses seed husk and pale green flecks, not an explosion or damage wound. Pinned critters remain visibly present under the same living sprout; they are restrained, not killed.
