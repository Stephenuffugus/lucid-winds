TOMATO MAN, THE WALK: FOUR WALK CELLS THAT ALTERNATE THE FEET, IN ALL FOURTEEN BODIES (7 Nov 2026)

Stephen played the hero round on his phone and said the walk still looks a little wonky. Fable measured the walk
cells in your sheets (the foot that touches the ground in each cell) and found the cause in the art, not the code:
- tomato_s.webp (south): cells 1 to 4 put the RIGHT foot down, then LEFT, LEFT, RIGHT. Played in order, two steps in
  a row land on the same foot: a limp.
- tomato_n.webp (north): LEFT, LEFT, RIGHT, RIGHT. The same limp, and north is the way he walks most (every level
  climbs).
- tomato_e.webp, tomato_se.webp, tomato_ne.webp (east, south east, north east): the cells are contact, pass,
  contact, pass, which is the right shape, but the SAME foot leads at both contacts (the near leg both times), so
  every step is the same step: a gallop.
The code now plays south as 1,2,4,3 and north as 1,3,2,4 so the feet alternate there; the gallop in the three side
views cannot be fixed by reordering. This round redraws the four walk cells as one strict cycle, in the tomato's five
sheets and in the sixty five produce sheets you composited on his rig. Fable wires your zip from the zip alone, so
write as if no one can ask you a follow up.

THE CYCLE (the same in every bearing)
  cell 1  CONTACT LEFT: the left foot forward and planted, the right foot back, legs apart.
  cell 2  PASS: the right leg swinging through under him, feet close, the body a touch higher (2 cell px at most).
  cell 3  CONTACT RIGHT: the mirror step, the right foot forward and planted, the left back.
  cell 4  PASS: the left leg swinging through.
  The forward foot at a contact stands about 96 cell px (256 px cells; 24 world px) ahead of the rear foot, so the
  code's 14 world px of ground per frame reads as walking, not skating. The head, the body's centre and the hat seat
  keep the same x and y in all four cells (no side to side wobble; the bob only in the pass cells, 2 px at most), so the
  hat does not swim. Left and right mean the character's own left and right: in the south sheet his left foot is on the
  viewer's right. In the side views (e, se, ne) the far leg is drawn a little darker, as your sheets already do, and it
  must be the LEADING leg in one contact and the trailing leg in the other.
