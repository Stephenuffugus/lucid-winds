TOMATO MAN, THE RUN: ART FOR THE NEW MAIN MODE (7 Nov 2026)

Stephen, 7 Nov: "Tomato man needs some enemies and something to work towards ... it's almost like a top scrolling
shooter so maybe we need to be shooting as we go or avoiding something else ... I love the way it looks." So the game
gets a new first mode, THE RUN. It is designed, not built: Stephen wants the art in hand before the build, so you draw
from this brief and your own packs, not from a live screen. Your job is the real art, in the look of your v2 pack and
your gameplay pack (the seed, the sapling, the scorpion, the mirage are yours already and stay), and your ideas first.
Fable wires your zip from the zip alone, so write as if no one can ask you a follow up.

HOW TO WORK (Stephen's standing order; it binds this pack)
- No tests. No checks. No validation scripts, no pixel audits, no reports about reports. The art is the deliverable.
- Look at each sheet ONCE with your own eyes when it is done, fix what is wrong, move on.
- One assessment, written once. One contact sheet per section. One zip. If something is ambiguous, pick the reading
  that ships and write one line about it in NOTES.md. Do not stop to ask.

THE RUN IN ONE PARAGRAPH (not live yet; this brief is the truth. Play the live game at
https://lucidwinds.com/satellites/tomato-man/ on a phone for the look, the seed toss and the critters; Shadow Run there
is the old endless mode and shows the scale of the sand, the awnings and the hero.) The screen climbs on its own. A band of hard light, THE BURN LINE, rises from the foot of the screen; below it he burns whatever
the shade. Shade is staggered left and right up the sand (awnings, umbrellas, carts, clouds) and he zigzags up it.
Holding the right side of the screen fires seeds up the screen three a second; a seed that lands sprouts a sapling
and its small round shade, so shooting ahead lays the road; a seed pins a critter and a second seed sends it under.
Five critters live in the light: your scorpion and mirage, and three new ones below. Every mile (1,200 px of climb)
THE SUN FLARES for twenty seconds (the Angry Sun's lunge ring, from your v2 pack), then three seconds of eclipse dark,
and the world steps on: Morning Tide, Midday Blaze, Tide Pools, Dunes at Dusk, Eclipse, then round again. A burn ends
the run: distance in metres, stars, critters pinned, the world reached, a best to beat, five world badges on the title.

THE UNITS (as your gameplay pack): 2 source px per world px; every anchor in anchors.json in SOURCE px from the file's
top left; discrete frames in horizontal strips, no gutters, never trimmed; a zero duration is a state picked by code.

YOUR IDEAS FIRST (write before you draw; one line each, up to ten, in ASSESSMENT.md)
Stephen's words: "I don't know what the objective needs to be." The design above is Fable's answer; yours is wanted
beside it. What should he be shooting at, what should he be running from, what is worth reaching, what would make a
player start one more run. Name enemies, hazards, pickups, a goal, a twist on the burn line, anything, each in one
line with how it plays. Stephen reads these; nobody builds them without his yes. Then draw the pieces below as
written, so the build has its art whatever he picks.

THE PIECES
A. THE CRITTERS (top down, drawn facing right where they turn; dark enough to read on every world's light sand, as
   your scorpion is)
   A1 the SUN MOTE (key mote): an ember the sun spits down the screen, 12 world px, a glowing core with a short tail
      trailing up (it falls down the screen). Four frames of flicker at 80 ms. It cannot be shot; it is light. No face.
   A2 the GULL (key gull): a beach gull, about 40 world px wingspan, seen from above, that swoops across the screen.
      Fly (four frames at 10 fps), swoop (two frames, wings back, when it dives at a sapling), hit (two frames when a
      seed strikes it, feathers), and a knocked off pose (one frame, tumbling away). It snatches a sapling in its beak:
      a one frame carry pose with your two leaf sprout in the beak.
   A3 the SAND CRAB (key crab): about 28 world px, walks sideways along the edge of a shadow. Walk (four frames at
      10 fps, sideways scuttle), pinned (two frames, your sprout on its back, as the scorpion's pin), flipped (two
      frames on its back, legs in the air, the state after three seeds; it stays).
   A4 a mark for each in your one colour icon grammar: tm-mote, tm-gull, tm-crab (for the cue line and How to play).
B. THE SAND AND THE LIGHT
   B1 THE BURN LINE (key burn_line): a horizontal band 64 world px tall that tiles across the screen, the hard light
      catching up: bleached, shimmering, the sand below it washed to white. Four frames of shimmer at 100 ms. One
      strip per world palette (five), or one strip the code tints (say which in NOTES.md).
   B2 the SEED POD (key seed_pod): a pickup, 16 world px, a dry pod that holds three seeds; a glint frame for the
      pickup sparkle (two frames).
C. THE RUN'S FACE
   C1 the title's RUN plate: the button that starts a run, 66 px tall in the set of your DASH and SHADE buttons' rim and
      ink, wide enough for the word RUN and a best distance under it (both in code).
   C2 five WORLD BADGES (keys run_badge_0 to run_badge_4), 48 px, one per world in that world's palette (a sunrise, a
      high sun, a tide pool, a dune at dusk, an eclipse), with a dark unlit state each.
   C3 the run card's header line art: the postcard frame from your v2 pack is reused; give us a 320 by 40 px band
      ornament for THE SUN WON (the words in code) if it reads better than the bands you drew; else say so.
   C4 milestone marks at 500, 1,000, 2,000, 3,000, 5,000 m: five small 24 px marks in the icon grammar (tm-mile).

SOUND
No audio files; the game synthesises. One line each in NOTES.md on how a mote, a gull and a crab should sound.

DELIVERY
ONE zip named tomato-man-astra-run.zip: ASSESSMENT.md (short), manifest.json (every file: file, id, kind, size, notes,
grid, durations), anchors.json (every anchor and frame cell in each file's own pixels), assets/ (a_critters, b_sand,
c_face), previews/ (one contact sheet per section, on a real capture of the run), NOTES.md. WebP for the sheets, under
1600 px a side. Stephen uploads it.
