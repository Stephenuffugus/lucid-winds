# Ideas for Stephen

1. Make reaching the first eclipse the clear first goal: five world badges form a visible journey, then the next lap becomes the score chase.
2. Let a rescued sapling count toward a small end-card garden, so even a short failed run leaves a record of something saved.
3. Give the gull a brief beak dip before stealing a plant, making a well-aimed seed feel like a rescue rather than random shooting.
4. Use crabs as deliberate route choices: spend three seeds to clear a shadow edge or save ammunition and cross elsewhere.
5. Arrange unshootable motes in sparse falling gates, asking the player to steer instead of holding fire through every threat.
6. Put occasional seed pods on the sunny side of a safe route, turning replenishment into a readable risk rather than a compulsory detour.
7. Show the previous best as a quiet sand marker, making one more safe crossing a concrete reason to replay.
8. During an eclipse, briefly show the next three shade islands so the darkness becomes a planning reward before the next world.
9. Let the burn line hesitate for one beat at each world change, providing a visual breath without removing the chase.
10. Offer a daily fixed route beside the random run, so friends can compare the same gull, crab and shade decisions.

These are proposals for Stephen, not additional mechanics assumed by the art.

# Assessment

The existing pale sand, faded cloth, slate shade and small red hero make a strong readable stage. THE RUN should keep the sand quiet: dark crab shells, charcoal gull wing tips and compact ember cores give each moving threat its own silhouette. The burn line needs a crisp leading boundary and washed-out ground behind it, visibly different from safe blue-grey shade. A hollow unlit badge and a filled earned badge can express progress without relying on colour alone.

The supplied design is authoritative for THE RUN. The live review uses Dawn Ribbon, its seed control and the old Shadow Run for visual scale; the new mode is not live. Only a desktop browser is available here, so this is not a physical-phone play review. Capture-backed previews distinguish new art overlays from the live game. Existing v2 and gameplay art, including the two-leaf sprout, stays in use.
