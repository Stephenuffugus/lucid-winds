# Sunforge Round 2 • the blocks
Astra • 6 November 2026

## The live board
I played the current Journey build, used turn, hold, movement and SLAM, and made a board drag and tap. The opening guided ring cleared and showed 1,208 points with 1/2 rings. A later run reached 1,328. The restart produced the seven-ring, partly built capture used for the comparisons. I am assessing art here, not certifying input timing or code changes. The browser stage was portrait; this was not Stephen's physical Pixel.

The new shell works: the steel stage, large acid pedal, fixed HUD and core all belong together. The blocks do not. The broad flat faces, repeated central stamps and dark outlines make each little square compete for attention. Even cells of the same ring read as a row of labels rather than one material being fed to the sun. The falling slash tiles are conspicuous, but feel like placeholders alongside the machine.

## Three directions, compared on the captured board
The three contact sheets show the same live seven-ring arrangement at a phone-like 390 px stage width, plus close views of the material and state cues. They are art-direction overpaints of the live capture, not screenshots of installed replacement assets. The highlighted charged band/notch is a proposed-state illustration; it must only trigger on a genuinely almost-complete ring in the game. The source capture is included.

### 1. Forged slugs
Chunky metal faces, broad hammered facets and heat-coloured weld boundaries. Falling cells are bright cold metal. Adjacent slugs merge into substantial metal strips.

**What works:** strong occupancy and a useful distinction between cold piece and hot structure. The chunky surface is legible without tiny details.

**Why it loses:** the rendered broad colourful facets drift toward polished gems. It is a richer version of a coloured block, but still risks the visual family Stephen wants to leave.

See `previews/direction_01_forged_slugs_contact.jpg`.

### 2. Obsidian shards • selected
A charcoal iron-glass body with broad ash facets and a molten fracture through it. Ring colour comes from the contained sun, not paint applied to a square. Fractures connect through same-ring neighbours; a boundary weld closes the band where the neighbouring ring changes. Falling pieces remain intact cold silver until the half-second resting interval heats them.

**What works:** the largest change from v1. The board looks like the machine's material, and a bright continuous fracture can reveal a square band without twelve flat colour swatches. The quiet face keeps colour and energy concentrated. The silver active piece has an immediate material distinction.

**Risk and response:** a thin crack alone disappears in daylight. Production uses a broad coloured shoulder under a bright vein, a visible neutral upper face, and an opaque charcoal body. Keep small-cell silhouette contrast; do not turn the whole face nearly black or add a forest of hairlines. Ordinary cell joins have a very low-contrast seam. No big center stamps in normal mode.

See `previews/direction_02_obsidian_shards_contact.jpg`.

### 3. Ember tiles
Scorched ceramic, with heat concentrated at the outline of each connected band. The face is quiet and the perimeter glows.

**What works:** the exterior ring silhouette is especially easy to follow. The old core and steel shell remain compatible.

**Why it loses:** too close to the original rimmed-square language. In the overpaint, the repeating soft chips also suggest padded or baked tiles, which weakens the sharp Sunbreaker attitude. The selected stone has more bite.

See `previews/direction_03_ember_tiles_contact.jpg`.

## The pick
**Obsidian shards, with daylight-strength veins.** Keep the game named SUNFORGE and the overall Sunbreaker identity. The one-sentence image is “the sun trapped inside broken stone.” A landed cell is dark mass carrying heat. A falling cell is cold intact silver. A resting cell warms before it joins the fracture network. A completed ring collapses into dark chips and light, so the material explains the action rather than merely decorating it.

The production set removes ordinary center stamps and full rectangular cell frames, retains the twelve ring indices, and uses seam and boundary overlays to join neighbours. The current HUD, controls, typography, background and all other v1 screens stay. The hero artwork is changed only for the requested arcade thumbnail and social card.
