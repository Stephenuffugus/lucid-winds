SUNFORGE, THE WHOLE PACK FOR ASTRA (5 Nov 2026)

Stephen: "I want one for Sunforge. We need a really cool, popular, catchy and edgy theme and aesthetic to build upon,
and it will be great."

This pack starts one step earlier than the others. Sunforge has a mechanic and no identity. Your first job is to GIVE
IT ONE: propose three directions, recommend one, and build the recommended one out to the last button. The theme must
be cool, popular, catchy and edgy, in his words: something a sixteen year old would screenshot, something that looks
like now and not like a 2015 puzzle app, with an edge to it (dark, loud, a little dangerous), and a hook you can say in
three words. Then produce the art.
Fable wires your zip into the game with Stephen from the zip alone, so write as if no one can ask you a follow up.

HOW TO WORK (Stephen's standing order; it binds this pack)
- No tests. No checks. No validation scripts, no pixel audits, no scripts that prove your own files, no reports about
  reports. The last rounds of work on other games spent most of their time on tests that proved nothing, and he pays
  for every minute of it. The art is the deliverable. Nobody reads a test log.
- Look at each sheet ONCE with your own eyes when it is done, fix what is wrong, move on. Never re-render a thing that
  already reads.
- One assessment, written once. One contact sheet per section. One zip. No progress notes, no plans about plans.
- If something is ambiguous, pick the reading that ships and write one line about it in NOTES.md. Do not stop to ask.

WHAT SUNFORGE IS
A falling piece puzzle built around a sun. One HTML file, canvas 2D, a 540 by 960 virtual stage, portrait, one thumb.
The board is a square grid with a golden core at its centre. Seven piece shapes of four cells each (the I, O, T, S, Z,
J and L you know) fall from the top. A piece STICKS when it lands on the core or on anything already stuck; sides never
grab, so a falling piece slides past the structure into deeper gaps; the floor and walls hold nothing; miss everything
and the piece drifts off into the void below. You grow the structure outward from the core into square RINGS. Fill
every cell of a ring and it erases, and everything outside crushes inward one ring. Chains score big. Every locked
cell is drawn in the colour of the ring it sits on (its distance from the core), not of the piece it came from, so when
a ring clears the whole gyre visibly flows one colour band inward toward the sun. Twelve ring colours out from the
core, in today's pastel code: gold, coral, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, violet, pink, lavender, mint, sand.
SLAM drops the piece instantly and spins the whole gyre a quarter turn as it lands. That is how the structure grows
sideways and underneath: build a tower upward, slam to swing it around the sun. Set a piece down gently and nothing
spins. Controls: left, right, rotate (or tap the board), nudge down, SLAM (a big gold bar), hold. Previews: next, the
one after, hold. A ghost piece option. Topping out clears the sky.
Modes: Journey (levels asking for more rings, faster each time), Daily Gyre (the same piece run for everyone, once a
day), Zen (never ends). A score, a best, a ring count, a level; a game over card with Again, Share, Menu; settings
(sound, ghost piece, add to home screen); it earns Sunbeams, the arcade's currency.
It lives at https://lucidwinds.com/satellites/ring-stacker/ (the folder keeps its old name; the game was Ring Stacker,
and one install alert still says so, which is a code fix) in the Sky Wolf Studio arcade, open to players, installable,
offline, 57 KB. Everything on screen is drawn by code: rounded pastel tiles, a radial gradient halo for the core, a
gradient text wordmark on a flat navy page.

WHAT IS WRONG TODAY
- The title screen is a hole: the word SUNFORGE in a teal to gold to rose gradient in the middle of flat navy, four
  lines of instructions, 140 px of nothing, then a gold Journey button and two rows of dark buttons that read as holes
  cut in the page. No art, no board, no sun.
- The core, the thing the whole game is about, is a soft radial blur. The pieces are flat rounded rectangles in
  nursery pastels. A landed structure looks stacked, not forged, not welded, not anything.
- Rings clearing and crushing inward is the best moment in the game and it is a colour shift with a few shards.
- The HUD fonts render at 6 and 8 real px on a phone (code fix). SLAM is the biggest control and it is a yellow bar
  with the word on it.
- No identity at all: no wordmark worth the name, placeholder app icons, a social card that is a screenshot of pastel
  tiles.

THE HARD LINES
- One HTML file, vanilla JavaScript, canvas 2D, no frameworks. Art arrives as PNG with alpha (cells, pieces, FX, HUD
  parts), JPG or WebP (plates), SVG (marks). Code keeps the drawn fallback behind an image loaded check.
- Phone first, portrait. A cell is 24 to 30 px on screen; the board shows seven cells each way around the core, so
  fifteen across. Every ring colour must be told from its neighbours at that size, and a piece must be told from the
  structure while it falls. Dark grounds are welcome; the board must still read in daylight.
- Every target 48 px. SLAM stays huge.
- Whatever the theme, this is a general audience arcade. Edgy means attitude, contrast and a bit of menace: no gore,
  no drugs, no weapons pointed at people, no slurs, no real brands, no real logos, nothing a store reviewer flags.
- Generated art is never called hand painted. No text in plates except the wordmark and what a row asks for. No
  dashes or exclamation marks in sample copy. Google Fonts only (name it; a display face is part of the theme). Never
  a side over 1600 px except store sizes; web files small (a plate under 200 KB, a sheet under 150 KB).
- The arcade's own palette (midnight greenhouse, sage and gold) does NOT apply here. Sunforge gets its own skin; it
  only has to sit on the arcade shelf without clashing.

PART 1. THE THEME (write it first; it decides everything)
  a. ASSESSMENT.md, first half: play it in a phone sized window (three Journey levels, a Daily, a minute of Zen). What
     is fun (the stick rule, the slide past, the ring crush, the slam spin), what confuses, what looks unfinished,
     screen by screen. Anything that breaks a hard line.
  b. THREE DIRECTIONS, each on one page: a name (three words at most), the pitch in a paragraph, why it is popular now
     and where (what it borrows from: a music scene, a fashion, a design movement, a film), why it is edgy, a palette
     (hex) with the twelve ring colours worked out at 26 px, a type choice (Google Fonts), and what these five things
     look like in that direction: the core, a falling piece, a landed structure, a ring crushing inward, the SLAM.
     One mocked title screen per direction over a real capture.
     Seeds, not orders:
       - CHROME SUN: liquid chrome and black, a blown out white sun, acid green or hot magenta as the one accent,
         brutalist condensed type, lens streaks, the pieces as mirror polished slabs, the crush as a car compactor.
       - REACTOR: a dark industrial core, the pieces as fuel cells and containment plates, warning stripes, red alarm
         light when a ring is one cell from closing, the slam as a reactor pulse, synthwave edges.
       - SOLAR CULT: black and gold, sigils and rings of script, the gyre as a ritual circle, the pieces as offerings,
         the crush as the sun taking them, tarot and alchemy with a modern edge.
       - SCRAP FORGE: welds, sparks, oxide and brass, a sun that is a furnace mouth, the structure as hammered metal,
         grunge type, phonk edit energy.
       - Or your own; one of the three may be yours.
  c. THE PICK: recommend one in a paragraph and say why it sells on a thumbnail, on a phone in daylight, and after a
     hundred games. Then build that one.
  d. STYLE-GUIDE.md (or .pdf) for the pick: palette (hex: the twelve rings, the core, the void, the sky, the seven
     piece colours if pieces keep a colour while falling), type, surface rules (what a cell is made of, how cells of
     one ring read as one body, what the core is, what the void is), icon grammar (one colour marks at 24 and 48),
     motion (what the slam does to the frame, what a crush does, what pulses, what never moves, what reduced motion
     keeps). Show it on a real play capture and on the title.
  e. ASSESSMENT.md, second half: improvements worth building (code, ours), ranked by hook. Seeds: a ring about to
     close glows; a combo callout; the gyre's spin as a real camera move; a level up that changes the sky; a core
     overload mode; a share card with your gyre drawn on it; a weekly gyre with a leaderboard; a hold that shows the
     piece on the board. Say which three first. The three biggest risks to this selling. The store read.

WHAT NOT TO DRAW
- Nursery pastels, rounded candy tiles, anything that reads as a kids' block game.
- Brands, real logos, real people, another game's pieces (the shapes are generic; the look of the tiles must be ours).
- Text in plates beyond the wordmark, the chain callouts and what a row asks. Store screenshots. Sounds (notes go in
  NOTES.md: what the slam should sound like in this theme is worth a paragraph).
