# Sunforge • identity and art assessment
Astra • 5 November 2026

## First: the game as observed
I played the live site in its portrait stage, used movement, rotation, hold, gentle down and SLAM in Journey, finished a Daily run, and spent approximately a minute in Zen. I captured the actual title and a settled Journey board. The stage was about 526 × 936 within a desktop browser, not a physical phone. I did not complete three Journey levels: the session stayed at level 1. I cannot claim direct observation of later levels or a completed ring crush. Those mechanics below are assessed from the supplied brief and the on-screen instructions, not invented play experience.

**What already works.** SLAM visibly rotates the board, with the falling piece and the whole structure creating a useful contrast between world motion and player input. The outward growth becomes a little sculpture worth looking at. Losing an offset piece to the void is legible once it happens. Hold and the second preview give room for deliberate play. The slide-past rule creates the interesting question: can I reach the deep pocket instead of making the tower taller?

**Title.** The central empty space wastes the one chance to show the sun and the unusual board. A prose paragraph explains several rules before the player has a visual model. A soundtrack reward initially sat over the lower menu. The identity should be visible before cross-arcade rewards compete with it.

**Journey play.** The current tiles are candy-like, but they are brighter and more saturated than the brief's word “pastel” suggests. The core is visible yet lacks a memorable silhouette. Small hold and next labels and the level/rings line lose hierarchy. The ring outlines are useful scaffolding but subtle against navy. The stage already rotates on SLAM; refine and preserve this existing hook rather than proposing rotation as if it did not exist. Ghosts can overlap the structure and compete with cell edges. The left and right controls displayed emoji-style orange arrow tiles while down and rotate used plain white glyphs. Replace that platform-dependent mix with one icon family.

**Daily.** I saw Daily #308, remaining pieces and rings in the header, then a result of 160 with zero rings and +4 Sunbeams. This is a run observation, not a statement about the mode's overall balance. The daily result has a lot of unused space and does not show the player's sculpture. “Again” remains present even after a daily completion mark appears on the title; make practice versus ranked eligibility explicit.

**Zen.** The shared controls are reassuring. Let the mode have a calmer perimeter and retain clear stick, void and rotation feedback. No endless background motion is needed to convey calm.

**How.** Four numbered prose blocks explain the rules. Use the four included picture plates beside concise live text; demonstrate the side gap and the empty floor explicitly.

**Settings and results.** Sound, ghost and installation appear in settings; reduced motion was not shown in the observed menu. The results need a real gyre window and stronger Again/Share/Menu hierarchy. The install wording problem is reported by the brief; I did not trigger installation to confirm it.

**Hard-line implications.** Preserve 48 CSS-pixel hit targets independently of the canvas scale. Use high-contrast cell faces, distinguish falling material from settled material, and keep the action board visually quiet. Do not infer actual phone daylight contrast from a desktop capture. Existing music is not replaced by this pack.

## Direction 1 • Chrome Sun
**Hook: Crush the chrome.** A blown-out square sun bites through polished black metal. The gyre becomes jewelry under a hydraulic press: bright silver edges, acid cuts, hard shadow, glossy slabs. Acid yellow is the brand accent; the twelve gameplay bands remain coloured metal so a monochrome concept does not erase the puzzle.

**Cultural fit.** Borrows from club flyers, chrome accessories and retro-future music graphics. Contemporary design reporting discusses chrome, acid accents and brutalist composition; that is evidence of visual currency, not evidence that a particular age group will buy this game. The edgy part is compression and overexposed hard material, without violent imagery.

**Palette, inside outward:** #FFE45A, #9A58F5, #30E1EA, #E64A6C, #BEF24A, #447DE3, #FF9C40, #C7D3EC, #BE45B4, #35BF8A, #A785EA, #D5A875. Void #080B10, chrome #EEF4F7, accent #DCFF43. Alternating warm/cool and light/mid-value neighbours, with the same 12 redundant centered stamps used in the selected direction. At 26 px use one flat colour plane and a two-pixel edge, never mirror noise.

**Type:** Barlow Condensed Black with Space Grotesk Medium, both Google Fonts. Core: a cut square white aperture. Falling: silver foil with an acid perimeter. Settled: enamel faces clamped in chrome. Crush: slabs collapse toward one white cut. SLAM: an acid press pedal with an angular turn glyph.

Mock: `previews/title_chrome_sun.jpg`. Strength: immediate thumbnail impact. Risk: metal can swallow ring colour and become generic chrome branding.

## Direction 2 • Solar Cult
**Hook: Feed the eclipse.** A geometric eclipse suspended in charcoal, surrounded by square ceremonial rings. These are invented abstract astronomical marks, never borrowed sacred script. Brass offerings and sharp ivory geometry give the puzzle the atmosphere of a modern art-deck rather than a fantasy RPG menu.

**Cultural fit.** Borrows from contemporary tarot packaging, occult record sleeves, astronomical diagrams and ornate display typography. This is a plausible audience association, not a measured popularity claim. Its edge is beautiful unease: the sun consumes what you build. No religious promises, gore or threatening character imagery.

**Palette, inside outward:** #F3CF58, #A96ADD, #5FD6C5, #D95564, #BED567, #648ADB, #E9974E, #DDD5B9, #B65B9C, #5AB28A, #A6A5E6, #C8956A. Void #100E16, brass #D8B768, ivory #F7EBD1. Faces stay bright enamel, not muddy antique shading. Alternating neighbour hue/value plus 12 marks at 26 px.

**Type:** Cinzel Black display with Space Grotesk Medium UI, Google Fonts. Core: a dark square eclipse edged in ivory. Falling: ivory offering plate. Settled: enamel in brass settings. Crush: rings fold into a square sigil. SLAM: a broad ceremonial seal split by a quarter-turn arrow.

Mock: `previews/title_solar_cult.jpg`. Strength: atmosphere and collectibility. Risk: fine ornament becomes visual fog at phone size and the premise can read as a different game genre.

## Direction 3 • Sunbreaker
**Hook: Break the sun.** Sunforge becomes a compact solar demolition machine. A white-hot square star is trapped in four black turbine jaws. Coloured enamel plates weld into its gyre. Each SLAM cranks the jaws one quarter turn; a completed ring folds into the hungry star. Acid yellow, blue-black steel, oversized condensed type and offset hazard cuts make a punchy, collectible arcade identity. It is industrial but playful: a machine doing an impossible job, not a grim facility simulator.

**Cultural fit.** Borrows from rave-poster type, technical streetwear panels, exaggerated tool packaging and kinetic music-edit composition. Bold architectural typography and texture against clean structure have support in current design coverage; the exact Sunbreaker combination is our original art direction. It offers that loud thumbnail language without illegible grunge in the board.

**Palette, inside outward:** #FFD84A, #9961E8, #2DD5DA, #F15370, #BCED55, #477DD9, #FF9947, #C8D5E8, #BA4FBE, #39BC8A, #A8A1E8, #D5A36B. Core #FFF5CB, accent #DFFF4F, void #080D15, sky #182431, graphite #263442, line #8297A5. Each adjacent ring alternates hue and broadly lightness. A dark centered stamp repeats one of 12 distinct simple shapes. No claim that colour alone supports every colour-vision deficiency.

**Type:** Barlow Condensed Black for display; Space Grotesk Medium/Bold for live UI, Google Fonts. Core: a square furnace star with four separated jaws. Falling: pale ceramic with dark slash, an unmistakable white rim and dark keyline. Settled: matte coloured enamel with welded exterior edges. Crush: inward rectangular pressure bands and a short star bite. SLAM: an acid mechanical pedal with a large quarter-turn symbol.

## The pick • Sunbreaker
Sunbreaker is the selected aesthetic; the product name stays **SUNFORGE**. The four-jaw square sun is a distinctive symbol that remains identifiable on an app tile. Bright enamel faces against a quiet blue-black board give daylight readability a better starting point than all-chrome reflections or hairline ritual decoration. After a hundred games, the silhouette and material rule still serve the mechanic: falling ceramic becomes welded enamel, exterior seams connect, and the star eats the completed band. Reserve noisy texture and dramatic lighting for title/store art. A loud shell around a precise quiet puzzle is the repeatable system.

## Improvements worth building, ranked by hook
1. **Show the game on the title.** Use the square sun and a small demonstrative gyre. One line: “Build. Turn. Collapse.” Journey remains the primary action. This sells the actual difference before the first tap.
2. **Make the almost-ring and crush one readable event.** Mark the missing cell with a steady notch and energize that ring's exterior seam. On completion, briefly hold the completed outline, move the outside cells inward, then settle the new ring colours. Do not cover the board with particles.
3. **Make SLAM a confident quarter turn.** Preserve the existing board spin, use a fixed HUD, a short pressure cue and distinct pressed/charged pedal states. Show a quarter-turn arrow on the control and teach gentle placement separately.
4. **Share the sculpture.** Draw the actual final gyre into the supplied share window, alongside score, rings, level and date for Daily. Avoid a generic branded score card.
5. **Fix the small text and input hierarchy.** Phone CSS sizes take precedence over scaling virtual pixels; use at least 14 px for supporting live labels and 16 px for buttons. Targets remain at least 48 px. Replace emoji arrows and legacy install name.
6. **Show the hold consequence.** On a deliberate hold press, briefly outline the new active piece at its real spawn position, not a fake landing promise.
7. **Sky milestones and restrained combo plates.** Change the quiet perimeter tint by chapter; show x2 through x6 outside the active falling corridor. Never recolour ring semantics for a level theme.
8. **Optional overload later.** Earned short cosmetic charged core can amplify a chain; avoid a new time-pressure system until the base mechanic has a clear repeat-play curve.
9. **Weekly competition later.** A serverless offline client cannot supply a trustworthy global leaderboard alone. Keep seeded weekly challenges local/shareable unless a separately approved service is introduced. Do not quietly add networking to this art handoff.

**Build the first three first.** Font and target sizing are foundational usability work alongside them, not an optional reward feature.

## Three biggest commercial risks
- **The picture promises a familiar stacker.** Lead with a gyre, off-floor cells and a quarter-turn in motion. A square board with a sun is the key, not a loose tetromino collage.
- **“Edgy” destroys tiny-cell clarity.** Keep chrome, scratches, hazard stripes and flare off the cell interiors. Use the included clean enamel atlas and redundant marks. Physical-phone daylight review remains an integration responsibility.
- **The strongest reward arrives too late to understand.** Teach one attainable ring with a short guided example and make its inward crush visible. My session did not reach a ring clear, which itself cautions against relying on prose onboarding.

## Store read
The icon is the four-jaw sun alone. The feature graphic has one wordmark and one hero gyre. The six screenshot frames deliberately have empty headline bands and transparent gameplay windows, not fabricated screenshots. Suggested live overlay headlines, in order: “Stick, don't stack”, “Close the ring”, “Make it collapse”, “SLAM to spin”, “Your daily gyre”, “Find your flow”. No promises about rankings, popularity or features that are not implemented.
