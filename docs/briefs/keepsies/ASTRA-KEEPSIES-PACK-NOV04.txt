Two jobs in one zip again, assess and produce. But this is a different kind of game from Music Studio and Flatulence
Fighter: it is not shipped (hidden behind the dev gate), it is 3D, and most of its art is procedural BY LAW. So your
pack is: assess what is there, give it a world, people and an identity, and leave the marbles alone.

WHAT KEEPSIES IS
A realistic 3D marble game where the marbles are the economy. Real Ringer in a ten foot ring on dirt: thirteen mibs in
a cross, you knuckle down at the edge, brace your thumb on your shooter until the reticle settles, then flick through
it (the Knuckle, the real thumb snap of playground marbles, the game's one input). Knock a mib out of the ring and you
shoot again from where your shooter lies; first to seven wins. House rules: keeps, slips (one redo), bombing (drop
shots), poison (shoot out the enemy shooter), ring size. Matches are played for keeps: both players ante marbles into
the pot, the pot sits in the cross so you are shooting at what you might win, and the winner takes it. Lose a rare and
you get a 24 hour ransom window to buy it back.
Sixty five designed marbles in five tiers, every one with a line of lore in an old man's swap meet voice ("Older than
the ring it plays in." "He's not resting. He's waiting."): fourteen commons (Dirt Plain, Redware, Chalkie, Milk Glass,
Bottle Green, Clearie, six Cat's Eyes, Peewee, Commie), twenty uncommons (Ribbon Candy, Nine Vane, Oxblood, Seafoam
Twist, Custard Swirl, Licorice Rope, Peppermint, Blue Onion, Auger, Double Helix, Barber's Due, Patch Pirate, Brick,
Slag, Bearing, Rusty, Chrome Dome, Riverstone, Limey, Lucky Chip), sixteen rares (Bloodstone Aggie, Bumblebee Aggie,
Onionskin, Clambroth, Lutz, Sulphide Blank, Indian Blanket, Tiger Iron, Frost Heave, Drop Anchor, Mercury, Snake Eye,
Kiln Kiss, The Widow, and two boss signatures: Coffee Tin Champ, The Thumbsplitter), ten epics (Galaxy, Molten Core,
Glacier, Thunderhead, Deep Sea, Obsidian Knapp, Bioluminous, Nebula Swirl, and the signatures House Edge and Old
Ironsides), and five grails, sulphides with a figure inside (The Drowned Knight, The Astronomer, Koi, The Ember
Dragon, and The First Marble: a sulphide containing a tiny marble, the Curator's own). The fiction: every marble in
this game has been owned before. You do not buy product, you inherit pieces, and keepsies is the tradition going on.
Pouches bought with sunbeams (Standard 300, Collector's 1200, Grail 6000) with pity counters; dupes become dust; a
clay pool of ten worthless marbles that refills daily, so anyone can always play for keeps. A showcase case where a
marble is safe and unstakeable. A ladder of five leagues, each five rung opponents (generated personas from a name and
a quirk) and a boss who stakes a signature marble found nowhere else: Dusty Coyle (a kid, taped glasses, a coffee tin;
"Beat everyone on his street. His street is small."), Marlene "Knuckles" Ito (a retired champion), The Pit Boss (runs
the Foundry, talks odds), Old Ironsides (nobody has seen him; the marble arrives by mail), The Curator (plays only for
grails). Lose to a boss and they display your marble in their collection; beat them to steal it back. Named
techniques the game discovers as you do them (Sticking, Bombing, Dirty English, The Lag, Knuckled Down, Clean Sweep,
Poison Pen, Doorstep, Cold Read, The Long Goodbye, Housebreaker, Provenance). A second mode, Arena (turn based marble
battle with visible damage, ring outs and hazards, in three arenas: The Ring, Foundry, Glacier), is designed and not
built. Onboarding: calibration ("Show me your hardest snap"), the break, meet Dusty, the tin (he rattles it: take
some, and the Heirloom choice, one of three rares laid on a cloth: Bloodstone Aggie, Lutz, Mercury), then a first
keepsies for a clay. Levels, daily chores, a Practice Ring that simulates a hundred games in a second. VR later.
Built from Stephen's design (DESIGN.md in this pack, 48 KB; read it) by Fable: Ringer plays end to end on a phone, the
Knuckle works with a real thumb, the collection, the pouches, the keepsies loop, the ransom and the ceremonies exist
as screens. Three.js and Rapier, ES modules, no build step. Dev gated at lucidwinds.com/satellites/keepsies/. No
money anywhere in v1 (sunbeams only); a store path comes after the Arena. The Arena is blocked on a design call, not
on art.

THE HARD LINES (they bind the art too)
- Procedural by law. Sixty of the sixty five marbles are shader recipes and already render as such (marbles/
  contact.jpg); the four grail figures and The First Marble are sculpts fitted into a 22 mm clear sphere. Never paint
  a marble surface (a texture would not turn with the marble), never draw a marble to replace one, never put a marble
  you drew in a plate where the real one is meant to sit. You MAY draw the reference images the grail sculpts are made
  from (section D), and you may draw marbles as small illustration in store and share art where no real render goes.
- Every sound is synthesised from the physics. No recordings.
- Three.js, no build step. Art arrives as PNG, WebP, JPG, SVG and JSON under satellites/keepsies/assets/. A glb is
  welcome only inside the budgets in section H; otherwise send concept boards and we sculpt.
- 60 fps on a Pixel 4a class phone and an 8 MB payload: textures 1024 at most, WebP where it can be, tileable where
  the row says, nothing baked that code draws already.
- One hand, portrait, 375 px wide must read; every target 48 px.
- Spare, wry copy, one line of lore each. No exclamation marks in system text, no dashes of any kind (the fleet's
  law). No text in plates except the wordmark and what a row asks for.
- No real brands, no real people, no mascot. The bosses are our characters; make them ours.
- Generated art is never called hand painted. Google Fonts only for anything new (today the game uses the system
  rounded sans). Wordmarks arrive outlined.
- One light. The design never fixes it; the lot is at dusk in the art notes. Choose it, write it down, keep it in
  every plate, portrait and board.

WHAT IS WRONG TODAY (our read; find the rest)
- The world is a tan void. The dirt runs to the top of the frame and meets black; no horizon, no lot, no lamp, no
  sky, no shadow but the marbles'. From under the floor (k1-under) it is plain blue. The ring itself, the thing in
  the game's one line pitch, is a thin white arc.
- The marbles are the only art, and they are good. Everything a player would screenshot is a marble on black.
- Dusty Coyle is a name in a chip and a line of text. The tin is a heading. The cloth is a dark strip with three
  marbles on it. The ceremonies the design calls the emotional core of the game are gold cards on black.
- The grails are grey moons until their sculpts exist.
- The pouches are three text buttons. Nothing opens.
- The title is type on black. The app icon is a generated marble on dirt. The arcade thumb is a frame.
- Every control is a text chip. Clean, but there is no identity, no mark, nothing you would remember.
- The snap card is the one piece of drawn explanation, and it is diagram dots.
- The calibration asks for your hardest snap into darkness: it is meant to be the hook and there is nothing to see.

PART 1. THE ASSESSMENT (write it first; it decides the rest)
Walk every screen in screens/ as a kid who has never played marbles, then as a collector on day 30 with forty marbles
and two grails to go. Then write:
  a. ASSESSMENT.md:
     - What already reads and should be kept (the marbles, the rules card's plainness, the voice in the bubble, the
       black and gold?).
     - What does not read, confuses, or looks unfinished, screen by screen. Be blunt.
     - Anything that breaks a hard line.
     - The smoothing plan: a numbered list, each with its size (S, M, L), what it depends on, and whether it is art
       (yours), code (ours), or a sculpt (the Meshy lane). Order by what most raises "this is a real place" and "I
       want to show someone my marble".
     - Improvements worth building (code, ours), ranked by hook. Seeds: the lot at dusk around the ring with a lamp
       and your own shadow; Dusty drawn, with the tin, as a presence at the ring edge; a marble card to share (the
       marble, its name, its line, and who you won it from); a provenance ledger per marble, where it has been, who
       lost it (the lore conceit made visible); the pouch opening as a scene; the heirloom choice on a real cloth; the
       showcase as a room; the Arena once it unblocks. Say which three you would ship first and why, and what each
       needs from the art.
     - The three biggest risks you see to this being chosen in the arcade and later sold, and what you would do about
       each. Ours: the world is a void so every screenshot is a marble on black; the Knuckle is hard to learn and the
       game soft locked once on a real thumb (code, but the calibration screen is where art earns the second try);
       losing a marble for keeps has to feel like a story and not a theft.
     - Naming: Keepsies is a working title. Say whether it should stay (it is the word for the thing), propose three
       names with a one line case each, and draw the wordmark for the one you recommend plus one for Keepsies so we
       can ship either.
  b. STYLE-GUIDE.md (or a PDF with the same name): the look the game ships with. Palette (hex), the light, type
     (Google Fonts only), icon grammar (stroke or fill, 24 and 48 px, one colour), surface rules (what a card is, what
     the dirt is, what chalk is, what a pouch is made of), the five tier identities (a colour, a mark, a card frame
     each), the five bosses' silhouettes, motion (the design fixes most of it: a 0.6 s hero glint per staked rare, won
     marbles rolling into your bag one by one, slow motion only on a shatter, a 0.4 s freeze on a technique; say what
     the art adds and what never moves). Show it on at least the ring (k1-brace) and the tin (k2-tin).

WHAT NOT TO DRAW
- A marble's surface, or a marble where a real render goes. The sixty five are code.
- Text inside plates, except the wordmark and anything a row asks for.
- Any real product, brand or person. Marble makers' names and trademarks stay out of the art.
- Store screenshots: we compose those from the real game inside your frames.
- Sounds. Notes about what a clink, a settle click and a shatter should feel like go in NOTES.md.
