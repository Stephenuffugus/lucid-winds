WHAT ORB ORCHARD IS
A run across a tiny world that curls away beneath your feet. One HTML file, canvas 2D, portrait, two thumbs: TURN
left, TURN right, JUMP (or tap either half of the field to turn). The world is a 32 by 32 wrapping checkerboard seen
from behind the runner, with a curved horizon, so the floor rolls over the edge like a small planet. You run forward
on your own; all you choose is where to turn (it takes at the next tile) and when to jump (two tiles). On the floor:
dew orbs, the harvest, gather every one to clear the stage; thorns, touch one and the run ends; sunbeads, optional gold
rings, take them all for a PERFECT; silver bumpers that knock you back; green springs that throw you three tiles.
The old orchard secret, and the best idea in the game: walk a closed ring of dew orbs around other dew, and everything
sealed inside turns to sunbeads in one burst. The pace speeds up as you linger. Tiles you have walked change colour,
so the floor records your path.
Twelve hand made stages, clear one to open the next: First Rows, Orchard Columns, Ring Cage, Thorn Hedge, Spiral Bed,
Four Gardens, Bumper Alley, Spring Meadow, Double Cage, Thorn Maze, Checker Storm, The Great Loop. Modes: The Orchard
(the twelve), Daily Sphere (one seeded stage for everyone, with a streak), Zen Stroll (never ends a run, pays nothing),
Blitz (a best time chase). A wardrobe earned by play, no purchases: three runners (Seedling free, Firefly for clearing
six stages, Comet for three perfects), three floors (Meadow free, Twilight for a daily streak of three, Ember for
clearing nine), three skies (Dawn free, Nebula for clearing all twelve, Aurora for five perfects). The Grove, a
gallery that grows a tree per clear and presses a golden bloom per perfect (24 blooms at most). A clear card (dew,
sunbeads, seconds, next stage, retry), a HOW TO STROLL page. Sunbeams, the arcade's currency, pay per clear, perfect,
daily and blitz. A run is under two minutes.
It lives at https://lucidwinds.com/satellites/orb-orchard/ in the Sky Wolf Studio arcade, open to players, 55 KB.
Everything on screen is drawn by code on a 270 by 410 canvas shown at twice its size with pixel snapping: the sky is
a three stop gradient with a sun disc and 24 stars; the floor is flat colour quads fogged toward the sky; every pickup
is a circle; the runner is a cream ellipse with a sprout and two feet; the grove trees are procedural; the UI is DOM
with 24 emoji for icons. No image file exists but the arcade card.
The lineage, said plainly: this is a homage to a 1990s console special stage, the one with blue spheres on a
checkered planet. The genre is open and the mechanic is ours to use. That game's look, names and characters are not.
Nothing you draw may resemble it.

THE HARD LINES (they bind the art too)
- One HTML file, vanilla JavaScript, canvas 2D, no frameworks. The floor is perspective projected flat colour quads,
  not a texture: it cannot take a mapped image without an engine rewrite, which is out of scope. What you can give the
  floor is colour (a tile pair and a walked pair per palette) and a painted horizon band. Pickups, the runner, the sky,
  the FX and every UI surface can take images: the renderer draws an image at a point scaled to a radius, so author at
  256 px cells and we scale.
- Phone first, portrait, two thumbs. The pads are 96 px; every other target 48 px. The game runs at speed: every
  silhouette must read at 24 px and in motion, and the five pickups must be told apart at a glance (today they are
  five circles). The runner must stand out against every floor, every sky and every orb.
- Not that other game. No blue spheres on a blue and white checker, no hedgehog, no star posts, none of its palette or
  its type. If a screenshot could be mistaken for it, redo it. No real brands, no real people, no mascot from anywhere.
- An earlier direction for this game carried a rule to avoid a garden or botanical look (the orchard is a place you
  run, not a plant catalogue). Confirm it or argue against it in the assessment; whichever, say why in a paragraph.
- Player facing words use no dashes of any kind and no exclamation marks (the fleet's copy law; the game's "SPEED UP!"
  is being removed). Google Fonts only for type. Generated art is never called hand painted. No text in a plate except
  the wordmark and what a row asks for. Never a side over 1600 px except the store sizes. Web files small: a plate
  under 200 KB, a sheet under 150 KB.
- The arcade's own palette (midnight greenhouse, sage and gold) is not required here. The game has its own hexes
  (below); you may keep, retune or replace them, and say which.

WHAT IS WRONG TODAY (the fleet audit's read and ours; find the rest)
- The game boots onto HOW TO STROLL, five paragraphs of body copy, before the title screen.
- The floor is a saturated bright green and dark green checker at full contrast. It is the loudest thing on screen, it
  fights the sky, and it meets the sky on a hard one pixel line with no haze, so the world looks pasted on.
- Five pickups and the runner are near identical spheres. The runner, a cream ellipse, vanishes against a blue orb;
  the thing you control has the weakest silhouette in a frame where nothing has a strong one.
- The pixel upscale makes every edge chunky and uneven (code).
- The Grove's empty state is a heading, one grey sentence and 470 px of black.
- Every icon is an emoji: the mode buttons, the wardrobe portraits, the grove marks.
- The name: Orb Orchard promises fruit and a garden. The game is a planet you run across. Stephen thinks it may need
  a different name.
- The feel (ours to raise; test each yourself): a turn commits at the next tile with no telegraph, so a late tap feels
  like the game's fault; the speed up punishes the exploring the stages ask for; a thorn ends the run on one touch
  with no shield, no stumble, no rewind; the seal rule, the best mechanic, is explained in text and never taught by a
  stage; long stages have no checkpoint; the 32 by 32 world has no landmarks, so you cannot tell where the last three
  dew are; the clear card is a table; nothing after a run is worth showing anyone.

WHAT NOT TO DRAW
- Anything that could be mistaken for the other game. No blue sphere on a blue and white checker, ever.
- A mapped floor texture: the floor cannot take one. Give it colour and a horizon.
- Text in plates beyond the wordmark and what a row asks. Store screenshots (we compose those). Sounds (notes go in
  NOTES.md; what the seal burst should sound like is worth a paragraph).
