WHAT MUSIC STUDIO IS
A phone first music maker in one HTML file (no frameworks, Web Audio, nothing installed). Pick a starter (Hip Hop,
Trap, Lo-Fi Chill, House, Pop, Reggaeton, Rock, Drum n Bass, Funk, Dreamy, or a blank canvas) and a full beat plays
at once; then every note can be changed. Four lanes (Drums, Bass, Chords, Melody), a 16 step grid per lane, three
sections (Verse, Chorus, Bridge) arranged into a song, 80 plus real instruments streamed on demand, a sampler that
records the phone's mic onto drum pads, a hum to melody tool, live keys, a circle of fifths, a world rhythm
generator, a full screen visualiser, and export as audio (WAV), as a music video (the visualiser recorded to a file),
or as a song code you can paste to a friend. Songs save on the device.
It lives at lucidwinds.com/studio.html and the arcade frames it at lucidwinds.com/play/song.html (card: Music Studio,
Creative). It also runs inside the Lucid Winds app as a mini game, where saving or exporting earns Sunbeams.
The plan is a Google Play listing of its own, like Shutterbug and Flock the World (a Trusted Web Activity of the live
page, so every deploy reaches store players at once).

THE HARD LINES (they bind the art too)
- One HTML file, vanilla JavaScript, no frameworks. Art arrives as files the page loads (PNG, JPG, WebP, SVG) or as
  CSS values. Nothing that needs a build step.
- Phone first, portrait, 360 to 430 px wide, thumbs. Every target 48 px. It must still work on a desktop browser.
- No audio files. Sound comes from synthesis and from General MIDI soundfonts streamed from a public CDN. You may
  write sound design notes; you cannot send sounds.
- No real brands. Nothing that reads as a specific product (no MPC pad grid lookalike, no Ableton clip colours as
  identity, no Roland orange as a logo). The current badge says "PADLAB  MPK" in four primary squares: treat that
  as the first thing to replace.
- Generated art is never called hand painted. No mascot. No text baked into a plate unless the row asks for it: we
  set every word in the app's fonts, so wordmarks and badges are the only lettering you draw.
- Player facing words use no dashes of any kind and no exclamation marks (the studio's own copy law). Keep that in
  any sample copy you write.
- Google Fonts only for type. Today: Bebas Neue (display, all caps) and DM Mono (labels and values).

- The Show: a radial spectrum ring round the song's name and BPM, trail fade, the section's colour, a footer

WHAT IS WRONG TODAY (our read; find the rest)
- Every icon is an emoji. On the test phone half of them render as empty boxes (see 01 and 02), and on real phones
  they are a different drawing on every device. The dice, the mic, the music note, the clapperboard, the rainbow,
  the bulb, the floppy disk: all of it.
- The badge "PADLAB  MPK" in four primary squares is the only saturated primary colour in the whole arcade and it
  names a real product line.
- The starters, the first thing anyone sees, are eleven flat dark boxes with a tofu glyph each. No cover art, no
  mood, nothing to pick with the eye.
- The lane stack is one undifferentiated dark field: every panel is the same flat fill, so Drums, Bass, Chords and
  Melody differ only by a 3 px coloured spine on the left.
- The instrument pickers are the browser's native select boxes. The sliders are the browser's with a green thumb.
- The transport wraps to two rows on narrow phones and reads as a row of text buttons.
- "+ Sample Pad (0/3)" is a tall grey box in the middle of the drums panel.
- My Songs is a text list: no cover, no waveform, no date art. A saved song has no picture of itself to share.
- The Show has one style. The music video has no title card and no end card, just the ring and a footer.
- There is no onboarding inside the studio. The arcade's generic How to play card (14) is all a new player gets.
- The Hum and Sampler tools ask for the mic with no picture of what is about to happen.
- Nothing says "make something and share it": no song card, no cover, no moment of pride after Save.

PART 1. THE ASSESSMENT (write it first; it decides the rest)

  a. ASSESSMENT.md:
     - What already reads and should be kept (the grid, the instant starter, the lane colours, the Show?).
     - What does not read, confuses, or looks unfinished, screen by screen. Be blunt.
     - Anything that breaks a hard line (a brand reference, a glyph that depends on the device, a target under 48 px).
     - The smoothing plan: a numbered list, each with its size (S, M, L), what it depends on, and whether it is art

       tier. Say which three you would ship first and why, and what each needs from the art.
     - The three biggest risks you see to this selling (or to it being chosen in the arcade), and what you would do

     - Naming: "Music Studio" is generic and unsearchable on Google Play, and PADLAB is only an in app badge. Propose

       others can wait).
  b. STYLE-GUIDE.md (or a PDF with the same name): the look the studio should ship with. Keep the midnight greenhouse

PART 2. THE ASSETS (in this order; send what you finish if you cannot finish all)
Formats: icons and UI marks as SVG (flat, they must read at 24 px and at 48 px, one colour unless the row says
otherwise, so code can tint them to a lane); raster UI art as PNG at 2x of the size named (a row that says 64x64
means a 128x128 file) with transparent backgrounds where it floats; full bleed plates as JPG or WebP; textures as
tileable PNG; store art at the exact pixel sizes given, opaque. Never a side over 1600 px except the store sizes
(the host's optimiser resizes anything bigger). Names lowercase_with_underscores. A manifest.json lists every file:
file, id, kind (icon, badge, cover, plate, texture, frame, store), size, notes.

  A. Identity: the wordmark (SVG and PNG, on dark and on light); the app icon (512 opaque for the store, a 512
     maskable with the safe zone respected, a 192); the store feature graphic 1024x500 (the idea at a glance: a grid

  C. The icon set (SVG, about 45): play, stop, record, metronome, new, tools, songs, video, audio, sampler, rhythm,

  D. The starters: eleven cover tiles 160x100 (Hip Hop, Trap, Lo-Fi Chill, House, Pop, Reggaeton, Rock, Drum n Bass,

  E. The tools: the circle of fifths ring (an SVG ring and its twelve key cells; code draws the current key and the

  G. The store: six screenshot frames 1080x1920, each with a headline band at the top and a transparent window where

WHAT NOT TO DRAW
- Text inside plates, except the wordmark and anything a row asks for.
- Any real product's control surface, colourway or name.
- Store screenshots: we compose those from the real app inside your frames.
- Sounds. Notes about sound (what a tap should sound like, how the metronome should feel) are welcome in NOTES.md.
