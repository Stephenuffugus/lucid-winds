LUCID WINDS, THE HUB, ROUND ONE: A SECOND OPINION, ONE STYLE, AND PROOF PIECES (12 Nov 2026)

Lucid Winds is Sky Wolf Studio's flagship: a botanical collecting game. A player plays small games, earns
Sunbeams, and mints one-of-one procedural plants, each with its own haiku. It is one web page built for a phone
held upright. It has four tabs: GAMES, GREENHOUSE, NURSERY, WILD. Its stated look is a midnight greenhouse: deep
blacks, sage greens, gold accents, cream text. Stephen wants it ready to publish, with the studio's smaller games
standing behind it. You have drawn packs for those games. This is the first time you are asked about the hub.

This round is small on purpose. No full pack yet. We want your eye first, then a few pieces that prove a direction.

HOW TO WORK (Stephen's standing order; it binds this round)
- No tests, no checks, no validation scripts, no reports about reports.
- Look at each piece ONCE with your own eyes when it is done, fix what is wrong, move on.
- If something is ambiguous, pick the reading that ships and write one line about it in NOTES.md. Do not stop to ask.
- Do not play the live game. The fifteen shots below are the hub as it stands today.

3. REVIEW.md (write this FIRST, before reading section 7)
Your own ranked critique of the hub from the shots. What is strong and must be kept. What is weak. What you would
change first if you could change three things. Be blunt: Stephen asked for honest assessments, never sugarcoated.
One page. Section 7 holds Fable's own findings; write yours before you read them, so we get two opinions and not
one echoed.

4. STYLE.md (one style for the whole hub)
Today five styles share the screen: jewel toned painted icons in the top bar, carved stone tiles in the nav,
parchment cards for actions and the nursery, dithered pixel painting in the shop, and a banner set that does not
match itself. Choose ONE direction and say why. Give: the palette (hex), the type roles (the hub uses Playfair
Display for names and haiku, Bebas Neue for labels, DM Mono for small text; keep or replace, with reasons), one
button shape, one frame treatment, how a word sits on a button so it reads at 12 px, and how the seven plant
grades (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic, Cosmic) show on a card. One page and one swatch sheet.

5. PROOF PIECES (small; these decide whether a full pack follows this direction)
A. THE PLANT CARD, the most important piece. The plants are drawn by code as vector shapes (pot, stem, leaves,
   bloom, aura, companion) and they are the plainest art on their own card (shots 06 and 07). Paint THREE target
   frames: take the plant in shot 07 and two plants from shot 06 and show what the same plant should look like on
   its card in your style: lighting, a ground for it to stand on, the aura, the frame, the name, the grade, the
   haiku. Keep each plant's silhouette and colours, because the renderer will be raised toward your frames in
   code and every plant must still come from its hash. Then one sheet of the pieces a renderer could reuse on
   every plant: a ground or pedestal, a soft aura, a light, a vignette. 1080 x 1620 per card frame.
B. ONE BANNER: the Puzzles category banner, 1680 x 720, in the chosen style. The banner in shot 15 shows a
   cannabis plant and must be replaced. Any other plant, or a puzzle motif. Title text "PUZZLES" set in the art.
C. ONE BUTTON SET at true size: Share, Details, Breed, Compost, each 192 x 192 with its word, and the same four
   at 96 x 96 to prove they read small. Compost destroys a plant; it should look like it.
D. THE FIRST SCREEN: one 1080 x 2400 frame that welcomes a new player and carries the beta notice without it
   being the first thing they read (shot 01 is today's).

7. FABLE'S FINDINGS (read only after REVIEW.md is written)
- The plants are the weakest art in a frame of rich painting; the collectible should be the richest thing there.
- A plant is drawn at about a quarter of its grid card, and the first card starts 45% of the way down the screen.
- Five styles share one screen; each is good alone.
- Share, Details, Breed and Compost have no words. Gold and pink labels on parchment are hard to read.
- The banners do not match each other. The Puzzles banner is a cannabis plant.
- Three pieces sit on a visible box: the SOW medallion (grey), the Forager's Lens (black), Color Garden (white).
- Most built in games put a dark board mid screen with small muted text and leave the lower 40% empty.
- Popups stack on every screen. That one is code, not art, and is being fixed separately.

8. SIZES AND RULES, ONCE
PNG, sRGB, transparent where the piece is not a full frame, nothing baked onto a box. Portrait phone, 412 CSS
pixels wide, drawn at 2.6x. No text smaller than 12 CSS pixels at true size. Names in files: lowercase, hyphens.
