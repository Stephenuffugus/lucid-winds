2. The game in two minutes

Live: https://lucidwinds.com/satellites/chaff-wars/, the only thing under review (the folder still has the game's old name, Chaff Wars). Open it on a phone, portrait. A neon 1980s b boy battle on top of a faithful falling pair puzzle (the family of Puyo Puyo and Mean Bean Machine).

  • The rules: a 6 x 12 board. Seed pods fall in pairs, 5 colours (Crimson, Sprout, Dewdrop, Sunbean, Nightshade). Four or more of one colour touching pop; pods above fall and can set off chains. Chains send grey Chaff to the rival's board; Chaff never pops on its own, it shatters only when a colour group next to it clears. Incoming and outgoing Chaff cancel out. Clearing your whole board adds 30 Chaff to your next attack. Your stack reaching the top loses.
  • Score and HYPE: points grow with chain length, colours and group size; one Chaff per 70 points, at most 30 a turn. A HYPE meter climbs through WACK, WARM, FRESH, DEF, DOPE, ILL; full ALL CITY HYPE pays a bonus.
  • Modes: Campaign "Keeper's Stand": you are the Keeper (the gardener writer) against a ladder of 13 rival pests and a secret 14th, the Ronin Hare, unlocked after the boss Baron Greymould. Each pest sets the colour count, fall speed, how well its computer plays and how hard it hits back; stages 7, 9 and 11 start with pods already on the boards; the boss and the secret fight are best of three. Solo Endless speeds up over time and keeps a best score. Versus is online by a 4 letter room code (needs a sign in). Powers mode is hidden (add ?powers=1 to the address): 17 powers are designed (one for each of the 14 pests, and a choice of three for the Keeper: Deep Roots, Bloom Surge, Greenhouse Glass), but only one, Solvent, is built.
  • The cast (each with a tag, a signature dance move and an accent colour): The Aphid Swarm (TOYZ, the crowd Wave), Gnat King Cole (KOLE, the DJ, turntable scratch spin), Mabel Cabbagewing (FLUTTA, the Butterfly into a backspin), Sir Reginald Slugmore (SLIME, the Robot), Chompers the Cutworm (KUTT, the Worm), Baron von Beetle (SCARAB, windmills and flares), Escargeddon (GEDDON, the shell spin, a boombox shell), Cawlin the Crow (KAWZ, the Crow freeze), Gustavo the Gopher (DIGZ, pop up freeze), Duchess Dapple (DAPPL, uprock with high kicks), Lady Bindweed (VINE, the pretzel freeze), Miss Mildew (HAZE, the moonwalk), Baron Greymould (GREYKING, the boss, the King's Freeze), The Ronin Hare (1BLADE, the secret, headspin into a sword arm freeze), and you, the Keeper.
  • Controls: buttons for left, right, rotate both ways and DROP; on the board, drag to move, tap to rotate, drag down to drop faster, flick up to slam. Keyboard too.
  • Screens: title (logo, Campaign, Versus, Solo Endless, How to play, Settings, music, an optional 80s beat), the campaign ladder, play with a stage intro and countdown, the versus lobby with a cassette room code card, how to play, settings (sound, ghost drop guide, big controls, fullscreen, tag shapes for colour blind players, add to home screen, reset), and a results screen (portrait, banner, the rival's line, Sunbeams, next, again, menu, share). A slow 4 second celebration plays at the end of a match.
  • Economy: a few of the studio's Sunbeams for first wins, at most 30 a day. Nothing to spend inside the game.

3. What is weak, honestly (go past these)

  • The studio's own words: "Pop n lock needs gameplay really fleshed out and improved". The puzzle engine is faithful and solid; what is missing is everything that makes people stay.
  • The board is a dark rectangle with a thin outline over a busy graffiti wall; the pods are small flat faces; on a phone the play reads small against the painted world.
  • The dancers are the theme, but nobody dances during play: the pests have only idle, win and lose.
  • Powers mode has 1 of 17 designed powers built, and it is hidden behind the address.
  • After the 14 pest ladder there is nothing: no daily, no puzzles, no unlocks, nothing to earn or spend, no reason to come back tomorrow.
  • Versus needs a sign in, has no rematch and no matchmaking. There is no two players on one phone.
  • The secret pest is too hard (a good player wins about 8 percent, the target was 15).

4. Part one: the gameplay design review

This is the main ask. Design the fleshed out game, keeping the faithful puzzle at its heart. Cover at least: (a) Powers: the design exists (a Sap meter that fills from play, one power per pest, three for the Keeper, a short warning before each lands); check it, cut or change powers, and say how they should look and sound; (b) reasons to come back: a daily, set puzzles in the classic "clear the board in N moves" tradition, crew unlocks, outfits or tags for the Keeper, a dance floor of collected moves, anything better; (c) the battle as a dance battle: how the pests should move during play when a chain goes off, when they take Chaff, when they are about to lose; (d) the campaign: story beats between pests, the boss, the secret; (e) versus: two players on one phone, rematch, quick match; (f) difficulty: the first three pests for a newcomer, the secret for an expert; (g) whether this is worth 99 cents on Google Play and what would make it worth more.

The bar every direction must respect: portrait phone first, 412 x 915 is the main viewport (also check 360 and 375 wide); every touch target at least 48 px; no text under 0.7 rem; player facing copy never uses dashes of any kind and never uses exclamation marks; every colour choice must work for colour blind players (say how: shape, icon or pattern, never colour alone); Sky Wolf Studio, singular, is the studio name. Be specific to what you saw on the live page: name the screen and the spot.

Keep the game's own look: neon 1980s on dark, each pest's accent colour on top. The tag shapes setting already gives colour blind players a second cue on the pods; build on it.

Format, strictly: one numbered block per direction: Title / What and why (3 lines max) / Where on screen / Effort S, M or L / Priority 1 to 3. Group them under these headings: Core gameplay, Powers, Modes and progression, Versus and social, The dance battle, Difficulty, Art and look, Bugs you saw. End the document with a json block: an array of objects with keys id, title, group, effort, priority, summary. No prose after the json.

5. Part two: the asset pack (only what is missing)

5.1 Style

Match the July pack exactly: bold 1980s b boy cartoon, thick black ink outline, flat neon colour with one hard shadow tone, spray paint texture in the backgrounds, chunky readable silhouettes. Each character keeps its accent colour, pants and gear from the list in section 2. Not a 3D render, not pixel art.

5.2 Rules for every file

  • PNG with a transparent background for every sprite, prop, icon and overlay. If your tool cannot make true transparency, use a flat pure magenta #FF00FF background with no magenta anywhere inside the art, and say so in the manifest; we key it out. JPG only for full bleed plates.
  • Deliver at the sizes given. Never a side over 1600 px.
  • Animations: one picture per frame, named like cheer_01.png, cheer_02.png. Every frame of a set on the same canvas size, the subject centred, the feet or base on the same line in every frame, the same scale across every set of one character. Characters face RIGHT; we mirror for left.
  • No text baked into any picture, except a logo or wordmark where the list asks for one.
  • No real trademarks, logos or known characters.
  • One light direction across the whole pack: upper left.
  • File names lowercase with underscores, exactly as listed. If you add something of your own, put it in a folder called extras/ and list it in the manifest.
