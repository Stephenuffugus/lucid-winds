2. The game in two minutes

Live: https://lucidwinds.com/satellites/shell-shuffle/, the only thing under review. Open it on a phone, portrait. A cup and ball shell game, one screen, part of the Sky Wolf Studio arcade.

  • A round: the cups lift for about a second to show the ball, drop, shuffle, then you tap the cup you think hides it. Right: coins, the level goes up and the next round starts. Wrong: the right cup is shown and the run is over; the button offers Play Again one level lower. No lives, no second chance.
  • The ramp: cups = level plus one, up to 6. Swaps grow with the level (up to 44), each swap gets faster (down to about a tenth of a second), and from level 11 two pairs can swap at once.
  • Coins per win: cups x 5, plus the win streak x 3, plus 8 above level 5. A best level is kept.
  • Daily: a 7 day coin calendar (15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 80), one day of grace, a weekly goal of 5 days, and six streak only toys to unlock: Toy Car (3 days), Toy Dino (7), Toy Alien (14), Robot (30), Rubber Duck (5 days in one week), Champion Cup (a 2 week streak). An optional reminder at 7 pm.
  • Shop: 70 cups (Sunset, Moonlit and Forest are free; owned cups rotate each level) and 46 balls, plus a ball designer (2 colours, 4 patterns: solid, stripes, dots, split). Coins only. The studio's shared currency (Sunbeams) is earned too: 3 a level won, 5 a daily claim, 30 a day at most.
  • Screens: the main screen (Level, Daily streak and Coins pills at the top, the title, a status line, the play area, the Start button, How to play, a link back to the arcade); How to play; Pause (only during a shuffle); the Shop; Daily Rewards. There is no settings screen, no results screen, no share.
  • Sound: none at all. Only phone vibration.
  • Art today: the cups and balls are small painted pictures (cups 90 px wide); everything else is code: a purple gradient background, a faint oval for the table, CSS buttons, a CSS coin. Fonts Fredoka and Nunito.

3. What is weak, honestly (go past these)

  • The title screen is an empty purple gradient: the title at the top, the button at the bottom, nothing in between.
  • In play, two or three small cups sit low on the screen in a sea of purple. No table, no stage, no hands, no character, no place.
  • No sound at all, so the shuffle has no rhythm and the reveal has no payoff.
  • One miss ends everything with no consolation and no results screen; nothing to share.
  • The shop is 70 cups three to a row with no filter; the cups are tiny 90 px pictures that go soft on a big phone.
  • It is one mode: watch, guess, repeat.

4. Part one: the review

The studio's note is one word: "improvements". The real ask: what would make this the best shell game on Google Play, the one people play every day and would pay 99 cents for. Think about: the feel of a round (the anticipation, the lift, the shuffle's rhythm, the reveal); modes beyond one endless run (a daily shuffle that is the same for everyone, tracking two balls, finding the empty cup, a timed run, a story or a dealer character, anything better); progression and collecting (should cups be a collection with sets, rarities and places to show them off); the scene (a place: a street corner, a carnival booth, a midnight greenhouse, your call); the results moment and sharing; sound and music; retention without guilt.

The bar every direction must respect: portrait phone first, 412 x 915 is the main viewport (also check 360 and 375 wide); every touch target at least 48 px; no text under 0.7 rem; player facing copy never uses dashes of any kind and never uses exclamation marks; every colour choice must work for colour blind players (say how: shape, icon or pattern, never colour alone); Sky Wolf Studio, singular, is the studio name. Be specific to what you saw on the live page: name the screen and the spot.

The house look across the arcade is midnight: deep blacks, sage #7ab356, gold #c8a84b, cream #e8dcc8. Shell Shuffle may keep its own warmer colours if you argue for it.

Format, strictly: one numbered block per direction: Title / What and why (3 lines max) / Where on screen / Effort S, M or L / Priority 1 to 3. Group them under these headings: Feel and juice, Modes and depth, Progression and collecting, Screens and UI, Art and look, Sound and music, Daily and retention, Bugs you saw. End the document with a json block: an array of objects with keys id, title, group, effort, priority, summary. No prose after the json.

5. Part two: the art pack

5.1 Style

Your call, argued in the review: we lean toward a cosy painted scene with warm lamp light (an old request asked for a walnut table, a plum curtain and a pool of lamp light), chunky readable cups that look like real objects you could pick up, and a ball that catches the light. Not pixel art, not photoreal, not a 3D render.

5.2 Rules for every file

  • PNG with a transparent background for every sprite, prop, icon and overlay. If your tool cannot make true transparency, use a flat pure magenta #FF00FF background with no magenta anywhere inside the art, and say so in the manifest; we key it out. JPG only for full bleed plates.
  • Deliver at the sizes given. Never a side over 1600 px.
  • Animations: one picture per frame, named like cheer_01.png, cheer_02.png. Every frame of a set on the same canvas size, the subject centred, the feet or base on the same line in every frame, the same scale across every set of one character. Characters face RIGHT; we mirror for left.
  • No text baked into any picture, except a logo or wordmark where the list asks for one.
  • No real trademarks, logos or known characters.
  • One light direction across the whole pack: upper left.
  • File names lowercase with underscores, exactly as listed. If you add something of your own, put it in a folder called extras/ and list it in the manifest.
