HOW TO WORK (Stephen's standing order; it binds this pack)

  reports. The last rounds of work on other games spent most of their time on tests that proved nothing, and he pays

WHAT SIXFOLD IS
A stance duel, under a minute a match, played in portrait with one thumb. Six stances on a wheel: Heaven (up), Tiger
(up right), River (down right), Earth (down), Crane (down left), Shadow (up left). Each round both fighters lock a
hidden stance; the clockwise distance between the two picks decides the exchange: the same stance clashes (blades
lock), one step ahead is a clean hit (2), two steps a glance (1), three a whiff, four and five are theirs. Every stance
beats two, loses to two, whiffs one and mirrors one, so nothing is safe and nothing is strong: there is no counterpick,
only a read. Four health. After round six every hit does one more. When blades lock, a Clash Bind: a fast three way
pick, Drive, Slip, Trap, best of one; the winner lands a glance, a tie sparks off harmlessly.
What makes it unique: the game reads YOU. A Predictor (an ensemble of cheap pattern readers: favourite stance, repeat,
rotation, markov, anti repeat) watches your picks. The AI uses it to read you; your Unpredictability score (0 to 100)
comes from it and is shown live for both fighters; after a match you are told your biggest tell and your most
exploitable round. Eight AI personalities, each a habit you can exploit and a quality of read that threatens you:
Berserker (repeats the stance that just hit), Stone (bunches picks in one arc), Drunkard (never repeats), Mirror
(plays your last stance), Metronome (marches a fixed step), Trickster (beats your counter), Ghost (near random with a
sharp read), Echo (learns you in real time). Difficulty is one knob: it scales the read up and the telegraph down. No
stat ever changes.
A Resolve meter (3 beads) fills on whiffs and binds and buys only information: Insight (see their last ten picks and
their Resolve), Foresight (highlight the Predictor's guess at their next pick on the wheel). Assist charms (Hourglass,
Eye, Compass, Scroll) are for casual play only and go inert in anything ranked or seeded. Accessibility is free.
Modes: The Ascent, a ladder of 70 rungs (each a personality at a difficulty on an arena, with a fighter skin as the
reward; the start is the Crimson Ronin in the Dojo); Daily Duel (seeded, the same foe for everyone); Ranked Duel
(Find a rival, a Firebase matchmaker, tiers Iron, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Jade, Onyx); a Collection; a Profile; a
result screen with the tell readout and a win streak. Its own music (two tracks, Locked Blades and Last Breath) and
synthesised stings. Sunbeams, the arcade's currency, pay out for skill and style, never power.
The pillar, which binds the art: SKILL IS THE ONLY CURRENCY, ART IS THE ONLY PURCHASE. A skin, an arena, a charm never
touches damage or odds. Make it beautiful; it stays cosmetic.
It lives at https://lucidwinds.com/satellites/sixfold/ in the Sky Wolf Studio arcade, open to players, installable,
offline. Source of truth is the GitHub repo Stephenuffugus/sixfold (the arcade carries a vendored copy). 34 MB in 123
files, most of it the skins and the arenas.

THE HARD LINES
- One HTML file at ship, vanilla JavaScript, no frameworks. Art arrives as PNG (sheets, sprites), JPG or WebP
  (plates), SVG (marks). The skin contract above is frozen: 3 by 2, facing right, anchor 50/88, square cells.
- Phone first, portrait, one thumb. The wheel is the control; the two fighters are the show. Every target 48 px.
- The pillar: nothing you draw may imply power. No rarity tiers that read as strength, no glow on a skin that
  suggests it hits harder. Rank seals and charms are about the player, not the fighter.
- Nothing from another game, no real people, no brands. Yokai and warriors are folklore and fair; do not copy a known
  character design.
- No text in plates except the wordmark and what a row asks for; no dashes or exclamation marks in sample copy; Google
  Fonts only (the game sets a serif today; propose one that reads like brushed type if the look wants it); generated
  art is never called hand painted; never a side over 1600 px except store sizes; a sheet under 200 KB at web size.

WHAT "FEEL INVOLVED" MEANS (the bar)
Today a duel is two cutouts on a blurred photograph with a wheel of arrows under them. The systems underneath are the
best in the arcade and none of it shows. Every exchange should be an event with four beats the player can feel: the
lock in (both commit, tension), the reveal (both pivot and lunge), the hit (something real happens to a body), the
consequence (a pip goes, the escalation rises, the read bar moves). Between duels: a ladder you can see yourself on,
foes who look like their tells, a collection that feels like a shrine, a rank that feels earned, a result card worth
sending. The six stances should be a real iconographic system you could put on a shirt. The Clash Bind should look
like a held breath. The Predictor's read of you should be visible as ink spreading, not a percentage.

WHAT NOT TO DRAW
- Anything that implies power. No rarity glow, no stat plaques.
- Left facing fighters, ground shadows baked under feet, cells at different scales. The contract is the contract.
- Known characters from anime or games wearing a yokai name.
- Text inside plates beyond the wordmark and what a row asks. Store screenshots (we compose those). Sounds (notes go
  in NOTES.md).
