# Sky Wolf Studio, the ninety day plan (Sep 06 2026, Fable, for Stephen)

Written the evening Stephen said the studio is six months in the red, has about two thousand
dollars, and asked for the best moves. This is the operating plan: what the studio is, the one
equation that matters, the rule for the next ninety days, the calendar, the numbers to read every
Friday, and the kill criteria. Everything in it costs nothing unless it says a price.

## 1. What the studio is today, in facts

- Production: one person plus Claude, shipping at a pace no five person studio matches. About a
  hundred and ninety web games on lucidwinds.com, twelve new ones with real physics and gates
  built in two days, Jumping Jimothy on Steam for Sep 18 at $2.99, Flock the World built for
  Google Play at $0.99 with its Steam page drafted, Fretwork prepared for Play, Lucid Winds on
  Pi, Blockspace, Loaf, Keepsies, Conduit, Tangent, Ripcord. Production is not the constraint
  and never will be.
- Distribution: near zero. No mailing list, no Discord, no following, no reviews, no wishlist
  number anyone has quoted, no store listing live yet. Every launch so far has happened to an
  empty room.
- Money: about two thousand dollars, tools of roughly three hundred a month, six months of
  losses, no business bank until Tuesday, a payments profile on Play, a Steam account with one
  title paid for.
- Time: Stephen's is the scarce resource; Fable's and Opus's are cheap and get cheaper.

## 2. The one equation

Revenue is reach times conversion times price. The studio has price and it has product. It has no
reach. Every dollar of revenue the studio has not made is missing on the reach side, and no
amount of new product changes that, because a new title launched into the same empty room adds a
zero to the sum. The correlated fact that matters most: a store hands out free visibility exactly
once per title, at launch (Steam's new releases lists, Play's new app windows), and it hands out
more of it to titles that arrive with a crowd (wishlists on Steam, day one retention on Play).
So a launch without a crowd spends the studio's one free moment on nothing, and a launch with a
crowd compounds. The studio's job for ninety days is to build the crowd for one title at a time,
and to build one asset that every title shares.

## 3. The rule for ninety days

1. **One flagship at a time.** Jimothy until Sep 18. Flock the World from Sep 19. A third from
   the twelve in November, chosen by data, not by fondness.
2. **The web is the funnel, the stores are the till.** Every flagship is free in the browser
   with no wall; a share becomes a play in one tap. The Play and Steam copies at their one price
   are the "own it" tier for the people who came back. This is already the Jimothy shape.
3. **One shared audience asset.** A Sky Wolf Studio newsletter (a free tier list, one email per
   launch) and a Discord, linked from every game's title screen and every post. Cost nothing.
   This is the only thing that carries a crowd from one title to the next.
4. **Thirty minutes a day of Stephen, not more.** The kits (`docs/JIMOTHY-LAUNCH-KIT.md`,
   `docs/FTW-LAUNCH-KIT.md`) are written so the daily act is a paste and honest replies. Fable
   drafts, Stephen posts and answers. Nobody but Stephen speaks as the studio.
5. **No paid acquisition until there is a retention number.** The free web link and the clips are
   the test. When Play's console shows day one retention over thirty percent for Flock, a hundred
   dollar ad test is allowed, once, to learn a cost per install. Not before.
6. **The twelve and the fleet are the pipeline, not the plan.** They ship to the web arcade as
   they are polished. None gets a store listing until it has shown plays per day on the web that
   the others do not.
7. **Stop building new games until December.** Polish and reach. Opus has weeks of listed work
   on the twelve; that is enough product for the year.

## 5. Money

- Keep the two thousand for tools. Three hundred a month is roughly six months of runway; that
  runway is the studio. Nothing on this plan needs it.
- Spend, in this order and only when the calendar says: the lawyer hour on the Flock title (two
  to four hundred, before its Steam page), Flock's Steam fee (a hundred, after Play numbers), one
  ad test (a hundred, after retention).
- Revenue expectations, stated plainly so nothing is planned on them: a $2.99 Steam title with a
  few hundred wishlists sells tens of copies in its first week, not hundreds; a $0.99 Play title
  nets eighty four cents a copy after the fifteen percent tier. Neither pays a month of tools in
  its first month. What they buy is a number: wishlists, retention, a review count, a following.
  The number is what the next launch is priced against, and the next, and that is the only way
  out of the red that is not luck.
- The B2B idea (the attention protocol) is the one thing in the studio that could pay bills on a
  different scale, and it is also the one thing with no product behind its central claim. It
  stays parked until a flagship has a crowd, because a pitch with a live audience number behind
  it is a different pitch.

## 7. Kill criteria, so the plan cannot drift

- If Jimothy has under two hundred wishlists on Sep 17, the release still happens, and the
  lesson is that the two week campaign started too late; Flock's starts on Sep 07, not Sep 19.
- If Flock's day one retention is under fifteen percent, no Steam page, no ad test; the first ten
  minutes get rebuilt from the Play reviews and the plan's November slot goes to Flock again.
- If by Dec 06 no number in section 6 has reached half its target, the ninety days after are
  spent on one title only, with everything else frozen, and the newsletter is the product.
