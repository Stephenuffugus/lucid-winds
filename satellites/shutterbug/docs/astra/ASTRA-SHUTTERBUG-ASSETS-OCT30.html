How Astra works

Astra makes the hero first, stops for Stephen's review, then makes everything else in batches and saves its work as it goes.

  1. Paste. Stephen pastes the s10 prompt into a fresh Astra chat. It is one self contained brief, the shape used since Rabbit Ronin (docs/briefs/rabbit-ronin).
  2. Batch 0, the hero. Astra makes the eight hero strips, a concept sheet, three review sheets and two GIFs. It saves a checkpoint, sets its Gate line to waiting, and stops.
  3. Stephen reviews. He judges the hero against the s3 checklist and replies APPROVED or CHANGES. On CHANGES Astra fixes only what he names, saves a checkpoint and stops again, until he writes APPROVED.
  4. Rulings, at any time. RULINGS may ride with APPROVED, CHANGES or CONTINUE, or come alone. A READY ruling turns a HOLD row into work, even in a finished batch; DROP removes it.
  5. Batches 1 to 5. Enemies and bosses, world, loot, screens and interface, effects. Each makes its READY rows, shows a review sheet beside the approved hero, saves a checkpoint and waits for CONTINUE.
  6. Save as you go. After every finished file Astra updates progress.md and manifest.json and posts the file's source lines. It saves a cumulative checkpoint zip after every 3 game files, each image tool picture and each batch. A new session resumes from the newest checkpoint plus any source lines posted after it, and RESUME alone never passes a gate.
  7. Delivery. One zip, shutterbug-assets.zip, lists every unruled row as missing. Stephen uploads it to assets/ here. Claude Code extracts it outside the repo, vaults it, checks it, cuts it into pixel strings and wires it in.

Before writing APPROVED, Stephen can send the batch 0 checkpoint here. Claude Code then puts the hero in the game and shoots it at 412 and 360 wide, as his phone shows it.

The look

Every file Astra makes is native size pixel art on one fixed palette of 16 colours, drawn over the player's own photo.

The level is the player's photo, quantized, dimmed and tinted per biome (BUILD-PLAN 7.3; index.html core-palette). So Astra paints no backgrounds and no tile sets.

The game stores every sprite as palette indices, one character per pixel, so each file must be exact. Sources are files under satellites/shutterbug/ unless a path says otherwise.

One game pixel is one CSS pixel, about 3 device pixels on most phones (DESIGN 3). Screens 768 CSS px wide or more show 2x, and under 384 the canvas shrinks to fit, 0.9375 at 360 (QUESTIONS Q5). So a 16 px enemy shows about 15 CSS px wide on a 360 phone, the moth's test (PROGRESS T0.16, T0.17).

Pixel rules

  • Every opaque pixel is exactly one of the 16 hexes. The sprites gate samples drawn pixels and fails anything between them (test/sprites.mjs; BUILD-PLAN T0.17).
  • Alpha is 0 or 255. The atlas writes every drawn pixel fully opaque (index.html buildAtlas).
  • Native size only: no anti aliasing, blends or smoothing, and never a painting shrunk down (DESIGN 2; docs/briefs/ASTRA-GRAPHICS-METHODS-2026-09-24.md C2).
  • Each frame is a string: '.' clear, 0 to 9 and a to f a palette index, rows split by '/' (BUILD-PLAN 1.7).
  • Each sprite name has one size and any number of frames. The game must stay under 40 names (tools/lint.mjs; harness.js H-spr-1).
  • Today it has 14 names and 36 frames (index.html SPRITES, read at 16:31 UTC on 30 Oct, after T1.10). The hero has 12 frames and the five enemies 2 each. Spike 2, crusher 3, speck 2, scroll 1, blueprint 1, chest 2, beacon 2 and tab 1.
  • Exceptions come only by Stephen's ruling. A row ruled READY may name its own palette, tool and size, such as a painted photo or a glyph set.
  • No sound files. Every sound is synthesised in the page, and the music is Stephen's own (DESIGN 1, 11; BUILD-PLAN law 7).

Outlines and colour roles

  • Ink outlines every creature, item and trap. Collision edges are Glare over Ink (BUILD-PLAN 7.1, 7.3).
  • The film tab is the exception: a tile laid edge to edge across its 2 cells. It has Ink perforations and an Ink bottom row, and no side or top outline. Loose Glare pixels outside an outline are allowed for rays, teeth, glints and highlights (index.html SPRITES tab, beacon, crusher).
  • The hero is a Sun fill with an Ink outline, an Ink feet row and a Glare eye (BUILD-PLAN 7.3).
  • Enemies get an Ink outline plus a Glare highlight pixel, never the hero's outline weight (BUILD-PLAN 7.3).
  • Danger is Brick and Magenta with an Ink outline and Glare tips. A fully saturated red or magenta pixel always means a sprite or a trap (BUILD-PLAN 7.3).
  • Sun belongs to the hero and the beacon: H-spr-1 fails any other sprite holding it. BUILD-PLAN 7.3 also gives Sun to the chest trim, an open conflict (harness.js H-spr-1).
  • Film is for repairs only, and the quantizer never produces it (BUILD-PLAN 7.1; QUESTIONS Q13). Today's speck and open chest placeholders use Film, and Astra's must not (index.html SPRITES speck, chest).
  • Loot is Amber with a Glare glint, so a speck never reads as the hero (BUILD-PLAN 7.3). Today's speck placeholder is Glare and Film instead.
  • Highest contrast belongs to collision surfaces and immediate danger (DESIGN 3).
  • No words in game art. Any word drawn, such as a wordmark, follows the copy rules: no dashes, no exclamation marks, "Sky Wolf Studio" singular (BUILD-PLAN rule 6).

Clay, motion and light

  • Living things are stop motion clay that reads through motion, not texture (DESIGN 2). That means squash on landing, stretch on jump, 12 fps, a one pixel idle wobble and no thumbprints.
  • The sim runs 60 steps a second and a sprite holds each frame 5 steps, 12 fps (TUNING STEP_HZ, ANIM_FPS).
  • The atlas stores a mirror of every frame (index.html buildAtlas). The game draws a mirror only for the hero, by its facing or its cling's wall, and for the thorn crawler, by its walk (index.html R.draw).
  • Everything else is drawn as stored: the moth, icicle, jelly, spark and their elites, the traps, the loot, the beacon and the tab.
  • No light direction, shading or dithering rule exists (DESIGN, BUILD-PLAN and QUESTIONS searched). A one sided light flips with the mirror on the hero and the crawler: an open question.
  • The grid is 24x48 as ruled. The 36x64 fallback with an 8x12 hero is still open, and choosing it redraws every sprite (DESIGN 3; QUESTIONS Q43).

The hero

The hero is the Shutterbug, a 12x16 clay bug, and its eight strips are batch 0: the only thing Astra makes before Stephen's review.

DESIGN gives it one line, "a thumb-sized clay bug that fell out of the photo and climbs back up to the light" (DESIGN 2). It climbs the player's own furniture to the ceiling light (DESIGN 0). No doc sets its species, limb count or props.

Rules for every hero frame

  • Exactly 12x16, the hitbox's size, with Ink in the bottom row of every frame: the feet row (harness.js H-spr-1).
  • A Sun body, an Ink outline, Ink feet and a Glare eye (BUILD-PLAN 7.3). Today's draft adds Walnut flecks.
  • Only Ink, Sun, Glare and Walnut. The dash's 4 invulnerable steps redraw Sun and Walnut as Glare and keep Ink (index.html buildAtlas; QUESTIONS Q47).
  • Sprite key hero, with the frame names in the table (index.html SPRITES).
  • Facing right. The cling frame has its wall on the right; the game mirrors it for a wall on the left (index.html R.draw).
  • The anchor is the bottom centre. After a landing the game squeezes whatever frame shows into 14x14 for 6 steps. That is usually land, or run1 if a dash starts then, or fall if the hero runs off an edge.
  • For 6 steps after any takeoff it stretches the frame into 10x18: jump, or dive, cling or fall if they start then. The dead frame is never deformed, and the hitbox never changes (index.html R.draw, core-sim; QUESTIONS Q35).
  • The cling frame also shows while the hero rises along a wall with the rocker held toward it (index.html core-sim step 7).

Frame order, first match wins: dead, dive, cling, jump or fall, run1 in a dash, land, run, idle (index.html R.heroFrame).

Names and sheets

  • A game file is <folder>/<stem>.png, with the stem exactly as the tables give it: lower case and underscores.
  • One strip per animation: frames left to right, each exactly the frame size, with no gaps, padding or trimming. A strip is frames times the width, by the height.
  • A single image is one frame at native size. A tile set is its tiles left to right, in the order the manifest names them.
  • Every frame keeps its whole canvas. Its anchor places it: "bottom" stands on the bottom row, "top" hangs from the top row, "cell" floats.
  • A file changed after Stephen saw it keeps its name. Its version rises, and the old copy moves to extras/superseded/<stem>_v<N>.png.
  • Stable names keep the cutter's map fixed. The old rule of a new name per change (ASTRA-THREE-SISTERS-OCT25) would break it.
  • Checkpoints are shutterbug-checkpoint-NN.zip, cumulative, counting up from 01. The delivery is shutterbug-assets.zip.
  • Over 150 MB the delivery splits by folder into shutterbug-assets-1.zip and -2.zip, the Three Sisters cap and the Rabbit Ronin split rule.

What the cut script does

  1. Intake: Stephen uploads the zip to /workspaces/lucid-winds/assets/. Zips there are git ignored (assets/*.zip), but the folder itself is tracked and deployed (.gitignore; CLAUDE.md ASSETS).
  2. The zip is extracted only into its own new empty folder, /tmp/claude-1000/shutterbug-astra/, read with python3 -I, and nothing inside it is run. The upload stays in assets/ until the vault copy is proven (HANDOFF-FABLE-OCT25.md, section 1).
  3. Vault: the originals go to a private release with a SHA256SUMS file before any cut, and the zip is never committed (same source).
  4. Verify each entry: its sha256, RGBA mode, and a size of frames times w by h. Then alpha is 0 or 255 only, and every opaque pixel is one of the 16 hexes unless a ruling says otherwise. Every hero frame has Ink in its feet row. Last, no Sun outside the hero and the beacon, and the PNG equal to its source grid.
  5. Cut: slice each strip into equal frames, never trimming or rescaling. Equal cells are exact here because every frame is authored on the grid; painted packs needed alpha trims and measured slices instead (scripts/cut_art_sheet.py; satellites/bubblenaut/tools/bncut.py).
  6. Write: each frame becomes a pixel string in the SPRITES block of shell-render, under its sprite key and frame name (BUILD-PLAN 1.7). Interface art becomes inline data URIs, since only icons/ may hold binaries (BUILD-PLAN line 7).
  7. Wire: keep the game under 40 sprite names. Give the five elites their own names and draw them from their own 24 px art; today R.draw draws the fodder key at ELITE_PX. That makes 19 names with today's 14.
  8. Wire, continued: teach the render kinds with more than two frames. Rerun tools/icons.mjs on the approved hero; T1.11 built it, and its first run used the placeholder hero.
  9. Prove: the lint, H-spr-1 and the sprites gate, then shots at 412x915 and 360x780 with three things wrong named (BUILD-PLAN rule 3).
  10. Report: anything missing, off palette or the wrong size goes back to Stephen as a plain list for Astra's next session.
