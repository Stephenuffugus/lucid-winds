# K1 on a phone, ten things to try

The builder cannot run this. Stephen runs it when he wakes, and his notes in `PLAYTESTS.md` are K1.5,
the feel pass, which reopens `input/knuckle.js` and `tuning.json` and nothing else.

K1 is a playable game of Ringer against Dusty. Thirteen mibs, a ten foot ring, seven to win, and the
Knuckle. What is being asked is one question in two halves: **does the snap feel like a snap, and does
the marble weigh anything.** Everything else on this list is in service of answering that.

1. Press PLAY. The rules card comes first, three lines. Read them once. Do they tell you enough to take
   your first shot without guessing.
2. Drag anywhere on the dirt. Your shooter should slide along the ring edge. Is that discoverable, or did
   you only find it because this list told you.
3. Put your thumb on the shooter and HOLD it. The ring around it should shrink and turn gold as your hand
   settles, in about a second. Can you feel the difference between a settled hold and a twitchy one, or
   does it just look like a countdown.
4. Now flick, hard, straight up the screen. That is a break. Watch what happens to the cross. Then say
   whether the marbles behaved like marbles: too floaty, too sticky, or about right.
5. Flick again, but start your thumb at the BOTTOM of the shooter and flick up through it. That is
   backspin, and the shooter should stop dead near whatever it hit instead of carrying on through. That
   shot is called Sticking and it is the heart of the input. Did it feel like you did it, or like it
   happened to you.
6. Try the opposite: start at the top of the shooter. That is follow, and it should carry through.
   Then start from the left or right for english. Are the three distinguishable by feel.
7. Flick along a curved path, a hook. The game should call that a wild one. A wild shot is not a worse
   shot, it is a less predictable one; does it read that way, or does it just feel like a mistake.
8. Barely brush the shooter. Nothing should happen and you should not lose your turn. Then flick so your
   thumb leaves the side of the screen mid snap: that is a slip and the game should hand the turn back.
   Both of those are forgiveness, and both should feel fair rather than fussy.
9. Press TOP DOWN and take a shot from there. Is the tactical view worth having, or would you rather it
   zoomed instead.
10. Play a whole game to seven. Time it. Then say the honest thing: were the four seconds a shot takes to
    settle too long, and did the sixteen millimetre marbles read at that distance or were they just dots.

## What the builder already knows is wrong, so you do not have to write it down

- The middle of the frame is a lot of empty dirt. Real scale in a three metre ring does that.
- The result card floats over a black wash. The ceremony that belongs there is K2.
- There is no sound but impacts yet: no rolling, no warming shimmer.
- There is no calibration step, so your power curve is a default rather than yours.

## The two questions the build is actually asking

Does the snap feel like a snap. Does the marble weigh anything.
