How to play

The idea

Two tops go into a dish. You build yours, you program one move, you wind it by drawing circles with your finger, and then you do not touch the screen again. A round takes about six seconds. First to four points wins the match.

Winding

Put a finger on the white dot and draw circles around the top. Three laps is a full wind, and the counter in the middle turns green when you are there.

  • Which way you draw is which way it spins. That is the one tactical choice you make before the launch, and it decides whether the two tops mesh or scrub when they meet.
  • A messy wind is not a weak wind, it is a wild one. Sloppy circles put the metal in the wrong place, so the top travels further, hits harder on one side and runs out sooner. Fast scribbling wins plenty of rounds.
  • Stop at three. Past about four laps the string binds on itself and you lose power instead of gaining it.

Points

  • Spinout, one point. The other top simply ran out.
  • Ringout, two points. You put them over the lip.
  • Knockout, two points. You toppled them with a strike.
  • Burst, two points. One huge connected hit popped them apart.

Why an unbalanced top is dangerous

Weights bolted to one side make the top wobble. A wobbling top hits enormously when its heavy side comes round at the moment of contact and feebly when it does not, and you can see the wobble before the hit lands. It also travels further, bites the rail harder, and burns through its spin. That trade is the whole game.

The workshop

  • Five slots. Core sets your spin direction and your one move. Blade is the weapon. Assist shapes the rim. Ratchet sets height and how hard you are to pop apart. Bit is the tip, and it decides what kind of top you are.
  • Weights. Up to four, in twelve holes. Spread them and they cancel out; bunch them and you get a monster.
  • Tuning. File, wax, drill and shim your parts. Every operation is a trade, all of it is free, and all of it undoes.
  • Rigs. Certain combinations of parts have a name. The workshop tells you the moment you have made one. Two can be active at once.
  • Bench. Runs two hundred rounds against your current opponent instantly, so you can prove a change worked instead of hoping.

Your one move

Your core carries a move and you choose when it fires: when it is charged, when the spin drops, on the third hit, on the rail, and so on. Charge comes only from the round itself, from landing hits, taking them, and riding the rail.

Where parts come from

Winning. Every rung of the ladder drops one and there is no shop, no currency and nothing to buy. Beating a league boss is the only way to get a Relic.
