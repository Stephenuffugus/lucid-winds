Deckhand Jimothy

assets/skins/deckhand/

AnatomyHe is a raccoon, so the body is Jimothy's body and every hero pose transfers one to one — the real animation work is the costume. The navy cape and the coil of rope are cloth and line, not stickers: the cape must hang wet and vertical at rest, snap flat behind him in the air, and flog in a run, while the rope must have weight, sag, and a paw or shoulder actually carrying it in all eighteen frames. The yellow hat is tied under the chin, which is the honest solution for the frames where a hat would fly off — it slips onto its cord and stays with him.

  1. 01idleconstantly

    Standing four-square facing camera, weight settled back on his heels, the rope coil hanging straight down from his right paw and pulling that shoulder a little lower than the other. Cape hangs in wet vertical folds, one fat bead of rain gathering at the front of the yellow brim, face calm and patient like a man watching for a boat that is nine minutes late.

  2. 02situntil they move

    Sat upright on his haunches, the rope coil set down in front of him and both paws folded on top of it, cape puddled around his hips. Hat tipped back off his brow so the whole face reads, eyes half-lidded and content, looking somewhere past the camera at water we cannot see.

  3. 03eatuntil they move

    Still sitting, using the coil of rope as a table — an open paper boat of ferry-galley clam strips balanced on it, which is the joke. One knuckle shoves the hat brim up, the other paw lifts a strip halfway to a full cheek, tartar sauce on the whiskers.

  4. 04crouch0.07s ×hundreds a run

    Flattened low on all fours, haunches up and loaded, cape slid forward over his shoulders and bunched at the neck. Both paws are on the ground so the rope coil is clamped in his teeth, hat brim jammed down to the eyebrows, eyes locked forward.

  5. 05leap0.15s ×hundreds a run

    Fully airborne, limbs spread wide and belly forward, cape snapped dead straight behind him like a signal flag. The rope has come loose in his paw and two open loops of it hang in the air behind him, hat held on by the taut chin cord, mouth open in delight.

  6. 06land0.17s ×hundreds a run

    Front paws planted, body low, weight absorbed through the shoulders, the rope hitting the ground beside him in a slack heap with dust and rain kicking up off it. Cape flops forward over one shoulder and the hat brim has bounced down over one eye; the free paw is already pushing it back up. Whiskers forward.

  7. 07run-r0.28s

    Side-on sprint to the right, body low, legs extended, cape streaming off to the left behind him. The rope coil is hooked over his far shoulder and swinging out of rhythm with his stride, one paw up clamping the hat down.

  8. 08dash-run0.19s

    Flat out to the right, legs at full stretch, cape horizontal, the rope coil bouncing hard off his hip and starting to unspool. Hat mashed flat to his skull by the wind with the chin cord snapped straight out behind, eyes wide, white speed streaks and rain torn sideways across the frame.

  9. 09coffee4.2s – 7.0s

    Standing, but vibrating — rigid, knees locked, fur bristled, holding a scorched ferry-galley paper cup at chest height with pink steam ripping off it. Gold-orange fire streaks curl up around his feet, the rope coil quivers on his shoulder, pupils gone to pinpricks under a trembling yellow brim.

  10. 10magnet6.5s

    Standing with both arms open and mouth wide in delight, having thrown one long loop of rope out to the left — gold bottlecaps, coins and fries spiral in along that line toward him like beads running down a mooring rope. Hat pushed back, cape flung open, whole body leaning into the haul.

  11. 11umbrellauntil it is hit

    He has lashed a black ferry-deck umbrella upright to his own coil of rope with a neat little hitch so it stands on its own and both paws stay free. He stands under it with arms folded, faintly smug, wearing a rain hat and a rain cape underneath it anyway, rain sheeting off the umbrella rim in a ring around him.

  12. 12shield0.55s

    Crouched and flinching at the instant of impact, one forearm across his face and the other holding the rope coil up in front of him like a buckler. A blue-violet energy dome closes around him with rain streaking off the outside of it and the rope fibres lit up along their edges inside; ears flat, one eye squeezed shut, hat brim pressed down inside the dome.

  13. 13scared0.82 – 1.15s

    Up on his hind legs, hugging the rope coil to his chest like a life ring, head tilted back and up so the hat brim rides off his brow and the eyes go huge. Cape hem lifting, tail rigid, a hard gull-shaped shadow falling across his chest and the wet ground beside him.

  14. 14fleeseconds at a time

    Sprinting hard to the left, mouth open in flat panic, the rope half unspooled and whipping out behind him in loose flailing loops he has no time to gather. Hat blown back onto its chin cord and bouncing on his shoulders, cape flogging, red speed streaks and two sharp "!!" marks over his head.

  15. 15cheer2.6s held

    Standing tall and thrown open, one arm flung to the sky, and at the top of the throw a loop of his rope hangs in the air above him as a perfect open circle, like a halo he tied himself. Mouth wide with joy, hat shoved back off the brow, cape thrown open, both feet planted proud.

  16. 16ko1.55s held

    Flat on his back with his legs in the air, wrapped shin to shoulder in three neat turns of his own rope like a badly parcelled package — undone by the one thing he is good at. The hat has fallen over his face leaving one dazed eye showing, mouth open, gold stars circling above the brim.

  17. 17dizzy1.55s held

    Sprawled and sitting up crooked, spiral eyes, tongue out, hat spun around backwards on its cord and the cape twisted up under his chin like a bib. The whole coil has come undone into a loose spiral on the wet ground around him, which for a deckhand is the actual injury; stars circling his head.

  18. 18splash1.55s held

    Chest deep in dark water, fur plastered flat, arms up and mouth wide in shock, a white crown of water bursting outward around him. One paw still grips the rope and the line runs taut up and out of the top of the frame — he never let go of the tie-off — with the yellow hat floating brim-up beside him, still knotted to his throat.

Cutting & layout

  • Crop hazards above the head: the thrown lasso circle in cheer and the lashed umbrella in umbrella both extend well above his ears. Leave real headroom in those cells or the cutter will shave the top off the gag.
  • Crop hazards sideways: the trailing rope in flee, the thrown magnet line, and the taut line in splash all run out to the frame edge. Keep every rope end inside its own cell with a visible gap before the divider line, or the cutter will read two components and slice the rope off.
  • He is asymmetric — rope coil on his right shoulder, brass cape clasp offset at the throat. Mirroring run-r puts the coil and the clasp on the wrong side, so paint run-l as its own frame rather than letting the engine flip it.
  • Scale is measured ears-to-feet, not cape-spread. The cape flares wide in leap, dash-run and cheer and will read as a size jump if the body is painted smaller to fit it inside the cell — keep the raccoon the same height in all eighteen and let the cape crowd the gap instead.
  • Lock the hat yellow and the rope tan to one value each across the whole sheet. At 190px on a rainy street those two colours are the entire silhouette read, and a frame painted a half-step darker will flicker in the hop cycle.
  • The hat is on a chin cord in every frame; that is what lets it slip in flee, ko, dizzy and splash without ever leaving him. Never paint it separated from the body — a loose hat somewhere in the cell becomes its own sprite when the sheet is cut.

Market Day Jimothy

AnatomyOrdinary raccoon body, so every pose in Jimothy's pack works here anatomically. The real problem is that both hands are already full, which is also the joke. Solve it by giving each hand a permanent job: the brown paper grocery bag rides the LEFT arm hugged at the hip, and the RIGHT paw stays free to grab, point, shield and cheer, usually with the baguette in it. The green satchel hangs on a chest strap and never comes off, so it can swing, flap open or slap his belly to sell motion without him ever setting anything down.

Cutting & layout

  • Silhouette is wider and taller than a bare raccoon because of the props. The baguette in cheer goes well above head height, and flying groceries break the body outline in leap, land, flee and magnet. Give every frame generous magenta margin and give the cheer row extra headroom so the baguette tip is not clipped.
  • He is asymmetric on purpose: satchel strap over the right shoulder, grocery bag always on the LEFT arm. Mirroring run-r would flip the bag to the wrong arm and the strap to the wrong shoulder, so paint the optional run-l by hand for this character rather than letting the engine mirror it.
  • Baguette length is a continuity prop. Full length in all seventeen other frames, visibly half eaten only in eat. Do not shorten it anywhere else.
  • The scallion in his teeth is the identity tell in the standing frames. It moves behind one ear for run-r and dash-run when the baguette takes the mouth, and hangs out of his slack mouth in scared and dizzy. It should never simply vanish.
  • Bottom-anchor on his hind feet, never on the grocery bag or the trailing satchel, or he will bob up and down between frames when the engine seats him on the row.
  • Head size is the scale check across all 18: keep the skull identical frame to frame. The bag is the thing that changes shape (full, crushed, open wide, tipped, dissolving), never him.
  • Lay out with white divider lines and clear gaps. Loose carrots, celery and bottlecaps float free of the body in several frames, so the cutter needs the dividers to know those pieces belong to that frame and not the next one.

Cutting & layout

  • The hat and the sash are the whole identity: orange hard hat, and two reflective stripes running left shoulder to right hip with the small violet clip where the sash crosses. Same stripe count, same clip, every frame — that pattern is what reads at 40px on a phone.
  • CROP HAZARD: the hat brim is the widest and highest point of his silhouette. Leave 8-10px of clear magenta above and to both sides of the brim in every frame, and the same around the raised paddle in umbrella, the raised arm in cheer and the outer water spray in splash.
  • CUTTER: the cut reads connected components, so nothing may float free on magenta. In ko the tipped-off hat must physically touch his ear; in magnet the bottlecap spiral must touch his paws or the brim; in splash the water burst must touch his fur. Anything detached gets dropped on the cut.
  • ASYMMETRY: the sash always runs over the LEFT shoulder to the RIGHT hip, so a mirrored run-r would flip the sash and the whole costume would read backwards. Paint a real run-l for this character rather than letting the engine mirror.
  • SCALE: the hat adds height that plain-fur skins do not have. Match his SHOULDER height to hero Jimothy, not his overall height, or he towers over the roster when they stand side by side.
  • Props come and go, the costume never does: the thermos appears in sit, eat and flee; the STOP paddle in umbrella and shield; the lunch pail only in eat. Keep them the same battered orange-and-white objects each time so they read as his, not as set dressing.
  • Rows separated by clear white divider lines with generous gaps. Never an even grid.

Cutting & layout

  • Identity in all 18: spawning colours are the costume. Sprites are bottom-anchored, so put real ground mass at the very bottom of the airborne frames (leap, cheer, splash): the puddle ring, the water crown, the thrown fan. Without it he will hover an inch above the road.
  • Asymmetry is severe on a fish. Eye, hooked jaw and the lure all read on ONE flank only, so mirroring run-r for run-l flips his eye to the wrong side and moves the lure across his back. Paint a real run-l for this character rather than letting the engine mirror.
