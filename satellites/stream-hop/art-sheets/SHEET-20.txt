JIMOTHY — SHEET 20
WAVE 5 — the three secrets

Found by playing, never sold in the bin. Sasquatch draws at 1.35x scale, so paint him larger in-
frame than the rest and hold that ratio across all eighteen.

ONE CHARACTER PER IMAGE SHEET. Never combine. Magenta #FF00FF. Same camera and same scale
across all eighteen frames. Clear gaps or white divider lines between rows — never an even
grid. Suggested layout 6 columns x 3 rows in the order below. Full rules: THE ANIMATION BIBLE.

====================================================================================================
GHOST JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/ghost/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He is a raccoon, so every pose reads straight off Jimothy's staging — the whole solve
is contact. He has no weight: every frame that was designed to hit the ground instead hovers a
finger's width above it with no cast shadow, his dark sooty paws are the only fully opaque part
of him, and anything he holds or that hits him is painted more solid than he is.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera, bone-white fur fraying into little drifting wisps at
    his shoulders and haunches, the wet street faintly visible through his belly. He floats a
    thumb's width off the asphalt with no shadow beneath him, only a thin ring of cold pale
    light, and his big dark eyes are calm and slightly apologetic, as if he knows he shouldn't
    be here.

 2. SIT
    Sitting upright on his haunches with paws folded on his belly, content, eyes half-closed —
    but his backside never quite reaches the curb, resting an inch above it. His faded tail
    curls round and its tip gives out into vapour instead of touching down, and two or three
    wisps of him drift up and off the top of the frame like steam off a manhole.

 3. EAT
    Sitting beside an open box of fries, holding one up to his mouth where it hangs half-
    eaten, because it never actually goes anywhere. One fry has already dropped straight
    through his chest and come to rest in his lap on nothing at all; the fries and the box are
    painted fully solid against his see-through paw, and he looks cheerful and mildly puzzled.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low with haunches up, front paws braced wide, eyes forward and huge — but his
    belly hangs a finger clear of the road, so nothing is actually being compressed. The
    gathered spring reads as pale vapour pooling under him and streaming backward off his
    hips.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, limbs spread wide, belly forward, mouth open and delighted, stretched
    further than a solid raccoon could manage. His face and front paws are the only crisp part
    of him; hind legs and tail trail off into a long comet-tail of blue-white smoke behind
    him.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted, body low, whiskers forward, eyes a touch startled that the ground was
    there at all — but there is no impact. The wisps that were streaming behind him fold down
    around his shoulders and puddle, and a soft ring of cold mist spreads outward from where
    his paws almost touch the asphalt.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on travel to the right, body low and legs extended, his dark sooty paws the only
    solid things and painted a hair above the road. His hind half smears into a long fog-
    streak, with three or four thinning afterimages of himself stacked behind him.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat-out sprint right, legs at full stretch, eyes wide, mouth open — and where Jimothy
    throws warm fire, he throws cold: blue-white contrails off his heels and a scatter of
    frost flecks. His rear third has gone entirely to vapour with the speed, two full ghost-
    afterimages stacked behind.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing wired and buzzing with a takeaway cup held against his chest, whiskers electric,
    eyes enormous, feet a clear inch off the pavement. The coffee is the most solid thing in
    the painting — a warm brown glow shining out through his translucent ribs — and its pale
    steam rises to join the wisps coming off his shoulders.

10. MAGNET
    Standing delighted with an open box of fries in one paw, mouth open, gold bottlecaps
    whirling in toward him from every side. Half the caps pass clean through his ribs and keep
    going, so the swirl is painted both in front of and behind his see-through body — he grins
    about it anyway.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing smug and dry-looking under an open umbrella held in a bleached paw, rain
    hammering down around it. The rain that misses the umbrella falls straight through him and
    splashes on the asphalt inside his own outline; the umbrella is painted fully solid, and
    he is not.

12. SHIELD
    Braced and flinching, paws up defensively inside a blue-violet energy dome, eyes screwed
    half shut in relief. The dome reads as more physical than he does — its light shines right
    through him and pins a hard bright rim along the near edge of his fur while rain streaks
    off the outside.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, paws clutched to his chest, eyes huge and fixed up-screen at whatever
    is lining up its dive. He is so alarmed he has gone faint: the bottom third of him has
    thinned almost to nothing and lifted clear, and wisps stream upward off his ear tips like
    he is about to come apart.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting hard to the left, mouth open in panic, two "!!" marks over his head, both hind
    feet entirely clear of the road. His panic streaks read cold rather than red — white-blue
    smears and frost flecks — and his tail is already a foot ahead of the rest of him as pure
    smoke.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall with one paw thrown to the sky, mouth open in pure joy, floating a full
    hand's height up with his hind legs dangling loose. For this one frame he is brighter and
    more solid than he has ever been, a warm glow coming up through his chest and blowing the
    fog back around him in a clean ring — somebody finally saw him and he loved it.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with legs in the air, mouth open, solid gold stars circling his head —
    except he has sunk halfway into the asphalt, his back half already gone through the road.
    Pale flakes of him drift slowly upward and away; funny and a little sad, never gory.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled with spiral eyes and tongue out, stars circling, wisps spiralling along with
    them. His outline has come uncoupled — a second fainter copy of himself has slid a few
    inches out to one side like a bad TV signal, and neither copy is quite touching the
    ground.

18. SPLASH
    Half sunk in the water with both arms up and a burst of spray around him, round shocked
    eyes and a comically open mouth. The burst goes right through him and out the far side, so
    the water reads in front of AND behind his see-through chest, and the current carries
    little white flecks of him off downstream.

    SHEET NOTES
    • IDENTITY CORRECTION — READ FIRST. Four frames already exist in assets/skins/ghost/
    (idle, crouch, leap, land) and they are NOT a bedsheet ghost. Stephen painted a
    translucent bone-white raccoon: bleached mask, faded tail bands, fur fraying into blue-
    white vapour at the shoulders, hips and tail tip, and dark sooty paws as the only opaque
    part. Those four are the reference for the new fourteen. Do not introduce a sheet.
    • He is the only character on the roster who never touches the ground. Leave clean
    magenta between his lowest paw and the bottom crop on EVERY frame so the engine's
    bottom-anchor preserves the float — cropping tight to the paws plants him and kills the
    whole character.
    • The vapour is a cutting hazard. Keep every wisp, contrail and afterimage physically
    ATTACHED to his silhouette as one connected component. Anything floating free in the
    magenta (stray flecks, detached stars, the outer bottlecaps in magnet, the second copy
    in dizzy) will be dropped or shipped as its own bogus sprite. If a star or cap must be
    separate, accept that it goes, or tuck it so it touches him.
    • run-l can be mirrored from run-r safely — no lettering, no asymmetric costume, only
    the dissolve pattern flips and nobody reads that as wrong. Painting it is optional here.
    • His effects run COLD: blue-white, frost, pale violet. Never Jimothy's warm orange fire
    streaks on dash-run or flee. This is the one place the roster deliberately diverges, and
    it is the tell that he is not alive.
    • Same size in all 18. The temptation on cheer, scared and leap is to float him bigger
    or looser — resist it, the engine does not rescale, and he must not grow.
    • No cast shadow anywhere, on any frame. Where the other characters get a contact
    shadow, he gets a thin ring of cold pale light or nothing at all.

RICH UNCLE JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/richuncle/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He is the same round raccoon body as Jimothy, so every pose maps one-to-one off the
hero sheet — the real work is that his hat, monocle and watch chain are HARD props sitting on a
body that squashes and stretches, so they have to be re-drawn honestly in each pose rather than
warped along with the fur (a smeared top hat reads as a mistake instantly). His comic engine is
that the body does the whole athletic pose while the face refuses to acknowledge it, and the hat
is the mood ring: dead level when he is fine, tipped when he is rattled, off his head entirely
when he is finished.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing the camera, weight settled back on his haunches, chin lifted,
    one forepaw resting on the waistcoat where the gold watch chain loops. Top hat dead level
    with rain beading on the wet silk, monocle catching a single bright glint, expression
    somewhere between amused and bored.

 2. SIT
    Sitting upright on his haunches the way a man sits in a leather club chair — spine
    straight, back ankles crossed, belly forward — holding his gold pocket watch open in one
    paw and half-lidding his eyes at it. He is checking a time he has no intention of hurrying
    for; the hat stays perfectly level, the chain runs taut from watch to waistcoat pocket.

 3. EAT
    Same club-chair sit, a dented takeaway fry box set on a little silver tray beside him and
    a white linen napkin tucked into his bow tie. He is eating exactly one fry, held between
    two claws with the pinky claw extended, eyes closed in mild approval — still a raccoon
    eating garbage, just served properly.

 4. CROUCH
    Gathered low on all fours, chest near the ground, haunches up, tail arched, front paws
    planted wide and loaded. Nothing in the face has changed at all — hat still dead level,
    monocle still seated, eyes forward and unhurried, like a sprinter who has done this a
    thousand times and finds it beneath comment.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, belly forward, front paws thrown wide and back legs trailing long. The top
    hat has lifted a half-inch clear of his head and hangs there, the watch chain flying out
    in a bright gold arc, the monocle somehow still in — and his face is serene, eyebrows
    barely raised, as though he is being carried rather than jumping.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted, body low and compressed, back legs still catching up, a small splash
    of street water bursting at his feet. The hat is settling back down onto his head with a
    fine ring of rainwater flicking off the brim, the waistcoat rippling — and his face is
    already composed again, whiskers forward, monocle level.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on sprint to the right, body low and long, legs extended, ringed tail streaming
    behind him — one forepaw clamped flat on the crown of the top hat to hold it down, the
    other reaching forward. Monocle and watch chain on the camera side and clearly readable;
    the wet street throws a smear of gold reflection under him.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out, legs at full stretch, coat tails and chain snapping straight back, gold speed
    streaks trailing off his heels — and from the neck up he is completely unchanged: hat
    level, monocle in, expression mildly bored. Gold bottlecaps kick up from his back feet and
    scatter behind him like sparks off a flint, all of them being left behind.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing, and for the first time in his life visibly rattled: fur bristling out through
    the waistcoat, eyes enormous, monocle popped out and swinging on its chain, a doll-sized
    espresso cup pinched between two claws with the pinky out. Pink steam and gold heat
    shimmer roll up around him and his back foot is a tapping blur — caffeine is the one thing
    old money cannot stay above.

10. MAGNET
    Standing, not so much as glancing at the money — he is reading his open pocket watch while
    a wide spiral of gold bottlecaps and fries funnels in toward him from every edge of the
    frame, drawn in with bright motion arcs. Some caps have already stacked themselves into
    neat little columns at his feet, and his head is tilted just enough to read as smug.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing under an enormous black silk umbrella with a brass handle, held one-pawed and
    perfectly upright, top hat still on underneath it because he sees no contradiction. Rain
    sheets off the canopy in a bright ring and the ground directly under him is dry and dusty;
    his eyes are half-lidded, entirely unbothered, faintly pleased with himself.

12. SHIELD
    The instant the shield eats a hit — braced in a quarter-turn away, one forepaw up, a blue-
    violet energy dome flaring hard around him with rain streaking off its surface. This is
    the single frame where the composure cracks: monocle knocked askew, top hat tipped down
    over one eye, mouth open in an undignified flinch.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, both forepaws clutched to his waistcoat, head craned up at the sky,
    eyes huge and round behind the monocle. The top hat has jumped a full inch straight off
    his head in fright and three gold bottlecaps are spilling out of the inside of it — that
    is where he keeps it.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left toward the camera edge, mouth open in real panic, one paw mashing the hat
    down onto his skull, watch chain and coat tails flying, red speed streaks and two hard
    "!!" marks over his shoulder. A trail of dropped bottlecaps bounces away behind him and he
    is not going back for them — which tells you exactly how frightened he is.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on his back legs, top hat swept off his head and thrown high in one paw in a
    grand full-arm doff, other arm flung to the sky, mouth open in genuine joy. A shower of
    gold bottlecaps pours out of the upturned hat and rains down over him like confetti,
    catching the light all the way down.

16. KO
    Flat on his back, legs straight up in the air, bow tie shoved sideways and the waistcoat
    rumpled. The top hat has rolled clear and sits wobbling on its brim beside him, the
    monocle has popped out and lies on his chest still tethered by its chain, and gold stars
    circle slowly above his head.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side, spiral eyes, tongue lolling, the top hat crushed down crooked over
    both ears so only his muzzle shows under the brim. Stars orbit the wreck of the hat and a
    fan of spilled bottlecaps lies scattered around him, one of them still spinning on its
    edge.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked to the skin with his fur plastered flat so he looks half his usual size, both arms
    up, water bursting white around him and his mouth open in shock. The top hat floats away
    just out of reach, brim-up like a little black boat with one bottlecap riding in it, and
    the monocle hangs full of water on its chain.

    SHEET NOTES
    • THE HAT IS THE MOOD RING — this is the whole read of the character, so paint it
    deliberately in every frame. Dead level = fine (idle, sit, eat, crouch, dash-run,
    magnet, umbrella). Tipped or lifted = rattled (leap, shield, scared, flee). Completely
    off his head = cheer, ko, dizzy, splash. If Stephen only paints one thing carefully per
    frame, it is the hat angle.
    • TALL HAT = CROP HAZARD. Sprites are bottom-anchored, so headroom at the top of the
    crop is free — take it. Do NOT shrink the raccoon to fit the hat in, or he changes size
    between frames. Watch cheer especially: the hat is thrown above him and the whole hat
    must be inside the paint area or it reads as a floating brim.
    • HE IS ASYMMETRIC. Monocle on one eye, watch chain looping one side of the waistcoat.
    If the engine mirrors run-r to fake run-l, the monocle jumps to the wrong eye and the
    chain reverses. This is a character worth painting the optional 19th frame (run-l) for.
    • HIS PAWS ARE EMPTY in the four paintings already shipped and playable (idle, crouch,
    leap, land). Do not give him a permanent cane or walking stick — every prop here is per-
    frame only (watch, fry tray, espresso cup, umbrella), so a partial delivery still
    matches what is in the game right now.
    • WET SILK is his silhouette tell. The hat should read glossy and rain-beaded in every
    outdoor frame — at 40px on a phone that gloss is the only thing separating him from
    First Frost and Nordic Jimothy.
    • BOTTLECAPS are his signature baked effect and appear in seven frames (dash-run,
    magnet, scared, flee, cheer, dizzy, splash). Same gold cap, same size, same paint every
    time, so they read as one currency and not as seven different bits of litter.

SASQUATCH      (files go to assets/chars/sasquatch/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He has hands and a fully upright body, so the raccoon poses transfer honestly — the
real problem is mass: an eight-foot shaggy man cannot bunny-hop, so he stands upright at rest
and drops to a gorilla knuckle-bound the moment he moves, contact points being two flat tan feet
and two knuckles. Everything Jimothy plays fast, Sasquatch plays one beat slow — read the speed
off his swinging coat, the flung rainwater and the streaming ferns, never off his face, which
stays calm in seventeen of the eighteen frames because that is the entire joke.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square and upright facing camera, long arms hanging past his knees,
    shoulders sloped, chin slightly down, the patient half-frown of someone who has waited out
    worse rain than this. Moss and sword-fern sprigs on both shoulders and the crown of his
    head, the orange chanterelle cluster on his heavy shoulder, water beading white in the
    dark fur, teal rim light down his edges and two soft puddle rings settling around his
    feet.

 2. SIT
    Sat straight down on the wet asphalt, cross-legged and enormous, knees wide, one knuckle
    planted on the road and the other palm turned up in his lap slowly filling with rainwater.
    Eyes half-closed, head tilted a few degrees like he is watching a bus go by that he
    remembers being a streetcar; ferns hang forward off his shoulders, rain rings spreading in
    the puddle he is sitting in.

 3. EAT
    Same cross-legged sit, but he has reached over and picked one little chanterelle off the
    cluster growing on his own forearm and is holding it up between two fingertips like a
    canapé, mouth just opening, eyes fondly crossed on it. The shoulder cluster is still there
    and one mushroom short; a few blackberries sit in his other cupped palm and rain keeps
    landing in them.

 4. CROUCH
    Down on all fours, knuckles and feet planted, back arched high so his mossy shoulders rise
    above his head line, haunches up, coat hanging in wet ropes off his belly. Brow low, eyes
    forward and perfectly calm, water squeezing out from under both knuckles in little
    flattened rings.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne and stretched long, arms flung wide like a flying squirrel, belly forward,
    both legs trailing behind, the whole coat and every fern streaming back off him. His face
    has not changed at all — still the same mild patient frown — while a trailing arc of flung
    droplets and two loose fir needles hang in the air behind his heels.

 6. LAND
    Four-point touchdown, knuckles and flat feet planted, knees folded, head coming back up
    under the brow. A big purple-white sheet of splash bursts up around all four contact
    points and the coat lifts a beat behind him before it drops, water flicking off his
    elbows; the ferns bounce, he does not.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on knuckle lope to the right, one long arm reaching forward to plant its knuckle a
    whole lane ahead, both back legs swinging through underneath him, the coat swinging as one
    heavy mass. Ferns and the mushroom cluster lean back off his shoulder, face level and
    unhurried, small kicked-up flecks of wet leaf litter behind the trailing foot.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same lope flat out — both feet clear of the ground mid-stride, arms straight out front
    and back, coat streaming behind him like a soaked cape, white speed streaks and a rooster-
    tail of ripped-up rainwater off his heels. Mouth still shut, eyes still calm, one eyebrow
    raised the smallest possible amount to acknowledge that he is now moving quickly.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing square to camera holding a takeaway cup so small in his palm he has to pinch it
    between two fingertips, pink steam curling up past his nose. Orange fire and speed streaks
    lick up from under his enormous flat feet, the whole coat lifted and bristling with
    static, ferns standing straight up — and his eyes are round and WIDE OPEN, the one frame
    in the set where he looks properly awake.

10. MAGNET
    Standing with both arms spread low and open like a man about to receive a hug he has been
    owed for forty years, mouth open in a slow delighted "oh". Gold bottlecaps, fries and
    blackberries spiral in toward his chest through the rain streaks, several already stuck in
    his wet coat and two lodged in the shoulder moss.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing dead calm in pouring rain holding a small rusted found umbrella — not over
    himself, but out to one side over the moss and ferns on his shoulder, keeping the little
    garden dry while rain sheets straight off his own brow and beard. Faint smug half-smile,
    water running down his chest, the ferns underneath the umbrella conspicuously fluffy and
    dry.

12. SHIELD
    Braced with one forearm crossed up in front of his face and the closed umbrella still
    gripped in the other hand, the blue-violet energy dome flaring hard around him with rain
    streaking off it sideways. Feet planted, coat and ferns flattened backward by the
    shockwave, road water blown out in a ring — and his reaction to being saved is a single
    raised eyebrow.

13. SCARED
    Hunched and looking straight up with his eyes huge and his mouth in a small open O, both
    enormous hands cupped protectively over the moss, ferns and mushrooms on his shoulder —
    guarding the garden, not himself. The diving bird's shadow falls in a hard shape across
    his chest, rain landing in his upturned face.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left in giant strides, mouth open in real alarm for the only time in the sheet,
    one hand thrown back and clamped over his shoulder to stop the mushrooms bouncing off. Red
    speed streaks, twin "!!" marks over his head, a loose fern frond tumbling away behind him
    and rainwater sheeting off his trailing arm.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall and square, chest out, both enormous arms raised slowly to the sky, head
    tipped back, eyes closed and a huge open-mouthed joyful grin — the only big grin he does.
    Water flicks off both arms in twin arcs, warm gold light catches the wet fur, and loose
    fir needles and rain drift upward around him as though the sky briefly let up on his
    account.

16. KO
    Flat on his back in the middle of the wet street, arms and legs sprawled up and out, the
    coat spread around him like a dropped rug, soft "oof" mouth open and one big toe
    twitching. Stars circle his head, a fern sprig has flopped down over one eye, and the
    little rusted umbrella lies open in the puddle beside him.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled back on his backside with legs straight out, spiral eyes, tongue out the side,
    head lolling and stars circling. The sweeper's brushes have scrubbed one absurdly clean
    bright streak through his soaked coat and combed his shoulder moss into neat parallel
    rows, and a small tidy pile of swept leaves and one hubcap has been dumped in his lap.

18. SPLASH
    Chest-deep and going under, both arms thrown up, an enormous white water burst blooming
    around him, eyes wide and mouth open in shock as the waterlogged coat drags him down. He
    has hoisted the mossy shoulder clear of the surface so the ferns and the chanterelle
    cluster stay perfectly, pointlessly dry above the churn.

    SHEET NOTES
    • Paint him the SAME size in all eighteen cells. The engine already draws Sasquatch at
    1.35x, so do not pre-enlarge him on the sheet or he will tower over his own frames — he
    only needs to match himself, not the raccoons.
    • He is asymmetric: the heavy moss crown and the orange chanterelle cluster ride one
    shoulder. Mirroring run-r would move the garden to the wrong side, so this is a
    character that should get the optional 19th painting, run-l, rather than a flip.
    • His silhouette is a wide top-heavy triangle and the tallest thing on him is a fern,
    not his head — leave clear magenta above the highest frond in every frame or the crop
    will mow the garden off.
    • The widest cells are leap, dash-run and cheer, where his arm span is nearly double his
    standing width. Give those three cells extra horizontal room and never let a fingertip
    touch a divider line.
    • Bottom-anchored means the lowest painted contact point sits on the bottom edge of the
    cell — for ko, dizzy and splash that contact is his back, hip and waterline, not his
    feet, or he will float a foot above the road.
    • Keep the two things that make him read at game size on a dark rainy street: the teal-
    black rim light down his edges and the white water beads scattered through the fur. They
    survive every pose.
    • Eighteen cells, six columns by three rows in the numbered order, white divider lines
    between every cell. No even butted grid — the cutter reads dividers and connected
    components.
