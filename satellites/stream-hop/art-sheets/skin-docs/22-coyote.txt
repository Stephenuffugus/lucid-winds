MAKE TWO SHEETS FROM THIS DOCUMENT:  22a  and  22b

================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Discovery Park Coyote — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set
on the rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  QUADRUPED, locked: a digitigrade canid who stands and moves on four toes-down feet in all
  eighteen frames — he never rises onto two legs, never has a free "hand", and the only
  frames where he is off his feet are leap, ko, dizzy and splash.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  NONE — no clothes, no permanent prop, jaws empty and nothing underfoot in fifteen of
  eighteen frames; the only props on the whole sheet are the torn takeout bag + one long fry
  (eat), the sleeved paper cup (coffee), the incoming bottlecaps and fries (magnet), and ONE
  umbrella that appears in exactly two frames (umbrella, shield) and never once leaves his
  teeth.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He has no hands, so every raccoon pose that holds something becomes jaws-and-forepaw: the
  cup and the umbrella are carried in the teeth, the takeout bag is pinned flat under one
  splayed forepaw, and every "raise your arms" beat becomes something a canid actually does
  — cheer is a full howl, scared is hackles up with one forepaw lifted, ko is belly-up with
  all four legs folded in the air. He is DIGITIGRADE, the exact opposite of Jimothy: toes
  down, heel and hock never touching, no flat palms, no grip, no thumb. Standing rules a
  painter must not break — all four feet stay under him at rest, a prop in any standing
  frame lives in the JAWS and never becomes a fifth limb, and the black-tipped brush tail
  must physically touch the body silhouette in every single frame or the connected-component
  cut orphans it and ships a coyote with no tail.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 22a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square in three-quarter view, weight settled on straight forelegs, tall
   ears pricked with one rotated slightly off-axis as if already tracking the next lane,
   gold eyes level with the camera. Brush tail hangs low with its black tip well clear of
   the bottom of the frame, mouth closed in that faint unbothered line, jaws empty and
   nothing underfoot — no pavement, no shadow, no puddle.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat back on his haunches, forelegs straight and tidy, chest high, brush tail curled
   around his front feet with the black tip laid over one paw. Head turned three-quarters
   away with ears half-back and one gold eye catching low dawn light, the gaze aimed off
   at something the sheet never paints — the patrol briefly off duty, and this is the one
   quiet, lonely frame on the sheet.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Hunkered over a torn paper takeout bag pinned flat under one splayed forepaw, head
   swung back around to camera mid-chew with a single long fry held crosswise in his teeth
   the way a dog carries a stick it will not give back, one end drooping. Ears up, eyes
   half-lidded and entirely unrepentant; every remaining fry stays inside the mouth of the
   bag under his paw, nothing scattered loose outside his outline.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Coiled to launch: chest dropped low, elbows out, hindquarters bunched high over
   gathered back feet, tail carried low with the black tip curving up to touch his own
   rump. Ears flat forward, gold eyes locked up-screen, a few grains of grit ticking off
   the back paw and still touching it — nothing painted beneath him.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   A GATHERED bound, not a stretched one: spine folded, forelegs reaching with the elbows
   kept in, hind legs tucked up under the belly, brush tail curved up and touching his
   back so the whole shape stays compact and roughly square. Ears back, mouth just open in
   the private delight of a working animal who is very good at exactly one thing and knows
   nobody is watching — he is commuting, not hunting.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Forepaws slapped down and splayed wide, shoulders sunk between them absorbing the drop,
   hindquarters still coming down behind and the tail flicked up against his flank for
   balance. Ears snapped forward, a tight burst of rain-spray and grit bursting AT the
   pads and touching them, spreading no wider than his own chest.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on facing RIGHT in a compact diagonal trot, near foreleg and far hind leg swinging
   together with the stride kept short of full reach, head carried dead level, tail low
   and steady. Chin up, unhurried — the posture of a coyote who is not crossing the street
   so much as permitting it — with a thin veil of pale dawn mist lying right along his
   back.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Same side-on RIGHT and flat out, but painted at the GATHERED moment of the gallop: all
   four feet off the ground with the spine folded and the legs collected under him, tail
   curved up against the rump. Ears pinned, eyes wide, tongue just out, three or four
   short warm-white speed ticks starting at his heels and touching them — no long trail,
   no grit under the belly.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   He is NOT running — all four feet planted and dead still, the whole body vibrating in
   place, because the engine holds this frame for up to seven seconds while the player
   stands there. A paper cup clamped by its cardboard sleeve in his teeth with the lid
   dented by tooth marks and PALE CREAM steam curling off it, pupils shrunk to pinpricks
   in the gold eyes, hackles standing in a dark ridge, tail up but angled back, jitter
   lines hugging his outline and small orange heat licks touching all four paws.

SHEET 22b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Up on his toes with weight rocked back and ears up, gold eyes wide with plain
   astonished joy as a gold bottlecap arrives right at the tip of his nose entirely by
   itself — he is not snatching, it is coming to him, and he cannot believe his luck. More
   bottlecaps and fries curve in on a tight spiral that stays within half a body width and
   overlaps his outline everywhere, tail sweeping a happy blur.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing four-square and bone dry, biting the umbrella SHAFT high — an inch or two
   under the canopy — so the open umbrella sits on top of his skull like a hat and the
   curved crook dangles down past his brisket. Head tilted slightly to balance it, ears
   dry under the rim, eyes half-lidded and insufferably pleased, short rain ticks
   streaking off both sides of the canopy; no puddle, no wet ground, nothing beneath his
   paws.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Braced and dropped low with forelegs splayed, head twisted away, eyes squeezed shut and
   ears pinned flat — and the umbrella STILL in his teeth, blown completely inside out
   over his skull, which is the joke. A blue-violet dome flares tight around him with rain
   and grit streaking off its skin: never violet, never dark, and never further off his
   fur than a hand's width.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Rocked back on his haunches in three-quarter view, chest lifted, one forepaw held up
   and out to the SIDE and the head craned up and back on an angle rather than stacked
   straight up, so the frame stays no taller than idle. Ears flat to his skull, hackles
   spiked into a dark ridge along the spine, tail tucked hard forward between the hind
   legs, gold eyes enormous and rimmed with white.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Full gallop to the LEFT, belly low, legs at reach but the hind pair folding under him
   and the brush tail curved and touching the rump so the shape never stretches into a
   horizontal streak. Ears pinned, mouth open in a thoroughly undignified yelp, three
   short warm-white ticks off his heels — all that athleticism, none of the composure —
   and nothing floating above his shoulders.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Front paws planted wide in three-quarter view, chest thrown out, head tipped all the
   way back with the muzzle pointed up in a full howl, eyes shut, ears laid back, tail
   high and waving. Three short song-lines curl out of his open mouth and stay touching
   the muzzle — no arm to raise, so he raises his voice.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with all four legs folded loosely in the air, tongue out one side,
   dazed swooning half-moon lids (never X eyes, never spirals — spirals belong to dizzy),
   one ear folded inside out against his own skull and the brush tail flopped along his
   flank inside the silhouette. A tight ring of small gold stars orbits close enough to
   overlap his head; the read is mortified professional dignity — the animal who never
   misses, caught belly-up.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side with his legs still walking on nothing, spiral eyes and tongue
   lolling, his whole coat combed into ridiculous swirls and cowlicks by the sweeper
   brushes. Top ear flops back while the under ear squashes flat, tail lies in line with
   the body, and two wet leaves stay stuck flat IN the combed fur — no circling stars
   here, and no pile of sweepings on any ground.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: head and shoulders exploding up inside a tight white and
   pale-cyan crown of water, forelegs flung high, ears pinned, gold eyes huge and mouth
   open in pure offended shock — soaked, out of play, and deeply insulted about it. Fur
   plastered so his legs and ruff read half their usual fluffy width while the HEAD stays
   exactly the same size as every other frame, brush tail hanging as a sad wet rope still
   touching his body.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• CANVAS IS THE WHOLE TRICK: cut the sheet, then re-pad all 18 PNGs to ONE identical canvas.
His four existing frames are 214x250 (aspect 0.86) — that is the box, and same canvas plus
same painted head size is the only thing that actually passes the head-pixel test, because
the engine normalises every frame independently.
• Every pose therefore has to be GATHERED to fit that box. For scale, Jimothy's own leap PNG
is 215x210 and his widest frame of twenty is dash-run at 236x178 (1.33). A stretched, tail-
streaming coyote leap measures 2.2:1 and would draw him at roughly 40% of his idle height
for the middle half of every hop, popping back up on landing. Paint idle, leap and cheer
FIRST and send those three for a 59px lane check before committing the other fifteen.
• NO GROUND ANYWHERE on this sheet — no wet asphalt, no puddle under the umbrella, no cast
shadow, no fries scattered by his toes, no drift of sweepings behind dizzy. The cutter is a
magenta border flood that keeps every non-magenta island, so each of those ships as floating
grey scum beneath an animal with no floor. Spray that TOUCHES his paws (land, crouch grit,
splash crown) is correct and wanted.
• EFFECT COLOUR LAW: pale cream steam on the coffee cup (NOT pink), warm-white speed ticks,
gold stars, and a bright blue-violet shield dome. The k.
• Loose pieces — the ko star ring, the magnet spiral, the cheer song-lines — must sit within
half a body width AND overlap his outline, and this sheet must be cut on painted white
divider lines rather than component detection or each of them ships as its own sprite. Keep
a clear magenta margin all the way round; nothing touches an edge or a divider.
• At 59px his entire identity is four things: tall ears, dark ruff and saddle, black tail
tip, two gold eyes. Never crop an ear tip, keep the eyes the brightest note on him in all
eighteen (including upside down in ko and soaked in splash). Direction: run-r and dash-run
face RIGHT, flee faces LEFT — do not paint all three the same way round — and still ship a
run-l file as a flipped export of run-r, because a fully animated character with no run-l
falls through to the procedural blob on every leftward hop.
