MAKE TWO SHEETS FROM THIS DOCUMENT:  24a  and  24b
Nine frames on each sheet, eighteen in total. Label the files exactly 24a and 24b.

================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Sockeye Salmon — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  No limbs and no feet: at rest he rears bolt upright on a fanned tail fin in a deep
  S-curve, a leap he never finished, and that tail-stand is his standing pose in every non-
  travel frame — travel is always a flip, never a run.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all 18 frames: spawning colours worn as the costume (crimson flank #d4685e, warm peach
  belly #f2b3a2, olive-green humped back and hooked kype) plus one snapped brass spoon lure
  tangled around — never through — the dorsal fin on a short frayed leader; the cheap black
  umbrella is the only other prop and it exists in frames 11 and 12 only.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  A salmon has no hands, so every raccoon pose is solved by the spine: the body curls,
  coils, arches and corkscrews, and the gill covers plus the one visible eye carry all the
  emotion. Pectoral fins sit low and BEHIND the gill covers and swing fore-and-aft through a
  few degrees only — they can never reach the eye, the top of the head, or under him as a
  weight-bearing prop, and they can never hold anything; anything he "carries" is hooked on
  the tip of the kype, stuck flat to the wet flank, tangled in the dorsal, or climbed into.
  He rears very tall, which blows the sizing envelope, so the painted box is held near
  square by making the S-curve deeper or shallower — never by shrinking the fish. Nose-to-
  tail and head size stay identical in all 18 frames; the head is the checkable proof.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 24a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Reared bolt upright on his fanned tail fin in a deep S, both pectorals held low and out
   for balance, olive kype level, the one big round eye fixed calmly on the viewer. Baked
   in: a wobble blur at the tail tip, rain beading down the crimson flank, the brass lure
   swaying on its leader against the dorsal — water only where it touches him, no puddle,
   no asphalt.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Balance abandoned: slumped onto his side and curled into a comma, gills working slow,
   tail fin idly slapping, one eye turned up into the rain, half-lidded and quietly
   melancholy. The frayed leader has coiled across his own belly with the lure resting on
   the flank, and a thin sheen of water clings to his underside without spreading past his
   outline.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Curled tight around a soggy paper tray of chips pressed against his own body, kype
   clamped sideways on a single chip like a caught minnow, eye blissfully shut, ketchup on
   the chin, lure glinting on the dorsal above. A real mayfly hovers an inch off his nose
   and is completely ignored — the one meal he was built for, and he has chosen chips.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Compressed into a tight loaded C, nose down, tail wound under him, pectorals flattened
   hard to the flank, the one eye locked up-screen, the whole body a spring at full
   compression. Water squeezes out along his belly line as a tight collar that touches him
   all the way round; the lure is pinned flat to the dorsal by the compression.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   HORIZONTAL frame, deliberately not cheer: a long low crescent travelling across the
   cell, tail kicked straight out behind, jaw SHUT, pale peach belly turned to camera,
   every scale catching the streetlight — pure speed, no celebration. A short sheet of
   water peels off the flank into a rope of droplets that stays on his body, lure
   streaming back along the dorsal.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Belly-flop: body slapped flat, head reared up with the jaw open in a silent oof, tail
   still up in the air a beat behind him so the box stays near square. A crown of splash
   bursts sideways right at his belly and stops there — no ripple, no ring, no wet road —
   and the lure has flipped forward over his shoulder on its leader.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Mid-flip to the right, body arced into a working comma, humped back cresting, one
   pectoral shoving off low while the tail whips through, head craned right, eye set and
   businesslike. Spray kicks off the tail close behind him and the lure and frayed leader
   swing back on the same beat.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same flip flat out: body stretched long, two or three tight afterimages of the tail
   beat, jaw clamped, eye wild, a short rooster-tail of spray at the tail. Warm orange
   speed streaks run along the flank and off the tail — never under his belly, there is no
   road — and the lure is snapped straight out behind on a taut leader.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   A STANDING frame, held up to seven seconds: planted on his tail and going nowhere, a
   takeaway cup hooked on the very tip of his kype and hanging clear to one side so the
   face stays open, coffee slopping over the rim. The whole body vibrates to a blur at the
   edges — gill covers flared, eye enormous with a pinprick pupil, jitter lines at the
   tail tip — with pale blush-cream steam (never pink) lifting off the cup and off his own
   hot flank.

SHEET 24b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared up on his tail with the jaw dropped open in flat astonishment — delight, not
   appetite — eye huge and shining as gold bottlecaps and chips spiral in on a short tight
   golden vortex that hugs his body. Four caps have already stuck flat to the wet flank
   like coins on glass, and the lure lifts on its leader as if it wants in too.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   He has flipped a cheap black umbrella canopy-up, let it fill with rainwater and climbed
   in — a fish in a bathtub — with the tub tipped toward camera so his whole head, kype,
   gill covers and dorsal ride clear above the rim, one eye half-lidded and unbearably
   smug. Canopy no wider than about 1.2x his tail-stand box so the crimson body beats the
   black bowl; lure and leader hang over the rim.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The instant of impact: the umbrella-tub slammed sideways, water sheeting out over the
   bent ribs, and a blue-violet dome (never blue-violet, it gets eaten by the cut) flaring
   tight around fish and umbrella together. Body arched hard, pectorals splayed stiff and
   low, eye squeezed shut, rain streaking off the dome in bright lines, the lure flung up
   against the inside of it.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Rigid as a bowling pin on his tail and leaning back past vertical, dorsal fin snapped
   bolt upright with the lure standing up in it, both pectorals flared stiff and LOW like
   flinched shoulders, gill covers wide, mouth a tiny O, the one eye rolled straight up. A
   gull's wing shadow falls across his crimson flank only — never on any ground — and one
   bead of water flicks off the trembling tail tip.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Full panic flop up-frame: body zig-zagged mid-slap, three tail beats blurring, mouth
   wide in a silent scream, panic bubbles bursting from the gill covers, lure whipping
   loose behind. Three or four short run-off streaks that start and end ON his body run
   down past him while he angles up against them — no background wash, no symbols, no
   lettering.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   VERTICAL, the money frame and the opposite silhouette to leap: reared at full stretch
   bolt upright on a fanned tail, body quivering, jaw wide open in a joyful gasp, gill
   covers flared pink, every scale lit gold by hard backlight. The thrown water is only a
   contact burst at the tail base, no more than a third of his height, and the battered
   lure hangs off the dorsal catching the light like a medal. He got upstream.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his side in a puddle an inch too shallow to be dignified, gills still working,
   one pectoral flopped limp against the gill plate where it actually lands, jaw open with
   one fat comic bubble at the corner, small stars circling close over his head —
   embarrassed, not hurt. The brass lure has swung round on its leader and lies across his
   eye like a hat pulled down; rain lands ON him and a thin sheen of water hugs his
   outline, with no water painted anywhere it does not touch him.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Wrung into a hard corkscrewed C with the tail crossing PAST his head so the fin ends up
   beside his own jaw and the face rotates to camera — never wrapped around his own girth
   — both eyes spiralled, jaw hanging, gills fluttering. Wet leaves and stiff sweeper
   bristles stuck all over the flank with the lure tangled in among them, and a small
   tight tornado of litter and orbiting stars kept close enough to overlap his body;
   nothing on the ground.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The burst: he explodes up inside a white crown of storm water in a joy-roll, body
   twisted, tumbling and clearly knocked off his line rather than swimming, crown no wider
   than about 1.2x his idle box. Gills finally flared pink and working properly, eye wide
   with relief, lure flicking off the flank on its leader, streetlight refracting gold
   through the spray — the one death on the sheet that is secretly a homecoming, and he is
   still out of the game.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND ANYWHERE. The draft's puddle rings, gutter puddle, rain rings, ripple across the
puddle, wet asphalt, storm drain and speed streaks 'under his belly' all survive the magenta
flood as floating grey scum under a fish with no floor. Water, spray, steam and streaks are
legal ONLY where they touch his body.
• Envelope: hold every painted box between about 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and inside about 1.2x the
idle box. A reared fish goes far too tall — control the box with the depth of the S-curve,
never by shrinking him. The head must measure the same pixels in all 18 frames; if an effect
shrinks the head, the effect is too big.
• Detached elements (stars, litter tornado, mayfly, magnet spiral, bottlecaps in flight)
must sit within half a body width AND overlap his bounding box, or the connected-components
cut emits them as their own sprite or drops them.
• Nothing readable anywhere. The flee exclamation marks are deleted on purpose — replaced
with gill-cover bubbles. Litter, leaves, caps and the lure read as shape and texture only.
• Crop hazards are the tail fin tip, the frayed leader and the lure. Lay out with white
divider lines and generous UNEVEN gaps, never a fixed grid, and leave clear magenta margin
on all four sides of every cell — a grid will slice a tail fin clean off.
• Left-facing run: Paint run-l separately: the lure and its frayed leader hang off one flank
and the kype hooks one way, so a mirrored run-r jumps the lure across the dorsal and swaps
the eye to the far side on every leftward hop while the other seventeen frames never move
it.
