================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Sasquatch — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on the rainy
streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Upright bipedal at rest on two flat tan feet; drops to a four-point knuckle-bound (two
  flat feet plus two knuckles, never fists, never palms) for every travel frame — crouch,
  leap, land, run-r, run-l, dash-run and flee. The words "four-square" never apply to him.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  No clothing at all, bare shagged pelt only — and in all eighteen frames: a thin moss cap
  over his crown, plus on his LEFT shoulder a thick moss pad carrying two sword-fern fronds,
  a cluster of orange chanterelles, and a small rust-brown found umbrella wedged butt-down
  through the moss and angled back along his spine (stowed and closed in sixteen frames;
  lifted out and open only in umbrella and ko, and in both it stays physically touching
  him).

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is an eight-foot, several-hundred-pound ape with full hands, so raccoon poses transfer
  honestly — the real translation problem is mass, and he must never read weightless.
  Because he stands upright at rest, both hands are genuinely free in idle, coffee, magnet,
  umbrella, shield, scared and cheer; the instant he travels he is on knuckles and feet, and
  no frame may silently swap the two. Every "coat" on this sheet is his own soaked shaggy
  pelt, not a garment — he wears nothing. Everything Jimothy plays fast, Sasquatch plays one
  beat slow: read the speed off the swinging pelt, the flung rainwater and the streaming
  ferns, never off his face, which holds the same mild patient frown through idle, sit,
  crouch, leap, land, run-r, run-l, dash-run, umbrella and shield — that hold IS the joke,
  and it only breaks for eat, coffee, magnet, scared, flee, cheer and the three deaths.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 28a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Upright on two flat tan feet, caught mid-step-back — weight rocking onto his heels, one
   huge palm half-raised as if he has just been seen, eyes cut off-camera under the
   patient half-frown of someone who has waited out worse rain than this. Long arms
   hanging past his knees, shoulders sloped, rain beading white through the dark pelt,
   teal rim light down both edges.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat straight down cross-legged and enormous, knees wide, one hand loose on his own
   ankle and the other palm turned up in his lap slowly filling with rainwater. Head
   tilted a few degrees, eyes soft and off to one side, watching a corner he remembers
   being something else — the quiet frame of the sheet; ferns hang forward off the
   shoulder garden.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Same cross-legged sit, but he has reached ACROSS with his far hand and picked one
   chanterelle off his own SHOULDER cluster, holding it up between two fingertips like a
   canapé, mouth just opening, eyes fondly crossed on it. The shoulder cluster is visibly
   one mushroom short; a few blackberries sit in his other cupped palm, collecting rain.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Dropped into the knuckle-bound gather — both flat feet and both knuckles planted, back
   arched high so the mossy shoulder rises above his head line, haunches up, pelt hanging
   in wet ropes off his belly. Brow low, eyes forward and perfectly calm, a tight collar
   of spray bursting where each knuckle presses.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   The airborne beat of a gorilla gallop: both long arms reaching FORWARD together, hands
   open and leading, shoulders and mossy back at the top of the arc, both legs gathered up
   under the belly, body long and heavy — mass in flight, never spread-eagle. Face
   completely unchanged, the same mild patient frown, pelt and ferns streaming back with
   flung droplets trailing off his heels and still touching them.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Four-point touchdown, knuckles and flat feet planted, knees folded, head coming back up
   under the brow. A tight cyan-white sheet of splash bursts at each of the four contact
   points, water flicks off his elbows, the pelt lifts a beat behind him before it drops —
   the ferns bounce, he does not.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on knuckle lope to the right with the garden shoulder rolled forward and nearest
   camera, the leading knuckle planting no further ahead than his own body length while
   the far arm swings back to sell the span. Both hind legs swinging through underneath,
   pelt moving as one heavy mass, ferns and chanterelles leaning back, face level and
   unhurried, one tight flick of water off the trailing foot.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same lope flat out — both feet clear of the ground mid-stride, arms driving fore
   and aft but the fingertip span held inside the aspect ceiling, pelt streaming behind
   him like a soaked rug, white speed streaks and a tight rooster-tail of rainwater
   breaking off his heels. Mouth shut, eyes calm, one eyebrow raised the smallest possible
   amount to acknowledge that he is now moving quickly.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   STANDING STILL — feet planted flat and square to camera, holding a takeaway cup so
   small he has to pinch it between two fingertips, pale cream-white steam curling past
   his nose. All the speed is in the body: pelt lifted and bristling with static, ferns
   standing straight up, jitter lines off his shoulders, orange fire licking up around his
   planted feet, and his eyes round and WIDE OPEN — the one frame where he looks properly
   awake.

SHEET 28b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Standing upright with both arms spread low and open like a man about to receive a hug
   he has been owed for forty years, mouth open in a slow delighted "oh", eyebrows up in
   pure astonishment. Gold bottlecaps, fries and blackberries spiral in tight to his chest
   — the outer ones overlapping his silhouette and never more than a hand's width off the
   fur, several already stuck in the wet pelt, two lodged in the shoulder moss.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing dead calm in pouring rain, the little rust-brown umbrella lifted out of the
   moss and opened across his own shoulder line with the canopy tipped toward camera, so
   the silhouette plainly says COVERED — canopy topping out level with the fern crown, no
   higher. Rain sheets off the near rim while his own face and beard get thoroughly
   soaked, the ferns underneath are conspicuously fluffy and dry, and he wears a faint
   smug half-smile.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Braced upright with both forearms crossed high in front of his face and both feet
   planted, umbrella left stowed in the shoulder moss; a bright blue-violet dome flares
   tight around him — a hand's width off the fur, no taller than the ferns — with rain
   streaking off it sideways. Pelt and ferns flattened back by the shockwave, and his
   entire reaction to being saved is one raised eyebrow.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Hunched down small and turned half away, hiding behind his own shoulder garden and
   peering out through the gaps in his huge splayed fingers — the alarm is being SEEN, not
   the bird. A hard wing-shaped shadow cuts across his chest, his other hand cups
   protectively over the moss and chanterelles, eyes enormous, mouth a small open O.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Barrelling toward camera on a three-quarter angle in the airborne beat of the bound,
   both hands off the road — near hand clamped flat over the shoulder garden to stop the
   mushrooms bouncing off, far arm already reaching for the next knuckle plant. Mouth open
   in real alarm for the only time on the sheet, white and red speed streaks, twin
   exclamation strokes tight above his head, rainwater sheeting off the trailing arm.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Standing tall and square, chest out, head tipped back, both enormous arms raised in joy
   but ELBOWS BENT so his hands stay level with the fern crown and the frame stays exactly
   as tall as idle — huge open-mouthed grin, the only one he does. Second read baked into
   the same painting: one eye already sliding sideways to check that nobody watched him do
   it; water flicks off both forearms in tight arcs, warm gold light on the wet fur.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back, arms and legs sprawled up and out, pelt spread around him like a
   dropped rug, mouth open in a soft "oof" and one big toe twitching; eyes are swooning
   half-moons with stars circling his head and a fern sprig flopped down over one eye. The
   open umbrella leans against his own forearm, touching him, and his free hand is half-
   raised in a tiny apologetic wave to nobody — mortified at having been seen go down.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled back on his backside with legs straight out, spiral eyes, tongue out the side,
   head lolling, stars circling. The street sweeper has scrubbed one absurdly clean bright
   streak straight through his soaked pelt and combed his shoulder moss into neat parallel
   rows, and a small tidy pile of swept leaves plus one plain dished hubcap sit in his
   lap, touching him.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Dropped straight down into the middle of an exploding crown of white water, water to
   his waist, both arms flung up clear of the burst — plainly out of play, not swimming,
   not sinking, the one thing in Seattle he is too big for and still losing to. Eyes wide
   and both eyebrows up in mild personal offence at the whole business, mossy shoulder
   hoisted high so the ferns and chanterelles stay perfectly, pointlessly dry above the
   churn.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• CENTRE-ANCHORED, not bottom-anchored — the engine centres the sprite on the lane point.
Keep his mass at the centre of the painted content in every cell: anything painted high
above him pushes the character DOWN off his lane line, anything painted below pushes him UP
off it.
• NO GROUND IN ANY OF THE EIGHTEEN. No puddles, no rain rings, no drying smears, no cast
shadows on the road, no curbs, no scattered leaf litter, no kicked-up flecks that are not
touching him — the cutter is a magenta flood and every one of those survives as floating
grey scum under a character with no floor. Contact effects that touch the body are correct
and wanted: spray bursting at a knuckle, water breaking off a heel, the crown at his
waterline.
• Aspect 0.80:1 to 1.35:1 in EVERY frame, effects included, inside one envelope no more than
about 1.2x the idle box. The single checkable test: his HEAD is the same number of pixels in
all eighteen — if an effect shrinks the head, the effect is too big. Worst offenders are
leap, dash-run and cheer, which is exactly why cheer's elbows stay bent and run-r's knuckle
plants only one body length ahead.
• COLOUR LAW — the knockout eats the magenta family, so it will bite ragged holes in pink or
violet effects. Coffee steam is pale cream-white (not pink), land and splash water is cyan-
white (not purple-white), the shield dome is blue-violet like Jimothy's own. Keep the teal-
black rim light down his edges and the white water beads through the fur in all eighteen —
those two are what make him read at 59px on wet asphalt.
• NO LETTERING ANYWHERE: the umbrella is plain rust-brown with one patched panel and no
logo, the dizzy hubcap is a plain dished disc. And no loose-fur debris in any frame — none
at all, and least of all on ko, dizzy and splash, where shed fur reads as injury instead of
motion.
• Do NOT pre-enlarge him; the engine already draws Sasquatch at scale 1.35, so he only has
to match himself cell to cell. Sell the eight feet with props instead — the pinched takeaway
cup, the toy-sized umbrella, the hubcap sitting in his lap like a coin. The tallest thing on
him is a fern, not his head, so leave clear magenta above the highest frond in every cell
and let nothing touch a divider line. Paint idle, leap and cheer first and send those three
for the envelope check before committing the other fifteen.
• Left-facing run: Painted separately: the moss pad, ferns, chanterelles and stowed umbrella
all ride his LEFT shoulder, which is the near camera-side in run-r — mirroring would swing
the entire garden to the wrong shoulder, so run-l is painted fresh with the garden on the
FAR shoulder, cresting over his back line so it still silhouettes.
