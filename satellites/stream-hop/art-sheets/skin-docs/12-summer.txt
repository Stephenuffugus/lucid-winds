================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Hot Jimothy Summer — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on
the rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadruped raccoon, plantigrade — flat palms, five long spread fingers, heels down, four-
  square at rest; he rears onto his haunches ONLY in the six frames that say so (coffee,
  magnet, umbrella, shield, scared, cheer).

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all eighteen frames, no exceptions: the oversized orange-and-cream striped life ring
  (rope trim, visible valve stem, teal patch repeated on BOTH flanks at the same height) and
  the orange-framed dark shades. Everything else — popsicle, slush cup, iced coffee,
  parasol, towel — is a per-frame prop.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  This is Jimothy's own body, so every hero pose maps one-to-one, but hold the canon: round
  and spherical, no visible neck, head perched high on the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and
  a SHORT STUBBY ringed tail that never streams, curls out behind or trails — strike every
  "streaming tail" from the old draft. Hands are plantigrade raccoon hands, flat palms and
  spread fingers with the heel down, in every frame including cheer and coffee; no knuckles,
  no tiptoe. The ring is a rigid torus and never a flat ellipse — highlight along the top
  curve, shadow tucked under the bottom, valve stem findable — painted at ONE constant outer
  diameter (about 1.4x his shoulder width, inner hole clearly wider than his shoulders) in
  all eighteen, so only its position and tilt ever change; if it must read smaller, tilt it
  into foreshortening, never shrink it. That deliberate oversize is what makes the costume
  honest: it rests on his barrel at idle only because his stance cocks it there, which is
  why it can then slide to the armpits, collar the neck, and drop clean to his ankles
  without him appearing to change size.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 12a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square three-quarter to camera, weight cocked onto one hip so the
   oversized ring hangs tilted around his barrel — front edge low, back edge caught on the
   slope of his shoulders, no paw needed to hold it. Chin lifted, mouth a small satisfied
   line, shades dead level, one fat drizzle bead sliding down the left lens.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   The quiet frame: he has sat straight down through the ring so it collars his hips like
   a donut chair, hind feet out over the front rim, flat palms folded on the top curve,
   head tipped back at a sky that is plainly not going to clear. Shades on, contented
   half-smile going soft at the corners, drizzle beading along the rubber.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Still seated in the ring, leaning on its top curve like a table — a paper cup of blue
   slush wedged against the rim at his side, a half-melted orange popsicle held up in both
   flat palms. He is licking a drip off his own wrist, shades on, while the rain melts it
   faster than any sun would have.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Repaint: coiled low and forward, shoulders down, haunches up, palms and heels flat —
   the same full-diameter ring has slid up to collar his neck, tipped steeply so it
   foreshortens into an oval instead of shrinking, squashing his cheeks out either side.
   Eyes forward over the top of the rubber, shades knocked one degree crooked.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, belly forward, mouth open in a whoop — limbs gathered inside the box
   rather than stretched flat, forepaws reaching and hind feet trailing close. The ring
   has ridden up to his armpits and rides the air with him, shades perfectly level, a
   short arc of water beads flicking off its trailing edge and still touching it.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front palms slapped down flat with the fingers spread, body low and absorbing, as the
   ring bounces up and clouts him under the chin, shoving cheeks and whiskers forward.
   Shades jolted one notch down his snout so a single wide eye peeks over the top rim, and
   a small white splash crown bursts right at his paws, touching them — nothing else below
   him.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on sprint to the right, body low, forelegs reaching and hind heels coming down
   through the stride, the stubby ringed tail short and level behind him. The ring is
   squashed against his ribs with two short wobble lines off its edge and one flat palm
   clamped on the rim holding it down; paint the teal patch on this flank exactly as on
   the far side so the mirror is invisible.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat out and lower still, tongue out, ears back, the ring shoved into a slipstream cone
   against his shoulders. Three short orange speed streaks along his flanks, a white glint
   flashing across both lenses, and a tight fan of spray bursting off his hind paws and
   attached to them — no rooster tail, no puddle, no street.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   He is NOT moving: reared onto his haunches with both hind feet planted flat and heels
   down, vibrating on the spot, both palms clamped around an enormous iced coffee with a
   bendy straw, ice cubes jumping just clear of the lid. Shades pushed up on his forehead
   — two enormous jittering pinprick pupils — with jitter lines and a motion-blur double
   edge held tight against the ring.

SHEET 12b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared up on his haunches, arms flung wide, mouth open in astonished delight as gold
   bottlecaps and stray fries spiral in tight around the ring — a couple pinging off the
   taut rubber, one already lodged in the rope trim. Two little gold streaks reflected in
   the dark lenses; every loose cap sits within half a body width and overlaps his
   silhouette.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Reared up and immovably smug: a candy-striped beach parasol gripped in one palm and
   leaned back against his shoulder, canopy no wider than about 1.3x him, other palm
   planted on his hip, ring level at his waist. A beach towel is draped over both
   shoulders with the ends hanging even on each side, and rain drums on the canopy and
   flicks off the rim in short streaks that die in mid-air — no sidewalk, no crack,
   nothing under him.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Braced sideways into the hit, parasol tilted across like a shield, both palms up,
   inside a blue-violet faceted dome that hugs his silhouette no more than a hand's width
   out — never blue-violet, the knockout eats that. Rain skids off it in hard short
   diagonals, the ring squashes flat on the impact side, shades jolt crooked, whiskers
   swept back, muzzle scrunched in a flinch.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Reared onto his hind legs with the ring hauled off his body and hugged to his chest in
   both arms, exactly like the life preserver it always was, head craned up and back.
   Shades slid to the very tip of his snout so two huge white eyes stare over the top,
   mouth a tiny o, and a hard wedge of gull shadow falls across his chest and the rubber.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting flat toward camera-left, mouth wide, ears pinned, the licked-bare popsicle
   stick still clenched in his teeth — nothing thrown, nothing tumbling loose in the air.
   The ring has flapped up around his neck like a horse collar, three short red streaks
   come off his heels, and one lens catches a hard slash of the sweeper's yellow light.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Up on his hind legs with heels down, stretched as tall as he goes, both arms punched up
   and the ring hoisted just over his head — tilted hard toward camera so it foreshortens
   into an ellipse instead of adding height. Mouth open in pure joy; the one real sunlight
   on the whole sheet, painted onto him as a warm gold rim-light down his fur and two
   sparks on the lenses, no beam and no shaft.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back, all four legs in the air with the palms flopped open, rocking gently
   on the ring like a turtle on its shell and suspiciously comfortable about it. Shades
   sit crooked with one lens popped out and resting on his chest fur — through the empty
   rim, one dazed swooning half-moon eye — tongue lolling, small stars circling tight
   around his head.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side, cheeks puffed, mouth in a woozy wobble, with spirals painted
   straight onto both dark lenses because the shades never came off. The ring is still on
   him at full diameter, dented soft where his shoulder pins it, a thin pale curl of air
   whistling from the valve, one hind paw twitching — spirals only here, stars belong to
   ko.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sat down hard in about six inches of water, arms straight up, fur plastered flat, mouth
   open in betrayed shock as a big white crown of water bursts around his hips and
   shoulders — no flat pool, no ripple rings, nothing detached. Shades shoved up on his
   dripping forehead so you can see exactly how personally he is taking this, and the ring
   has dropped clean around his ankles like a fallen hula hoop with water blowing up
   through the hole. He is not going under; he has been humiliated by a puddle.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• ENVELOPE, CHECKABLE: the four existing PNGs are 220x202, aspect 1.089. All eighteen must
land in that same box with effects included and must stay UNDER 1.12 aspect — at 1.12 the
engine switches from height-fit to width-fit and he visibly jumps size mid-hop. His head
must measure the same number of pixels in all eighteen, and his body mass must sit at the
CENTRE of the painted content: the code centre-anchors, so anything above his head pushes
him below the lane line and anything below his paws lifts him off it. The old 'bottom-anchor
to his hind paws' note is wrong — delete it.
• NO GROUND IN ANY FRAME. No pavement, wet-asphalt texture, puddle pools, ripple rings, cast
shadows on the floor, sidewalk cracks or scattered litter — the cutter is a magenta flood
and every one of those survives as floating grey scum under a raccoon with nothing beneath
him. Contact water only: the splash crown at his paws in land, the spray off his hind paws
in dash-run, the crown bursting around his body in splash.
• NOTHING DETACHED. No tumbling popsicle, no thrown cup, no lens lying on the floor, no cap
drifting off the edge. The loose caps in magnet, the ice cubes in coffee and the stars in ko
must sit within half a body width AND overlap his silhouette or a connected-components cut
emits them as separate sprites. Keep a clear magenta margin around every cell — nothing
touches a divider line.
• COLOUR LAW: no magenta or hot pink in any effect, the knockout eats that whole family.
Shield dome is bright blue-violet, cheer's sunlight is warm gold-cream, coffee's steam and
jitter are pale blush-cream with the green channel kept high. At 59px his entire read is the
orange-and-cream striped ring plus the dark bar of the lenses under an orange frame — keep
both high-contrast and unbroken, and let the ring overhang the body; being the widest thing
on the roster is the joke.
• THE RING IS A LOCK and the crouch.png already painted has NO ring on it — repaint it or
the hop cycle pops the costume in and out of existence. The shades never leave the sheet: on
the eyes in most frames, down the snout in land and scared with the eyes peeking over the
top, up on the brow in coffee and splash, crooked in shield and dizzy, and in ko one lens is
popped out but resting on his chest fur — never on the floor. (The old note claiming 'only
coffee shows his eyes' was wrong; coffee, land, scared and splash all show them.).
• Rain falls in every frame except cheer, which is the only sunlight on the sheet — do not
leak warm light anywhere else or the payoff dies. No lettering on anything: not the ring,
the towel, the cup, the parasol or the slush. Paint idle, leap and cheer first and send
those three for a 59px lane test before committing the other fifteen; idle.png doubles as
the skin-select portrait, so make it the best single picture of him.
