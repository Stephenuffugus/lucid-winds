================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Rich Uncle Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on
the rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  UPRIGHT AT REST — he sits back on his haunches or stands on his hind legs for every still
  frame (idle, sit, eat, coffee, magnet, umbrella, shield, scared, cheer) and drops to all
  fours ONLY for the six athletic frames (crouch, leap, land, run-r, dash-run, flee); he is
  never four-square with anything in a paw.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  IN ALL 18: black wet-silk top hat with an oxblood band and a soft battered brim; gold-
  rimmed monocle over his RIGHT eye (frame-left when he faces right) with a fine gold chain
  dropping to the waistcoat; cream wing collar with spread points; big oxblood bow tie with
  one gold stud; dark olive-brown buttoned waistcoat with small gold buttons; a heavier gold
  watch chain swagged across the belly of the waistcoat; and a SHORT oxblood shoulder cape
  over BOTH shoulders fastened at the throat — there is NO tailcoat and NO coat tails in any
  frame, the cape is the only garment that trails. Per-frame props only, never carried
  outside their frame: gold pocket watch (sit, magnet), takeaway fry box on a small silver
  tray (eat), doll-sized espresso cup (coffee), compact black umbrella (umbrella), and the
  identical gold bottlecap in dash-run, magnet, scared, flee, cheer, dizzy, splash.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is Jimothy's own body — round and spherical with no visible neck, the head perched high
  on the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a SHORT STUBBY ringed tail; never the long
  streaming trash-panda tail, in any frame, including the sprints. Plantigrade throughout:
  flat palms, five long spread fingers, heels down — no knuckles, ever, and the hands are
  half of why the character works, so keep them readable even at speed. The hat, monocle and
  the two gold chains are HARD props sitting on a body that squashes and stretches, so they
  must be re-drawn honestly in each pose rather than smeared along with the fur — a warped
  top hat reads as a mistake instantly. His comic engine is that the body performs the
  entire athletic pose while the face declines to acknowledge it, and the hat is the mood
  ring.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 27a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Reared up and settled back on his haunches the way a man settles into a leather club
   chair — spine straight, belly forward, chin lifted, one flat forepaw laid on the
   waistcoat over the watch chain, the other hanging loose. Hat dead level with rain
   beading on the wet silk, monocle catching a single bright glint with its fine chain
   hanging plumb, stubby tail curled against his hip; amused, bored, and slightly damp.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   The same club-chair seat gone soft: hind ankles crossed, gold pocket watch open on his
   flat upturned palm, the heavy chain running taut back to the waistcoat, hat still dead
   level. This is his quiet frame — the monocle fogs a little with his breath and he is
   checking the time for an appointment nobody is coming to.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting the same way with a dented takeaway fry box on a small silver tray balanced on
   his knee and resting against his belly, a white linen napkin tucked into the bow tie.
   He eats exactly one fry pinched between two claws with the pinky extended, eyes closed
   in mild approval, monocle fogged over from the steam and both chains undisturbed —
   still a raccoon eating garbage, just served properly.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Gathered low on all fours, chest near the ground, haunches up, flat plantigrade palms
   planted wide and loaded, stubby tail arched short over his back. Nothing in the face
   has changed: hat dead level, monocle seated with its chain hanging plumb, eyes forward
   and unhurried, like a sprinter who finds the whole business beneath comment.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, belly forward, long forelimbs thrown wide with the fingers spread, hind
   legs trailing, the oxblood cape flared back off both shoulders. The top hat has lifted
   half an inch clear but still overlaps his ears, the monocle is somehow still in with
   its fine chain flying in a bright gold arc across his chest, and his face is serene —
   as though he is being carried rather than jumping.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front palms slapped flat, body compressed low, hind legs still catching up, a tight
   white crown of street water bursting where his paws are and touching them — no puddle,
   no pavement, no shadow painted anywhere. The hat is settling back down with a fine ring
   of rainwater flicking off the brim, monocle level, watch chain still swinging, and his
   face is already composed.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Three-quarter sprint to the right — turned toward camera exactly like the shipped leap
   and land, never flat profile, so the monocle side stays visible — body low and long,
   forelimbs at full reach, stubby ringed tail held short and straight out behind. One
   flat palm clamped over the crown of the hat to hold it down, the other reaching
   forward, cape and both gold chains snapping back; absolutely nothing under him, no wet-
   street reflection and no shadow.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat out, legs at full stretch, cape and both gold chains snapping straight back and
   the bow tie blown up under his chin — no coat tails, the cape is the trail. Short gold
   speed streaks touch his heels and four gold bottlecaps kick off his hind paws and stay
   tight behind him, while from the neck up he is entirely unchanged: hat dead level,
   monocle in, expression mildly bored.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   Feet planted, standing upright on his hind legs and going nowhere for seven seconds,
   and for the first time in his life visibly rattled — fur bristling out through the
   waistcoat, eyes enormous with pinprick pupils, monocle popped out and swinging on its
   chain, back heel a tapping blur. A doll-sized espresso cup pinched between two claws
   with the pinky out, pale blush-cream steam and warm gold jitter lines close around his
   shoulders; the hat is level but vibrating, and caffeine is the one thing old money
   cannot stay above.

SHEET 27b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Up on his hind legs with the pocket watch forgotten and swinging open on its chain,
   head up, both eyes wide and mouth open in unguarded delight — the one second old money
   forgets itself, astonished that the world is coming to him. A tight spiral of gold
   bottlecaps and fries curls in close around his chest and shoulders, every piece
   overlapping his body, three of them already caught in the upturned brim of the hat;
   monocle knocked cheerfully crooked.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing on his hind legs under a compact black silk umbrella with a brass handle held
   one-pawed, canopy no wider than his own body and tilted back so it stays well clear of
   the frame edge, top hat still on underneath it because he sees no contradiction. Rain
   beads and runs off the canopy rim in a tight bright ring touching the fabric — nothing
   on the ground, no dry patch and no shadow; monocle glinting, both chains still, eyes
   half-lidded and faintly pleased with himself.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The instant the shield eats a hit: braced in a quarter-turn away, one flat palm up, a
   blue-violet dome flaring tight around him with rain streaking off its skin — bright and
   pale, never blue-violet. This is the only frame where the composure cracks: monocle
   knocked askew on its chain, top hat tipped down over one eye, mouth open in an
   undignified flinch.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his hind legs, both forepaws clutched to the waistcoat, head craned up at the
   sky, eyes huge and round behind the monocle with its chain trembling. The top hat has
   jumped clear of his skull but still overlaps his ears, and three gold bottlecaps spill
   out of the inside of it and land on his head and shoulders — that is where he keeps it.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting on all fours hard toward camera-left but well clear of the frame edge, mouth
   open in real panic, one palm mashing the hat flat onto his skull, cape and watch chain
   flying, monocle bounced out and swinging wildly. Short red-gold speed strokes off his
   heels and two small exclamation strokes touching the hat brim; exactly two dropped
   bottlecaps tumble close behind, overlapping his stubby tail, and he is not going back
   for them.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Up on his hind legs in a grand full-arm doff, the top hat swept off and held high in
   one paw but angled inward over his own head so the whole hat stays inside the paint
   area, other arm flung up, mouth open in genuine unguarded joy. A short shower of gold
   bottlecaps pours from the upturned hat straight down onto his head and shoulders,
   touching him the whole way; the monocle catches one hard gold star of light and both
   chains swing.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with his long legs straight up in the air and his flat palms limply
   open, bow tie shoved sideways, waistcoat rumpled, stubby tail folded under him and the
   cape spread out beneath his shoulders. Swooning half-moon lids — no X eyes — with slow
   gold stars circling above; the hat has come off and leans brim-down against his
   shoulder still touching him, and the monocle lies on his chest on its chain. The read
   is wounded dignity, not injury: he is mostly offended.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side, spiral eyes, tongue lolling, the crushed hat jammed crooked over
   both ears so only his muzzle shows under the brim, monocle hanging off his cheek and
   full of rainwater on its chain. Stars orbit the wreck of the hat; one bottlecap sits on
   his chest, two are heaped against his shoulder, and one spins on its edge where it
   touches his open palm — none of them on the ground.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Caught inside the burst — soaked through with his fur plastered flat so he looks half
   his usual size, both forepaws thrown up, mouth open in pure shock, a white crown of
   water exploding tight around his middle. The top hat rides the near lip of the crown,
   brim-up like a little black boat with one bottlecap aboard and still overlapping his
   shoulder, and the monocle hangs full of water on its chain.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• CANVAS AND SCALE, MEASURED OFF THE FOUR SHIPPED PNGs. All four (idle, crouch, leap, land)
are exactly 218x250 px, painted content between 168x229 and 218x215. Paint every frame's
content — effects included — inside 218 x 229, then pad all 18 to one identical 218x250
canvas with the body's mass centred. Sprites are CENTRE-anchored, not bottom-anchored, so
anything painted above his head pushes the whole raccoon below the lane line; the identical
canvas plus centred mass is what keeps his head the same pixel size in all 18, which is the
one checkable test on the sheet.
• NO GROUND, ANYWHERE, IN ANY FRAME. The cutter is a magenta border flood that keeps every
non-magenta island, so painted puddles, wet-street gold reflections, the dry patch under the
umbrella, cast shadows, skid marks and caps scattered on pavement all survive the cut as
floating grey scum under a character with nothing beneath him. Contact effects only: the
water crown bursting at his paws in land, the spray in splash, caps landing ON him. Every
loose element must overlap his bounding box and sit within half a body width, or the
components cutter emits it as a separate sprite file.
• COLOUR LAW. Keep the oxblood on the RED side — the shipped art measures about #7A1E28 to
#8C3232 with the blue channel low, and it has zero pixels in the knockout range. Push it
plum, wine-purple or magenta and the knockout eats ragged holes out of the cape, the hatband
and the bow tie. Shield dome BRIGHT blue-violet.
• THE HAT IS THE MOOD RING — paint the angle deliberately every time. Dead level: idle, sit,
eat, crouch, dash-run, magnet, umbrella. Clamped or lifted: leap, land, run-r, coffee,
scared, flee. Tipped over one eye: shield. Off his head entirely: cheer, ko, dizzy, splash.
Keep the silk glossy and rain-beaded in every outdoor frame — at 59px on a phone that wet
gloss on a black cylinder is the only thing separating his silhouette from First Frost and
Nordic Jimothy.
• BOTTLECAPS ARE ONE CURRENCY, NOT SEVEN BITS OF LITTER. Identical gold cap, identical size,
identical paint in all seven frames that use them, and never more than five in a frame. Caps
that touch him read; caps drifting free inflate the crop and shrink the animal.
• NO LETTERING ANYWHERE, INCLUDING THE PLACES A PAINTER WILL LETTER BY REFLEX: no monogram
on the hatband, no numerals or engraving on the pocket watch (blank cream disc, two hands),
no print on the fry box, the napkin or the umbrella. Also note that the new idle supersedes
the shipped idle.png, which is currently four-square with empty paws and breaks the stance
lock; the shipped crouch, leap and land already match this sheet and do not need repainting.
• Left-facing run: MUST BE PAINTED SEPARATELY — mirroring run-r jumps the monocle onto his
left eye and reverses both gold chains and the hat-clamping palm; paint run-l as the same
three-quarter sprint facing camera-left with the monocle still over his RIGHT eye (now
frame-right), chains and clamping paw re-drawn on the correct side, and note that a full
character with a missing run-l falls through to the procedural coloured-blob fallback on
every leftward hop.
