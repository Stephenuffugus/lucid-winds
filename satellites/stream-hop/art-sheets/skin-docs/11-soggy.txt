================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Soggy Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Hunched quadruped raccoon: four-square on all fours at rest and in every travel frame,
  rearing back onto his hind legs (tripod, stubby tail counterweighting) for any frame that
  needs a free forepaw — never a full human upright.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  ALL 18 FRAMES: the folded newspaper hat (fold points to his right; print is fine grey
  column TEXTURE, never a legible word) plus a live drip line coming off him somewhere. Per-
  frame props, and nothing else ever: soggy cardboard fry tray (eat, magnet), takeaway cup
  (coffee), crook-handled umbrella (umbrella).

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  Jimothy's own body under the water: round and spherical, no visible neck, head perched
  high on the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a STUBBY ringed tail that hangs or holds
  short — it never streams, never flicks a rope of water and never fans a wake. He is
  plantigrade in every single frame including both runs, the leap and the landing: flat
  palms, five long spread fingers, heel down, never a knuckle. Standing rules: he is four-
  square only when nothing is in his paws, and the instant a prop appears he is up on his
  hind legs — no frame gets a fifth limb. Soaked-fur clumping and the hat's slump state are
  the only things allowed to change across the sheet; his height, head size and silhouette
  mass are identical in all 18.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 11a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square facing camera exactly as the existing master, weight even, soaked
   fur clumped into dark dripping spikes, the newspaper hat still holding its crisp fold.
   A small contented smile while drips hang and fall from his chin, elbows and hat brim —
   no puddle, no wet mark, nothing at all under his paws.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting up on his haunches, forepaws folded on a soaked belly, water still wringing out
   of his chest fur in one thin steady thread. The hat has slumped a quarter turn so the
   brim rests across one eyebrow and he doesn't fix it — he just watches the rain over the
   camera's shoulder, content and a little alone. This is the quiet frame. No puddle
   beneath him.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting with a rain-collapsed cardboard fry tray wedged against his shin and belly so
   it physically touches him, holding one fry up in both flat forepaws; it has gone so
   soft it folds double before it reaches his mouth and he watches it bend with genuine
   admiration. A thin stream drools off the hat brim straight down into the tray, topping
   it back up.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on all fours as in the existing master, haunches up, elbows out, coiled
   to spring, water sheeting off his back and shedding down his flanks in short runnels
   that stay on the fur. The hat has slid forward into a wet paper visor and he peers out
   from under it, eyes locked up-screen; nothing pools between his paws.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, limbs spread wide, flat palms open, belly forward and delighted,
   throwing a tight fan of droplets off his fur that stays inside the reach of his own
   limbs. Stubby ringed tail short and straight behind him; the hat has lifted a finger's
   width off his skull by one corner, still attached by sheer sogginess.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front paws slammed flat, body low, spine compressed, whiskers forward, a contact crown
   of splash bursting up around all four feet and rising no higher than his knees. The hat
   drops back onto his brow with a fresh slap of water off the brim; his eyes are wide and
   pleased.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on sprint to the right, body low, legs at full extension, flat palms reaching,
   stubby ringed tail held short and level with two or three beads flicking off its tip —
   no rope, no wrung-mop trail. The hat streams back tight against his skull with one
   corner unfolded into a short curled ribbon flapping against his ear; hat print is grey
   column texture only.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat-out sprint, legs at maximum stretch, eyes wide, no fire on this one — a short
   dirty rooster tail of gutter water breaks off his heels and dies within half a body
   width, and the rain flashes into thin white steam off his shoulders where he is moving
   too fast for it to land. The hat is pinned flat to his skull, its column texture
   stretched taut.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   STANDING STILL and vibrating: reared on his hind legs, both feet planted, both forepaws
   clamped around a takeaway cup the rain has already refilled past the lid, pupils shrunk
   to pinpricks, soaked fur bristled into spikes. Pale blush-cream steam off the cup,
   short jitter lines either side of him and a couple of warm orange fire licks tight at
   his feet — even the drip hanging off his chin is vibrating.

SHEET 11b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared up hugging the rain-collapsed fry tray to his chest, mouth open, eyes bright
   with pure astonishment that for once things are coming TO him. Gold bottlecaps spiral
   in on a warm amber ribbon that stays within half a body width and overlaps his
   shoulder, and the wet hat's ink has begun to run — one dark blue-black streak painting
   down his cheek like a happy tear.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Reared on his hind legs, one flat forepaw gripping the crook of an open umbrella held
   close over his skull, rain hammering the canopy and shedding off both edges in short
   curtains no wider than the canopy itself. He is still wringing wet and still dripping,
   but the hat is gently steaming as it finally begins to dry and his smile is quiet and
   enormously smug — no puddle, no dry patch of ground.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The instant of impact: crouched low, forepaws thrown up flat-palmed, eyes squeezed
   shut, chin tucked — and just as soaked as every other frame, same dark dripping fur
   spikes, same sagging hat, nothing dries. A bright blue-violet dome snaps up tight
   around him with rain shattering off the outside, while the water it knocks loose hangs
   inside the dome as a rimlit burst of droplets and a hard blue-white edge light runs the
   length of his near side.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Reared on his hind legs, both forepaws clutched to his chest, ears flat back, eyes
   enormous and fixed on something above the frame; the hat brim has flopped up on its
   own, so the full downpour is now hitting him square in the face. Water runs into his
   open, worried mouth and he does not move to stop it.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting left flat out, mouth open in panic, two small red '!!' strokes stamped in the
   air right beside his ear and overlapping his head, a short tight burst of filthy gutter
   spray off his heels. The hat has torn half off and is pinned by one soaked corner
   against his shoulder rather than trailing — and even now the corner of his mouth is
   still trying to smile.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Reared tall on his hind legs, one arm punched to the sky, mouth open in a shout of pure
   joy; the newspaper hat has just left his fingertips and spins at head height,
   overlapping his raised paw, its droplet halo kept inside the hat's own arc. Underneath,
   the revealed head fur is a flattened, comically wet little dome.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back, all four legs up with flat pads to the sky and the drips now running
   off his upturned palms, a ring of gold stars circling his head and swooning half-moon
   lids on the eye that shows. The hat has landed over his face like a little pitched
   paper roof, headline band up and brim tented off his nose, one dazed happy eye peeking
   through a torn column — quietly pleased to finally be lying down. Nothing spreads
   beneath him.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side, spiral eyes, tongue lolling, stars orbiting his head, the hat
   spun down over one ear at a drunk angle. Ink from the soaked newsprint runs down both
   cheeks in two long dark blue-black spiral smears that match his eyes exactly, and a
   last fat drip swings off his ear.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Caught in the burst: arms thrown straight up, flat palms spread, eyes bulging, mouth
   wide in shock as a crown of pale blue-white water erupts around him and blows him
   backwards — nothing sinking, nothing going under. The hat has finally given up and gone
   to warm-grey pulp, sliding down his brow in soft ribbons, and under it he is
   unmistakably still smiling. Wettest he has ever been.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• ENVELOPE AND THE HEAD TEST. Every frame, water included, lands between 0.80:1 and 1.35:1
and inside roughly the 250x243 box the four existing masters already use. The checkable
rule: his HEAD is the same number of pixels in all 18 — if an effect shrinks the head, the
effect is too big. leap, cheer, land and splash are the blowout risks. Paint idle, leap and
cheer first and send those three for a 59px lane cut before committing the other fifteen.
• NO GROUND, ANYWHERE — this is the biggest change from the old brief. The puddles under
idle, sit, umbrella and ko are DELETED; the sprite is cut off magenta and floats over lane
art, so a painted puddle, damp smear or cast shadow survives as grey scum under a raccoon
standing on nothing. Drips off chin and paws, sheeting runnels on the fur, and the contact
splash crowns at his feet in land and splash all touch the body and are correct. A puddle
never is.
• DETACHED ELEMENTS STAY CLOSE AND OVERLAPPING. The magnet bottlecaps, the flee '!!' strokes
and the thrown cheer hat must each sit within half a body width AND overlap his bounding
box, or the cutter emits them as separate sprites and the wider crop shrinks him. Nothing
touches a frame edge or a divider line — leave a clear magenta margin all the way round
every cell.
• NO LETTERS. The newsprint is texture only: fine grey ruled column ticks with a darker
banded block where a headline would sit. It reads as 'newspaper' at 59px and it survives the
mirror; a real word does not. This applies to idle, sit, dash-run, run-r, ko and splash,
which all previously specified readable print.
• COLOUR LAW. The knockout eats the magenta family, so: shield dome BRIGHT blue-violet (
Running ink is dark blue-black, never pure black, and wet newsprint grey goes warmer and
softer the more it soaks — ink is the only colour allowed to shift across the sheet.
• TWO FRAMES ARE DELIBERATELY DRIER AND MUST NOT BE SOAKED BACK DOWN: umbrella (hat
steaming, finally drying, standing in his own rain-shadow) and shield (a light trick only —
his fur clumping and hat sag are identical to every other frame; the dome sheds water off
him, it does not dry him). That contrast is the joke, and shield staying wet is what stops
it reading as the wrong sprite popping in for half a second.
• Left-facing run: Paint separately — the hat's fold points to his right, the unfolded
ribbon flaps off his right ear and the ink streak runs down his right cheek, so a mirror
throws all three to the wrong side and reverses the newsprint texture; run-l is the same low
sprint reversed with fold, ribbon and streak re-pointed to his left.
