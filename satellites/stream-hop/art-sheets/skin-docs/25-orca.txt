================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Orca — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the rainy
streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  His own thing — a beached orca: belly flat on the road at rest, fluke as both engine and
  brake, and every lift is a SPINE-arch with the flippers flared out sideways as balance
  outriggers, never as legs.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  One short frond of honey-brown bull kelp looped over the base of the dorsal fin and lying
  flat down both flanks — mid-value so it still reads as a band under any shop finish — and
  that is the entire wardrobe, present in all 18; the only other props are per-frame: paper
  chip tray (eat), takeaway cup plus two crumpled empties (coffee), patio umbrella (umbrella
  and shield).

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  Every raccoon pose is rebuilt from four parts: a fluke that is both the engine and the
  push-point, an enormous belly he travels on, two stiff pectoral flippers, and a dorsal fin
  that carries his whole mood. STANDING RULE ONE — the pectorals are low-set paddles with no
  elbow and no weight-bearing role: they flare OUT and DOWN as outriggers with the tips just
  kissing the asphalt, and they never go underneath him, never in front of or across the
  face, never above the head, and never hold anything against gravity. STANDING RULE TWO —
  every rear is a spine rear: a big arch from mid-body, chin thrown high, chest sliding up
  off the road with the fluke jammed down behind as the push point, silhouette of a rising
  black wave with two wings, never a propped-up sea lion. He does not hop, he porpoises in
  wet galumphing belly-arcs, and props are wedged behind the fin, laid on the flat of a
  flipper, or carried crosswise in the teeth — never gripped.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 25a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Belly flat to the road in three-quarter view, near flipper laid forward on the asphalt,
   fluke curled loose behind, dorsal fin upright with the notch clean against the sky and
   the kelp glossy over its base. White eye patch bright, mouth a small closed smile, rain
   beading across the wet black hide, and only a thin lace of contact spray where his
   belly meets the ground — no puddle.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   The quiet frame: a low spine-arch lifts his chest a few inches clear, fluke laid flat
   behind, both flippers slack out to the sides, dorsal fin tipping with the lean and the
   kelp hanging limp down his flank. Head turned away, chin low, eyes half-shut and far
   off — a pod animal listening for a pod that is not there.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Chin up on a low spine-arch with a battered fish-and-chips cod hanging crosswise out of
   his grin because he has nothing to hold it with, the soggy paper tray tipped up against
   his near flipper and chips stuck to the white of his jaw. The tray is no bigger than
   his eye patch; kelp flat along his back, rain pocking the hide.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Loaded and compressed: the whole mass pressed low, chin down, flippers flared hard out
   to either side with the tips just kissing the asphalt, fluke cocked up and back like a
   spring, dorsal fin raked forward with the gather and the kelp bunched at its base. A
   tight collar of spray squeezes out along the contact line under his belly; eyes locked
   forward, mouth a flat determined line.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   A three-quarter porpoise arcing toward the camera on a shallow diagonal — head near and
   large, back arched, fluke kicked small and far behind so the body foreshortens into the
   landscape box — flippers flared wide as wings, white belly turned to the viewer, kelp
   lifted along his spine. Short sheets of water peel off his flanks and one small burst
   ring hangs tight at his tail; jaws open in absolute delight, eyes squeezed into happy
   crescents.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Belly-first touchdown: chin down, jowls squashed flat against the road, flippers braced
   forward and splayed, fluke slapping down behind, kelp thrown forward over the fin. A
   fat crown of street water bursts sideways along his contact line and clings to his
   sides, and he blinks half-shut, entirely pleased with the mess he has made.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Full side profile travelling right in a long low galumphing arc, body barely clearing
   the road, near flipper reaching forward to pull and the far one paddling back, fluke
   kicked up mid-stroke, notch and kelp reading clean along the top of the silhouette.
   Spray fans off his belly as a short attached fringe — he carries no prop of any kind in
   this frame, and there is no skid line, wake or puddle behind him.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same galumph flat out: body stretched long and low, fluke a blur, both flippers
   slapping hard, kelp streaming tight along his flank, jaw open in a huge grin and eyes
   wide. Speed streaks rake short and tight against his flanks and the thrown water is
   cropped to a stubby attached rooster tail at his hip — nothing trailing off toward the
   frame edge.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   A STANDING frame — he is not travelling: belly planted dead flat, body held still and
   vibrating in place, pupils shrunk to pinpricks, dorsal fin quivering between short
   jitter marks and the kelp trembling with it. A tiny takeaway cup sits on the broad flat
   of his near flipper like a side table with pale blush-cream steam curling off it, two
   crumpled empties tucked in against that flipper's edge, and warm heat licks flickering
   along his contact line.

SHEET 25b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   A big spine-arch rear — chin high, chest sliding up off the road, fluke jammed down
   behind as the push point, flippers flared wide as outriggers with the tips only
   brushing the asphalt — jaws open in astonished joy, eyes round and shining, kelp
   slipped back along the arch. Gold bottlecaps, chips and fries spiral in tight against
   his chest and around the curve of his back, close enough to overlap him and glinting
   hard on all that black.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   A full-size café patio umbrella, shaft wedged behind the dorsal fin and lashed there
   with his own kelp, raked hard forward so the canopy sits over his skull like a hat brim
   with rain sheeting off the rim in bright arcs. Head and shoulders dry while the entire
   rest of that enormous back is still being hammered; half-lidded eyes, faintest smug
   smile — the canopy has to read as a roof in pure silhouette at 59px.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The instant he eats a hit: chin jammed into his chest, head turned hard away from the
   impact, both flippers swept forward and cupped either side of the skull like blinkers,
   tips almost meeting but plainly framing the face rather than covering it. A bright
   blue-violet dome flares white-hot at the contact point with rain streaking off it, his
   mass shoved back a few inches with scraped spray at the belly line, one eye screwed
   shut, and the umbrella still lashed to the fin but blown inside out.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   A spine-arch rear staged as a steep diagonal, never a vertical column: chest up, fluke
   jammed down behind, flippers flared out and trembling with tips barely touching, head
   craned back and up at the sky. Eyes huge and perfectly round, mouth a tiny O in all
   that black bulk, kelp standing on end and a single bead of sweat flicking off his brow.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Galumphing away hard to the left in a flat panic, mouth wide, flippers flailing out of
   rhythm, fluke thrashing, dorsal fin leaning steeply into the turn and the kelp whipped
   back against his flank. Two fat exclamation marks sit tight over his shoulders
   overlapping his back, short red speed streaks rake his flanks, eyes white-rimmed and
   locked forward.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   A breach angled straight at the viewer on a leaning diagonal — front end thrown up and
   toward the camera, chin high, both flippers flung wide and open, jaws wide in a joyful
   open-mouthed shout, kelp flung up off the fin base but still looped on. A tight crown
   of spray bursts at the bottom of his own silhouette where the fluke slapped, a few gold
   bottlecaps confetti in close over his back, warm light catching the rain on all that
   wet black.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Rolled onto his side, white belly to the viewer, flippers limp in the air, fluke
   flopped over, dorsal fin lying flat sideways with the notch still readable and the kelp
   slid down over one brow. Eyes shut into swooning upturned half-moons, mouth slack in a
   small sheepish smile, three stars circling his head — wounded dignity, not injury, and
   nothing whatsoever painted on the road beneath him.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled belly-down in a loose S, spiral eyes, tongue lolling out one side of his jaw,
   dorsal fin bent right over like a wilted leaf with the kelp slipped down around his
   chin. Both flippers splayed uselessly out to the sides and three short wobble arcs
   tucked in against his cheek — spirals only, no stars, and no swept leaves or litter.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: a white crown of floodwater explodes up around him and he is
   wedged in it to the shoulders, flippers flung up, a short spout puffing off his
   blowhole, kelp plastered flat and wet. Face caught between shock and a huge daft grin —
   the biggest animal in the game has finally found water and it only comes up to his
   chest — stuck fast and plainly out of play; he never sinks and never goes under.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• ONE BOX FOR ALL 18. Paint every cel into the same 230 x 185 landscape frame, aspect about
1.24. The engine width-sizes anything over 1.12 and height-sizes everything else, so a
single cel that drifts under 1.20 snaps him to a different size mid-animation: never taller
than wide, and never in the 1.00-1.19 dead band. The checkable test is his HEAD — same
number of pixels in all eighteen. Measure him snout-to-fluke, not by the bounding box,
because spray and the umbrella will fool a size-match by eye.
• NO GROUND, ANYWHERE, IN ANY FRAME. The cutter floods magenta and keeps every non-magenta
island, so puddles, spreading water, skid lines, swept leaves, dropped cups and cast shadows
all survive as floating grey scum — and because they sit below him they drag the centre-
anchor and float him above his lane line. Every puddle in the previous draft (idle, sit,
magnet, ko) and all ground scatter (coffee's tipped empties, dizzy's swept litter, run-r's
skid line) is deleted. Wet sheen on the hide plus contact spray that physically touches the
body is the entire vocabulary.
• READABILITY IS THE WHOLE BATTLE. He is #14161a on wet black asphalt and the shop finishes
darken him further. Every frame needs a cool bright rim running the length of his back and
dorsal fin, a white spray or steam edge breaking his outline somewhere, and the mid-value
kelp band across the fin base. All FX in white, cream, cyan and pale blue — the knockout
eats the magenta family, so no hot-pink steam (the draft's coffee steam is.
• THE NOTCH IS HIS NAME. The notch in the trailing edge of the dorsal fin is his ID the way
a hard hat is somebody else's: same edge, same position, all 18. Leave clear magenta margin
above the fin so the crop never shaves it, and keep every cel well inside its divider lines
— nothing touches an edge or a gutter, because the divider lines get trimmed by an inset and
anything near one merges two cells into one sprite.
• DETACHED BITS STAY CLOSE. Bottlecaps, chips, stars, exclamation marks, wobble arcs, spray
and the crumpled empties must all sit within half a body width AND overlap his silhouette,
or they cut out as separate sprites and shrink the animal. Nothing on this sheet carries
readable text: the cup, the chip tray and the umbrella are pattern, colour and shape only.
• TWO ENGINE ASKS AND A PAINT ORDER. Set CHARS scale 1.25 on orca (index.html:719 currently
has none) — painting him bigger on the sheet does literally nothing because each cel is
normalised on its own. Paint idle, leap and cheer FIRST and cut them at 59px on a real lane
before committing the other fifteen. And nobody on this roster is food: eat and magnet use
battered cod, chips, fries and bottlecaps, never a salmon or any silver fish, because the
Sockeye is a playable castmate.
• Left-facing run: Mirror — run-r is deliberately prop-free and bilaterally clean (no cup,
no umbrella, kelp draped centrally over the fin base onto both flanks, and the notch is in
the fin's trailing edge so it flips true), so ship run-l as a flipped duplicate PNG rather
than a repaint; it must exist as a file, because full characters do not auto-mirror and a
missing run-l drops him to the procedural blob on every leftward hop.
