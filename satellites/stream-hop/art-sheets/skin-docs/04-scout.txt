================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Scoutmaster Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on
the rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Upright plantigrade biped at rest — heels down, flat palms free, never four-square —
  dropping onto all fours ONLY in the six travel/impact frames (crouch, leap, land, run-r,
  dash-run, flee) plus the painted run-l; locked, no silent switches anywhere else.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all 18 frames: navy patch vest with the top two buttons fastened and the hem left open,
  brass buttons down the placket, a wordless square cream merit patch on HIS RIGHT chest, a
  small round brass badge low on HIS LEFT hip, and a cream neckerchief knotted square at the
  throat — nothing else is permanent.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  This is hero Jimothy's body in a costume, so every reference pose transfers one-to-one:
  round and spherical with NO visible neck, head perched high above the shoulders, long
  skinny limbs, and a SHORT STUBBY tail that never streams, ropes or curls out behind him in
  any frame. Plantigrade throughout — flat palms, five long spread fingers, heel down; the
  word "knuckles" does not appear on this sheet. The vest is stiff and swings as one unit
  from the shoulders, flaring only from the open hem, while the neckerchief is loose cloth
  that streams, flops and soaks. The single checkable rule: his HEAD is the same pixel
  height in all 18 frames — if an effect shrinks the head, the effect is too big.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 4a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing upright at attention on his hind legs, heels flat, chest pushed out, chin
   level, long skinny arms straight at his sides with flat palms turned in. Vest square
   and formal, neckerchief knot dead centre, rain beading on his shoulders and both badges
   catching a little streetlight.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting upright on his haunches, back straight and knees together, one flat palm
   smoothing the merit patch against his chest and the other laid on his knee, stubby tail
   tucked. Off duty and still sitting like he expects to be inspected, mouth a small
   content line.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting on his haunches toasting a marshmallow on a bent wire held with enormous
   procedural correctness, cheeks already packed, neckerchief tucked into the vest as a
   bib; the little Sterno tin rests against one hind foot and the dented enamel mess-cup
   is hooked in his other paw. A second marshmallow is already threaded on the wire for a
   troop that did not come — this is the quiet frame.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, elbows out, flat palms spread wide, eyes
   locked up-street. Neckerchief tails hang forward past his chin and the brass hip badge
   glints — coiled like he is demonstrating correct technique to a troop that is not
   there.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, limbs spread but reaching no further than a body width, belly forward,
   mouth open and delighted, stubby tail up. The open hem of the vest flares back off his
   ribs while the fastened top holds the square merit patch flat and readable, and the
   neckerchief streams straight back like a troop pennant.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front paws slammed down flat with the fingers splayed, knees deep, stubby tail up as
   counterweight, vest dropping back against his chest and the neckerchief flopping over
   it. A tight ring of spray bursting off his own palms and nothing else beneath him —
   whiskers forward, extremely satisfied with the landing.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on sprint to the RIGHT, body low, front and back legs at full extension,
   neckerchief streaming back to the left, vest hem flaring from the waist. His right
   flank is to camera so the square cream merit patch and the brass placket buttons read
   clearly along the near side.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same rightward side-on sprint pushed flat out, ears pinned, legs at full stretch,
   warm gold-white speed streaks kept short and overlapping his hindquarters. One corner
   of the merit patch has torn loose from its stitching and flutters against his chest —
   eyes wide, faintly appalled at his own speed.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   A STANDING frame, not a run: upright with both heels planted and going nowhere, a
   dented enamel camp percolator mug clutched in both flat palms at his chest. All the
   energy is in the body — fur bristled out, pupils shrunk to pinpricks, jitter ticks off
   his ears and elbows, pale blush-cream steam boiling past his mask and small warm fire
   licks curling off his heels.

SHEET 4b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Upright with both arms thrown wide and his mouth open in astonished delight as gold
   bottlecaps and snack scraps spiral in tight toward his chest, the innermost few already
   stuck flat to the navy vest like brand new merit badges. He is not grabbing anything —
   he is being awarded it, and he knows it.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing dead straight at parade posture under a small, carefully hand-mended umbrella
   gripped in one flat palm, canopy tucked low over his ears and barely wider than his
   shoulders, rain bouncing off it close around him. Neckerchief bone-dry and perfectly
   knotted, one eyebrow raised: he brought this, obviously.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Crouched hard with one forearm slammed across his face and the other palm flung out
   flat, a bright blue-violet energy dome ringing him tight with rain streaking off its
   curve. Eyes screwed shut, teeth gritted, the vest and both badges lit cold by the glow.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his hind legs with both flat palms clutched over the badges on his chest, ears
   flat, eyes enormous, head tipped back at whatever is lining up on him. His neckerchief
   knot has pulled crooked for the first time in his life — he is guarding the badges, not
   himself.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Panicked three-quarter run straight toward camera, veering to his own left so BOTH the
   chest patch and the hip badge stay readable, mouth open, ears back, vest hem flapping
   wide from the waist. One palm is clamped flat over the merit patch to stop it shaking
   loose, with short warm amber-white streaks tight behind his heels.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Standing tall on the spot, one arm up in a crisp three-finger scout salute that has
   completely lost its composure into a whoop — elbow bent so the paw stays close over his
   skull — the other palm pressed to his chest. Mouth wide open, eyes shut with joy, small
   gold sparks popping right at the badges.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with his legs straight up and his arms flopped out either side, mouth
   open, eyes closed into swooning half-moon lids, a tight ring of stars circling close
   over his skull. The neckerchief has flipped up over one eye and the little brass hip
   badge has popped its pin and come to rest on his own belly — mortified about the
   uniform, not hurt.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on one hip and trying to sit back up wrong, spiral eyes and tongue out and NO
   stars at all, vest dragged half off one shoulder and the neckerchief spun round so the
   knot sits at the back of his neck. One flat palm is still gamely attempting a salute
   and missing his forehead by a mile.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Soaked through and clearly out of play inside a white and pale-cyan crown of water
   bursting around his waist, arms up, mouth open in shock, mask fur plastered flat and
   the vest gone black with wet. One corner of the merit patch has lifted and the water is
   pulling at it, still stitched at the other end — plainly the worse tragedy.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND IN ANY FRAME. No asphalt, pavement, puddles, cast shadows, drains, kerbs or
scattered litter anywhere in the 18 — the cutter is a magenta border flood and every one of
those survives as floating grey scum under a character who has nothing beneath him. Contact
spray that touches his own palms (land, splash) is correct and welcome.
• Sprites are CENTRE-anchored, not bottom-anchored (the old sheet note was wrong), and every
frame is normalised on its own, so anything a frame adds is subtracted from the animal. Keep
every frame between 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and inside about 1.2x the idle box, with his body mass
at the centre of the painted content — umbrella canopy, cheer salute, ko stars and the
splash crown are the four that will blow it, so keep all four tucked in close.
• Anything not touching him — bottlecaps, sparks, stars, speed streaks, rain — must sit
within half a body width AND overlap his bounding box, or the cut orphans it into its own
sprite. Nothing may touch a divider line or a frame edge; draw the white dividers first and
paint a visible margin inside them, magenta #FF00FF everywhere else, and do not use an even
grid.
• NO lettering, numerals or punctuation anywhere, props included: the merit patch is a
wordless embroidered shape (a simple fir-tree silhouette), the brass badge a plain stamped
disc, and there are no '!!' or shock letters behind the flee frame. It is illegible at 59px
and it mirrors into gibberish.
• Colour law and the 59px read: steam is pale blush-cream with the green channel up (never
hot pink), the shield dome is blue-violet like Jimothy's own. At 59px he must read as one
dark navy block, a bright cream diagonal at the throat, one pale square on the right chest
and one gold dot at the left hip; keep those four values far apart and everything else
quiet.
• One-frame props only, and all of them in physical contact with him: Sterno tin,
marshmallow wire and mess-cup (eat), percolator mug (coffee), umbrella (umbrella). Nothing
else ever leaves the body — the popped badge in ko lands on his own belly, the peeling patch
in splash stays stitched at one end. Badge placement is otherwise identical in all 18; the
only allowed deviations are the torn patch corner in dash-run, the popped hip badge in ko
and the lifted patch corner in splash.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately as a real 19th frame: the square cream merit
patch (his right chest), the round brass hip badge (his left hip) and the placket buttons
are all asymmetric, so a mirror throws his badges onto the wrong side and flips the
neckerchief knot — paint run-l as a side-on sprint to the LEFT with his LEFT flank to camera
so the brass hip badge and the neckerchief tails read on the near side, same pose and same
head height as run-r.
