================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Nordic Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadruped: at rest he stands four-square on four flat plantigrade paws, and any frame that
  needs a hand rears him fully up onto his hind legs — never four-square while holding
  something, never a half-measure tripod.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all 18 frames, exactly two things: the heavy navy fair-isle sweater (cream/red/white
  horizontal bands with pale-blue flecks, ribbed cuffs ending at the WRIST, ribbed hem to
  mid-thigh) and the navy knit bobble hat with its red-and-cream band and oversized oatmeal
  pom — the fries carton (eat), takeaway cup (coffee) and umbrella (umbrella) each appear in
  one frame only and stay gripped on the body.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  Nordic is Jimothy's own body, so every pose transfers one to one: round and spherical, no
  visible neck, head perched high on the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a STUBBY ringed
  tail — never a long streaming trash-panda tail, in any of the eighteen. He is plantigrade:
  flat palms, five long spread fingers, heels down, and the ribbed cuffs stop at the wrist
  so the bare dark paws and the short tail below the hem stay readable in every frame (this
  overrides the old "sleeves swallow his forepaws / every prop handled through a cuff" note,
  which contradicts all four shipped frames — he grips with bare hands). The sweater is a
  second body that lags a beat behind him and the pom is the lag weight that sells the
  timing, but the wool never becomes his hands; the one exception is coffee, where he tugs
  the cuffs down over his palms as pot-holders with the fingertips still showing. Standing
  rules a painter must not break: no frame is four-square and holding a prop, the hem never
  drags on a floor because there is no floor, and the pom never leaves clear margin at the
  top of the cell.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 13a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square, three-quarter to camera, the heavy navy fair-isle sweater hanging
   to mid-thigh, bare dark plantigrade paws flat below the ribbed cuffs, stubby ringed
   tail low behind the hem. Calm, patient, faintly damp; the oatmeal pom droops to one
   side like it has given up on the weather.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   THE QUIET FRAME. Sitting upright on his haunches with the sweater pooled all around him
   like a knitted tea cosy, only his bare back toes and the tip of the stubby tail poking
   out, both paws resting on the woolly belly. Hat slipped low on the brow, eyes half-
   lidded, waiting out rain he stopped minding hours ago — content, and completely on his
   own.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting on his haunches with an open plain paper carton of fries hugged against the
   woolly belly in one paw, the other pinching a fry up to his mouth, cheerfully
   unbothered. Salt and crumbs caught in the fair-isle bands and two spare fries tucked
   down inside the ribbed cuff for later — nothing dropped, nothing scattered below him.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, the sweater rucked and bunched over his
   shoulders with the hem crumpled under his chest; flat palms spread, heels down. Hat
   tipped forward so the pom swings down in front of one eye and he looks past it,
   forward, ready to go.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, limbs spread wide, belly forward, the sweater ballooned with air like a
   knitted sail and the hem flared open. Pom lagging just behind the crown of the hat,
   mouth open in delight, three or four rain flecks and one stray wool fibre kept tight
   against his back.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front paws planted flat with the fingers splayed, body low, weight absorbed, the
   sweater slumping down into a woolly ripple. Pom whips forward over his brow, whiskers
   forward, a small spray of droplets bursting off the paws themselves — no puddle, no
   pavement, no cast shadow.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on lolloping sprint to the right, body low, legs extended, bare paws reaching well
   past the ribbed cuffs. The hem swings opposite to his stride, the pom trails close
   behind the hat, the stubby tail stays short and level, jaw set with grim Ballard
   determination.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat out to the right, legs at full stretch, the sweater pressed flat against him by
   the speed with the hem snapping behind. Pom horizontal, eyes wide, three short cream-
   and-rust speed streaks starting at his tail and stopping well inside the margin, and a
   thin curl of pale grey steam off the overheating wool.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   STANDING FRAME — planted dead still, up on his hind legs with heels flat and knees
   locked, both sleeve cuffs tugged down over his palms as pot-holders (dark fingertips
   still poking out) around a plain unbranded cup at chest height. Fur bristling through
   the knit, pupils shrunk to pinpricks, hat shoved back with the pom standing bolt
   upright on static, jitter lines at the shoulders, pale steam off the sweater and small
   orange fire licks right at his feet.

SHEET 13b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared up on his hind legs holding the ribbed hem out with both bare paws like an apron
   pouch, mouth open in astonishment and pure delight — everything is coming to him and he
   cannot believe it. Gold bottlecaps and fries spiral in tight on a warm gold-and-cream
   swirl (never pink) and drop into the wool, two already snagged in the fair-isle.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Reared up on his hind legs, one bare paw gripping the crook of a small compact umbrella
   held low and tilted back over his shoulder so the canopy sits beside the head rather
   than above it. Dry and smug from the shoulders up with half-lidded satisfied eyes, but
   the hem is already dark and heavy with soaked-up water and dripping; rain streaks short
   and tight to the canopy, nowhere near the cell edge.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Braced and flinching, reared up with both forearms thrown across his face, a blue-
   violet energy dome snapping shut close around his outline — never violet or purple —
   with the light catching every strand so the fair-isle pattern glows through. Pom bolt
   upright with static, rain sliding off the dome, one eye squeezed shut and the other
   peeking.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his hind legs, both forearms clutched to his chest, ears flat under the hat, eyes
   huge and locked on something up-screen. The hat has slipped so he is peering out from
   under the brim, pom quivering, loose wool bristling along his shoulders.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting left in flat panic, mouth open, hat blown back and hanging off one ear by its
   brim with the pom bouncing wildly. The hem tangles around his back legs so he is half-
   tripping as he runs, short rust speed streaks tight behind him and two small
   exclamation strokes (no letters) close over his head.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Standing tall on his hind legs, one forearm punched up but bent so the paw stays level
   with the top of the pom, the other paw planted on his woolly hip, mouth open in a
   whoop. Hat knocked askew, a tight burst of gold confetti and three flecks of wool fluff
   hanging right against his shoulders — nothing rises above the pom.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Seen from a high three-quarter angle so the frame stays near square: dumped on his back
   with the body diagonal, all four bare paws in the air, stubby tail pressed flat beneath
   him, sweater rucked halfway up his chest and the hat slid down over his eyes with the
   pom flopped beside his head. Mouth open in a stunned little O, five small stars
   circling above the brim — quietly embarrassed, and privately relieved the sweater took
   the whole hit.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled and spun out on his haunches, spiral eyes, tongue out, the hat turned fully
   backwards so the pom dangles over his nose. The sweater has twisted a quarter turn so
   the fair-isle yoke sits sideways across one shoulder, and a single loose thread loops
   out, curls back and ends in a little tangle against his hip — well inside the frame,
   touching no edge; spirals only, no stars.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: soaked through, both forearms up, eyes wide, a pale white-
   green crown of water exploding around his middle so his mass stays centred in it. The
   knit sags dark and heavy and the hem stretches to just below his feet and no further
   (1.15x idle height maximum), pom a sodden dripping lump over one ear — mouth open in
   shock, less worried about the water than about the three days that wool will take to
   dry.

• ENVELOPE AND HEAD SIZE. Every frame, effects included, between about 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and

• EFFECT COLOUR LAW: Magnet swirl warm gold and cream, coffee and dash steam pale grey-
cream, shield dome bright blue-violet ( The navy sweater sits safely outside the knockout
range; keep it there.
