MAKE TWO SHEETS FROM THIS DOCUMENT:  7a  and  7b

================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

City Pigeon — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Two-legged ground bird: body carried level and forward over two coral feet in every
  standing frame, never reared upright like a person, and "four-square" never applies to him
  because he has two feet, not four.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  No costume and no carried kit — the only thing present in all 18 frames is the dried fleck
  of white crosswalk paint centred across the base of his tail; props appear in exactly
  three frames (fry cone in eat, takeaway cup in coffee, blown-out umbrella in umbrella) and
  every one of them touches his body.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He has wings instead of arms and a beak instead of hands, so every raccoon "holding" pose
  becomes standing IN the container with his head down inside it, and every "arms up" pose
  becomes a wing throw — wings go up, out and mantled forward over the crown, and never
  cross the chest, never point, never hold. He has no grip: pigeon feet are weak walking
  feet, so unlike the crow and the gull he never pins, clamps or tears food — he pecks and
  swallows whole, and his own body weight is the only thing that ever holds a prop still. He
  cannot fold onto his haunches, so sit is a settled curb loaf with the feet tucked away
  underneath, and on his back the two stiff coral feet point straight up. He can technically
  fly and never does: the hop in leap is the only air he ever gets, and flee is him
  hammering his wings and still not lifting off — that failure is the character.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 7a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   LIVE ART — do not repaint. Both feet planted wide, body level, chest out, head up and
   cocked so the orange-ringed eye reads to camera; slate wings folded tight with the two
   black bars showing, green-violet neck sheen catching the streetlight, nothing painted
   under him. He owns the crosswalk and knows it.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   THE QUIET FRAME. Settled into a full curb loaf, feet folded away out of sight, head
   sunk so deep between the shoulders he has no neck at all — a warm grey brick with one
   eye, feathers puffed a size larger against the rain, lids half down and aimed at
   nothing in particular. The swagger switches off the moment nobody is watching; no
   concrete, no puddle, no ground.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Standing INSIDE a tipped-over paper cone of fries with both feet, head down in the
   mouth of it and his own body weight — not a grip — stopping it rolling, one fry
   crosswise in his beak and the visible eye rolled up at camera mid-theft. Cone
   completely blank, no printing; nothing scattered on the ground.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   LIVE ART — do not repaint. Body dropped low and horizontal, legs folded deep, feathers
   slicked flat to the skin, wings unlatched and just cracked open at the shoulders; head
   thrust forward, eye locked up-screen, tail dropped low. Loaded like a spring, nothing
   loose, no ground.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   One hop's worth of air: wings only HALF-open with the wrists lifted and the tips no
   wider than about 1.25x his idle silhouette, breast forward, tail fanned, coral feet
   trailing curled beneath him, beak open in a delighted coo. Two loose down feathers hang
   right at his tail, overlapping his body — this is one of only three frames allowed to
   shed.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   LIVE ART — do not repaint. Wings swept back and cupped hard against the air to brake,
   tail fanned wide and angled down like a flap, both coral feet stabbing down with the
   toes splayed, head low and neck feathers blown backward by his own stop. A tight burst
   of grit and rainwater breaks off the toes and touches them — contact only, no puddle
   beneath.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on to the right in the flat-out pigeon scurry, wings clamped shut against the
   body, head thrust forward on a stretched neck mid-bob, one coral foot forward and one
   back at full stride. Body tipped nose-down, tail up with the white paint fleck centred
   on it, all business, no flying involved.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same scurry absolutely committed: neck at full extension, wings half-cocked out for
   balance, both feet blurred into a coral smear, crown feathers streaming back. A short
   spray fan bursts off his own toes and two speed streaks trail off the tail — both stay
   tight to the body and well clear of the frame edge; no puddle, no ground.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   A STANDING FRAME, held up to 7 seconds: both feet flat and planted, whole body rigid
   and vibrating, bill sunk into the mouth of a takeaway cup lying on its side against his
   chest with the lid leaning on its rim — pigeons drink by suction, so the head stays
   down but the eye clears the rim, enormous with a pinprick pupil, cut straight to
   camera. Pale blush-cream steam vents around his neck and warm orange jitter licks
   flicker up his own legs; cup blank, feet never leave the spot, nothing on the ground.

SHEET 7b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Standing tall on one foot with the other tucked, beak open in an astonished coo and one
   wing half-raised as if checking whether this is really allowed, eye enormous and
   shining with pure disbelief. Three gold bottlecaps and a chip arrive in a tight ring
   that overlaps his chest and wings, nothing further out than half a body width — delight
   at being chosen, never appetite.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   A full human-sized umbrella, blown out with its ribs folded inward, has come to rest ON
   him: the crook of the handle hooked over his back between the wings, the shaft lying
   along his spine, the canopy tipped over his skull like a roof no wider than about 1.3x
   his own width. He does not look up — chest out, eye to camera, bone dry and entirely
   smug, with rain skidding off the canopy edge only; no storm drain, no dry circle, no
   ground.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Braced low with both wings mantled UP and FORWARD over the crown, wrists meeting above
   the skull into a feathered roof — never crossed in front of the chest — head pulled
   down between the shoulders, one eye peeking out from under a wing edge, tail splayed
   for balance. A bright blue-violet dome snaps tight around him with rain streaking off
   its curve; no shed feathers anywhere.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Frozen rigid, neck periscoped straight up and beak pointed at the sky, one foot still
   lifted mid-step, neck hackles ruffed out into a collar, the visible eye huge and white-
   ringed. A gull's wing shadow crosses his chest, neck and one wing and stops dead at his
   outline — none of it falls on the ground.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting flat out with his wings hammering the air and still not lifting off — that
   failure is the joke — beak wide open, neck stretched dead ahead, coral feet a blur.
   Short red speed streaks and two shed feathers trail off his tail, and a small wordless
   shock burst sits tight above his crown, touching it; no lettering, no punctuation
   marks.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   HIS FRAME ALONE: up on his toes in a full courting strut with the iridescent neck
   inflated into a glossy green-violet balloon bigger than his own head, beak open in a
   triumphant coo, tail fanned and dragging low, wings only slightly out and DOWN — never
   thrown up into a V. A little confetti of loose down drifts close around his shoulders,
   overlapping the body.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with both stiff coral feet straight up and one crossed over the other,
   wings splayed loose to either side, beak ajar and both eyes shut into blissful half-
   moons — he reads less knocked out than suspiciously comfortable, like a pigeon
   sunbathing on purpose. Stars circle his head and a little drizzle drifts through; no X
   eyes, no shed feathers, no asphalt.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Still up on his own two feet but buckling, one foot crossed over the other mid-stagger,
   neck gone completely rubbery so the head hangs back upside-down over his shoulders,
   wings drooping loose at his sides. Spiral swirls where his eyes were and stars orbiting
   the crown — spirals belong to this frame only; no sweeper bristles, no litter, no shed
   feathers, no ground.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: a pale grey-white crown of gutter water explodes around him,
   wings flung up, beak open, eye bulging with pure personal offence, his soaked neck
   iridescence running down his front like petrol on a puddle. He stays inside the crown,
   out of play and deeply insulted — nothing sinks, no ripples, no floating feathers, no
   ground.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• SCALE LOCK. The four live PNGs (idle, crouch, leap, land) are all 191x250 portrait — paint
the other fourteen on that same canvas proportion, effects included, and never let a frame
go wider than tall or the engine flips to width-sizing and the bird visibly changes size
mid-hop. The checkable test: his head is the same pixel height in all 18. If leap is
regenerated with the corrected half-open wing arc, regenerate and swap all four live frames
together — never mix a new leap with the old three.
• CENTRE-ANCHORED, NOT BOTTOM-ANCHORED (the old sheet note had this backwards). The engine
centres the sprite on the lane point, so keep the bird's mass at the centre of the painted
content in every frame, ko, dizzy and splash included — content above him pushes him below
the lane line and content below pushes him up off it.
• THERE IS NO GROUND IN A SPRITE. No asphalt, wet concrete, puddles, dry circles, ripples,
cast shadows, storm-drain grates, crumbs, salt or scattered litter in any of the 18 — they
all survive the magenta cut as floating grey scum under a bird with nothing beneath him.
Contact effects that touch his body are correct and wanted: grit off the toes in land, spray
off the toes in dash-run, fire licks up his own legs in coffee, the water crown in splash.
• COLOUR. Keep the violet half of the neck sheen away from the silhouette edge — ring the
neck in slate grey — because a violet pixel touching the background can open a bite in the
knockout. No magenta, hot pink or dark blue-violet in any effect: steam is pale blush-cream,
the shield dome is blue-violet like Jimothy’s own, the water crown is pale grey-white. The
coral feet and the orange eye ring are the only warm colours on an entirely grey animal and
they are the whole read at 59px — keep them saturated.
• NO TEXT OR PRINTING ANYWHERE: blank takeaway cup, blank fry cone, blank umbrella, and no
'!!' over his head in flee — a wordless shock burst instead. SHED FEATHERS APPEAR IN EXACTLY
THREE FRAMES: leap, flee and cheer. Never on a held death frame, where drifting debris reads
as plucking and injury.
• SIZE JOKE RUNS IN REVERSE FOR HIM. The engine draws every character at one height, so sell
'small bird' through the props: the takeaway cup is nearly as tall as he is, the fry cone
reaches his chest, and the umbrella is a full human one that swamps him. Do not paint him
smaller to make the point — it does nothing.
• Left-facing run: mirror
