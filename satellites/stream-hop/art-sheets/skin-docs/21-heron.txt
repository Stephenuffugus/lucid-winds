JIMOTHY — SKIN 21: GREAT BLUE HERON

MAKE TWO SHEETS FROM THIS DOCUMENT:  21a  and  21b
Nine frames on each sheet, eighteen in total. Label the files exactly 21a and 21b.

================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Great Blue Heron — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Upright biped — he stands over two long stilt legs with the body carried vertically and
  the neck folded into an S; never on all fours, never "four-square", and a wing is never
  used as a hand.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  No costume and no permanent prop — the heron is bare; props appear in exactly four frames
  and always touch the body: plain paper chip cone in the bill (eat), plain takeaway cup
  skewered on the bill (coffee), incoming chips/battered scraps/bottlecaps on tight arcs
  (magnet), plain umbrella hooked over the gape (umbrella).

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He has no hands, so the bill and the gape do every carry: the dagger skewers or grips, and
  food goes down the throat whole in one visible travelling lump. The wings only go up, out
  and forward-mantled over the crown — they never cross the chest, never point, never hold,
  and never take weight. Where Jimothy panics with his whole body, the heron panics from the
  ankles up: feet planted, face unchanged, stillness as the gag. Because his neck folds and
  extends, his total height is the one thing that can wreck the sheet — every frame is
  capped at about 1.15x the idle height so his head stays the same pixel size in all
  eighteen.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 21a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing square on both stilt legs, dead still, neck folded into a shallow S so the
   head rides low over the shoulders and the yellow dagger bill points level at the
   camera. Shaggy slate chest plumes hang without stirring, the single black crest feather
   trails off the back of the skull, the gold eye is fixed on the viewer and very slightly
   narrowed — nothing on him moves, and that is the pose.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   The quiet frame: one leg vanished entirely up into the belly feathers, the whole bird
   settled into a soft grey bell on a single stilt, neck folded flat so the bill lies
   along the chest, eyes half-lidded. Rain beads on his back and the plumes puff over the
   tucked foot — a wet lawn ornament nobody is coming to look at. No loose feathers,
   nothing painted beneath him.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Bill tipped straight up with a plain paper cone of chips gripped in the bill tip and
   tipping its last inch down the throat, a fat comedy lump already halfway down the long
   neck and visibly stretching the feathers. Both feet planted, cone touching the bill and
   nothing spilled or scattered anywhere, one gold eye rolled down to hold eye contact
   with the camera the entire time he swallows.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Coiled to strike: hocks folded back deep, body dropped low over the toes, neck
   compressed into a tight spring-loaded Z with the bill aimed forward like a nocked
   arrow. Wings shut tight to the body, crest feather flattened back, gold eye wide and
   locked on the row ahead — all the energy stored, none of it leaked out yet.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Airborne with the wings only HALF-spread, wrists high and cupped and the primaries
   closed rather than fanned, and the long legs folded up tight under the belly instead of
   trailing. Neck tucked into the shoulders, bill forward, two or three flecks of loose
   down close in under his chest — this is a hop that never quite becomes flight, and the
   frame is no wider than idle.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Wings cupped forward to brake but still only half open, toes splayed enormous and
   reaching down, knees folding to absorb it, neck flung out forward for balance with the
   bill level. One tight white splash-ring bursting at the touching foot and physically
   touching it — no puddle, no pavement, no cast shadow.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on to the right at a long heron stride, one leg fully extended forward and the
   other trailing straight back, wings cracked barely open as outriggers held close to the
   body, neck folded neatly into place. He is moving fast and looks like he is merely
   walking somewhere on purpose; a slight forward tilt is the only concession to speed. He
   carries nothing and has no asymmetric marking, so this frame mirrors cleanly.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat out: legs at full scissor stretch, body low and level, wings held half-open and
   steady, short white speed streaks touching his shins and a tight spray flicking off the
   back toe and staying attached to it. The neck stays folded and the face stays utterly
   composed — the legs are a blur, the head glides along a perfectly straight line.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   Standing absolutely still and badly wired: the dagger bill run clean through the sip
   hole of a plain unmarked takeaway lid so the cup hangs skewered at eye level, pale
   blush-cream steam curling past the gold iris, coffee already running back down the
   bill. Feet planted and going nowhere, crest bolt upright, chest plumes fanned electric,
   pupil shrunk to a pinprick in a huge gold iris, one stilt leg blurred with vibration.

SHEET 21b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Feet rooted, bill cranked wide open at the sky like a delivery chute, gold eye huge and
   bright with pure astonishment as chips, battered scraps from a paper tray and gold
   bottlecaps spiral in on short tight arcs that all overlap his body, one chip already
   dropping down the gullet. Wings barely lifted — he has not taken a single step toward
   any of it, and he is quietly thrilled that he did not have to.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   The umbrella's crook hooked over the base of his upper mandible right at the gape,
   shaft running up and back so the open canopy rides above and slightly behind his skull
   like a parasol on a stick. Bill level, gold eye clear and thoroughly smug under the
   rim, one leg tucked away, rain streaking off the canopy edge only — no dry circle, no
   pavement, no print on the fabric.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The instant of the hit: both wings mantled up and forward over the crown, wrists nearly
   meeting above the skull with the primaries curtaining down past his cheeks, and a
   bright blue-violet energy dome flaring tight around him with rain sparking off it. One
   gold eye peers out under the wrist, and his feet have not moved a millimetre from where
   they were.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Reed alarm, capped: neck stretched and slimmed to a stick but only about 1.15x the idle
   height, bill raised to roughly 60 degrees rather than vertical, every feather clamped
   flat to the body. Eyes rolled to the top of their sockets doing all the work, crest
   feather quivering, two thin nervous motion-ticks tight either side of the head — the
   most dignified terror in Seattle.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting at a full comic stride, wings half-open for balance, short red speed streaks
   touching both heels, two small exclamation ticks close over his head and a couple of
   body feathers shaken loose beside his flank. The neck is still folded and the bill
   still level — he is running for his life with his head travelling along a perfectly
   flat line, which somehow makes it worse.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   The courtship display let loose but capped like leap: wings thrown up and out only
   HALF-spread, lacy chest plumes fanned into a shawl, head arched back and bill wide open
   in a great creaky rusty-gate honk, both feet finally off the ground by an inch. Rain-
   glitter close around him and no loose feathers; the whole frame stays inside the idle
   box — a lifetime of composure spent all at once.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   He does not go feet-up, he FOLDS: collapsed straight down onto his keel like a
   deckchair, legs concertina'd underneath him, wings slack out to either side, neck
   poured down into a loose loop. Bill primly shut, eyes swooned to half-moon lids (no
   X's), three stars circling the skull — mortified, and quietly determined that nobody
   saw.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side, neck gone completely loose in a dropped-garden-hose S, one wing
   trapped under him and the other folded up over the crown and drooping across one eye.
   Spiral eyes, bill hanging ajar, crest feather bent in three directions, both stilt legs
   crossed at the ankles in the air — nothing painted underneath him.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: chest-deep in an exploding white and pale-cyan crown of
   water, one wing thrown up and the other half-spread slapping the surface, plumes
   plastered flat so the mighty neck reads as a soaked pipe cleaner. Bill wide open in an
   outraged squawk, gold eye enormous — a water bird betrayed by water and taking it
   extremely personally. He stays inside the crown; he is not going anywhere and he is
   certainly not going under.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• SCALE IS CHECKED ON THE HEAD, NOT THE FEET. The engine centres each sprite on the lane
point and normalises every frame on its own, so the old 'lock the ground line' note is
wrong: centre his body mass in the painted content of all 18 (including the wide
ko/dizzy/splash frames), keep every frame between 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and inside about 1.2x
the idle box, and paint him so his head is the same number of pixels in every frame.
• NO GROUND PLANE ANYWHERE. No puddles, no dry circle under the umbrella, no wet pavement,
no cast shadow, no storm drain, no spilled carton, no scattered chips. Only contact effects
that physically touch him are allowed: the splash ring at the landing foot, the toe spray in
dash-run, the water crown at his chest.
• DETACHED ELEMENTS STAY CLOSE. Stars, exclamation ticks, speed streaks, magnet arcs, down
flecks and steam must all sit within half a body width AND overlap his bounding box, and
nothing may touch a frame edge or a divider line — the crest plume and the toes are the
parts most likely to stray into the gutter.
• THIN PARTS DIE IN THE KNOCKOUT: the single black crest plume, the needle tip of the bill,
the wire legs and the splayed toes. Paint every one of them a few pixels thicker than feels
natural, and no aggressive despeckle or auto-trim — a heron missing a leg reads as a
floating bird.
• COLOUR LAW. The yellow dagger bill and the gold eye stay the highest-chroma thing in every
frame, including under the canopy, under the dome and inside the water. Steam is pale blush-
cream (green channel high), the shield dome is blue-violet like Jimothy's own. .
• NO LETTERING OF ANY KIND: plain cup, plain lid, plain paper cone, plain umbrella fabric,
no logos, no headline, no branding. Loose feathers appear in exactly TWO frames — leap and
flee — and nowhere else; on held frames like sit, ko and dizzy they read as plucked and
injured, which is the opposite of the joke.
• Left-facing run: mirror
