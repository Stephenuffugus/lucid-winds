================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Opossum — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the rainy
streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadruped: four-square at rest on flat plantigrade paws, heel down and five long spread
  fingers, and he only frees his forepaws by sitting back onto his rump — never by standing
  upright like a person.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all eighteen frames: a faded orange hi-vis night-crew vest, always on and hanging open,
  two silver reflective bands with the lower one peeling on his LEFT side; a blank
  unlettered laminated badge on a lanyard on his LEFT chest; a dented steel thermos clipped
  to the vest on a short cord at his RIGHT hip. Nothing else, and nothing ever comes off.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  The prehensile tail is his spare hand and it does every job Jimothy does with a free paw,
  but it is anchored at the rump and only about body-length: sat back, it can reach forward
  between his hind feet to his own chest, or run up his spine to just behind his ears — it
  can never hold anything over the head of a stretched-out quadruped. Like the raccoon he is
  plantigrade: flat palms, five spread fingers, heel down, opposable rear thumb on the hind
  foot; no knuckle-walking anywhere. Reared frames sit back on the rump with both hind feet
  flat, which frees both forepaws honestly and keeps the tail free as a third hand rather
  than a fifth leg. His head must be the same pixel size in all eighteen frames, so the
  reared frames have to reach the same head height as the four-square ones.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 10a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Three-quarter quadruped foreshortened toward the camera so he stays taller than wide,
   four-square on flat palms, pale pointed face, pink nose, black bead eyes, nicked left
   ear. Tail curled under and around beside him in a tight S that touches his flank the
   whole way; vest hanging open with the peeling band on his left, blank badge swinging on
   its lanyard, thermos clipped at his right hip.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat back on his rump, hind feet flat, forepaws folded on his belly, ears down and
   eyelids at half mast, looking at nothing at the end of the shift. Tail coiled beside
   him in a flat spiral resting against his own foot, vest slumped open, badge hanging
   dead still, the dented thermos tipped empty against his thigh.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat back on his rump with a to-go tub of cold clam chowder brought up to his chest by
   the tail, which comes forward between his hind feet and wraps the tub twice. Both
   forepaws work a little plastic fork, cheeks packed, eyes squeezed shut with
   contentment, a chowder smear on his chin and one oyster cracker balanced on the blank
   badge; thermos at his hip.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened right down on all fours, staged three-quarter and foreshortened toward the
   camera so the box stays tall, elbows out, haunches up, nicked ear pinned flat, eyes
   locked dead forward past the viewer. Tail swept around beside him in a shallow arc
   touching his flank rather than trailing straight back, vest bunched over his shoulders,
   thermos pressed flat to his ribs.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, all four legs splayed wide, pale belly turned to the viewer, mouth open
   in a delighted little gape. Tail whipped into a full loop that stays inside his own
   silhouette, thick and fully opaque with the tip tucked back toward his hip and never
   thinning to a wisp; vest ridden up around his ribs, badge and thermos flung out on
   their cords but still overlapping his body.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front paws planted flat, spine bowed, hindquarters still dropping, whiskers thrown
   forward, eyes wide with the impact. His tail slaps down and throws a tight spray fan
   that touches only his tail and paws — no puddle and no road under him — while the
   reflective bands buckle across his chest and the thermos bounces off the vest.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on bounding gallop to the right showing his RIGHT flank, body stretched long but
   head lifted and the tail curled up in an arc over his rump rather than streaming back,
   so the frame stays taller than wide. Hind feet gathered under him with the rear thumb
   splayed, thermos swinging forward on its cord, the badge swung across from his far side
   and blank on both faces.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat-out four-legged bound, legs at maximum stretch and still visibly not very fast,
   eyes huge and ears back. Exactly one trailing element: a single short arc of coffee no
   longer than his own tail, curving up into the box from the popped thermos cap still
   dangling on its cord — plus two small fire licks under his belly and nothing else
   behind him.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   A STANDING frame, held up to seven seconds: sat back on his rump with both hind feet
   planted flat and absolutely still, wired to the eyeballs, tail brought up to grip the
   dented thermos upright while both forepaws clamp the little screw-cap cup at his chin.
   Pinprick pupils, fur bristled along his spine, whiskers in tight jitter arcs, steam
   painted pale blush-cream and never pink, two fire licks at his planted feet.

SHEET 10b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Sat back on his rump with the tail brought forward between his hind feet and curled
   into a scoop at his chest, already heaped with gold bottlecaps, mouth open and eyes
   round with astonishment that things are coming TO him. A tight inward spiral of more
   caps and stray fries curls in close around his shoulders, every one overlapping his
   silhouette and none near the frame edge; vest pockets brimming.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Sat back on his haunches so tail root and skull are close together, the tail running up
   his own spine to hook a small black umbrella just behind his ears with the canopy
   tipped FORWARD, so the dry zone covers his head and chest and his tail root is the wet
   bit. Both forepaws free and deeply smug — one tucked in the vest pocket, one cradling
   the thermos — blank badge hanging dry against his chest, rain sheeting off the canopy
   edges in short strokes that stop well inside the margin and never reach the ground.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The flinch: crouched low with both forepaws crossed over his face and one black eye
   squinting through the gap, tail clamped tight around his own ankle. A bright blue-
   violet dome — never violet — flares white at the point of impact just clear of his
   back, and inside it the reflective bands are the brightest thing on him, the blank
   badge flipped up and hanging on its lanyard, thermos swung out against the vest.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Reared back on his rump with the enormous opossum gape cranked wide open — teeth
   rounded off, no drool, no snarl — eyes rolled up and back with the whites showing in
   pure panic. Fur puffed out, forepaws clutched tight to his chest, tail stiff and curved
   up close behind his back, badge flipped up and stuck in his chin fur clear of his face;
   his scariest face is an obvious bluff and everyone can tell.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting left with everything he has, legs blurred into a scribble, mouth open in a
   panicked wail, vest flapping with the thermos and blank badge swinging wild. Two short
   red speed streaks and two fat exclamation marks sit close behind his shoulders
   overlapping his body — no skid mark, no ground, nothing touching the edge.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Reared up tall on his rump with both forepaws punched skyward but bent at the elbow so
   they stay inside the box, tail thrown into a triumphant question-mark curl beside his
   head rather than above it, mouth wide in the same huge gape — joyful this time.
   Reflective bands blazing, badge swinging, a low tight arc of gold bottlecaps around his
   shoulders, none of them near the frame edge.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Playing dead and badly overselling it: flopped on his side with the spine curled into a
   compact C, legs stiff and curled in the air, tongue lolled out over his teeth, tail
   curled up over his flank, one eye squeezed shut and the other cracked open a slit
   peeking straight at the viewer. Stars circle his head and two small green stink curls
   rise tight off his flank; vest twisted up under his shoulders with one band catching
   the streetlight, blank badge lying face up beside his ear on its lanyard, thermos
   rolled to a stop against his back.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   On his back, all four feet up in the air, tail flopped in a loose corkscrew across his
   own chest, vest twisted around backwards with one reflective band still showing. Spiral
   eyes and tongue flopped sideways, wet leaves and brush bristles stuck ON him — snagged
   in the vest, caught in the tail, one draped over his snout — with two more circling his
   head in place of stars; this is the one time he genuinely was not faking it.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: soaked through with his fur plastered flat so he looks half
   his usual size, forepaws thrown up, mouth open in a shocked gape, tail hanging like a
   wet rope against his leg. A white and pale-cyan crown of water explodes around his
   middle with the droplets kept well inside the margin, and the vest is a sodden dark
   orange rag with both bands still showing — he is startled, not going anywhere under the
   surface.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• FOUR FRAMES ALREADY SHIP AND THEY HAVE NO COSTUME.
assets/chars/opossum/{idle,crouch,leap,land}.png are live at 216x250 and show a bare animal
— no vest, no badge, no thermos. Repaint all eighteen together and replace them; fourteen
vested frames delivered beside four bare ones means he puts a vest on and takes it off mid-
hop. Landing the full set also ends his folded 4-frame status, which switches the engine's
stand-in effects OFF (the flashing '!', the orange speed lines on flee/dash/coffee, the ko
tip-over spin), so every one of those has to be painted in.
• KEEP EVERY FRAME UNDER 1.12 ASPECT — target the shipped 216x250 box, about 0.86. At
exactly 1.12 the engine flips from sizing by height (59px) to sizing by width (74px) and the
sprite visibly jumps size mid-animation. Effects, spray and stars all count toward the box.
Check it by head size: his head is the same number of pixels in all eighteen.
• THE TAIL IS ALREADY FAILING THE CUT. In the three shipped frames the thin pink tail has
broken into floating fragments — a severed pink shard in crouch.png and leap.png, and a
stray dark blob plus a pink shard sitting off in the top-left corner of land.png, which also
drags the centre-anchored body off its lane line. Paint the tail thick and fully opaque, in
contact with the body silhouette along its whole visible length, and never taper it to a
wispy translucent tip against the background.
• COLOUR LAW. Paint the tail, nose and ears a warm salmon or putty pink with the BLUE
channel kept low — a mauve or lilac pink falls inside the magenta knockout (high red, low
green, high blue) and gets bitten away in ragged chunks. Same law: coffee steam is pale
blush-cream and never hot pink, the shield dome is blue-violet like Jimothy’s own and.
• NO GROUND IN ANY OF THE EIGHTEEN. No wet asphalt, no puddles, no dry ring under the
umbrella, no skid mark behind the flee, no cast shadows, no leaves or litter lying on the
floor — all of it survives the cut as floating grey scum under a character with nothing
beneath him. Contact spray that touches his paws, tail or belly is fine and correct.
• THE BADGE CARRIES NO LETTERS IN ANY FRAME, EVER. Blank laminate: a scuffed photo shape, a
colour bar, a worn corner. It shows in both run frames, and lettering is illegible at 59px
and mirrors into gibberish. Nothing else on the sheet gets words either — not the vest, not
the thermos, not the chowder tub.
• Left-facing run: Paint it separately: run-r shows his RIGHT flank, run-l shows his LEFT —
where the badge, lanyard, peeling reflective band and nicked ear all live — so a mirror puts
every one of them on the wrong side, and the engine does not mirror full characters at all,
meaning a missing run-l drops him to the procedural blob on every leftward hop.
