================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Ghost Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadruped at rest — a plantigrade raccoon, four-square with flat palms, five long spread
  fingers and heels down, hovering a thumb's width above a ground that is never painted; he
  rears onto his hind legs ONLY in the frames that say so (coffee, magnet, umbrella, scared,
  cheer) and sits on his haunches in sit and eat.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  No costume and no permanent prop — he is bare; the only constants are anatomical (bone-
  white bleached fur, dark bandit mask, near-black OPAQUE sooty paws, faded stubby ringed
  tail, blue-white fray at shoulders, hips and tail tip), and there are exactly three frame-
  local props: a plain unprinted fry carton (eat, magnet), a plain unprinted takeaway cup
  (coffee) and one small umbrella (umbrella), each gripped in an opaque dark paw and
  touching the body.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is a raccoon, so Jimothy's staging transfers pose-for-pose; the translation here is
  weight and opacity, not skeleton. Plantigrade in all eighteen — flat palms, five long
  spread fingers, heel down, never knuckles. Body canon holds: round and spherical, no
  visible neck, head perched high above the shoulders, long skinny limbs and a STUBBY tail
  in every frame — the vapour streams, the tail never does. The standing rule is that
  nothing bears weight: toes hang slack, no limb compresses, there is no cast shadow
  anywhere, and he never floats higher than the thumb's width he already floats — the float
  lives in the pose, not in extra height.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 26a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Four-square and plantigrade three-quarters to camera — bone-white bleached fur, dark
   bandit mask, stubby ringed tail, and near-black sooty paws as the only fully opaque
   part of him. Nothing takes weight: toes hang slack, no limb compresses, no shadow, just
   a thin ice-blue underglow touching the pads, and his big dark eyes are calm and faintly
   apologetic, like he knows he shouldn't be here.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   The quiet frame: sitting up on his haunches, dark paws folded on his belly, eyes half-
   closed, content but a shade lonely. His backside never quite settles and his hind feet
   hang slack; the wisps rising off his shoulders curl up and fold back into his ruff,
   never off the top of the frame.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting with a plain unprinted fry carton tipped against his chest, one sooty paw
   holding a fry at his mouth that never gets any shorter. A second fry has already fallen
   through him and lies solid and gold on his lap fur, a faint pale-blue trail down his
   chest marking the way it went; cheerful and mildly puzzled.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low, haunches up, forepaws braced wide with flat palms and five long spread
   fingers, eyes forward and huge. Nothing is compressed — the belly hangs clear and the
   gathered spring reads as ice-blue vapour clinging to his underside and streaming a
   short, tight way back off his hips.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, limbs spread, belly forward, mouth open and delighted, stretched
   further than a solid raccoon could manage. Face and forepaws stay crisp; the hind legs
   give out into a blue-white vapour trail no longer than a third of his body, and the
   tail itself stays stubby — the vapour streams, the tail does not.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Forepaws down flat, palms and fingers spread, body low, whiskers forward, eyes startled
   that anything was there to land on. There is no impact: the trailing wisps fold forward
   and curl UP around his wrists and forelegs, attached to him — no ring, scuff or mist
   spreading out across a floor.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on travel right, body low, legs at full extension, the near-black plantigrade paws
   the only solid things in the painting. His hind quarter smears into pale blue fog with
   exactly ONE afterimage, offset a quarter of a body width so the two silhouettes overlap
   heavily; stubby tail throughout.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat-out sprint right, legs stretched, eyes wide, mouth open — where Jimothy throws
   warm fire he throws cold: short ice-white contrails off the heels and frost flecks that
   touch his hocks. His rear third has gone to vapour but is still opaque paint inside a
   continuous rim, with one heavily overlapping afterimage and nothing loose in the
   margin.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   A STANDING frame — he is holding still and vibrating: reared up on his hind legs, both
   sooty paws clamped round a plain unprinted takeaway cup at his chest, fur bristled,
   pupils shrunk to pinpricks, short jitter lines ticking off his ears and shoulders. The
   coffee is the most solid thing in the picture, a warm amber glow shining up through his
   ribs, its steam pale cream-white, with frost curls licking at his ankles where the
   living get fire.

SHEET 26b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared up on his haunches, mouth open in flat astonishment, one dark paw round the fry
   carton and the other thrown open wide as blank gold bottlecaps whirl in to him. The
   whirl hugs his torso so every cap overlaps his body — the ones passing behind him
   painted as dimmed, cooled discs ON his chest rather than gaps — and he is pure delight
   about it, never greed.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Reared up on his hind legs, smug and dry, one clearly opaque near-black paw closed
   round the handle of a small umbrella tilted forward over his skull, canopy compact and
   well inside the frame. The rain that misses it is painted as pale blue streaks ON his
   forearm just above the wrist, breaking into spray where it meets his paws — that
   contrast of solid paw and rained-through arm is the whole character in one frame.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Braced and flinching inside a close-fitting blue-violet dome that hugs his silhouette,
   paws up, eyes screwed half shut in relief. The dome's light pins a hard bright rim
   along the near edge of his fur and glows through him; short rain streaks skid off its
   outer surface.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his hind legs, dark paws clutched to his chest, eyes huge and fixed up-screen at
   whatever is lining up its dive. He is so alarmed he has gone faint — the bottom third
   of him painted as a pale ice-blue wisp mass, still opaque inside a solid rim, curling
   up against his shins, with thin wisps streaming off his ear tips.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting hard to the left, mouth open in panic, both hind feet clear, stubby tail
   bristled. His panic reads cold rather than red — short white-blue smears and frost
   flecks attached to his flanks and heels, plus a small burst of ice-star shock sparks
   touching his head where a comic would letter punctuation; no marks, no lettering.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Reared tall with one sooty paw raised beside his head — compact, not a full overhead
   stretch, so his head stays the same pixel size as every other frame — mouth open in
   pure joy, hind feet dangling loose. Brightest and most solid he ever gets: a warm
   amber-gold glow rising through his chest, the fog blown back into a tight ring on his
   shoulders, because somebody finally saw him.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   On his back, front legs up, swooning half-moon lids and solid gold stars circling and
   overlapping his head — never X eyes, and never a hard horizontal cut line. His lower
   half is breaking into pale wisps that curl back UP toward his chest in a loop, re-
   gathering rather than dissolving, while one sooty paw lifts in a small apologetic wave:
   he cannot even be knocked over properly.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled with spiral eyes and tongue out, gold stars and wisps spiralling together and
   touching his head. His outline has come uncoupled into a second copy offset only a
   quarter of a body width, so the two silhouettes share most of their area — painted
   solid pale blue at reduced contrast, never a genuine fade.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of shock inside an exploding crown of water: both sooty paws up, round
   shocked eyes, comically open mouth, fur soaked flat. Nobody sinks — the crown bursts
   white and ice-blue against his body, the water passing behind him painted as a dimmed
   cooled band ON his chest rather than a hole, and no piece of him is carried off
   downstream.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• ENVELOPE: paint all eighteen on the same 242x250 canvas as the four existing frames
(aspect 0.968) and check them by head size — his head must be the same number of pixels in
every frame. Effects included, no frame may go below 0.80:1 or above 1.35:1, and nothing may
touch a frame edge or a divider line.
• THE FLOAT LIVES IN THE POSE, NOT IN THE MARGIN. The earlier note about leaving blank space
under his paws is wrong twice over: the cutter crops tight to painted content so that margin
is deleted, and the engine centres the sprite on its content rather than bottom-anchoring
it. Sell the hover with slack toes, uncompressed limbs, no shadow and a faint ice-blue
underglow on the pads.
• NO GROUND, EVER, IN ANY OF THE EIGHTEEN. No asphalt, curb, road, puddle, splash-on-
pavement, drying smear, drag streak, scattered litter or cast shadow — they all cut out as
floating grey scum under a character who has nothing beneath him. Contact effects that touch
his body are correct and welcome.
• HE IS OPAQUE PAINT THAT LOOKS TRANSLUCENT. Every see-through area is a pale blue-grey or
white wash with the green channel high (150+), inside a continuous solid rim around the
whole silhouette — never a blend toward the magenta background and never a background-
coloured pixel inside his outline, or the flood eats a hole through his belly in every
frame.
• EFFECT COLOUR: cold means cyan, ice-blue and white with green up; warm means amber-gold.
No hot pink, Keep every wisp, contrail, star, cap and afterimage physically attached to or
overlapping his silhouette; anything loose in the magenta is dropped or shipped as a bogus
sprite.
• NO PRINTING ON THE PROPS — a painter will letter these by reflex. The takeaway cup is
plain with no logo or sleeve print, the fry carton is a plain kraft or red-and-white box
with no words, the bottlecaps are blank fluted gold discs with no cap face. At 59px his
reading anchors are the dark bandit mask, the dark eye patches and the near-black paws; give
the whole silhouette a thin cool grey-blue outline so a bone-white animal does not vanish
against pale rain and fog lane art.
• Left-facing run: mirror — nothing on him is asymmetric, there is no lettering and no side-
carried prop in run-r, so only the vapour fray flips and nobody reads drift as wrong in
either direction.
