MAKE TWO SHEETS FROM THIS DOCUMENT:  1a  and  1b

================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Deckhand Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadruped at rest — all four flat plantigrade palms down, heels flat — and he rears back
  onto his haunches (hind feet plus rump) whenever both forepaws are needed, standing full
  upright on his hind legs only in scared and cheer.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  Every one of the eighteen frames: yellow sou'wester rain hat on a knotted chin cord, navy
  rain cape with one brass clasp offset at his RIGHT throat, and one tan coil of rope that
  never leaves his body — riding his right shoulder by default, otherwise in a paw with the
  line running back to him, or wrapped around him.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is a raccoon, so every hero pose transfers one to one — but paint Jimothy's body canon,
  not a trash panda: round and spherical, no visible neck, head perched high above the
  shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a SHORT STUBBY tail that never streams, curls out behind
  or ropes water. Plantigrade always: flat palms, five long spread fingers, heel down —
  never knuckles, never knuckle-walking. Standing four-square means all four palms are down
  and no limb is free, so any frame that holds a cup, a coil or an open-armed gesture is sat
  back on his haunches instead; that swap is already written into each frame below and must
  not be reversed. The animation work is the costume: the cape is cloth (wet vertical folds
  at rest, snapped flat behind him in the air, flogging in a run), the rope is line with
  real weight and sag, and the hat is tied on, so it slips onto its cord in flee, ko, dizzy
  and splash without ever separating from him.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 1a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square facing camera, all four flat palms down, weight settled back on
   his heels, the tan coil riding his right shoulder and dragging that shoulder visibly
   lower than the other. Cape hangs in wet vertical folds, one fat bead of rain gathering
   at the front of the yellow brim, face calm and patient like a man watching for a boat
   that is nine minutes late.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat upright on his haunches, the coil lifted off the shoulder and cradled in both flat
   palms against his belly, cape gathered in wet folds over his hips and never spreading
   onto anything below him. Hat tipped back off his brow so the whole face reads, eyes
   half-lidded and content, looking somewhere past the camera at water we cannot see.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Still sat on his haunches with the coil resting across his own knees as a table and an
   unprinted paper tray of ferry-galley clam strips balanced on it, touching his chest —
   that is the joke. The back of one flat palm nudges the hat brim up while the other
   lifts a strip halfway to a full cheek, tartar sauce on the whiskers, cape hanging wet
   down his back.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on all fours, palms flat and heels down, haunches up and loaded, cape
   slid forward over the shoulders and bunched at the neck. The coil stays lashed on his
   right shoulder and swings down against his ribs — nothing in the jaws — hat brim jammed
   to the eyebrows, eyes locked forward.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, limbs spread wide and belly forward, cape snapped dead straight behind
   him like a signal flag, hat held on by the taut chin cord, mouth open in delight. One
   end of the rope has sprung free into two open loops that stay tight behind his hips and
   overlap his body, the coil itself still on the shoulder.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Body low and the weight absorbed through the shoulders, one forepaw planted flat with
   the fingers spread while the other is already reaching up for the brim that has bounced
   down over one eye, whiskers forward. The coil has slid forward off the shoulder and
   heaps against his own forearm, cape flopped over the far shoulder, a tight burst of
   grit and spray coming off the planted palm alone.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on sprint to the right, body low, legs at full stretch, cape streaming off to the
   left behind him. The coil is hooked over his right shoulder — the far one at this angle
   — swinging out of rhythm with his stride, the brass clasp showing at his right throat,
   one flat palm clamping the hat down.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat out to the right, legs fully extended, cape horizontal, the coil bouncing hard off
   his right hip with one loop just starting to unspool but staying inside his own
   outline. Hat mashed flat to the skull with the chin cord snapped straight back, eyes
   wide, short white speed streaks and torn rain kept tight behind his heels.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   Held completely still and vibrating: sat back on his haunches with the hind feet
   planted and locked, both flat palms strangling a plain scorched paper cup at chest
   height, fur bristled, pupils gone to pinpricks under a trembling yellow brim, the coil
   quivering on his shoulder. Pale cream-white steam rips off the cup, short jitter lines
   hug his silhouette and gold-orange fire licks at his heels — the feet do not move.

SHEET 1b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared up on his haunches, both flat palms thrown open and mouth wide in pure
   astonishment, hat pushed back and cape flung open, a short bight of his own rope arcing
   off one paw and back to the shoulder coil. Gold bottlecaps, coins and fries come
   spiralling in along that bight like beads running down a mooring line, every one of
   them close enough to overlap his body — joy that things come to him, never grabbing.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Reared up on his haunches with arms folded and faintly smug, still wearing the rain hat
   and rain cape underneath anyway, because he has lashed a black ferry-deck umbrella to
   his own shoulder coil with a neat little hitch so both paws stay free. The canopy sits
   directly over his head with the lashing visible running down to the coil, rain shedding
   off the rim in short streaks that stop at shoulder height.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Crouched back on his haunches at the instant of impact, one forearm across his face and
   the other holding the coil up in front of him like a buckler, ears flat, one eye
   squeezed shut, hat brim pressed down, cape bunched at the neck. A blue-violet dome hugs
   him just past the ears and elbows with rain streaking off the outside and the rope
   fibres lit along their edges inside.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his hind legs, hugging the coil to his chest like a life ring, head tilted back
   and up so the brim rides off his brow and the eyes go huge. Cape hem lifting, stubby
   tail rigid, a hard gull-shaped shadow falling across his chest and face only — nothing
   cast anywhere below him.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting hard to the left, mouth open in flat panic, hat blown back onto its chin cord
   and bouncing on his shoulders, cape flogging. Half the coil has unspooled into short
   flailing loops he has no time to gather, tucked behind his hip and kept inside his own
   outline, with red speed streaks close to his heels and two sharp shock marks over his
   head.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Up on his hind legs, both feet planted proud, one arm flung high with the rope still
   running down from that raised paw so the loop he threw hangs as a perfect open circle
   just above and slightly behind his head, overlapping his ears — a halo he tied himself.
   Mouth wide with joy, hat shoved back off the brow, cape thrown open and hanging low to
   keep his mass in the centre of the frame.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with his legs in the air, wrapped shin to shoulder in three neat turns
   of his own rope like a badly parcelled package, undone by the one thing he is good at —
   the face is wounded professional pride, not pain. The hat has come off onto its cord
   and lies against his ear with the brim tipped across one eye, the visible eye a dazed
   swooning half-moon, cape spread flat beneath him, gold stars circling above the brim.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled and sitting up crooked, spiral eyes, tongue out, hat spun around backwards on
   its cord and the cape twisted up under his chin like a bib. The coil has come undone
   around HIM — two loops round one ankle, one across his chest, the rest heaped against
   his own hip — which for a deckhand is the actual injury; stars circling his head, one
   connected silhouette, nothing loose beside him.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of shock inside an exploding white crown of water that bursts up around his
   ribs: soaked, fur plastered flat, both arms up, mouth wide, eyes huge, cape sodden and
   pinned to his flank. One flat palm still grips the rope and the line runs taut for a
   body length before ending in a frayed knot well inside the frame — he never let go of
   the tie-off — the yellow hat knocked off onto its cord and resting brim-up against his
   shoulder.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND IN ANY OF THE EIGHTEEN. No puddles, no damp smears, no cast shadows on pavement,
no dock planks, no curbs, no rope lying loose on a floor, no scattered crumbs. Only contact
effects that touch the body are allowed: the grit and spray off the planted palm in land,
the water crown at his ribs in splash, the fire licks at his heels in coffee.
• NOTHING TOUCHES A FRAME EDGE OR A DIVIDER LINE. The splash tie-off line, the flee loops,
the magnet bight and the cheer halo all end well inside their own cell with a clear magenta
margin. Every rope end is inside its own cell or the cutter reads two components and slices
the rope off.
• COLOUR LAW. Hat yellow and rope tan locked to ONE value each across the whole sheet — at
59px those two colours are the entire silhouette read and a half-step darker frame will
flicker in the hop cycle. Steam is pale cream-white, the shield dome is blue-violet like
Jimothy’s own; saturated PINK or MAGENTA is what the knockout bites out — blue-dominant
violet is safe and is what the hero uses.
• NO LETTERING ANYWHERE. The paper cup, the clam-strip tray, the brass cape clasp and the
hat carry texture and shape only, never words, headlines or a logo — it is illegible at 59px
and it mirrors into gibberish.
• ENVELOPE. Every frame including its effects sits between about 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and
inside about 1.2x the idle box. The check is the HEAD: it must be the same number of pixels
in all eighteen. The cape flares in leap, dash-run and cheer — let it crowd the gap, never
shrink the raccoon to make room for it.
• THE HAT AND THE ROPE NEVER LEAVE THE BODY. The hat is on its chin cord in all eighteen; a
loose hat anywhere in a cell becomes its own sprite when the sheet is cut. Same for the rope
— shoulder, paw with the line running back to him, or wrapped around him, and nothing else.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately — the coil rides his right shoulder and the
brass cape clasp sits offset at his right throat, so mirroring run-r puts both on the wrong
side.
