================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Garage Band Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on
the rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadruped at rest on the hero's own skeleton — plantigrade, flat palms down; he rears onto
  his hind legs only for coffee, magnet, umbrella, shield, scared and cheer, and sits on his
  haunches for sit, eat and dizzy.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all 18 frames: sleeveless denim vest worn OPEN (never buttoned), deep plum-violet
  bandana knotted at the throat with the knot off-centre to his left, exactly three enamel
  pins on the left lapel (pale eye button top, amber sunburst middle, small orange dot
  bottom), a coiled quarter-inch amp cable over his RIGHT shoulder, and one guitar pick that
  never leaves him — plus two rationed set pieces, held on the body only: pizza box (eat,
  magnet) and guitar-case lid (umbrella, shield). The practice amp is CUT from the sheet
  entirely.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is the hero's skeleton, so every pose maps one-to-one — but hold Jimothy's body canon:
  round and spherical, no visible neck, head perched high above the shoulders, long skinny
  limbs and a SHORT STUBBY tail that never streams, curls out behind or ropes water.
  Raccoons are PLANTIGRADE: flat palms, five long spread fingers, heel down, in every
  contact frame — there are no knuckles anywhere on this sheet, that gait belongs to the
  Sasquatch. The costume must not inflate him: vest shoulders and the cable coil sit tight
  to the body, and his head must measure the same number of pixels in all eighteen frames,
  which is the only checkable scale test. Cloth and rope behave — vest hangs open at rest,
  flares in the air, presses flat in a sprint, goes dark and heavy when soaked; the cable
  stays a coil, not a trailing line.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 6a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square facing camera, all four palms flat and turned slightly out with
   the fingers spread, weight rocked back, vest hanging open, cable coiled on his right
   shoulder, pick tucked under the bandana knot. Ears up, brows faintly raised, patient
   and hopeful — he unlocked the garage twenty minutes ago and nobody has turned up; this
   is the sheet's quiet frame.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting upright on his haunches, back rounded, the whole cable coil resting in his lap
   with one hind foot hooked through it, stubby tail tucked at his side. Eyes half closed,
   ears drooped a touch, pick clamped in the corner of his mouth, humming something and
   still half listening for footsteps at the door.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting on his haunches with the open pizza box balanced on his knees and touching his
   chest, a folded cold slice held up in both flat paws, cheek packed, eyes shut in
   contentment, pick parked behind his right ear and one short sag of cheese running
   slice-to-chin. A second slice lies untouched in the box, cut for somebody who never
   came — nothing on the floor, no plate, no crumbs.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on all fours with both palms flat and splayed, haunches up, elbows out,
   vest bunched around his shoulders, bandana shoved up under his chin, cable coil pulled
   tight against his back. Eyes locked dead forward, pick gripped crossways in his teeth —
   the held breath of a count-in before the downbeat.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne and stretched, limbs spread wide with the palms open and fingers
   splayed, belly forward, denim vest flared off his ribs like two stiff wings, bandana
   streaming back, stubby tail up. Mouth open in a delighted whoop with the pick parked
   behind his ear; the cable stays coiled on the shoulder with one short loop of slack,
   half a body long, curling straight back into the coil.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Both palms slapped FLAT with the fingers splayed and the elbows bent to absorb, body
   low and compressed, shoulders up around his ears, vest slapping back down against his
   ribs, one pin still swinging on its clasp. A tight puff of grit bursts off each palm,
   whiskers and eyes forward, pick behind his ear — a drum hit arriving exactly on the
   beat.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on sprint to the right, body long and low, plantigrade heels striking, vest
   streaming back so all three pins turn to camera and the bandana flies horizontal. The
   cable stays coiled on his right shoulder with a single short whip of slack curling back
   into it; pick crossways in his teeth, ears laid back.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat out and side-on, legs at maximum stretch, denim pressed flat to him, ears pinned,
   eyes wide with white showing all round, pick in his teeth. Baked in tight to the body:
   warm amber amp-glow streaks off his flanks, a few orange sparks off his heels, two
   small sound-wave rings overlapping his hindquarters, and the coil throwing one short
   snapping loop of slack — no long line behind him.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   STANDING frame, not a run: reared on his hind legs with both feet planted flat and the
   whole body vibrating in place, a paper cup gripped in one flat paw, the other paw
   frozen mid-windmill on an air guitar, jaw clamped on the pick, fur bristled, pupils
   shrunk to pinpricks. Pale blush-cream steam shivers off the cup, short orange fire
   licks at both heels and jitter lines hug his outline — he can hear the entire song at
   once and he is not moving an inch.

SHEET 6b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared on his hind legs holding the empty pizza box open at his chest like a collection
   plate, mouth open in an astonished laugh, one hind foot lifting as the pull takes him.
   Gold bottlecaps and two spare picks spiral in on a tight bright-amber ribbon that hugs
   the box rim — every loose piece overlapping his silhouette — tugging the bandana
   sideways as they arrive; his own pick stays under the knot.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Reared on his hind legs holding the battered guitar-case lid over him at a three-
   quarter TILT, no more than about 1.3x his own width, so his face, bandana and one
   raised brow read clearly underneath and rain sheets off the near edge only. Wordless
   sticker shapes on the case, smug little line of a mouth, bone dry, cable still coiled
   on his shoulder and the pick under the knot.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Reared and crouched behind the tilted case lid with both flat palms braced against it,
   one eye screwed shut, all three pins rattling on the denim as the hit rings the case
   like a gong. A blue-violet energy dome flares close around him with rain streaking off
   the curve, one white shock ring and a thin feedback shimmer at the contact point —
   nothing dark violet, nothing reaching the frame edge.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Reared up on his hind legs, both flat paws clutched to his chest over the vest, ears
   flat, eyes huge and fixed up-screen, whole body leaning back from whatever is lining
   him up, bandana flopped half over one eye. The pick has just slipped from his teeth and
   hangs an inch from his nose, still overlapping his face — it never leaves him.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting to the left, mouth wide open in panic, vest flapping, one flat paw clamped
   over the bandana knot so he doesn't lose it and the other pinching the pick he snatched
   back out of the air mid-stride. The cable stays coiled with one short whipping loop of
   slack, warm orange-red panic streaks come off his heels and two small exclamation marks
   sit tucked close beside his ear.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Reared tall on his hind legs, head thrown back, mouth open in a joyful whoop, one arm
   up (elbow bent, so the frame stays square) with the pick pinched glinting between two
   claws, the other fist punched against his chest, vest thrown wide and bandana snapping.
   A warm amber amp-glow halo sits close behind his shoulders with three small sound-wave
   rings and a tight scatter of gold sparks, all inside the idle envelope.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back, all four legs loose in the air with the palms open, vest splayed
   around him like a burst cushion, bandana over one eye, mouth open in a soundless ow,
   eyes gone to swooning half-moons — never X's — under a ring of circling yellow stars.
   All three pins stay on the denim with one swinging on its clasp, the pick spins in the
   air just above his belly, and one hind foot is still tapping out the four-count,
   because the set isn't over.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his rump with his legs out in front, wound ankle to shoulder in his own amp
   cable like a maypole gone wrong, bandana spun round so the knot sits at the back of his
   neck, stubby tail flat under him. Spiral eyes (spirals belong to dizzy alone — no stars
   in this frame), tongue out one side, ears pointing two different directions, one pin
   swinging and the pick stuck flat to his damp forehead.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Caught in the instant of the burst: both flat palms up, soaked through, fur spiked,
   denim gone dark and heavy, bandana clinging to his throat and the pins dulled to grey,
   eyes shocked wide and mouth open. A white-and-cream crown of water explodes around his
   middle with droplets frozen mid-air, and one paw still holds the pick clear above it —
   the only thing worth saving. He does not sink.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND, IN ANY OF THE EIGHTEEN. The cutter floods magenta and keeps every non-magenta
island, so wet pavement, puddles, cast shadows, the old greasy paper plate in eat and the
two pins that used to 'wobble to a stop on the asphalt' in ko all survive the cut as
floating grey scum under a character with no floor. Contact effects that touch him are
correct and wanted: grit bursting off both palms in land, sparks off the heels, the splash
crown at his waist.
• THE PRACTICE AMP IS CUT. It was a torso-sized object present in only two of eighteen
frames, so his apparent size changed in exactly those two — sit now runs on the cable coil
in his lap, eat balances the pizza box on his knees against his chest. Set pieces are
rationed and always in contact with the body: pizza box in eat and magnet, case lid in
umbrella and shield, nothing else, ever.
• ENVELOPE. Every frame including its effects must land between about 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and
inside roughly 1.2x the idle box (the hero's own idle is 192x221). The five hazard cells are
leap, magnet, umbrella, shield and cheer — trailing cable, bottlecap swirl, case lid and
raised arm all want to blow the crop, and a bigger crop paints a smaller raccoon. The one
checkable test: his head is the same pixel count in all eighteen.
• THE CABLE IS A COIL, NOT A LINE, AND THE PICK NEVER LEAVES HIM. Travel frames (leap,
run-r, run-l, dash-run, flee) get the coil on the shoulder plus one short whip of slack,
half a body long, curling back into the coil — a long trailing line defines the crop and
shrinks him in the frames the player sees hundreds of times a run. Pick ledger: under the
bandana knot (idle, magnet, umbrella, shield), in his teeth (sit, crouch, run-r, run-l,
dash-run, coffee), behind his ear (eat, leap, land), hanging by his nose (scared), pinched
in claws (flee, cheer), spinning above his belly (ko), stuck to his forehead (dizzy), held
clear of the water (splash).
• DETACHED BITS AND MARGINS. Spare picks, bottlecaps, sparks, the falling pick and the
exclamation marks must each sit within half a body width AND overlap his bounding box, or
the connected-components cut emits them as their own sprite. Nothing touches a frame edge or
a divider line — draw the dividers first, then paint with a visible margin inside them.
Paint idle, leap and cheer FIRST and send those three for a 59px lane test before committing
the other fifteen.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately — the three pins on the left lapel, the off-
centre bandana knot and the cable coil on the right shoulder all jump sides under the
engine's mirror; in a true run-l the coil is the near-side read and the pins are largely
hidden behind the far shoulder, which a flip cannot fake.
