================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

First Frost Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on
the rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Upright bipedal at rest — he stands, waits and gestures on his hind legs (idle, sit, eat,
  coffee, magnet, umbrella, shield, scared, cheer), and drops forward onto his ONE free
  front palm only for the six travel frames (crouch, leap, land, run-r, dash-run, flee); the
  cup paw never touches the ground in any of the eighteen, so he is never four-square.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all 18: the red hand-knit scarf, double-wrapped at the throat with its fringed end down
  his viewer-LEFT chest, and the kraft-and-white takeaway coffee cup with a brown lid,
  always in his viewer-RIGHT front paw and always physically touching his body — the only
  frame-only additions anywhere on the sheet are the donut (eat) and the small slate-blue
  umbrella (umbrella), both held against him.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  Jimothy's body canon holds exactly: round and spherical, no visible neck, head perched
  high above the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a SHORT STUBBY ringed tail — never a long
  streaming trash-panda tail in any of the eighteen. He is plantigrade — flat palms, five
  long spread fingers, heel down — and never knuckle-walks, in the crouch, the land or
  anywhere else. Because one front paw is permanently occupied by the cup, every two-paw
  pose the hero solves with two paws (cheer, magnet, umbrella, shield, splash) is re-solved
  here with one free paw plus the scarf and the jaw, and the answer is always the same
  character truth: he protects the coffee first and himself second. The scarf is his second
  limb — it streams, catches, wraps and covers an eye — and the breath fog is his signature
  effect, faint at rest and heavy under exertion.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 5a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing upright and settled on his hind legs, front three-quarter high angle, scarf
   double-wrapped with the fringe down his viewer-left chest, stubby ringed tail curled
   out to his viewer-left, free paw hanging relaxed, cup up at shoulder height in the
   viewer-right paw. Eyes squinted content against the chill, one faint white breath plume
   at the muzzle, pale blush steam rising no higher than his ears — this is the existing
   painting and it is the master for camera, scale, lighting and costume.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat back on his haunches with the stubby tail curled around his own feet like a lap
   blanket and the scarf loosened one turn so it pools in his lap, both paws finally
   cradling the cup together under his chin, eyes half closed, steam and breath merged
   into one soft plume across his face. The only frame where he gets to hold it properly,
   and he is in no hurry — nobody is expecting him anywhere this early.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting, cup hugged low and upright against his belly in the cup paw (it never leaves
   his body), dunking a half-eaten old-fashioned donut into it with the free paw, cheeks
   packed, eyes shut in total bliss. Sugar already dusted down the red knit and a fat puff
   of breath fog blooming around the donut.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Compressed low on all threes, hind legs folded, the free palm flat and spread on
   nothing — plantigrade, five long fingers, heel down — while the cup paw stays up and
   out at shoulder width, rock steady in a barista carry so nothing tips. Head low, ears
   back, eyes locked forward, scarf bunched at the jaw, one tight jet of breath from the
   corner of his mouth and a crackle of frost crystals ONLY where the palm presses; no
   pavement, no shadow.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Airborne in a compact arc, belly toward camera, hind legs tucked rather than trailing,
   free paw thrown forward and the cup paw bent so the cup rides dead level no higher than
   his ears — not a drop moving, and he knows it. Mouth open in a delighted hup, scarf
   curling in one short red S behind his shoulder and two beads of breath at the muzzle;
   nothing trails further than half a body width in any direction.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Free palm and both hind feet slamming down, knees folding deep to absorb it, body low,
   the cup swung in against his chest with the coffee crested up at the lip and not one
   drop over. Whiskers forward, eyes wide, scarf flopped up past his cheek, one breath
   punched out of him, and a tight collar of frost dust bursting right at the paws — the
   grit touches him, and there is nothing painted on the ground below it.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on to the right in a low fast lope, hind legs at full extension, stubby ringed
   tail level behind him, cup carried forward and perfectly level in the lead paw like a
   lantern. Scarf streams back in a curling banner no longer than his own body, breath
   comes off as three short beads over his shoulder, expression cold-eyed and businesslike
   — he has somewhere to be and it is thirty-four degrees.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same rightward sprint flat out, but the crop held tight: cup pulled hard in against
   his chest under the free arm (he protects it), scarf curling in one short whipped loop
   instead of a rigid banner, pale cold-vapour speed streaks only half a body long. Eyes
   huge, mouth open, breath reduced to three short beads at the muzzle and frost glitter
   flicking off the heels where they break contact — aspect must not exceed 1.35:1.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   STANDING FRAME, held up to seven seconds: hind feet planted flat and dead still, every
   hair bristled, cup tipped to his mouth mid-gulp, ears bolt upright, pupils shrunk to
   pinpricks — the whole animal vibrates without travelling an inch. Baked in: short
   jitter lines tucked either side of him, a dense pale blush plume no taller than his own
   head, and two tight vapour rings hugging his shoulders that lift the scarf off his
   chest.

SHEET 5b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Standing, cup held high and safely out of traffic in the cup paw while the free paw
   pulls the scarf end out and open like an apron basket to catch the haul. Gold
   bottlecaps and fries spiral in tight — the nearest already touching the scarf hem, two
   ringing off the lid — mouth open and eyes wide with pure astonished delight, a laughing
   puff of breath; nothing orbits further than half a body width.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing under a small folding umbrella in pale slate-blue, canopy half-open and tipped
   forward into the weather, foreshortened so it is no wider than his shoulders plus a
   head, held in the free paw with the cup in the other and its steam pooling under the
   canopy like a small warm room. Sleet beads ice along the rim and bounces off short of
   the frame edge; chin up, eyes half-lidded, scarf hanging dead still in the dry air —
   the smuggest raccoon in Seattle.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Hunched and braced at the instant of the hit, knees bent, free paw flung up across his
   face, the cup hauled into his chest and hugged — he is shielding the coffee, not
   himself, and nothing spills. A blue-violet dome (never blue-violet) flares tight to his
   silhouette with a hot white impact ripple where it was struck, sleet skating off it,
   eyes screwed shut, teeth gritted, scarf blown flat, one shocked plume squeezed out.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his toes, ears flat, head craned back and up at whatever is lining up above him,
   eyes enormous with the whites showing, free paw clutched to his chest over the scarf
   fringe and the cup paw out no further than his own shoulder width, still perfectly
   level. He is holding his breath, so instead of a plume there are two short frozen-
   vapour exclamation marks sitting within a head's height of his ears and overlapping his
   silhouette.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting flat out in full panic, mouth wide open, eyes rolled back over his shoulder,
   hind legs at full stretch, stubby tail straight out, scarf whipping across in front of
   his own chest rather than out of the cell, cup clutched hard against his ribs. The one
   frame where the coffee genuinely loses three or four dark drops — painted overlapping
   his body — with pale blue-white panic streaks half a body long and frost spray only
   where his heels break contact.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Standing tall on his hind legs, head thrown back, mouth wide open in a shout, free arm
   punched up but bent so the paw clears his crown by no more than a head, cup raised to
   eye level in a full toast. Scarf loops up behind him in a short red arc, steam and
   breath pop together in one dense low burst just above his head with frost sparkles held
   inside half a body width, and the first low sun of the morning rakes warm across his
   chest and muzzle — light on the fur, not a painted beam.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with his legs in the air, scarf fallen loose around his throat and
   back over his ears, mouth open in a soft ahh, tongue just showing, eyes gone to
   swooning half-moons with stars circling close above his head. The cup landed upright
   and leaning against his cheek, lid still on, still gently steaming — the coffee is
   completely fine and he is not, and he looks quietly proud of that; one last breath
   plume drifts off sideways.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his haunches and wobbling, spiral eyes, tongue out, ears at two different
   angles, the scarf wound right around his head by the sweeper brushes so it covers one
   eye and the fringe hangs over his nose. The cup is still gripped but upside down, the
   last of the coffee draining in one thin dark thread straight down his own muzzle and
   chest — no puddle, nothing on the ground — with stars circling above and wobble arcs
   tucked either side.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: caught inside a cold blue-white crown of water with cracked
   skim-ice at its rim, fur plastered flat, free paw flung up, scarf curling wet around
   his throat inside the crown, eyes wide and mouth a small shocked oh — soaked and
   clearly out of play, nobody going under. The cup is still held as high as one arm
   reaches with its lid popped off and one fat icy drop landing in it; keep the crown low
   and tight so his body mass stays at the centre of the painted content.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• CROP HAZARDS ARE THE TWO PLUMES. The pink cup steam and the white breath fog are the
character, but they are also what shrinks him: cap both at roughly his own head height, keep
the scarf curl and every speed streak inside half a body width, and leave a clear margin so
no plume, scarf tail or streak touches a divider line or a frame edge. Every frame, effects
included, sits inside about 255 x 270 px at aspect 0.80:1 to 1.35:1 — the same envelope as
the existing idle.png (255 x 270, 0.94). The checkable test: his HEAD is the same number of
pixels in all eighteen.
• NEVER PAINT THE FLOOR. No pavement, no frost sheet, no puddles (including the one deleted
from dizzy), no cast shadows, no curbs, no scattered crumbs — the cutter floods magenta from
the border and every one of those survives as floating grey scum under a character with no
ground. Frost crackle, frost dust, spray and grit are allowed ONLY where they touch a paw or
a heel.
• COLOUR LAW, MEASURED OFF THE MASTER. The knockout eats the magenta family, and idle.png's
steam sits at roughly 240/150/200 — it survives only because its green channel is up at 150.
Paint the cup steam as pale blush-cream with green at 155 or above, never a saturated hot
pink, and paint the shield dome BRIGHT blue-violet.
• NO LETTERING ANYWHERE, PROPS INCLUDED. Blank kraft cup with a wordless band or ring, plain
umbrella, plain donut, no logo on the lid. run-r is the frame that mirrors, and text flips
into gibberish and is illegible at 59px regardless.
• THE COFFEE NEVER SPILLS, AND THE CUP NEVER LEAVES HIM. Only flee (three or four drops,
painted overlapping his body) and dizzy (draining upside down onto his own muzzle) break the
gag — do not add a spill anywhere else, including the leap, the land and the shield hit. The
cup must physically touch him in all eighteen; in ko it leans against his cheek, because a
detached prop cuts out as its own sprite or inflates the crop and shrinks the animal.
• WHAT HAS TO READ AT 59px: the red scarf mass, the kraft cup, the black bandit mask. Keep
the scarf a single saturated red block rather than fringe detail, keep the cup silhouette
clear of the scarf so the two do not merge into one shape, and keep the stubby tail short in
every frame — a streaming ringed tail is a different raccoon and it also blows the envelope.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately — the cup lives in his viewer-RIGHT paw and
the scarf fringe hangs down his viewer-LEFT chest, so a mirror swaps both and on an epic
skin the coffee visibly changes hands mid-run.
