================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Barista Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Upright bipedal in all 18 — plantigrade with flat palms, five long spread fingers and
  heels flat down, he stands, crouches, leaps, lands, runs and flees on his hind feet only;
  he is never four-square and never knuckle-walks.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In every single frame: the green apron with leather shoulder straps crossing LEFT over
  RIGHT and a brown patch pocket, one kraft-sleeved takeaway cup with a plain white lid that
  lives either in the RIGHT paw or stowed upright in that pocket, and a stub of pencil
  behind the LEFT ear — no other props ever, no tray, no lettering or logo on anything (the
  coffee frame is the one licensed exception: a second identical cup in the left paw).

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is hero Jimothy's body in an apron: round and spherical with no visible neck, the head
  perched high above the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a STUBBY tail that never streams,
  curls out behind or flicks a rope of water — it is a short stub in all eighteen frames.
  Because the stance lock is upright, the poses originally designed as three-point quadruped
  scrambles become two-foot squats, two-foot jumps and a hind-leg sprint, with the stubby
  tail and the free left arm doing all the counterweighting. The cup permanently occupies
  his right hand, so any pose that needs both hands stows it upright in the apron pocket —
  it never goes on the ground, because there is no ground. Hands and feet stay plantigrade
  in every frame: flat palms, spread fingers, heels down, never knuckles.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 14a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing upright and square on flat hind feet facing camera, apron hanging straight,
   straps taut, stubby tail behind, the cup level in his right paw at chest height with
   one thin curl of steam going straight up. One eyebrow raised and a small closed-mouth
   smile that says he already knows what you are going to order and he has opinions.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   The quiet one: sat back on his haunches, apron pooled over his knees, the cup cradled
   in both paws against his belly, ears relaxed, eyes half closed and pointed at nothing
   in particular. He opened at five, the coffee has gone lukewarm, and nobody has come in
   yet.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat with a fat almond croissant gripped in both paws, cheeks packed, eyes shut in pure
   relief, flakes and powdered sugar caught on the front of the apron and none falling
   loose. The cup is stowed upright in the brown apron pocket, lid on and still steaming,
   because he saves the coffee for after.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Coiled low in a two-foot squat, heels flat, knees to his chest, apron crumpled against
   his shins, ears back, eyes locked forward on the gap he is about to take. The cup is
   held in close beside his ribs under the free left paw, dead level and kept inside his
   outline so nothing sticks out sideways.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne off both feet, body curled forward, hind legs tucked, apron pressed flat
   to his belly, whiskers swept back, mouth open in delight. The cup is punched forward
   just past his chest, dead upright, the coffee inside not even reaching the lip —
   nothing trails behind him.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Both hind feet planted hard with knees deep and the body low, apron swinging forward
   past his knees, a tight spray ring of rainwater bursting off his soles and touching
   nothing else. The cup arm has snapped up level with his shoulder to keep it steady, and
   one single bead of coffee hangs suspended just above the sip hole, about to drop back
   in.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Hind-leg sprint to the right, body tipped forward, long skinny legs at full stride,
   ears pinned, eyes on the crossing, apron flat against his chest and the stubby tail
   counterweighting. The cup rides level in the leading right paw held in close to the
   chest, its steam trailing straight back in one calm ribbon — the only calm thing in the
   frame.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Same hind-leg sprint tipped further over with a longer stride, ears blown back, eyes
   wide and pupils shrunk to pinpricks, warm orange and gold fire streaks licking tight
   off his heels. Same single cup, still level in the right paw close to the ribs, its
   steam ripped into one hard straight ribbon behind him — no tray, no second prop,
   nothing overhead.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   The money frame and he is not moving an inch: planted facing camera in a wide heels-
   down stance, fur bristled out into a halo, ears bolt upright, pupils shrunk to
   pinpricks, jitter lines doubling his outline, an identical cup gripped in each paw at
   chest height with his face left completely clear. Pale blush-cream crema steam boils up
   no more than one head above his crown, warm gold fire licks close around his feet, and
   the apron strings float straight out on the updraft.

SHEET 14b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Standing upright with his free left arm thrown wide open, mouth open in undisguised
   delight and eyes shining — the best tip-jar night of his life, and it is all coming to
   him. The cup stays resolutely level in the right paw while bottlecaps, sugar packets
   and wooden stir sticks spiral in on a warm gold ribbon tight against his chest and arm,
   every one of them overlapping his body, the apron pocket already sagging full of caps.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing dry and insufferably smug under a small striped café umbrella whose shaft he
   holds in the left paw, the canopy sitting close over his head and no wider than his
   shoulders. Rain streaks off the fringe in short strokes that stop dead at the hem and
   never reach anything below, cup level in the right paw, ankles crossed, eyes half
   lidded, apron spotless — he has all day and you do not.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Braced in a half crouch with one eye shut and the left paw thrown up, a bright blue-
   violet energy dome flaring close around him and rain streaking off its curve in hard
   bright arcs. The right paw has clamped the cup flat against the apron bib, and the
   coffee in it is still perfectly level.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Upright and leaning back, ears flat, eyes huge and white-rimmed, both paws pulled in
   tight to his chest with the cup pinned upright between them. He is craning up and back
   at whatever is lining up above him, and the steam off the lid is being blown flat
   sideways across his own face by the dive.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Bipedal panic sprint to screen left, mouth wide open, ears back, apron flapping up
   around his ribs, warm orange speed streaks kept tight behind his heels and one small
   exclamation mark tucked in beside his ear. His trailing right paw still holds the cup
   out level and unspilled behind him, because the coffee is the very last thing he is
   willing to let go of.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Standing tall with heels down and chest out, apron straps proud, ears straight up,
   mouth open in a whoop, free left paw punched out to the side. The cup is raised in the
   right paw only to just above his ear, not to full stretch, and a small firework of gold
   steam pops out of the lid.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with long skinny legs in the air, swooning half-moon lids, tongue out
   one side and a small ring of stars circling his head, the apron flopped back over his
   chest and the pencil still jutting up from behind his left ear. The cup has come down
   upright and square on his belly, lid on, one thin curl of steam rising — and under the
   daze he looks quietly vindicated.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side with spiral eyes, tongue out, ears at two different wrong angles,
   the apron twisted round backwards over his hip and the pencil hanging half out from
   behind his left ear — spirals only, no stars anywhere in this frame. The cup is
   balanced improbably upright on the crown of his skull, gently steaming, which somehow
   makes it worse.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: soaked through and plastered dark, mouth open in shock and
   eyes wide inside a tight crown of bright blue-violet water exploding up around his
   knees, apron slapped flat up his chest with one strap flying. His right arm is punched
   straight up out of the spray, holding the cup bone dry and dead level above the entire
   disaster.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND, EVER — no pavement, puddle, wet smear, beaded rain, cast shadow, curb or loose
crumb in any of the eighteen. Every water, fire and grit effect must physically touch him
(spray off the soles, fire at the heels, rain stopping at the umbrella hem) or the magenta
flood cuts it out as floating grey scum under a character with nothing beneath him. The cup
has exactly two homes — the right paw or the apron pocket — and never sits on the floor.
• ENVELOPE, the one checkable rule: every frame between about 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and inside
roughly 1.2x the idle box. The old full-arm's-length cup carries (crouch, run-r, flee) and
the raised cup in cheer are what blow it — keep the cup arm at half extension against the
ribs, keep the coffee plume to one head above the crown, keep the umbrella canopy no wider
than his shoulders. His HEAD must measure the same number of pixels in all 18; if an effect
shrinks the head, the effect is too big.
• NO LETTERING ON ANYTHING. The kraft sleeve, the white lid, the apron, the pocket and the
pencil carry texture only — no café logo, no chalk scrawl, no printed cup, no numbers. It is
illegible at 59px and it mirrors into gibberish.
• THE THREE ANCHORS ARE IN ALL 18 — apron, cup, pencil behind the LEFT ear. Where a frame's
text does not explicitly move them they stay in their locked home positions; a frame missing
one reads as plain Jimothy. At 59px the readable silhouette is three blobs: solid saturated
green apron against his grey, the kraft cup, and an unshaded white lid — keep the lid clean
and bright or the cup reads as a paw. Keep the cup the SAME cup at the SAME size in every
frame; it is the gauge the eye uses to check his scale.
• LAYOUT AND TEST ORDER: rows with white divider lines and clear gaps, never a butted even
grid, and nothing touching an edge or a divider (the ko sprawl and its star ring are the two
most likely to drift into the gutter). Paint idle, leap and cheer first and cut them at 59px
on a real lane before committing the other fifteen.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately, never mirrored: he is asymmetric three ways
at once — cup in the RIGHT paw, pencil behind the LEFT ear, apron straps crossing left over
right — so a mirror jumps the pencil to the other ear, swaps the strap cross and moves the
cup to the wrong hand mid-run; paint run-l as the run-r sprint reversed with all three
anchors staying exactly where the wardrobe lock puts them.
