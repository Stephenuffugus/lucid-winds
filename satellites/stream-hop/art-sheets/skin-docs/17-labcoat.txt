================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Jimothy MD — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the rainy
streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Plantigrade raccoon quadruped — flat palms, five long spread fingers, heels down —
  travelling on all fours in crouch, land, run-r, dash-run and flee, but reared up on
  planted hind feet whenever he is at rest, because the clipboard permanently occupies a
  forepaw and he is therefore NEVER standing four-square.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all 18 frames: rain-spotted warm off-white lab coat worn OPEN and unbuttoned (never
  fastened, never buttoned shut), narrow blue tie, stethoscope round the neck with both ends
  hanging down the chest, brow mirror centred on a headband, two pens in the coat's breast
  pocket, and the wooden clipboard with its metal clip and wordless chart pages carried in
  the LEFT forepaw — the pen comes out into the right paw in idle and sit only, and the pens
  leave the pocket in flee only.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  Identical body to the hero, so every pose ports one-for-one: round spherical torso, no
  visible neck, head perched high on the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a SHORT STUBBY
  ringed tail that stays close to the body and never streams, curls out behind or flicks
  water in any of the eighteen. The job costs him a limb, so the clipboard is a permanent
  extension of the left forepaw and is re-staged in every frame — lap desk when he sits,
  clamped under the elbow when both paws are needed, pressed flat beneath the palm on all-
  fours frames, riot shield when he is hit — which frees the right paw to do whatever plain
  Jimothy's right paw does. Because a paw is always occupied, he is never four-square: at
  rest he rears up on his haunches with both hind feet planted, and he gallops three-legged
  with the chart pinned to his ribs. The brow mirror, stethoscope and tie ride the head and
  neck and supply the trailing motion the hero gets from his whiskers and tail.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 17a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Reared up on his haunches with both hind feet planted and the round body square to
   camera, clipboard held flat against his chest in the left forepaw, capped pen loose in
   the right. Calm, unhurried, faintly bored bedside face, ears easy, stubby tail tucked
   close; the one hard white glint on his brow mirror is the brightest note in the frame.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat back on his haunches, coat hanging open around his belly, clipboard flat across his
   knees as a lap desk with the pen working down a row of wordless ink squiggles. Head
   tipped, eyes half-lidded, ears relaxed — the exact posture of a man doing his paperwork
   on a bus bench because there is nowhere better to be.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat on his haunches with the clipboard clamped under his left elbow against his ribs so
   both forepaws are free around a half-unwrapped deli sandwich, cut on the diagonal by
   somebody at home. Cheeks packed, eyes shut in bliss, brow mirror slid crooked down over
   one eye and no attempt made to fix it.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on four flat palms with heels down and haunches loaded, the clipboard
   pressed flat beneath his left forepaw, coat rucked up over his shoulders. Stethoscope
   swung forward and hanging clear under his chin, whiskers back, eyes locked forward down
   the street, absolutely about to go.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne and stretched long, belly forward, hind legs trailing, clipboard thrust
   out ahead in the left paw like a doctor twelve minutes late to rounds. Coat tails and
   stethoscope stream back close behind him, tie flipped up over his shoulder, stubby tail
   tucked, and he looks openly delighted about it.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front palms slapped down wide and body low, the clipboard taking part of the weight
   flat under the LEFT forepaw, coat billowing forward past his shoulders from the stop.
   Ears up, whiskers forward, a small spray of wet grit bursting off the palms and
   touching them.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on three-legged gallop to the right: the near left forepaw pins the clipboard to
   his ribs the way a chart gets carried down a corridor while the far forepaw and both
   hind feet do the work. Coat hem flaring back, stethoscope swung out on the curve, mouth
   shut, eyes on the far side of the street.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat-out horizontal sprint, legs stretched fore and aft, coat streaming straight back,
   brow mirror flashing, eyes wide with pinprick pupils. Orange fire licks at his paws and
   exactly ONE chart page tears back over his stubby tail, still caught in the clip and
   overlapping his body, with speed streaks no longer than half a body.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   Feet planted and body vibrating in place, standing bolt upright, fur bristled all over,
   a cafeteria paper cup in the right paw trailing pale blush-cream steam and the
   clipboard clamped under his left arm. That free left paw has the stethoscope bell
   pressed to his own chest and his eyebrows are climbing at what he can hear; pinprick
   pupils, short jitter lines tight to the body, small orange fire licks at his feet — all
   the energy, none of the travel.

SHEET 17b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Standing tall on his hind feet, mouth open in an astonished delighted grin, clipboard
   held up flat in both forepaws like a serving tray while bottlecaps, fries and wrapped

   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing on his hind feet under a black umbrella hooked in the crook of his right

   inside the coat against his chest and held there by his left forearm where the
   paperwork will stay dry. Rain breaking off the rim in short grey strokes that stop well
   inside the frame; utterly smug, utterly dry, one eyebrow up at the weather.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Dropped onto his haunches with the clipboard snapped up flat in front of his face like
   a tiny riot shield and the free right paw braced behind it, one eye squeezed shut,
   teeth gritted. A BRIGHT CYAN-WHITE energy dome flashes tight around him with rain
   streaking off its curve and two chart pages blown up off the board, still tethered at
   the clip.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his hind feet, clipboard clutched flat to his chest in both paws like a shield he
   has not thought to raise, head tipped back to watch the sky. Eyes huge and white-
   rimmed, ears pinned flat, brow mirror tilted up and catching the light, stethoscope
   swinging loose off his neck.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Bolting three-quarter away toward the lower left, mouth open in flat panic, clipboard
   tucked under his left arm like a football, coat unbuttoned and flapping wide behind
   him. Red speed streaks off his heels and exactly two pens shaken out of the breast
   pocket, both close in and overlapping the flying coat so they cut as one shape.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Up on his hind feet, clipboard punched up in the left paw to just above the crown of
   his head and the right forepaw fisted at shoulder height, mouth wide open in a joyful
   whoop, eyes shut with the size of it. Brow mirror catching a star of light and one
   chart page fluttering up off the board, still clipped at its corner.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with all four long limbs in the air, coat splayed open around him,
   stethoscope tangled comically across his snout, stubby tail pinned beneath him. The
   clipboard has landed face up on his belly and the trace on the chart dips flat then
   kicks straight back into a fat healthy bounce — one eye cracked open reading it with an
   eyebrow up, the other a dazed half-moon, gold stars circling his head.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled sitting up crooked with his tongue out and both eyes drawn as full spirals,
   the clipboard fallen flat across the top of his head at an angle like a slipped roof
   tile, its edge down over one spiralled eye. Headband shoved right round so the brow
   mirror sits behind one ear, chart pages fanned loose but still caught in the clip with
   one slid down over that ear, one forepaw half-raised in apology — spirals only here, no
   stars.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Soaked through and shocked rigid inside a bursting white and pale-cyan crown of water
   at his hips, both forepaws thrown up, coat plastered translucent to his fur and the
   brow mirror running with drips. The clipboard is still up in his left paw and its chart
   has gone to grey pulp, peeling off the board in one sad wet curl but never leaving it —
   he is far more offended about the paperwork than about the water.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND, ANY FRAME. No pavement, puddles, damp smears, cast shadows, kerbs, drains, skid
marks or pages scattered on the road — all of that survives the magenta cut as floating grey
scum under a character with nothing beneath him. The only legal ground-adjacent effects are
the ones TOUCHING him: grit bursting off his palms in land, fire licking at his paws in
dash-run and coffee, the water crown at his hips in splash.
• ENVELOPE, MEASURED FROM THE HERO'S OWN 20 FILES: 180-236 px wide by 174-224 tall, aspect
0.81 to 1.33. Every frame including its effects stays inside that, and the checkable test is
his HEAD — it must be the same number of pixels in all eighteen. If an effect makes the head
smaller, the effect is too big. The engine centre-anchors, so keep his body mass at the
centre of the painted content: no tall stack of content above him in cheer or scared,
nothing spread below him anywhere.
• TETHER LEDGER. Loose chart pages appear in exactly four frames — dash-run (one), shield
(two), cheer (one), splash (one peeling) — and every single one stays physically caught in
the metal clip and overlapping his body. Flee's two pens overlap the flying coat so they cut
as one shape. Nothing floats free, nothing sits further than half a body width out, and
nothing touches a frame edge or a divider line; leave a clear magenta margin all the way
round every cell.
• COLOUR LAW. No magenta or hot pink anywhere in any effect — the knockout eats that family
and leaves ragged bites. Coffee steam is pale blush-cream with the green channel up, the
shield dome is blue-violet like Jimothy's own. Keep the coat a warm off-grey-white rather
than pure white so it does not blow out against the rain, with a fine warm-grey ink outline
all the way round the coat edges so the cutter has an edge to find.
• NO LETTERING ANYWHERE. Chart pages are wordless rows of ink squiggles plus one wavy trace
line; no name tag, no badge, no print on the coat, no numbers on the mirror. The draft's
floating red '!!' marks in flee are CUT — they are detached islands that inflate the crop
and push him off his lane line; the panic reads from the open mouth, the flat ears and the
heel streaks instead.
• READ AT 59px. The silhouette cues are the round no-neck body, the hard white glint on the
head-high brow mirror, and the pale wedge of the open coat. Keep the stethoscope a dark hard
line against that coat and keep the clipboard warm wooden brown against the white — never
white-on-white, or both props vanish at lane size. He carries no scale key, so he must sit
at exactly the hero's height in the lane, coat and all.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately: the clipboard rides the LEFT forepaw, and in
run-r that is the near, camera-side arm pinning the chart to his ribs — mirroring throws the
clipboard, the breast-pocket pens and the three-legged gallop to the far side of the body
and the whole doctor read collapses.
