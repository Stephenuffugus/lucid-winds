================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Skunk — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the rainy
streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadrupedal plantigrade at rest — heel down, flat palms, all four feet on the ground — and
  he rears back onto a haunches-plus-tail tripod whenever a frame needs front paws; he never
  stands upright without that rear-up.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  None — no costume and no carried prop in any frame; the tail is his only permanent
  equipment. The four frame-local props are: takeaway box + grub (eat only), gold bottlecaps
  and fries (magnet only), paper coffee cup (coffee only), folded thrift-store umbrella
  (umbrella only) — they appear nowhere else.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is a short-legged plantigrade quadruped with no true hands, so every pose that needs
  arms is solved the way a real striped skunk solves it: he rears back on his haunches into
  a tripod of two hind feet plus the tail and works with two dextrous front paws. The great
  plume is the third limb and does the jobs Jimothy's arms do — parasol, windbreak, cushion,
  banner, backstop — but it is NOT prehensile: it fans, arches over the back, bottle-brushes
  and drapes, and it never grips, wraps, carries or scoops anything. He is famously near-
  sighted, so the squint is always aimed slightly wide of whatever he is actually looking
  at. Standing rule: four-square only when both front paws are empty and on the ground (idle
  only); any frame with a prop or a raised paw must be sat back on the haunches or reared on
  the hind legs.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 18a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square at three-quarter camera, glossy black guard hairs beaded with
   rain, the double white stripe running clean from forehead over the back, the great
   plume carried high like a parade banner and curling forward past his ears. Small amber
   eyes in a polite near-sighted squint, a faint contented smile, and nothing at all under
   his paws — no puddle, no reflection, no shadow; this is the existing idle.png and his
   head size here is the measuring stick for the other seventeen.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat back on his haunches, back rounded, front paws tucked into the white chest fur,
   eyes half-lidded and aimed slightly wide of whatever he is watching — the near-sighted
   stare at a blur of lights he cannot quite resolve. The plume has swept down and forward
   to lie against his forefeet like a dropped stole, so he is sitting warm inside his own
   tail while the rain keeps coming down; this is the sheet's quiet, slightly lonely beat.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat up on his haunches with a rain-softened takeaway box tipped open between his hind
   feet, fries ignored, one fat pale garden grub held in both front paws like corn on the
   cob, eyes shut in genuine bliss. The plume arches over his back and curves down behind
   the box as a windbreak so the rain cannot get in — considerate, slightly gross,
   entirely him.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, chest almost to the ground but with nothing
   painted beneath it, whiskers forward, eyes locked up-screen. The tail is pressed low
   and long behind him like a loaded spring with the plume tip waved up to stay inside the
   box, and his forepaws blur mid-drum in the little warning stamp a skunk does before
   anything happens (existing crouch.png).

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne and stretched, all four short legs spread wide, belly forward, mouth
   open in delight, near-sighted eyes as wide as they go. The plume streams back and then
   CURVES UP behind him into a tall banner arc — tip painted, never cropped, never
   stretched flat across the cell — so the frame stays the same 207x250 portrait as idle
   and he does not shrink for the middle of every single hop.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front paws planted with a tight splash ring bursting off the pads and up his forelegs —
   contact spray only, no puddle and no ring on the floor — chest low, haunches still
   dropping, weight visibly absorbed. The tail has whipped forward over his own back and
   overshot past his nose, plume fanned across his face, so he is momentarily half-buried
   in his own tail and squinting out through it.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on to the right, body low and stretched long, short legs at full extension, the
   plume held back and waved up in a lazy S so it climbs inside the box rather than
   lengthening the cell. In profile the forehead stripe and one amber eye carry the
   identity, and his face wears a small determined scowl, because he is not fast and he
   knows it.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat out and side-on, legs at full stretch and barely touching down, ears pinned, eyes
   huge, tongue out the side of his mouth, the plume swept back and curved up into a white
   torpedo with short speed lines that stop well inside the margin and two loose plume
   hairs tumbling in the streak. A soft pastel mint-and-cream clover puff blooms at the
   tail's base like a little booster — sparkly, aimed back at empty road, never yellow-
   green or brown, never a cloud or a jet, and nothing in frame reacts to it.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   Reared up on his hind legs and DEAD STILL, both feet planted flat, a paper cup clamped
   in both front paws, coffee slopping over the rim onto his own wrist, steam curling off
   in pale blush-cream (never hot pink). Every hair of the plume has stood on end and
   bottle-brushed to twice its width with jitter lines ticking off it, pupils shrunk to
   two dots, two small warm-orange fire licks curling off his hind feet — all the energy
   is vibration and bristled fur, no speed lines, no travel.

SHEET 18b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Sat up on his haunches with both front paws cupped open at his chest, mouth open in
   pure astonishment and delight — things are coming TO him and he cannot believe his
   luck. The plume is fanned wide and vertical behind him as a catcher's screen: gold
   bottlecaps and fries fly in from just inside the frame, stick in the fur, bounce off it
   and cascade down the plume into his paws — the tail is a backstop, never a basket, and
   nothing collects on a floor because there is no floor.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Sat up on his haunches with the whole plume arced forward over his head as a fat white
   parasol, rain sheeting off it in bright ribbons and not one drop reaching him. A cheap
   folded thrift-store umbrella is tucked unopened under one front paw, purely for show,
   and his squint is enormously smug about owning two roofs.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The instant of the hit: flinched down flat, ears back, eyes squeezed shut, one front
   paw thrown up over his face. A blue-violet energy dome snaps tight around him — never
   blue-violet or purple, which the cutter eats — dented outward at the crown where the
   bolt-upright plume shoves against it, rain streaking hard down the outside.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   The real skunk warning display: front half dropped low, back arched high, all four feet
   down, both forepaws blurred mid-stamp in a panicked drum roll, plume snapped bolt
   upright and fanned into a full flag. His head twists up and back but the near-sighted
   squint is aimed at the wrong patch of grey sky, one ear cocked, a single fat painted
   exclamation mark tucked against the plume so it overlaps his silhouette, and the
   faintest pastel mint sparkle at the tail base.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting left, low and scrambling, mouth open in panic, wet fur plastered over eyes
   gone huge, a tight burst of water flicking off the pads themselves (on the paws, never
   on a floor) and short warm orange-red speed streaks stopping inside the margin. The
   plume drags in a wobbling panicked wave instead of a proud banner, because for the
   first time in his life the thing behind him is not getting out of his way.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Reared up on his hind legs, compact rather than stretched, both front paws flung up
   beside his head, eyes squeezed shut in a huge grin, chest stripe blazing. The plume
   fans straight up into a white firework and the whole celebration stays INSIDE that fan
   — pastel mint and cream clover puffs and sparkles held within the plume, none of it
   near the frame edge — so the cell keeps the 207x250 box and his head stays exactly
   idle-sized.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with all four short legs sticking straight up, tongue lolling to one
   side, one ear folded over, eyes closed into swooning half-moon lids (never X's) and
   three little stars circling his head. The great plume has spread flat beneath him like
   a fur fainting couch so he has landed in completely unearned luxury, rain falling
   gently on his upturned white belly — suspiciously comfortable, funny-sad, never grim.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled belly-down and splayed out like a dropped rug, spiral eyes, tongue out, stars
   wobbling around his head. The plume is bent up above him into a lazy question mark and
   his back is dusted with swept-up leaves and wet grit that all sit ON the fur — nothing
   scattered on the ground, and no loose hairs drifting free in this frame.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: front half heaved up inside an exploding crown of water, both
   forepaws thrown skyward, mouth open in shock, eyes enormous, soaked through and plainly
   out of play. Behind him the glorious plume has collapsed into a sad wet rope a third of
   its size with a few bubbles off the tip — the ruined tail is the joke and the small
   heartbreak — and there is no separate water plane or surface line painted below him.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• CANVAS AND SCALE (this replaces the old note entirely). All 18 frames go on the same
207x250 portrait cell as the four existing files — aspect 0.83, well inside the 1.35 cap and
safely on the height-sized 59px branch, matching Jimothy's own envelope of 180-236 x
174-224. The old instruction that 'his cells are simply bigger than the other critters'
cells' is WRONG and will halve him: every frame is normalised on its own, so a bigger cell
shrinks the animal. His body and head are painted at one constant size in all eighteen and
the TAIL arranges itself to fit the box — it never sets the box. The single checkable test
is that his head is the same pixel count in every frame.
• NO GROUND PLANE, ANYWHERE, IN ANY OF THE EIGHTEEN. Struck from the old sheet: the wet
gutter reflection under idle's paws, and any pile of caps at his feet in magnet. There is no
pavement in a sprite — puddles, reflections, cast shadows, curbs, litter rings and scattered
props all cut out as floating grey scum under a character with no floor. Contact effects
that touch his body are correct and wanted: the splash ring bursting off his pads in land,
spray off the paws in flee, the water crown around his body in splash, grit and leaves
sitting on his fur in dizzy.
• COLOUR LAW. The knockout eats the magenta family, so: shield dome BRIGHT blue-violet,
Against that, near-black fur is the other real hazard — keep a thin warm rim-light along the
top of the guard hairs in all eighteen so the silhouette survives a dark lane, and keep the
white stripe crisp. The stripe and the plume are the only two things a player actually sees
at speed.
• THE SPRAY GAG appears in exactly three frames and nowhere else: the booster puff on dash-
run, the confetti burst on cheer, and the faintest hint at the tail base on scared. Pastel
mint and cream only, shaped as clover puffs and sparkles — never yellow-green, never brown,
never a cloud, never a jet, never a stink line. It is always aimed behind him at empty road,
and no bystander, hazard or object ever reacts to it. It is fireworks, not weaponry: he is a
shy, polite animal who simply has the whole lane to himself.
• CENTRE-ANCHORED, AND NOTHING TOUCHES AN EDGE. The engine centres the sprite on the lane
point, so the body's mass must sit at the centre of the painted content in every frame —
this matters most where the plume goes straight up (coffee, scared, cheer) and where water
goes up (splash), all of which will otherwise push him below his lane line. Keep a clear
magenta margin all the way round every cell, including full clearance for the tail tip,
which is never cropped; speed lines, sparkles and incoming magnet items all start inside the
frame and overlap his silhouette, never at or beyond the divider lines.
• FOUR FRAMES ALREADY EXIST AND ARE GOOD — idle, crouch, leap and land at 207x250. The
fourteen new ones must match their camera, lighting and scale exactly; if you would rather
repaint, repaint all eighteen in one pass and never mix an old scale with a new one on the
same sheet. Paint idle, leap and cheer first and send those three for a 59px lane check
before committing the other fifteen. Note for the build: run-l ships mirrored, so the
engine's mirror fallback must be enabled for full 18-frame characters or every leftward hop
falls through to the procedural blob.
