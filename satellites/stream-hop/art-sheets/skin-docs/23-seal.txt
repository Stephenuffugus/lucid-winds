MAKE TWO SHEETS FROM THIS DOCUMENT:  23a  and  23b

================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Harbor Seal — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Pinniped, not quadruped and not biped: belly-down on his ribs in every frame, chest lifted
  only by the arch of his own spine, back half a heavy sausage that never leaves the ground
  except in leap, ko, dizzy and splash.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  None — he wears nothing in any frame; the only constants are bare silver-grey spotted
  hide, cream belly and a rain sheen along the spine. Every prop is single-frame and named
  in that frame: chip basket (eat), takeaway cup on the nose (coffee), flying chips and gold
  bottlecaps (magnet), black umbrella bitten by the shaft (umbrella, and the same umbrella
  blown inside out in shield). Nothing else appears anywhere.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He has no hands and no hind legs, so every raccoon solve is done with the belly: he
  balances things on his nose instead of holding them, pins things under a flat flipper
  instead of gripping, and rears only from the ribs up. LOCKED FLIPPER LAW — the fore-
  flippers reach OUT and DOWN only: they steady him at his flanks but never bear his weight
  (the chest comes up off the spinal arch, never off a propping flipper), never rise above
  the shoulder line, and never touch the face. When he is inverted in ko and splash, "out
  and down along the body" points at the sky, and that is the only way a flipper is ever up.
  He can never stand four-square and can never hold a prop in a limb.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 23a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Three-quarter to camera with the rear half foreshortened away so his box stays near
   square: chest lifted on the arch of his spine, fore-flippers lying flat at his flanks,
   head high and tipped a fraction back, big black wet eyes on the lens, whiskers relaxed.
   Rain sheen down the spine and the belly visibly flattened where it meets the ground —
   nothing painted underneath him, no smear, no puddle, no shadow.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Fully off duty in the harbour-seal banana, head and rear flippers lifted together so he
   rocks on his ribs, one fore-flipper laid low across his belly, eyes half shut and mouth
   curved in a small closed smile. This is the quiet one: he is looking past the camera up
   the canal at a boat that already went by, and the waiting is as real as the
   contentment.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Head thrown back mid-gulp with a battered cod half swallowed and its tail still out of
   his mouth, a red-and-white paper basket pinned flat under one fore-flipper against his
   chest — matchbox-sized against a seal — and one chip stuck to a whisker. A hard-edged
   gull-wing shadow falls across his own back and across the paper (no gull, and no shadow
   on the ground); he is side-eyeing it without stopping.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Poured flat and loaded forward: spine compressed into a low hump behind the shoulders,
   chin almost on the deck, fore-flippers flat and turned in at his sides steadying rather
   than pushing, rear half foreshortened toward camera, eyes locked up-screen. A tight fan
   of spray flicking off the outer edge of each fore-flipper where he has already begun to
   shove — touching him, nothing on the road.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Airborne and briefly beautiful: body arched into a shallow banana at three-quarter,
   fore-flippers swept tight to his sides, rear flippers trailing and fanned open, belly
   forward, mouth slightly open in delight. A compact burst of water breaking off his own
   belly and rear flippers with every droplet touching that mass — no trail, nothing
   reaching for the frame edge.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Belly-first touchdown, chest low, shoulders taking the whole weight, fore-flippers
   splayed out and down for balance, a ripple travelling backwards through his blubber,
   whiskers forward, eyes squeezed half shut. A low contact crown of water bursting at the
   impact and clinging to his chest, kept inside the same box as idle.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on three-quarter travelling right mid-galumph: fore-flippers planted flat and
   turned out, spine humped into the inchworm arc, rear flippers whipping forward to catch
   up, head craned round just enough for the camera to get his face. Spray flicks off the
   flipper edges only — no drag streak, no wet road, and nothing asymmetric anywhere on
   him, because this frame gets mirrored.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same galumph flat out — inchworm arc snapped straight at full extension, flippers
   blurred, eyes wide, mouth open in a bark of pure commitment. Warm orange-and-white
   speed streaks laid along his flanks and belly so they touch the hide, plus one short
   tight burst of water off the rear flippers; no rooster tail, no long trail.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   He is NOT moving: planted dead still at three-quarter, chest up on the spinal arch, a
   lidded takeaway cup balanced perfectly on the very end of his nose because he cannot
   hold one, pupils shrunk to pinpricks, whiskers buzzing, mouth a tight flat line. Pale
   blush-cream steam off the lid (never pink), short warm fire licks and jitter ticks
   hugging his flanks — all the energy in the fur, none of it in travel.

SHEET 23b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Pure astonished delight: reared up from the ribs, mouth wide open, catching one hot
   chip clean out of the air, eyes enormous and lit up like the world just started
   working. Chips, fries and gold bottlecaps spiral inward on a tight orbit that overlaps
   his body all the way round, each piece touching the next so the ring cuts as one mass;
   no live fish, no spilled basket, nothing on the ground.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   A big black umbrella bitten by the SHAFT just under the canopy and laid back along his
   own spine so it rides over his shoulders like a lean-to roof, handle dangling past his
   chest; it covers his head and shoulders and comes nowhere near his back half, which is
   getting hammered. He is propped at three-quarter under the front rim looking straight
   down the lens, half-lidded and deeply smug — he does not need it and he knows it — rain
   sluicing off the canopy behind him, no dry patch, no ground.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The hit lands: a bright blue-violet dome flares tight around him (never violet, never
   dark), and he turtles — chin jammed into his chest, neck compressed to nothing,
   whiskers flattened back, eyes screwed shut, fore-flippers clamped flat to his flanks.
   The umbrella is still in his teeth and blown clean inside out, ribs snapped upward into
   a bowl above him — the prop never leaves his body, and the whole thing stays inside the
   idle envelope.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Reared as high as a seal can rear: chest and neck stretched straight up while the back
   half stays a heavy sausage, foreshortened toward camera to keep the box near square.
   Head cranked back at the sky, eyes enormous, whiskers splayed, fore-flippers pinned in
   tight and low against his chest — only the top third of him is physically capable of
   panic.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Galumphing away in complete undignified terror, spine buckling in a big travelling
   wave, blubber a beat behind the motion, one rear flipper flailing clear, mouth open in
   a bark, head glancing back over his shoulder. Warm red-orange speed streaks painted
   onto his flanks and two small wordless shock marks tucked in close behind his head so
   they overlap his silhouette and survive the cut.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   The money shot — the one job his body is better at than anyone else's on the roster:
   reared up tall on his belly, head thrown back, mouth wide open barking with joy, both
   fore-flippers caught mid-clap LOW across his chest at belly height where a seal
   actually claps, never overhead. Water flicks off the flipper tips and a couple of gold
   sparks are caught between them, all of it touching him, and the reach stays inside the
   idle box so his head does not shrink.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Rolled onto his back on the road with all four flippers hanging out and up — inverted,
   so that is simply out-and-down along his body — tongue out the side, eyes closed into
   swooning half-moons (never X's), a ring of stars circling his head with each star
   touching the next so the ring cuts as one piece. One rear flipper crossed lazily over
   the other: he looks suspiciously comfortable, as though the nap was always the plan.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Belly-down and splayed, turning slowly flat on the spot like a compass needle that has
   lost north — he is a torpedo, never a coin balanced on edge — spiral eyes, tongue
   lolling, whiskers bent the wrong way. Motion arcs curl off both fore-flipper tips with
   a short spray ring flicking off them and a few sweeper bristles and grit grazing his
   flank, everything touching him; nothing whatsoever painted on the road.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Buoyant and furious: rolled onto his back inside a bursting white-blue-grey crown of
   water and carried off sideways at speed, whiskers bent back, one fore-flipper folded on
   his chest and the other flung out in a can-you-believe-this appeal to nobody, wearing
   an expression of enormous personal offence — this half of the map was supposed to be
   his. He rides high in the froth the whole time, mass centred, crown kept tight and well
   clear of every frame edge.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• ENVELOPE, the one checkable rule: match the four existing frames at 219x178 (aspect 1.23).
Every frame including its effects must land between 1.0:1 and 1.3:1, and his HEAD must be
the same number of pixels in all 18. Stage him three-quarter with the rear half
foreshortened toward camera in every frame — a flat side-on seal reads 1.7:1 and the engine
then draws him 74 wide by about 43 tall while cheer draws 59 tall, so his head visibly
changes size mid-animation.
• NEVER PAINT THE FLOOR — this is the single biggest hazard on this character. No damp smear
under the belly, no drag streak behind the galumph, no skid marks, no dry patch under the
canopy, no ground-level gull shadow, no puddle, no cast shadow. The cutter is a magenta
border flood and every one of those survives as a detached grey stain floating under a
bottomless seal, and it drags the centre-anchor so he lifts off his lane line. His wetness
reads through the sheen ON his hide and the flattened blubber contact line. Contact spray
that touches his body is correct and wanted. The engine already draws its own ellipse shadow
under him in drawCritter — a painted one double-stacks.
• Detached bits — droplets, bottlecaps, sparks, stars, grit, shock marks — must sit within
half a body width, overlap his bounding box, and chain to him or to each other. The cut is a
connected-components pass: a loose speck either disappears or is emitted as its own sprite
file.
• COLOUR: the knockout eats the magenta family, so no pink steam (paint it pale blush-cream
with the green channel high) and Water is white-blue-grey. At 59px his silver-grey hide is
almost exactly the colour of wet asphalt, so the cream belly and a bright rim light along
the spine have to be present in all 18 or he vanishes into the lane.
• LAYOUT: divider lines, never an even grid. Rows of three with generous white rulings and a
visible margin inside them — nothing touches a divider or a frame edge, including the
umbrella canopy, the inside-out umbrella bowl and the splash crown. His long silhouette will
overrun any fixed grid and the cutter will slice him in half.
• ENGINE ASKS for the owner: set scale:1.10 on the seal entry in CHARS (index.html:715) —
painting him bigger on the sheet does literally nothing, because each frame is normalised
independently. Add full:1 only once all 18 land, and make sure the mirror fallback for full
characters is in before that, or every leftward hop drops to the procedural blob. The four
existing frames (idle, crouch, leap, land) are the reference for camera height, hide colour,
spot pattern and spray language; match them exactly.
• Paint idle, leap and cheer first and send those three for a 59px cut test before
committing the other fifteen — they are the three most likely to blow the envelope.
• Left-facing run: mirror
