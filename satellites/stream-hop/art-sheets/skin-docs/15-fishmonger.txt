================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Fishmonger Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on
the rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Plantigrade quadruped at rest — four-square on flat palms and heels-down hind feet with
  the stubby tail low; he rears onto his haunches or hind legs ONLY in sit, eat, coffee,
  magnet, scared and cheer, and never knuckle-walks.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all 18 frames, no exceptions: the slate-blue waxed bib apron (neck strap over the head,
  waist ties knotted at the back, one patch pocket low on his RIGHT side, ragged wet hem,
  zero lettering) and the whole silver market salmon — carried three ways only, clamped
  crosswise in the jaws, cradled across both flat palms, or laid across his lap or chest.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  This is Jimothy's own body, so every hero pose transfers one for one: round and spherical
  with no visible neck, head perched high above the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a
  STUBBY ringed tail that never streams, curls out behind him or flicks a rope of water. He
  is plantigrade — flat palms, five long spread fingers, heel down — never knuckles. The one
  real constraint is the salmon, which is nearly as long as he is tall: in every jaws-carry
  frame it is angled three-quarter with the nose swung toward the lens and the tail swung
  back past his shoulder, so it foreshortens to roughly his own shoulder width instead of
  doubling his silhouette and shrinking his head. A frame cannot be four-square and also
  hold a prop, so any frame needing hands is written as haunches or hind legs, and every
  jaws-carry frame keeps all four paws free to be down.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 15a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square on flat palms and heels, blue bib apron hanging straight, stubby
   ringed tail low, chin lifted in calm quiet pride. The silver salmon is clamped
   crosswise in his jaws but angled three-quarter — nose swung toward the lens, tail back
   past his shoulder, coral stripe turned to camera — with one bead of water hanging off
   its tail fin, not yet fallen.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   THE QUIET FRAME: up on his haunches at the end of market day, shoulders dropped, apron
   rucked at the waist, the salmon laid across his lap with one flat palm resting on its
   flank the way you rest a hand on a sleeping animal. Half-lidded eyes aimed off past the
   camera at nothing, nobody left to holler at, rain beading on his ear tips — and no
   puddle, no wet floor, nothing painted under him.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting up on his haunches with the salmon tucked along one forearm and pinned under
   the elbow, stripe out — he is absolutely not eating that — while the free paw tips a
   paper cup of clam chowder to his mouth and oyster crackers stick to the apron bib. The
   fish's blank glass eye happens to line up on the cup, deadpan, as though it would also
   like some.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, apron bunched under his belly, the salmon held
   forward in his jaws at the same three-quarter angle like a bowsprit aimed at the far
   kerb. Brow down, eyes locked ahead, two ice chips flicking off the fish's tail and
   dying within a finger's width of it — nothing floating loose out at the side of the
   cell.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, belly forward, long skinny hind legs trailing, apron ties whipping
   back, stubby tail tucked, salmon locked crosswise in his jaws at the three-quarter
   angle. Eyes wide with joy and ears laid back in a grin around the fish, with a tight
   comet of water beads flung off its tail that stops within half a body width of him.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Flat palms slammed down and fingers splayed, elbows buckling, body low through the
   shoulders, apron flaring, the salmon still gripped crosswise in his jaws with its snout
   dipped from the impact. A contact burst of spray and three ice chips fires up off his
   own palms and touches his fur; one eye is cut sideways to check the fish is still
   there, and there is no water painted on the ground.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on sprint to the right, body long and low, heels striking flat, apron ties
   streaming back and the stubby tail bobbing level behind him. The salmon rides dead
   level in his jaws like a relay baton and his head is held unnaturally steady so it
   cannot wobble, with spray kicking only off the pads of his own paws.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Flat out, all four legs at full stretch, ears pinned, eyes wide and glassy, the salmon
   clamped level in his jaws with its nose knifing forward. Warm-white speed streaks rake
   close past his flanks and a spray of ice chips comes out of the apron pocket, every
   piece of it overlapping his body.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   HE IS NOT MOVING — planted up on his hind legs with both heels flat and knees locked,
   apron hanging straight, vibrating hard enough to blur his outline: paper cup in one
   paw, salmon jammed in the crook of the other elbow with the stripe to camera. Fur
   bristled into spikes, pupils shrunk to pinpricks, short jitter lines hugging his edges,
   pale blush-cream steam off the cup and warm orange fire licks at his own back heels.

SHEET 15b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared up on his hind legs in astonished delight, apron swinging, the salmon held up
   across both flat palms at chin height like a tray being presented, mouth open and eyes
   huge and shining — things are coming to him and he cannot believe it. Gold bottlecaps,
   stray fries and ice chips spiral in tight toward the fish on a warm amber trail, every
   piece inside half a body width and overlapping his silhouette.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing four-square and enormously smug with the salmon still crosswise in his jaws;
   the umbrella's crook is hooked over the apron's NECK strap behind his skull so the
   shaft rides up his back and the small canopy opens above and slightly behind his head,
   tilted back so it foreshortens no wider than his shoulders plus a palm each side. Rain
   slides off the canopy rim in a short curtain that stops at his shoulder line — head fur
   dry and fluffed, paws soaked, and absolutely no dry circle or wet patch painted on the
   pavement.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Crouched low on all fours and curled bodily around the fish, one shoulder into the
   blow, apron bunched tight, protecting the salmon instead of his own face with one eye
   screwed shut and teeth gritted around it. A blue-violet dome flares close around them
   both with rain streaking off it in pale sparks.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his haunches and hunched small, ears flat, apron bib crumpled, the salmon
   clutched upright to his chest in both flat palms like a swaddled baby with its stripe
   out, eyes huge and aimed straight up. A gull's shadow slides across his own face and
   apron — painted on him, never on the floor — with three panic droplets flicking off his
   ear tips.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Full-panic three-legged scramble to the left, mouth open in a yelp, apron flying, the
   salmon tucked under one arm like a football with a flat palm locked over its nose. Warm
   red speed streaks crowd in close at his heels and two hard exclamation strokes sit just
   above his ears overlapping his outline — nothing is dropped and nothing is left behind
   him.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Reared tall on his hind legs, apron swinging, both arms flung up in the full Pike Place
   throw caught EARLY — the salmon has only just left his palms, angled up and back with
   its tail fin still overlapping his fingertips so the whole silhouette stays one
   connected piece. Mouth open in a joyful holler, eyes tracking the fish, gold sparkles
   and a few ice chips bursting close around his hands, his body mass still dead centre of
   the painted content.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back with the long skinny legs loose in the air, apron flopped askew across
   one shoulder, gold stars circling close above his head and his eyes gone to swooning
   sideways half-moons. The salmon has landed alongside his cheek, nose to nose and
   touching him, blank-eyed and utterly deadpan, and his whole expression is a small
   apology to the fish.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side with spiral eyes and tongue out, apron flopped over one ear, stars
   wheeling close above him. The salmon has somehow come to rest balanced upright on his
   belly, standing on its tail and wobbling, touching him the whole time — perfectly
   ordinary, perfectly dead-eyed, no spirals and no stars of its own.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of shock inside an exploding crown of grey-green water bursting at his own
   body: arms up, apron plastered flat, ears streaming, eyes shocked wide, thoroughly out
   of play. Both palms hold the salmon up clear and dead level through the chaos, stripe
   to camera and entirely unbothered, and the crown stays tight to his shoulders so his
   body mass sits centre-frame.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND PLANE IN ANY OF THE 18. The old sit puddle, the run-r wake splashes, the
umbrella's dry circle of pavement, the flee dropped glove and the ko bottlecap are all
deleted — they cut out as floating grey islands under a character with no floor. Spray is
allowed only where it touches him: off his palms in land, off his paw pads in run-r, and the
crown at his body in splash.
• ENVELOPE, THE ONE CHECKABLE RULE: his HEAD must be the same number of pixels in all 18
frames. Every cell including its effects sits between 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and inside roughly
236 wide by 224 tall of content — the hero pack's own range, and the four already-painted
Fishmonger frames (idle, crouch, leap, land) are the calibration. Sprites are CENTRE-
anchored, so keep his body mass at the centre of the painted content in cheer and splash
rather than adding headroom above him.
• DO NOT COMPONENT-CUT THIS SHEET. Lay it out on white divider lines with a clear magenta
margin all round every cell, and nothing may touch an edge or a divider. The circling stars
(ko, dizzy), the magnet spiral, the speed streaks and cheer's sparkles are separate islands;
a connected-components cutter drops them. Every one of them stays within half a body width
AND overlaps his bounding box.
• COLOUR LAW: the knockout eats the magenta family, so no hot pink or magenta anywhere in
the FX. Coffee steam is pale blush-cream (green channel high), the shield dome is blue-
violet like Jimothy’s own and.
• THE FISH IS A PROP, NOT A CASTMATE. The market salmon is chrome-silver with a blue-green
back and a coral lateral stripe turned to camera in all 18 (edge-on it reads as a grey club
and the joke dies): straight body, straight jaw, blank glass eye, no lure. It must never be
mistaken for the playable Sockeye (crimson, hooked kype, brass lure), and it is never
bitten, wounded, bloodied, or given eyes, expressions or stars of its own — deadpan straight
man throughout. It leaves his grip in exactly one frame, cheer, and even there its tail
overlaps his fingers.
• NO READABLE TEXT ANYWHERE — no price cards, no apron print, no market signage, no
lettering on anything. Ice-chip and water debris is capped to land, dash-run and cheer only;
the held frames (ko, dizzy, splash) stay clean of debris so nothing reads as damage.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately: the apron's patch pocket sits low on his
right side and the waist-tie knot and salmon carry are asymmetric, so a mirrored run-r jumps
the pocket to the other hip mid-hop.
