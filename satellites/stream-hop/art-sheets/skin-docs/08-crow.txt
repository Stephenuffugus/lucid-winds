MAKE TWO SHEETS FROM THIS DOCUMENT:  8a  and  8b
Nine frames on each sheet, eighteen in total. Label the files exactly 8a and 8b.

================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Ballard Crow — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Ground-standing two-legged bird — horizontal body carried level over two scaly feet,
  three-quarter to camera facing right, hopping rather than walking, wings acting as arms
  and never as hands; he is never "four-square" and never stands upright like a raccoon.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  No costume and no permanent prop — nothing is worn in any frame; the only constants are
  black plumage with an oil-slick blue-green sheen, shaggy throat hackles, a cool pale blue-
  grey rim light down the top and back edge, and the full-saturation gold-amber eye on the
  camera. Frame-local props exist in exactly six frames and touch his body in every one: the
  dull gold bottlecap (sit, magnet, dizzy, splash), the paper fry carton (eat), the torn
  takeaway cup (coffee), the found umbrella (umbrella), the single chip (shield).

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  A crow has no hands, so the bill and one pinning foot do every job Jimothy's paws did —
  nothing is ever held in a free limb; props are stood on, mantled over, or gripped in the
  beak. The wings are the arms but they only travel up, out, and forward-mantled over the
  crown: a wrist may fold across the top of the head, but a wing never crosses the chest,
  never points, and never carries. The legs bend the bird way — the visible joint is an
  ankle that folds backward — so he can loaf, squat and fold his feet up beside his own
  flanks, but he can never stick straight human legs out in front of him. He stands on two
  feet, so "four-square" applies to no frame on this sheet, and the gold-amber eye finds the
  camera in all eighteen, including the two where he is on his back.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 8a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing squarely on both feet, three-quarter to camera facing right, wings folded
   tight so the oil-slick blue-green sheen runs across the coverts and the nape hackles
   sit slightly ruffled. Head level, beak straight out, gold-amber eye locked on the
   viewer — calm, unhurried, absolutely taking notes; nothing whatsoever painted beneath
   the feet, not asphalt, not shadow, not grit.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sunk into a loaf on nothing at all: feet vanished up under the belly feathers, body one
   rounded black cushion, neck gone into the shoulders, rain beading and running off his
   back. Sheltered under his own breast is a single dull gold bottlecap he is keeping dry
   — his entire fortune — and one half-lidded gold eye still finds the camera. This is the
   quiet, sad frame of the sheet.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Head cranked low over a spilled paper fry carton with one scaly foot planted flat on
   top of it, pinning it the way crows actually do, three fries already jammed crosswise
   in the bill while he tugs a fourth free. Every loose fry stays touching the carton
   mouth or his own toes — none scattered on the ground — and one gold eye rolls up to the
   camera, daring you to come closer.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Body dropped horizontal over deeply flexed legs, plumage slicked tight so the
   silhouette narrows to a loaded dart, tail angled down, wings cracked open a hand's
   width at the wrists. Head thrust forward and low, beak level, eye wide and fixed dead
   ahead: all spring, no lift yet.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Airborne and climbing, wings beating down and forward in a tight half-spread with the
   primaries fanned but the whole span held inside about 1.4x his body height, feet folded
   back under the tail. Chest thrown toward the camera, beak slightly parted, gold eye on
   the viewer — no trailing feather, no spray, nothing left behind him.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Wings cupped hard forward to brake with the tail fanned wide beneath as an airbrake,
   both feet thrown out ahead and the toes spread to grab. Head already coming back up and
   the eye forward on the next row, with a small tight crown of water droplets bursting
   right at the toes — contact spray touching the feet only, no puddle and no cast shadow
   under him.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Mid two-footed side-hop travelling right, both feet together and just clear, body low
   and level, wings tucked but flicked half-open at the wrists for balance with the tail
   flared to steer. Head stays square to the camera even as the body travels sideways —
   cocky, watching you the whole way across; nothing asymmetric anywhere in the frame so
   this one mirrors cleanly.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   A grounded full-tilt bounding scramble — both feet slapping forward together, wings
   hammering a hard downbeat that half-lifts him and never quite gets him up, body
   stretched long and level, tail flared for steering. He does NOT fly: the joke is a city
   crow too heavy with chips to get airborne, so wings stay short of full extension, spray
   is two small bursts at the wingtips and feet, and streaks run no longer than half his
   body.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   Feet planted and going absolutely nowhere, one foot clamped on the rim of a torn-open
   takeaway cup tipped in against his chest, bill up, beak parted, wired to the eyeballs
   and holding still. Every feather bristled, hackles standing straight up, pupil pinned
   to a black dot inside the gold iris, jitter lines shivering off the whole body and pale
   blush-cream steam curling tight off the cup — warm cream, never pink.

SHEET 8b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Wings tented forward and down in the full crow mantle, but the face is pure
   astonishment — beak wide in a delighted caw, both gold eyes huge, head lifted: things
   are coming TO him and he cannot believe his luck. Fries and gold bottlecaps arrive in a
   close ring that overlaps his wings and body, on warm gold-cream trails no longer than
   half a body width; nothing streaks in from off past the edges.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Standing bone dry and deeply smug on one foot with the other tucked up into the belly,
   feathers perfectly smooth, under a blown-out found umbrella whose bent ribs lean
   against his own shoulder — the prop touches him, and there is no curb and no ground
   anywhere. Canopy tilted low and back so his body stays the centre of mass, faded teal-
   and-ochre fabric (never pink), rain running off the rim in short strings well inside
   the frame, head tilted at the camera: yes, this is mine now.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   One wing hooked up and over the crown as a roof of feathers while the OTHER stays
   mantled down over a single chip he flatly refuses to abandon — this is the crow's own
   solve, never the two-wings-over-the-face the other birds use. A bright blue-violet dome
   flares tight around him with rain streaking off the curve, two or three loose black
   feathers knocked free and hanging over his own outline, one gold eye glaring out from
   under the wrist; feet braced, no skid marks, no road.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Crouched low with the head cranked straight up at the sky, beak wide open in an alarm
   caw, nape and throat hackles flared into a spiky ruff. Wings half-lifted off the body
   ready to bail, tail clamped hard down, both eyes huge — a crow being bullied by a bird
   with worse manners.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Panicked half-flying scramble travelling left: feet still pedalling, wings beating
   badly out of sync, body tipped forward past its own balance, beak open mid-caw. Warm
   red-orange speed streaks kept to half his body length behind him and a thin trail of
   three or four shed black feathers overlapping his own outline — no floating symbols, no
   exclamation marks, no lettering of any kind.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Head thrown all the way back, chest arched forward, beak wide in a rattling caw with
   the shaggy throat hackles flared out into a beard — the crow's own cheer, not the
   gull's shriek and not the pigeon's balloon. Both wings go UP but crooked hard at the
   wrist so the tips stay inside about 1.15x his idle height, tail fanned, one foot lifted
   in a little hop, and two loose feathers popping free BELOW the wingtips so nothing adds
   height.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   On his back and rolled slightly toward the camera, legs bent with both tarsi folded to
   one side and the toes curled loose in the air, one wing flopped up across his own face
   because he cannot bear to be seen like this. The other wing lies limp on the near side,
   beak open, and one gold eye peeks out from under the wrist while pale stars circle the
   head — dazed and mortified, wounded pride only; no X eyes, no spirals, no loose
   feathers.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sat back on his rump with both legs bent the bird way — tarsi folded so the feet come
   back up beside his own flanks, toes curled loose, knees buried in the belly feathers —
   and both wings drooping off the shoulders like empty sleeves. Head lolling onto one
   shoulder, beak hanging open, gold eyes spiralled, and three tiny gold bottlecaps
   wobbling in a close orbit around his head instead of stars; no wing over the face, no
   loose feathers.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Caught in the instant of the burst — a white and pale-blue crown of water exploding up
   around him, wings out and up in an appalled shrug, beak wide, both gold eyes outraged,
   soaked to half his own width with the feathers plastered to a scrawny pencil neck and
   knobbly bare legs. One gold bottlecap is still pinched in the tip of his bill, because
   the water got the bird but not the treasure; he is inside the burst and never under it,
   and nothing sinks.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND IN ANY FRAME. Struck from the draft: wet asphalt (idle, run-r), the curb (sit,
umbrella), fries scattered on the ground (eat), puddles (dash-run), skid marks on wet road
(shield), and every cast shadow. The four existing paintings need the same pass —
assets/chars/crow/idle.png has a small grey grit island at the bottom right that will cut
out as its own floating sprite; clean it. CONTACT effects that touch the body are correct
and wanted: the droplet crown at the toes in land, the spray bursts at wingtips and feet in
dash-run, the water crown in splash.
• ENVELOPE AND SCALE. The four existing paintings are all exactly 219x242 px, aspect 0.905,
three-quarter facing right — that box IS the anchor. Paint the other 14 into it and match
camera, lighting and head size exactly, or repaint all 18 in one sitting; never mix two
sittings. Every frame including its effects must land between 0.80:1 and 1.35:1 and inside
about 1.2x that box, and the one checkable test is that his HEAD is the same number of
pixels in all 18. Wingspan capped at roughly 1.4x body height in leap, land, dash-run and
flee; cheer wingtips inside 1.15x idle height. Paint cheer, magnet and umbrella first and
cut-test them at 59px — those three are the envelope risks.
• COLOUR AT 59px. He is a near-black bird on a dark lane, so the cool pale blue-grey rim
down the top and back edge plus the full-saturation gold-amber eye ARE the silhouette;
without them he is a hole in the road. The cutter deletes the magenta family, so: coffee
steam is pale blush-cream with the green channel up around 150+, never pink; the shield dome
is blue-violet like Jimothy’s own.
• LOOSE BLACK FEATHERS are capped at three frames — shield, flee and cheer only. Ko and
dizzy get none: on a frame held 1.55 seconds under a camera lean, drifting feathers
accumulate into a plucked, injured read, which is the opposite of funny-sad. Leap also loses
the trailing feather it had in the draft.
• DELIBERATE RECONCILIATION ON THE TWO DEATH FRAMES. The reviewer notes on dizzy and on ko
both asked for a wing across the face, which would have made the two 1.55s frames the same
silhouette under the same camera treatment. The wing-over-face is assigned to KO alone (on
his back, wounded pride, pale circling stars, one eye peeking); dizzy keeps the corrected
bent-tarsus rump sit with empty-sleeve wings, spiral eyes and three orbiting bottlecaps.
Spirals never appear on ko, stars never on dizzy, and the two silhouettes — a rolled bundle
versus an upright slump — read apart instantly.
• LAYOUT AND ENGINE FOLLOW-UP. Lay out in rows of three or four with hand-drawn dividers and
generous gaps, never an even grid; a shaved primary reads as a broken bird, not a fast one.
Nothing touches a frame edge or a divider, and every detached element (stars, orbiting caps,
streaks, incoming fries) must sit within half a body width AND overlap his bounding box.
Once the art lands, the roster entry at index.html line 699 needs full:1 alongside art:1 or
all 14 new poses fold straight back onto idle/crouch/leap/land — and keep this bird visually
distinct from sprites/haz-crowdive.png, the diving crow hazard.
