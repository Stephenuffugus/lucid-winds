JIMOTHY — SKIN 03: HARD HAT JIMOTHY

MAKE TWO SHEETS FROM THIS DOCUMENT:  3a  and  3b
Nine frames on each sheet, eighteen in total. Label the files exactly 3a and 3b.

================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Hard Hat Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a hand-painted hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadruped at rest — plantigrade raccoon, four flat palms down, heel down; he rears onto
  his hind legs ONLY in coffee, magnet, umbrella, shield, scared and cheer, and never
  knuckle-walks.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all 18 frames: scuffed orange hard hat on a thin chinstrap; hi-vis sash running LEFT
  shoulder to RIGHT hip, two reflective stripes, small brass clip where it crosses; a BLANK
  orange-and-white octagon paddle on a short pole, slung on his back under the sash by
  default (comes into paws only in sit, eat, umbrella, shield, and lies on his chest in ko);
  a dented steel thermos on a short cord clipped at his right hip. The black lunch pail is
  the one extra, in eat only, and it rests on him.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is hero Jimothy's skeleton exactly, so every pose transfers one for one — round and
  spherical, no visible neck, head perched high above the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and
  a STUBBY ringed tail in all eighteen frames (never streaming, never a rope of water).
  Hands are the giveaway: flat palms, five long spread fingers, heel down, plantigrade in
  every frame including the runs. The honest work on this skin is wardrobe physics, not
  anatomy — the chinstrap is what lets the hat survive leap, land, dizzy and splash, and the
  sash is a loose loop that swings and skews but never leaves the left-shoulder-to-right-hip
  diagonal. A frame that needs a paw for a prop must rear him up; four-square means no free
  limb, which is why the paddle rides on his back.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: hand-painted, warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 3a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square facing camera, round and scrunched, weight even on four flat
   palms, stubby ringed tail hanging straight down, no limb free. Hat level and scuffed on
   its chinstrap, both sash stripes catching the streetlight, blank octagon slung on his
   back with the pole angled past his right shoulder, thermos still on its hip cord — face
   calm and faintly tired, a raccoon who has stood in this exact spot for thirty years.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat back on his haunches on nothing — no curb, no kerbstone, no pavement — with the
   paddle pulled round and tucked under one arm so the octagon props behind his shoulder
   like a backrest. Hat pushed back to show the sweat-flattened dent ring in his head fur,
   both flat palms cupping the thermos lid with one curl of pale cream steam; shoulders
   down, eyes soft, off duty for exactly ten minutes.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting with the paddle laid flat across his lap as a tray and the open black lunch
   pail resting on it against his chest, half a wax-paper sandwich held in both palms mid-
   bite, cheeks full. Hat tipped back, thermos lid balanced on the pail lid, one crumb on
   his chin — the joke is that somebody at home still cuts it in half for him.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Flattened low on all fours with his chest almost down and his haunches loaded high,
   palms spread flat, stubby tail low — nothing painted under him. Hat brim shoved down so
   his eyes glint out from beneath it, sash swung forward loose on his chest but still on
   the same diagonal, octagon riding flat on his back, whiskers pinned.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, limbs thrown wide but tucked inside the frame, round belly forward,
   stubby tail flicked up, mouth open in a delighted whoop. The hat lifts a half inch and
   is caught by the chinstrap under his jaw, the sash flags back off his shoulder, and the
   back-slung octagon swings out level with his hip — nothing trails, nothing sprays.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front paws planted hard on flat spread palms with the elbows bent, body low and round,
   stubby tail up for balance, whiskers forward. The hat drops over his brow on impact and
   the sash swings across his chest; a tight puff of grit sprays off his palms and stays
   touching them — no dust cloud, no cast shadow, no gravel lying on a floor.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on hop to the right, spine bowed and compact rather than stretched long, back legs
   extended, one flat palm reaching forward. Hat pinned low over his eyes, sash streaming
   back with the two stripes smearing into a short light trail, octagon riding above his
   hip on his back, thermos bouncing on its cord.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same side-on sprint flat out — legs at full stretch, ears flat, eyes wide, cheeks
   pushed back by the wind, body still round. Hat brim pressed flat, sash horizontal like
   a banner, and short orange-and-white speed streaks plus warm gold sparks starting at
   his hind claws and touching him, none reaching more than half a body width back.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   He is NOT MOVING: reared up on his hind legs with both heels flat and planted, whole
   body vibrating in place, fur bristled, pupils two different sizes, teeth clenched in a
   wired grin. One paw round the thermos lid slopping over the rim, the other clamped on
   the hat brim to hold it down on his shaking head, pale blush-cream steam, short jitter
   lines at his elbows and small warm-gold heat licks at his feet — no lean, no stride, no
   speed streaks.

SHEET 3b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Reared up with the hat lifted off and held upturned in both flat palms as a bucket,
   chinstrap still round his throat so it never leaves him, the sweat-dent ring in his
   head fur finally showing, mouth open in astonished delight. A tight spiral of gold
   bottlecaps and fries pours in and touches his paws and the hat rim the whole way; the
   ones he misses ring off the rim with little warm-orange impact sparks.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Reared up holding the blank orange-and-white octagon flat overhead in both paws with
   the elbows bent so it clears the hat brim by about an inch — never at full arm stretch.
   Short rain ticks tight around him and two proper gutter streams pouring off the hat
   brim; eyes half-lidded, thoroughly smug, because the paddle is decoration and the hat
   is doing all the work.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   The instant of the hit — the blank octagon snapped up flat in front of his chest with a
   bright blue-violet dome flaring off its face, rain and grit streaking around the curve
   and staying inside half a body width. Head turned away, one eye squeezed shut, hat
   knocked down over his brow, one hind heel skidding back: braced, flinching, saved.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Reared up with his head cranked back to the sky, both flat palms clamped down on the
   hat brim because three generations of instinct say the hat handles it. Eyes huge and
   white-ringed, mouth a small flat line, sash jumped up loose on his chest but still on
   the same left-to-right diagonal, thermos swinging out on its cord.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Sprinting to the left in open-mouthed panic, one flat palm jammed on the hat so it
   cannot fly off, the other pumping, hind legs at full stretch. The thermos stays clipped
   and bounces off his hip with a short arc of coffee flicking back onto his own flank,
   short red-orange streaks at his heels and one small white shock-burst at his shoulder —
   nothing dropped, no spill trail, no painted punctuation.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Reared up tall, one arm punched up with the elbow bent so the fist stops just above the
   hat rather than at full stretch, the other paw a fist at his chest, mouth wide open in
   a whoop. Hat knocked back on his head, both reflective stripes lit, octagon riding on
   his back — the road is still never finished, but this bit is.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back, all four skinny limbs in the air, tongue out, eyelids swooning half-
   moons, three gold stars circling close enough to touch his ears. The hat has tipped
   onto its side against his ear and stays touching him brim-up, the sash lies slack
   across his belly, and the octagon has swung round to lie flat on his chest — his face
   says occupational hazard, third time this shift.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side with spiral eyes and his tongue lolling, stars orbiting close
   overhead and overlapping the hat. The hat has been spun clean backwards so the brim
   sits over the back of his neck, the octagon has slid round and lies across his upper
   flank still hooked under the sash, and two stray broom bristles and a little grit are
   stuck in his fur.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: soaked through, both arms up, mouth open in shock, fur
   plastered flat to a much smaller raccoon inside an exploding white crown of water that
   touches him all the way round. Hat tipped back on his crown with water bursting past
   and under the brim so it is still the one dry thing in the picture, stripes glowing
   hard in the dark water, octagon just clearing his shoulder — nothing detached, nothing
   going under.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• NO GROUND IN ANY FRAME. He floats on magenta and is drawn over lane art, so there is no
curb in sit, no pavement under crouch, no gravel scattered by land, no spill puddle behind
flee, no cast shadow anywhere. Contact effects that touch the body are correct and wanted:
grit off the landing palms, sparks off the dash claws, heat licks at the coffee feet, the
splash crown bursting against his fur.
• NOTHING DETACHED, EVER. The cut keeps non-magenta islands, so every loose element must
touch him and sit inside half a body width: ko's tipped hat rests against his ear, magnet's
bottlecap spiral touches paws and hat rim, ko and dizzy stars overlap ears and hat, splash
water touches fur. The thermos stays on its hip cord in all 18 and never tumbles free.
• ENVELOPE AND HEAD TEST. Every frame including effects must land between about 0.80:1 and
1.35:1 and inside roughly 1.2x the idle box; the checkable version is that his HEAD is the
same pixel size in all eighteen. That is why umbrella's paddle sits an inch above the brim
and cheer's fist stops just above the hat instead of at full stretch, and why rain, streaks
and water crowns stay short and tight.
• ZERO TEXT, ZERO LOGOS. The paddle is a BLANK orange octagon with a plain white border ring
on a short pole — no lettering, no numerals, no symbols. Nothing printed on the hat, sash,
thermos or lunch pail either; texture and shape only.
• COLOUR LAW. The knockout eats the magenta family, so no hot-pink or magenta anywhere:
coffee steam is pale blush-cream (green channel high), the shield dome is blue-violet like
Jimothy’s own. The sash cross-clip is small BRASS.
• CROP MARGIN AND SCALE. Leave 8-10px of clear magenta above and either side of the hat brim
(his widest and highest point) and around the raised paddle, the raised fist and the outer
water crown; nothing touches a divider line or a cell edge. Match his SHOULDER height to
hero Jimothy, not his overall height, or the hat makes him tower over the roster. Draw the
divider lines first, uneven rows, generous gutters.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately — the sash diagonal (left shoulder to right
hip), the brass cross-clip, the right-hip thermos and the back-slung octagon all reverse
under a mirror, so the whole costume would read backwards on every leftward hop.
