================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

River Otter — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on the
rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Quadruped at rest — long low spine, short bandy plantigrade legs with flat palms and heels
  down — who rears onto his haunches with the thick rudder tail braced behind as a third leg
  whenever he needs his forepaws.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  No clothing and no costume: one prop only, a smooth green river stone, present in all 18
  frames — held in a forepaw or clamped chin-to-chest when he is still, carried in his jaws
  when he is travelling, and loose at his own hip in flee alone.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He has a raccoon's dexterous forepaws and none of a raccoon's legs: long low spine, short
  plantigrade limbs, and a tail thick enough to work as a third leg, so every raccoon
  standing pose becomes a rear-up onto the haunches with the tail braced behind, and every
  raccoon run becomes the true humpback otter bound or a belly slide. He is four-square in
  idle only, and a four-square frame cannot also hold something in a forepaw — that is
  exactly why the stone rides in his jaws there. There is no armpit pouch and no chest-anvil
  floating on his back: that is a sea otter, and nothing he carries may depend on either. He
  is never dry in any frame — wet fur peaked into dark spikes along the spine, all eighteen.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 20a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Standing four-square, all four palms and heels flat down, long body in a lazy S with
   the thick tail laid in a shallow arc to his right and the green stone carried gently in
   his jaws. Head up, whiskers dripping, eyes soft and a little far away — an otter who
   has been waiting on this corner a while.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sat bolt upright on his haunches, tail flat behind him doing the work of a third leg,
   both forepaws folded on his pale belly cradling the green stone. Shoulders dropped,
   eyes half-lidded, rain running off his chin in a steady thread — no puddle and no
   floor, he is simply soaked and in no hurry to be anywhere.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Hunched belly-down and angled three-quarter to camera, elbows out, hind feet splayed
   behind, tail flat; the green stone is pinned under his left forepaw as an anvil while
   the right brings a mussel down onto it, head cocked to watch the crack. A tipped paper
   tray of fried clams and two shell halves lean against his elbow and shoulder in
   physical contact — nothing loose, no puddle, no pavement painted.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Coiled low with the spine compressed into a tight hump, elbows tucked under the chest,
   hind feet planted wide, chin almost down and eyes locked up-screen. The stone is
   clamped in his teeth ready for the jump, tail laid straight behind him, nothing painted
   beneath him.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Airborne on a rising diagonal toward the camera, body curved like a banana rather than
   stretched flat, forepaws reaching forward, hind feet trailing and the tail curled up
   behind to keep the box square. Mouth open in a delighted hoot, the stone clamped in the
   crook of his left elbow against his chest, a tight shake of water beads coming off his
   fur no further out than a paw's width.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Front paws slapping down flat, spine humping up behind them, hind feet skidding in
   underneath, whiskers thrown forward and the head coming back up. A low crown of rain
   bursts right at his palms where they make contact and the tail whips upward for
   balance; stone still in the crook of his left elbow, no ground and no cast shadow under
   him.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on humpback bound to the right in the true otter rhythm, back arched high at the
   middle, fore and hind paws gathering underneath him, ears pinned flat, tail arced up
   and behind rather than streaming out flat. The stone rides in his jaws on the centre
   line — deliberately, so this frame mirrors clean — with spray flicking off the paws
   themselves and no puddles painted behind.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   Belly slide angled three-quarter toward the camera with the spine curved rather than
   dead straight, forelegs tucked back along his flanks, hind feet still shoving, chin an
   inch off nothing, stone switched into his teeth. Cheeks pushed back, eyes wide with
   glee; wake is two short curls at the shoulders and the speed streaks run no longer than
   half his body — box capped at 1.35:1, run-r's silhouette is the target.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   Standing dead still and vibrating: reared on both hind feet with the tail braced behind
   as a tripod, a takeaway cup already half crushed in both forepaws, the green stone
   clamped under his chin against his chest. Pinprick pupils, whiskers straight out, every
   wet spike bristled, pale blush-cream steam off the lid, short jitter lines hugging his
   outline and small warm heat licks touching his own feet — no fire on the ground, no
   forward lean, no travel in this pose at all.

SHEET 20b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Up on his haunches with the tail as tripod, head back and mouth open in pure
   astonishment, both forepaws juggling three gold bottlecaps in a low arc that rises no
   higher than his own crown — delight at being given things, never appetite. More caps
   and a few fried clams spiral in and touch his chest and shoulders, everything inside
   half a body width; the stone stays clamped under his chin and the tail thumps hard
   enough to fling water off its own tip.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Sat upright on his haunches, one forepaw on the shaft of a small plain umbrella held at
   a lazy tilt low over his left shoulder, the other cradling the green stone in his lap.
   Half his face still gets rained on and he could not care less — eyes lidded, deeply
   smug; one short bright curtain of rain sheets off the canopy rim and stops well inside
   the frame, and there is no lettering or logo anywhere on the canopy.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Dropped low and braced, both flat forepaws up in front of his muzzle, whiskers
   flattened back, eyes screwed shut, tail clamped tight along his own flank. A bright
   blue-violet dome flashes close around him with rain and grit streaking off its skin,
   and the stone is jammed between his chin and chest where he clamped down on it.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Reared into the bolt-upright periscope stand otters really do, hind feet together and
   the tail curved out behind as both tripod and wide base, neck stretched as tall as it
   goes. Both forepaws clutched to his chest around the green stone, eyes huge and rolled
   upward, mouth a small round O, every wet hair on his back standing in spikes.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Panic belly-slide to the left, angled three-quarter to camera with the spine curved,
   forepaws thrown out ahead, mouth wide open, tail thrashing behind. The green stone has
   bounced out of his teeth and tumbles just off his hip, overlapping his own tail —
   clearly dropped but still on him; short white shock spikes and flung water beads only,
   no punctuation marks, no fanned water on a floor that does not exist.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Reared to full height on both hind feet with the tail arced out behind as a broad
   tripod, one forepaw punched up holding the green stone overhead like a trophy, elbow
   bent so the stone clears his crown by no more than a head. Head thrown back, mouth open
   in a whooping chirp, a short bright arc of water flung off the tail tip, belly fur
   gloriously soaked.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back but rolled three-quarter toward the camera so the box stays near
   square, all four short legs in the air, thick tail flopped out flat to one side.
   Swooning half-moon lids, tongue out the corner, gold stars circling his head, and the
   stone has landed on his belly and simply stayed there — he looks suspiciously
   comfortable, as though this was always the plan.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side in a loose comma angled toward the camera, tail curled up over his
   own nose, spiral eyes, tongue lolling, ears at half mast. One forepaw pats vaguely
   around for the green stone, which is resting against his own pads the entire time —
   spirals only here, no orbiting stars, so he stays visually distinct from ko.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: caught inside a wide low white water crown that rises no more
   than a head above him, soaked flat, forepaws thrown up with the stone still clamped in
   the right one. Ears pinned, whiskers plastered, eyes wide and jaw open in an outraged
   squeak — the one animal on the roster built for this and he is deeply offended by it;
   nobody sinks, nobody swims, the burst is the whole read.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• ENVELOPE: every frame 0.80:1 to 1.35:1 and inside about 1.2x the idle box. The tail is a
third of his silhouette — arc it up, curl it, or lay it along the body, but never let it
stream out flat behind, because that alone width-normalises him down to half height mid-
animation.
• HEAD TEST: his skull must be the same number of pixels in all 18 frames. dash-run, flee,
eat, ko and dizzy are the five that will fail it — all of them are angled three-quarter to
camera for exactly that reason, and the head stays forward and full size.
• NO GROUND, ANY FRAME: no puddles, no wet pavement, no cast shadows, no drains, no shells
or crumbs resting on a floor. Water is only allowed off the body — contact spray at the
palms in land, curls at the shoulders in dash-run, flick off the tail tip in cheer and
magnet.
• COLOUR LAW: steam is pale blush-cream (never pink), the shield dome is blue-violet like
Jimothy's own. Saturated magenta, hot pink and blue-violet get bitten out by the cutter.
• NO LETTERING ANYWHERE, props included: the cup, the umbrella canopy and the clam tray
carry texture and pattern only. flee gets shock spikes, not exclamation marks.
• Paint idle, leap and cheer first and send those three for the 59px cut test before
committing the other fifteen — they are the three most likely to blow the envelope.
• Left-facing run: mirror — the stone was moved off his side and onto the centre line (in
his jaws) in run-r specifically so the flip is clean, and nothing else on him is asymmetric,
so no separate left-facing painting is needed.
