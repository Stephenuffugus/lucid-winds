================================================================================================
THE CHARACTER
================================================================================================

Market Day Jimothy — one of the playable characters in Jimothy, a storybook hopper set on
the rainy streets of Seattle.

STANCE (locked — the same in every frame):
  Upright biped at rest — hind feet planted, heels down, stubby tail as counterweight —
  dropping to plantigrade all-fours ONLY in the six travel frames (crouch, leap, land,
  run-r, dash-run, flee), where the bag-carrying left forearm still never reaches the road;
  he is never four-square, because the bag always occupies one arm.

WARDROBE AND PROPS (locked — these appear in ALL EIGHTEEN frames, including the deaths):
  In all eighteen: brown kraft grocery bag on the LEFT forearm (full-length baguette, carrot
  tops, celery, one apple inside), green box satchel on a single diagonal strap over the
  RIGHT shoulder to the left hip, one scallion (teeth, or behind the right ear when the
  mouth is busy) — no clothing; the takeaway cup, the umbrella and the dome are power-up
  props belonging to their one frame only.

HOW THIS BODY WORKS:
  He is a raccoon, so every pose in Jimothy's own pack works skeletally — the constraint is
  the hand budget, not the body: the left forearm is permanently spent on the grocery bag,
  only the right paw is ever free, and the two frames that need both forelimbs (magnet,
  shield) put the bag over the head or open across the chest, never on the floor. Jimothy
  body canon holds in all eighteen: round and spherical, no visible neck, head perched high
  above the shoulders, long skinny limbs, and a SHORT STUBBY ringed tail — never the long
  streaming rope of a lean trash panda. Plantigrade always: flat palms, five long spread
  fingers, heels down; no knuckles, and no tiptoes even in cheer. Head size is the scale
  check — the skull must be the same pixel height in every frame; the bag is the thing that
  changes shape (full, crushed, open wide, tipped, splitting), never him.

================================================================================================
RULES — THESE APPLY TO BOTH SHEETS, AND TO EVERY SHEET IN THIS PROJECT

  LAYOUT
  • NINE frames per sheet, laid out 3 across and 3 down, in the numbered order given below.
  • Background is flat magenta #FF00FF, edge to edge.
  • ⛔ Put a clear gap, or a white divider line, between every cell. NEVER let two frames touch.
    The cutter separates frames by finding the background between them; frames that touch get read
    as one object and come out sliced wrong.
  • Nothing may run off the edge of its own cell. Keep tails, wings, capes and effects inside.

  THE CAMERA — IDENTICAL IN ALL NINE
  • Three-quarter high angle looking slightly down, character facing the viewer.
  • The one exception is the sideways run frame, which is a side-on profile.

  SCALE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE ON THE SHEET
  • The character must be THE SAME SIZE in every frame. The game normalises each frame on its own,
    so a frame that is drawn bigger or wider than the others makes the character visibly change
    size mid-animation.
  • Target proportions: roughly as wide as it is tall, and never more than about 1.3 times wider
    than tall. If a pose wants to sprawl wider than that, tuck it in.
  • ⛔ Effects count as part of the frame. A big spray of water or a long trail of speed lines makes
    the game shrink the animal to fit them in. Keep every effect tight to the body.

  WHAT MUST NEVER BE PAINTED
  • ⛔ NO GROUND. No floor, no pavement, no puddle, no curb, no drain, no cast shadow, no scattered
    leaves or crumbs on the deck. The character is cut out and dropped onto the game's own street —
    anything painted on the ground survives the cut and floats there as grey scum.
  • ⛔ NO DETACHED PROPS. Anything not touching the body becomes its own separate sprite, or makes
    the character shrink. Props stay in a hand, a mouth, a pocket or against the body.
  • ⛔ NO READABLE TEXT. No lettering on badges, signs, newspapers, diplomas or paddles. Text is
    unreadable at game size, and one of these frames gets mirrored, which reverses it. Suggest
    print with texture instead.
  • ⛔ NO HOT PINK OR MAGENTA in the artwork itself — not in steam, glow, fabric or effects. Those
    tones get deleted along with the background. Keep effects warm, cool or neutral.

  STYLE
  • Match the existing Jimothy artwork: warm, funny, a little melancholy, rainy
    Seattle at night. Rich shadows, wet highlights.
  • Effects are painted INTO the frame — fire streaks, water bursts, circling stars, swirling
    bottlecaps. The game does not add them.
  • Deaths are funny-sad, never grim and never gory. Circling stars, not X-eyes.

SHEET 2a  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. IDLE
   In the game: standing, at rest — the default  ·  on screen constantly
   Upright on his hind legs facing camera, heels down, stubby tail counterweighting
   behind; left forearm cradling the overfull kraft bag at his hip, right paw hanging
   loose and open, scallion clamped sideways in his teeth. The bag is stuffed past its rim
   with free carrot tops three different stallholders kept adding, and his eyes cut just
   off camera with the mild embarrassment of a raccoon who did not ask for any of this.

2. SIT
   In the game: 4 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting back on his haunches off duty, the bag leaned against his thigh with one flat
   palm resting on top of it out of habit, satchel slid into his lap with the clasps
   undone, scallion still in his teeth like a toothpick. Ears relaxed, eyes half-lidded on
   nothing in particular — the quiet frame: he shopped for a bigger household than the one
   waiting at home.

3. EAT
   In the game: 8 seconds without input  ·  until the player moves
   Sitting beside the slumped-open bag with the baguette out and visibly half gone, one
   broken end held up in his right paw and the other cheek packed full, scallion parked
   behind his right ear. Eyes squeezed shut in bliss, crumbs caught in his chest fur and
   in the open satchel and none falling free — dinner, several hours early.

4. CROUCH
   In the game: first quarter of every hop  ·  0.07s, hundreds of times a run
   Three-point and loaded: both hind feet and his flat right palm down, haunches up, left
   forearm clamping the bag hard against his chest with the baguette standing straight up
   out of it like a mast. Shoulders tilted from the missing limb, eyes locked forward,
   scallion clenched in his teeth, the produce already leaning back from the launch.

5. LEAP
   In the game: middle half of every hop  ·  0.15s, hundreds of times a run
   Fully airborne, belly forward, back legs kicked wide, bag hugged to his chest with the
   baguette raked back along his spine like a rudder, its tip no more than a head past his
   rump, satchel flown up on its taut strap. One escaped carrot hangs against his ear and
   overlaps it, and he is grinning far too wide to have noticed.

6. LAND
   In the game: last quarter of a hop, plus a hold  ·  0.17s, hundreds of times a run
   Weight absorbed low, right palm planted flat with fingers spread and a tight spray
   bursting off it, left forearm still clamping the bag to his chest, satchel swung
   forward and thumped against his belly. Whiskers pushed forward, one eye scrunched from
   the impact, a carrot and a celery stalk floating an inch above the bag mouth and still
   overlapping it.

7. RUN-R
   In the game: a sideways hop to the right  ·  0.28s
   Side-on sprint to the right, right flank to camera, body low and legs at full
   extension: the satchel streams back off his near hip and the bag is clamped under his
   far arm, its brown rim and one carrot showing above his chest line with the baguette
   raked back along his spine. Ears laid flat, scallion still in his teeth, mouth shut and
   grimly professional.

8. DASH-RUN
   In the game: hopping while the coffee dash is up  ·  0.19s
   The same sprint flat out — legs stretched to the limit, ears pinned, eyes wide and
   white-rimmed, satchel flying horizontal, baguette raked back hard over the bag rim.
   Warm amber speed streaks tear off his heels and hips and touch him, and one carrot
   tumbles just behind his tail, overlapping it.

9. COFFEE
   In the game: STANDING STILL while the dash is up  ·  4.2s, or 7.0s on a Double Shot
   Standing dead still and vibrating: hind feet planted, heels down, fur lifted in
   bristles, pupils shrunk to pinpricks, jitter lines hugging his outline and a faint
   double image on the scallion in his teeth. A takeaway cup is wedged into the bag mouth
   beside the baguette because he has no paw left for it, pale blush-cream steam curling
   off it, small amber fire licks at his heels.

SHEET 2b  —  NINE FRAMES, 3 ACROSS AND 3 DOWN, IN THIS ORDER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MAGNET
   In the game: standing while the Snacks magnet is up  ·  6.5s
   Upright with the bag hauled wide open in both arms like a net, head tipped back, mouth
   open in pure astonishment that the world is handing him things, scallion clamped in the
   corner of his teeth. Gold bottlecaps and loose produce spiral in tight and drop through
   the top — the outermost cap overlapping his ear — and the satchel has flapped open and
   is catching two of them.

2. UMBRELLA
   In the game: standing while an umbrella shield is held  ·  indefinite, until it takes a hit
   Upright under an open umbrella held high in his right paw, tilted right off to his left
   so the bag underneath stays bone dry while his own right shoulder takes the whole
   soaking. Smug, ears flat, a drip hanging off his chin, short rain streaks shedding off
   the canopy rim only.

3. SHIELD
   In the game: the instant the shield eats a hit  ·  0.55s
   Braced low with the bag hoisted over his head in both arms and his eyes screwed shut, a
   bright blue-violet dome flaring tight around him with rain streaking off its curve. One
   carrot has been knocked loose and hangs against the bag mouth inside the dome, satchel
   swung round behind his back from the force.

4. SCARED
   In the game: a gull or crow is lining up a dive  ·  0.82 to 1.15s
   Up on his hind legs, bag clutched flat to his chest with his left arm, the baguette
   pulled out and held up in his right paw like a sword he has no intention of using, eyes
   huge and rolled straight up at the sky. Ears pinned back, scallion drooping out of his
   slack open mouth.

5. FLEE
   In the game: the street sweeper is closing in  ·  seconds at a time
   Three-quarter sprint toward camera, mouth open in panic, the bag jammed under his left
   arm, one carrot and one celery stalk spraying tight behind him and overlapping his tail
   and the bag mouth. Short red-orange speed streaks off his heels and two fat exclamation
   marks tucked in against his shoulder.

6. CHEER
   In the game: LEVEL CLEAR — the camera zooms in on them  ·  2.6s, the biggest moment in the game
   Up on his hind legs, heels down, stretched tall, the baguette punched up on a diagonal
   in his right paw like a torch with its tip no more than a head above his crown, the bag
   hoisted in his left with celery and carrot tops popping out of it. Mouth wide open in a
   joyful shout, eyes shut, scallion behind his right ear, two or three bottlecaps
   flicking up against his shoulders.

7. KO
   In the game: death by traffic, gull or steam  ·  1.55s, camera leans in, light closes
   Flat on his back, long skinny legs in the air, the bag tipped over against his head
   with produce spilling in a short arc across his ear and cheek and every piece touching
   him, baguette laid across his chest, satchel flopped open with a carrot resting on his
   belly. Swooning half-moon eyes, a small dazed smile, scallion still in his teeth, stars
   circling — first time he has been off his feet all day and he looks quietly relieved
   about it.

8. DIZZY
   In the game: death by the street sweeper  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   Sprawled on his side with spiral eyes and his tongue lolling, the emptied bag upside
   down over one ear like a hat he has given up removing, the baguette still spinning with
   one end resting across his shins in a blur arc. Scallion drooping from his slack mouth,
   one lone carrot balanced on his nose, and a tight swirl of motion arcs instead of stars
   so he never reads as the ko.

9. SPLASH
   In the game: death by water  ·  1.55s, same treatment
   The instant of the burst: soaked through, right arm thrown up, a white and pale-cyan
   water crown exploding around his waist, the sodden kraft bag going soft and splitting
   inside his own left grip. The baguette lies across the crown at his elbow and one
   carrot is plastered to his shoulder, scallion stuck flat to his cheek, his face pure
   shock — not at the water, at the bag.

NOTES FOR ME WHEN I CUT THIS
================================================================================================
• ENVELOPE IS THE KILL RULE. Every frame, effects included, sits between 0.80:1 and 1.35:1
and inside one box no more than ~1.2x the idle box; the character's HEAD must measure the
same pixel height in all eighteen. cheer is the one most likely to blow it — that is why the
baguette goes up on a diagonal, tip a head above the crown, not vertically to the sky.
• NO GROUND, ANYWHERE. No puddles, cast shadows, kerbs, drains, drying smears, skid marks or
spilled crumbs — the cutter keeps every non-magenta island and they come out as grey scum
floating under him. Contact effects that touch the body are correct: the spray off the
landing palm, the fire licks at his heels, the water crown at his waist.
• DETACHED PIECES: half a body width, and they must overlap his bounding box. Every loose
carrot, celery stalk and bottlecap in leap, land, dash-run, flee, magnet, shield, cheer and
ko is tucked against fur, ear, tail or the bag mouth. Nothing floats out on its own or it
cuts as a second sprite and shrinks him.
• CENTRE-ANCHORED, NOT BOTTOM-ANCHORED — the engine centres the sprite on the lane point, so
a high baguette or a trailing satchel drags the whole animal off his line. Keep his body
mass at the middle of the painted content in every frame.
• NO LETTERING ON THE PROPS: plain kraft bag with a rolled fold and creases, satchel with
stud and latch texture only — no shop name, no print, no logo, it mirrors and it is
illegible at 59px. Continuity: baguette full length in seventeen frames and half eaten only
in eat; the scallion never vanishes. Lay out 6x3 with white divider lines and a clear
magenta margin — nothing touches a divider or a frame edge.
• Left-facing run: Must be painted separately: the bag rides the LEFT forearm and the strap
crosses right shoulder to left hip, so mirroring swaps the entire costume — and it is not
the same picture reversed anyway, because in run-r the bag is on his far side and half-
occluded behind his chest, while in run-l it is near-side and fully visible.
