JIMOTHY — SHEET 18
WAVE 3 — the costumes you already animated once

Sheet 17 gave these four good frames each. Best-looking characters on the roster and closest to
done.

ONE CHARACTER PER IMAGE SHEET. Never combine. Magenta #FF00FF. Same camera and same scale
across all eighteen frames. Clear gaps or white divider lines between rows — never an even
grid. Suggested layout 6 columns x 3 rows in the order below. Full rules: THE ANIMATION BIBLE.

SOGGY JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/soggy/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — Same raccoon skeleton as the hero, so every pose ports across one-to-one — dexterous
front paws hold the cup, the fry tray and the umbrella handle exactly the way Jimothy's do, and
he rises onto his hind legs for the standing frames. The real work is not the body, it's the hat
and the water: the newspaper hat is a mood ring that must lose structure as the frame gets
wetter (crisp fold at idle, limp brim when he sits, pinned flat under wind and impact, pure grey
pulp at splash), and every single frame needs a live drip line coming off him somewhere.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera, weight even, soaked fur clumped into dark dripping
    spikes, the newspaper hat still holding a crisp fold with its printed columns readable. He
    wears a small contented smile while a steady drip line falls from his chin and jaw into a
    shallow puddle spreading out under his paws.

 2. SIT
    Sitting upright on his haunches in his own spreading puddle, front paws folded on a soaked
    belly, water still wringing out of his chest fur in a thin steady stream. The hat has
    slumped a quarter turn so the brim rests across one eyebrow and the headline creases over
    his forehead — he doesn't fix it, he just looks out at the rain, content.

 3. EAT
    Sitting beside a rain-flooded cardboard tray of fries, holding one up in both front paws —
    it has gone so soft it folds over double before it reaches his mouth, and he is watching
    it bend with genuine admiration. The hat brim drools a thin steady stream straight down
    into the tray, topping it back up.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, elbows out, coiled to spring, with water sheeting
    off his back in a curtain and pooling between his front paws. The hat has slid forward
    over his brow so he peers out from under a wet paper visor, eyes locked up-screen.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, limbs spread wide, belly forward, delighted, flinging a wide fan of water
    off his fur and tail behind him. The hat lifts off his skull by one corner and hangs a
    finger's width above his ears, trailing its own comet of droplets, still attached by sheer
    sogginess.

 6. LAND
    Front paws slammed down, body low, spine compressed, whiskers forward, with a full crown
    of splash bursting up around all four feet. The hat drops back onto his brow with a fresh
    slap of water off the brim; his eyes are wide and pleased.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on sprint to the right, body low, legs at full extension, tail flicking a long rope
    of water off behind him like a wrung mop. The hat streams backward and one corner has come
    unfolded into a wet ribbon of newsprint trailing past his ear.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat-out sprint, legs at maximum stretch, eyes wide — no fire on this one: he throws a
    dirty rooster tail of gutter water off his heels and the rain flashes into white steam off
    his back where he is moving too fast for it to land. The hat is pinned flat to his skull
    by the wind with the headline stretched taut.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing on his hind legs and visibly vibrating, both front paws clamped around a takeaway
    cup the rain has already refilled past the lid, pupils shrunk to pinpricks, wet fur
    bristling into spikes. Pink steam curls off the cup, white speed streaks and steam puffs
    scribble the air around his feet, and the hat has gone bolt rigid, standing at attention.

10. MAGNET
    Up on his hind legs hugging a rain-collapsed cardboard tray of fries to his chest, mouth
    open, eyes bright and delighted, with gold bottlecaps spiralling in toward him along a
    bright swirling ribbon. The wet hat's ink has started to run and one dark headline streak
    paints down his cheek like a happy tear.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing under an open umbrella in a clean dry rain-shadow, rain hammering the canopy and
    pouring off both sides in curtains — the one moment in his life when nothing new lands on
    him. He is still wringing wet and still standing in a puddle, the hat gently steaming as
    it finally begins to dry, and his smile is quiet and enormously smug.

12. SHIELD
    The instant of impact: crouched low, front paws thrown up, eyes squeezed shut, chin
    tucked. A blue-violet energy dome snaps up around him and rain shatters and streaks off
    the outside, while inside the dome the water sheets off him all at once — for this one
    flash frame his silhouette is rimlit and almost dry, hat blown flat against his skull.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, paws clutched to his chest, ears flat back, eyes enormous and fixed
    on something above the frame; one claw has tipped the hat brim up so he can see the sky,
    which means the full downpour is now hitting him square in the face. Water runs into his
    open, worried mouth.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left flat out, mouth open in panic, red '!!' marks stamped in the air beside his
    head, a wide rooster tail of filthy gutter spray blasting off his heels. The hat has been
    torn half off and streams behind him held by one soaked corner — and even now the corner
    of his mouth is still trying to smile.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on his hind legs, one arm thrown straight to the sky, mouth open in a shout
    of pure joy; he has whipped the newspaper hat off and flung it, so it hangs spinning just
    above his head throwing a full halo of droplets outward in a starburst. Underneath, his
    head fur is revealed as a flattened, comically wet little dome.

16. KO
    Flat on his back, all four legs in the air, mouth open, a ring of stars circling his head
    and a puddle spreading beneath him in a wide halo. The hat has landed neatly across his
    face like a small grey shroud, headline side up, with one dazed eye peeking through a torn
    column.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side, spiral eyes, tongue lolling out, stars orbiting his head, hat spun
    down over one ear at a drunk angle. Ink from the soaked newsprint runs down both cheeks in
    two long spiral smears that match his eyes exactly.

18. SPLASH
    Caught in the burst with arms thrown up, eyes bulging and mouth wide open in shock as a
    huge white and violet crown of water erupts around him. The newspaper hat has finally
    given up and gone to pure pulp, sliding down over his brow in grey ribbons — and under it
    he is, unmistakably, still smiling. Wettest he has ever been.

    SHEET NOTES
    • Hat corners, the trailing newsprint ribbon, tail spray and drip lines all break his
    silhouette — leave a generous margin on every side, especially leap, run-r and flee
    where the hat streams well past the body. Do not crop the hat.
    • The puddle under his feet is part of the character, not the ground. Keep it small and
    centred so the bottom-anchor baseline is identical in all 18; splash's water crown is
    the only frame allowed to rise above his knees.
    • The hat is asymmetric (the fold points one way, the ink streak runs down one cheek),
    so paint the optional run-l rather than letting the engine mirror run-r — a mirror flips
    the headline backwards and jumps the ink smear to the wrong side.
    • Two frames are deliberately drier than the rest — umbrella (steaming, drying out) and
    shield (the dome sheds his water for one instant). That contrast IS the joke; do not
    soak them back down for consistency.
    • idle, crouch, leap and land already exist in assets/skins/soggy/ and are the scale,
    camera and palette master for the other fourteen. Match his height, that brown-grey
    soaked-fur palette and that exact hat fold; he must not change size across the sheet.
    • Ink is the only colour allowed to shift: as the newsprint gets wetter its grey goes
    warmer and softer, and the running ink reads dark blue-black, never pure black.

HOT JIMOTHY SUMMER      (files go to assets/skins/summer/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — Same raccoon body as base Jimothy, so every hero pose maps across one-to-one and the
paws are hands — props are easy. The honest problem is the floatie: it is a rigid torus around
his middle that hides his belly and stops him folding flat, so it has to TRAVEL frame to frame —
resting on his hips at ease, shoved to his armpits airborne, ridden up to his shoulders when he
crouches, slipped down to his ankles when he goes under. The shades never leave his head; they
only move up the brow or down the snout.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera on wet pavement, weight on one hip, ringed tail curling
    out behind, the orange-and-cream floatie resting level on his hips. Chin lifted, mouth a
    small satisfied line, shades dead straight over his eyes with one fat drizzle bead sliding
    down the left lens.

 2. SIT
    Off duty: he has sat straight down INTO the floatie, so the ring props him up like a donut
    chair with his hind legs sticking out over the front rim. Paws folded on the ring's top
    curve, head tipped back, shades on, contented half-smile, a shallow puddle spreading
    around the rubber.

 3. EAT
    Sitting in the ring again, using its top rim as a table — a paper cup of blue slush wedged
    in the hole beside him, a half-melted orange popsicle held up in both paws. He is licking
    a drip off his own wrist, shades on, while the drizzle quietly melts the popsicle faster
    than the weather ever could.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours, haunches up and shoulders down, which shoves the floatie up
    around his neck like a collar and squishes his cheeks out either side of it. Eyes forward
    over the top of the rubber, shades knocked one degree crooked, back paws dug into the wet
    asphalt.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, belly forward, all four limbs spread wide, mouth open in a whoop — the
    floatie has ridden up to his armpits and is riding the air with him. Shades perfectly
    level as always, beads of water flicking off the ring's trailing edge in a little arc.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted, body low, weight absorbed — and the floatie bounces up and slaps him
    under the chin, pushing his cheeks and whiskers forward. Shades jolted one notch down his
    snout so a single wide eye peeks over the top rim, a thin crown of splash bursting at his
    feet.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on sprint to the right, body low, legs at full extension, tail streaming flat behind
    him. The floatie is squashed against his ribs with wobble lines trailing off it, and one
    front paw is clamped on the rim holding it down so it does not ride up over his head.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out, legs stretched to their limit, tongue out, the floatie shoved back into a
    slipstream cone behind his shoulders. Orange speed streaks along his flanks, a white glint
    flashing across both lenses, a rooster-tail of puddle spray kicking up off the wet street
    behind him.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing up on his toes and vibrating, both paws clamped around an enormous iced coffee
    with a bendy straw, ice cubes leaping out the top. The shades are pushed up onto his
    forehead for the one and only time on the sheet, showing two enormous jittering pupils,
    and the floatie buzzes around him in a motion-blur double outline.

10. MAGNET
    Standing with both arms flung open and mouth wide in delight as gold bottlecaps and stray
    fries spiral in toward him, several caps pinging off the taut rubber and one lodged in the
    floatie's rope trim. Two little gold streaks reflected in the dark lenses, ring level on
    his hips.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing dead centre under a candy-striped BEACH PARASOL jammed into a crack in the
    sidewalk, rain drumming loudly on it and sheeting off the edges. Floatie on his hips, one
    paw on his hip, a folded beach towel over the other arm, chin up, absolutely and immovably
    smug.

12. SHIELD
    Braced sideways into the hit, parasol tilted like a shield, both paws up, as a blue-violet
    faceted dome flares around him and rain streaks off it in hard diagonals. The floatie
    squashes flat on the impact side, his shades jolt crooked, whiskers swept back, muzzle
    scrunched in a flinch.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs with the floatie hauled off his hips and hugged to his chest like the
    life preserver it actually is, head craned up and back. Shades slid to the very tip of his
    nose so two huge white eyes stare over the top, mouth a tiny o, a wedge of gull shadow
    falling across his chest and the ring.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left with his mouth wide in panic, red speed streaks off his heels and two fat
    '!!' marks over his head, the abandoned popsicle tumbling in the air behind him. The
    floatie has flapped up around his neck like a horse collar and one lens catches a hard
    slash of the sweeper's yellow light.

15. CHEER
    Up on tiptoe, standing as tall as he goes, both arms thrown overhead holding the floatie
    up like a championship inner tube, mouth open in pure joy. A single real shaft of sunlight
    has broken through and lands squarely on him, sparks glinting off both lenses — for one
    and a third seconds he was completely right about summer.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with legs in the air, rocking gently on the floatie like a turtle stuck
    on its shell, mouth open and tongue lolling, stars circling his head. The shades sit
    crooked with one lens popped clean out and lying on the pavement beside his ear.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side, tongue out, stars orbiting his head — and spiral eyes painted right
    onto the dark lenses of the shades, which never came off. The floatie has gone limp and
    curled under him with a small sad hiss line escaping the valve, one back paw still
    twitching.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked through and going under, arms straight up, fur plastered flat, mouth wide in shock
    and a big white crown of water bursting around his shoulders. The cruel punchline: the
    floatie has slipped all the way down to his ankles and bobs uselessly at the surface while
    he sinks, shades shoved up on his dripping forehead.

    SHEET NOTES
    • The floatie makes him the widest character on the roster — roughly 1.4x base Jimothy
    across. Keep his HEAD at the same scale as every other sheet and let the ring overhang;
    pad every cell generously left and right, because the ring's rounded outer edge is the
    first thing a tight crop shaves off.
    • The existing painted crouch.png has no floatie on it at all. Repaint it with the ring
    ridden up around his shoulders — idle, leap and land already carry the ring, and the
    costume has to survive all 18 or the hop cycle pops the ring in and out of existence.
    • Never draw the ring as a flat ellipse. It is a torus: highlight along the top curve,
    shadow tucked under the bottom, valve stem visible somewhere, so it reads three-
    dimensional under the three-quarter camera in every pose.
    • The shades never leave the sheet. They ride up the brow (coffee, splash), slip down
    the snout (land, scared), sit crooked (shield, ko), and in ko one lens pops out — but it
    stays in frame on the pavement beside him. Only coffee shows his actual eyes.
    • Bottom-anchor to his hind paws in every frame. In sit and ko the ring dips below the
    paws — anchor to the ring's lowest curve there instead, and keep his eye-line at the
    same height as the other frames so he does not bob when the engine swaps poses.
    • The teal patch on the floatie is the only asymmetric detail on him. Paint it dead-
    centre on the front of the ring so a mirrored run-l is invisible; if it ends up on his
    flank, paint a real run-l instead.
    • Rain is constant on the wet-street frames — this is a summer costume in an unbroken
    Seattle drizzle, and that contrast IS the character. cheer is the single frame that gets
    real sunlight; do not leak sun into any other frame or the payoff dies.
    • All effects baked in: parasol runoff, the blue-violet dome, gull shadow, swirling
    bottlecaps, circling stars, the water crown. Magenta #FF00FF ground, clear gaps or white
    divider lines between rows, never a butted even grid.

NORDIC JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/nordic/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — Same round raccoon body as Jimothy, so every pose transfers one-to-one — the real work
is the knitwear, which behaves like a second body. The heavy hand-knit sweater swallows his
forepaws (he handles every prop through a sleeve cuff, never with a bare hand), its hem swings a
beat behind him, and the bobble hat's pom is the lag weight that sells the timing of every
frame.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera, the heavy navy fair-isle sweater hanging to his knees,
    sleeves long enough to swallow both forepaws, ringed tail curling out from under the hem.
    Calm, patient, faintly damp; the oatmeal pom droops to one side like it has given up on
    the weather.

 2. SIT
    Sitting upright on his haunches with the sweater pooled all around him like a knitted tea
    cosy, only his back toes and the tip of his tail poking out. Both cuffs folded on the
    woolly belly, hat slipped a little low on the brow, eyes half-lidded and thoroughly
    content.

 3. EAT
    Sitting beside an open box of fries, holding one fry pinched in the very end of a sleeve
    because his paws are lost somewhere up inside the wool. Cheerfully unbothered, crumbs and
    salt caught in the fair-isle bands, two spare fries already stashed in the other cuff for
    later.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, the sweater rucked and bunched over his shoulders
    with the hem dragging in the wet street. Hat tipped forward so the pom swings down in
    front of one eye; he looks past it, forward, ready to go.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, limbs spread wide, belly forward, the sweater ballooned with air like a
    knitted sail and the hem flared open. Pom flung straight out behind him, mouth open in
    delight, a few loose wool fibres and rain flecks trailing in his wake.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted, body low, weight absorbed, the sweater slumping down and settling
    around him in a woolly ripple. Pom whips forward over his brow, whiskers forward, a small
    splash burst kicked up off the wet pavement at his feet.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on lolloping sprint to the right, body low and legs extended, empty sleeve-ends
    flapping well past his paws. The hem swings opposite to his stride, pom streams back, tail
    out straight behind, jaw set with grim Ballard determination.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out to the right, legs at full stretch, the sweater pressed flat against him by the
    speed with the hem snapping behind like a flag. Pom horizontal, eyes wide, cream and red
    speed streaks raking the frame and a thin curl of steam rising off the overheating wool.

 9. COFFEE
    Sprinting with a takeaway cup gripped in a sleeve cuff — he cannot feel the heat through
    four ply of wool and it shows on his face. Orange fire streaks under his feet, pink steam
    off the cup, eyes wired and enormous, hat shoved back with the pom bouncing.

10. MAGNET
    Standing, holding the sweater hem out with both cuffs like an apron to catch everything
    coming his way, mouth open in pure delight. Gold bottlecaps and fries spiral in on a warm
    pink swirl and drop into the pouch of wool, a couple already snagged in the knit.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing under an open umbrella whose handle is clamped in a sleeve cuff, rain coming down
    hard all around the canopy. Dry and smug from the shoulders up, half-lidded satisfied
    eyes, but the hem is already dark with soaked-up water and dripping steadily.

12. SHIELD
    Braced and flinching, both cuffs thrown up over the face, a blue-violet energy dome
    snapping shut around him with the light catching every strand so the fair-isle pattern
    glows through. Pom stands bolt upright with static, rain streaking off the dome, one eye
    squeezed shut and the other peeking.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, both sleeve-ends clutched to his chest, ears flat, eyes huge and
    locked on something up-screen. The hat has slipped so he is peering out from under the
    brim, pom quivering, loose wool bristling along his shoulders.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left in flat panic, mouth open, hat blown back and hanging off one ear by its
    brim with the pom bouncing wildly. The hem tangles around his back legs so he is half-
    tripping as he runs, red speed streaks and two hard "!!" marks over his head.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall, one arm thrown to the sky with the empty cuff flapping a foot past his
    actual paw, other paw planted on his woolly hip, mouth open in a whoop. Hat knocked askew,
    pom swinging, a little burst of golden confetti and drifting wool fluff around him.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with all four legs in the air, the sweater rucked halfway up his chest
    and the hat slid down over his eyes so the pom rests on the pavement beside his head.
    Mouth open in a stunned little O, stars circling above the brim.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled and spun out, spiral eyes, tongue out, the hat turned fully backwards so the pom
    dangles over his nose. The sweater has twisted a quarter turn so the fair-isle yoke sits
    sideways across his shoulder, one long loose thread unravelling off toward the edge of the
    frame, stars circling.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked through, arms up, water bursting around him, the sweater now hugely waterlogged and
    stretched down past his feet like a wet dress. Pom a sodden dripping lump over one ear,
    mouth open in shock — he is less worried about the water than about the three days it will
    take that wool to dry.

    SHEET NOTES
    • Four frames already exist in assets/skins/nordic (idle, crouch, leap, land). Match
    them exactly: navy ground, cream/red/white banded fair-isle, ribbed cuffs and hem, navy
    knit hat with a red-and-cream band and an oatmeal pom. Do not re-invent the palette on
    the new fourteen.
    • He reads noticeably wider than base Jimothy because of the sweater bulk. Lock the
    scale to the four existing frames — do not shrink him down to make room for the pom.
    • The pom and the swinging hem break the body silhouette on leap, dash-run, land and
    flee. Leave real magenta margin above the head and on the trailing side, or the cutter
    shaves the pom off and he loses his hat.
    • Fair-isle bands are horizontal and the hat motif is symmetric, so run-l can be safely
    mirrored from run-r. Painting a dedicated run-l is optional for this character.
    • Bare dark paws below the cuffs and the ringed tail below the hem are the only raccoon
    actually showing. Keep both visible in all 18 or he reads as a sack of wool with a face.
    • ko, dizzy and splash each hold 1.55s under a camera lean and a closing iris. The
    unravelling thread, the sideways yoke and the waterlogged stretched hem all get looked
    at closely — paint them properly.

BARISTA JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/barista/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — Same round raccoon body as hero Jimothy, so every pose transfers one to one. The only
real anatomy problem is the cup: it permanently costs him one front paw, so in every four-limbed
pose he is running, crouching and landing on three points while the fourth stays out at arm's
length holding the coffee dead level. Counterweight all of it through the ringed tail and the
free paw, and let the tail do the work Jimothy's second arm normally does.

 1. IDLE
    Standing square on his hind feet facing camera, green apron hanging straight, leather
    straps taut over the shoulders, ringed tail curled behind. The takeaway cup rides level in
    his right paw at chest height with a thin curl of steam going straight up, one eyebrow
    raised and a small closed-mouth smile that says he already knows what you are going to
    order and he has opinions.

 2. SIT
    Sitting upright on his haunches with the apron pooled over his knees and both paws folded
    on his belly, ears relaxed, eyes half closed. The cup is set down on the wet pavement
    beside his foot, upright, still steaming, exactly where he can keep an eye on it. This is
    the ten minute break he has been owed since five in the morning.

 3. EAT
    Sitting with a fat almond croissant in both paws, cheeks packed, eyes shut in pure relief,
    flakes and powdered sugar scattered down the front of the green apron. The cup stands
    untouched on the ground beside his foot, lid on, steaming, because he saves the coffee for
    after.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on three paws with haunches up and the apron hem brushing the pavement, ears
    back, eyes locked forward on the gap he is about to take. The fourth paw is already out to
    his side at full arm's length, holding the cup perfectly level and clear of his body so it
    survives the launch.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, belly forward, hind legs trailing and the apron pressed flat against him
    by the wind, whiskers swept back, mouth open in delight. The cup is thrust out ahead of
    him in the leading paw, dead upright, the coffee inside not even reaching the lip.

 6. LAND
    Front paw and both hind feet planted hard, body low, the apron swinging forward past his
    knees and a small splash ring of rainwater kicked up around his feet. The cup arm has
    snapped straight up above the impact to keep it steady, and a single suspended drop of
    coffee hangs just over the open lid, about to fall back in.

 7. RUN-R
    Side on sprint to the right, body low and legs extended, apron and tail streaming back
    behind him, head up and eyes on the crossing. The cup is carried level in the leading paw
    with the steam trailing backward off it in one straight ribbon, the only calm thing in the
    frame.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out side sprint, legs at full stretch, ears blown back, eyes wide and pupils tiny,
    orange and gold fire streaks tearing up under his feet and the apron pinned flat to his
    chest. One paw is raised overhead balancing a cardboard tray of four to go cups perfectly
    flat, not one of them tipping, because he has done this run a thousand times.

 9. COFFEE
    The money frame. Braced facing camera in a wide planted stance, fur bristled out into a
    halo, ears bolt upright, pupils shrunk to pinpricks and visibly vibrating, a cup gripped
    in each paw and a third clamped sideways in his teeth. Hot pink and gold steam roars up
    behind him in a huge crema swirl, fire streaks lick around his feet and the apron strings
    float straight out on the updraft.

10. MAGNET
    Standing with his free arm thrown wide open and his mouth open in undisguised delight, cup
    still held resolutely level in the other paw. A swirling gold ribbon spirals bottlecaps,
    sugar packets and wooden stir sticks in toward him from the right, and the front pocket of
    the green apron is already sagging full of caps. This is the best tip jar night of his
    life.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing dry and unbothered under a big striped cafe patio umbrella, rain sheeting off the
    fringe all around him and beading on the pavement. Cup level in the free paw, ankles
    crossed, eyes half lidded with a smug little smile, apron spotless. He has all day and you
    do not.

12. SHIELD
    Crouched and braced with one eye shut and one paw thrown up, a blue violet energy dome
    flaring hard around him and rain streaking off its curve in bright arcs. The other paw has
    clamped the cup flat against his chest inside the apron bib, and the coffee in it is still
    perfectly level.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, ears flat, eyes huge and white rimmed, both paws pulled in tight to
    his chest with the cup pinned between them. He is craning up and back at whatever is
    lining up above him, and the steam off the lid is being blown sideways across his face by
    the dive.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting screen left with his mouth wide open in panic, red speed streaks behind him and
    two big exclamation marks over his head, apron flapping up around his ribs. His trailing
    paw is still holding the cup out level and unspilled behind him, because he would
    genuinely rather be run over than pour it out.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on his toes with the cup raised to the sky like a trophy and the free arm
    flung out, mouth open in a whoop, ears straight up, apron straps proud across his chest. A
    little firework of golden steam bursts up out of the lid above his head.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with legs in the air, mouth open, tongue lolling, stars circling his
    head, the green apron rucked up under his shoulders. The cup has come down upright and
    landed square on his belly, lid still on, one thin curl of steam rising straight up, not a
    single drop spilled.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side with spiral eyes, tongue out and ears at two different wrong angles,
    apron twisted round backwards over his hip, stars orbiting his head. The cup is balanced
    improbably on the crown of his skull, upright and gently steaming, which somehow makes it
    worse.

18. SPLASH
    Chest deep and going under, fur plastered flat and soaked black, mouth open in shock,
    white and violet water bursting up in sheets around him and the green apron floating out

    • Keep the cup the SAME cup at the SAME size in all 18. Do not scale it up in the close

    • PAINT run-l for this one, do not let the engine mirror it. He is asymmetric in three

    jumping ears.
    • Lay the sheet out in rows of three or four with white divider lines and clear gaps,
    never a butted even grid. The wide cup arm frames are exactly the kind of sprite a fixed
    grid slices in half.

chowder cup and a coffee can all be worked in around the fish, and the one frame where the
salmon leaves his grip (cheer) it is a deliberate Pike Place toss, still mid-air, still his.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera in the blue bib apron, tail low, calm and quietly
    proud, with the whole silver-and-coral salmon clamped crosswise in his jaws so it lies

    hangs off the salmon's tail, not yet fallen.

 2. SIT
    Sitting upright on his haunches, shoulders dropped, apron rucked at the waist, the salmon

 3. EAT

    apron bib. The salmon's snout tilts toward the cup like it would also like some.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, apron bunched under his belly, the salmon held
    straight forward in his jaws like a bowsprit aimed at the far kerb. Brow down, eyes locked

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, belly forward, hind legs trailing, apron ties streaming behind him, both

 6. LAND

 7. RUN-R

    wake.

 8. DASH-RUN

    forward in both paws like a ship's figurehead. White speed streaks rake past him and ice

 9. COFFEE

10. MAGNET

11. UMBRELLA

    hammers down all around the canopy, a neat dry circle of pavement under his back paws.

12. SHIELD
    Braced side-on and curled bodily around the salmon, one shoulder into the blow, protecting
    the fish instead of his own face, one eye screwed shut and teeth gritted. A blue-violet
    energy dome flares around them both with rain streaking off it in sparks.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, hunched small, ears flat, the salmon clutched to his chest in both

14. FLEE

15. CHEER

16. KO

17. DIZZY

18. SPLASH
    Soaked and sinking, arms thrown up, apron plastered flat, eyes shocked wide as a burst of

    • He is asymmetric — bib pocket and apron ties on one side, salmon carried on one side —

    • The salmon's coral-pink lateral stripe must face the camera in every single frame.

    • Umbrella is the frame most likely to come back wrong: the umbrella crook must visibly
    hook through the apron bib strap. If it looks like he is holding it, he has dropped the
    salmon, and he never drops the salmon.
