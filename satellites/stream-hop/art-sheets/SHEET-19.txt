JIMOTHY — SHEET 19
WAVE 4 — the rest of the critters, hardest last

Banana Slug, Sockeye Salmon and Orca have no limbs at all and eighteen poses designed for a
raccoon. Their sheets solve every frame honestly rather than pretending. Read the anatomy note
before you start each one.

ONE CHARACTER PER IMAGE SHEET. Never combine. Magenta #FF00FF. Same camera and same scale
across all eighteen frames. Clear gaps or white divider lines between rows — never an even
grid. Suggested layout 6 columns x 3 rows in the order below. Full rules: THE ANIMATION BIBLE.

SKUNK      (files go to assets/chars/skunk/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He is a short-legged plantigrade quadruped with no true hands, so every pose that
needs arms is solved the way a real striped skunk solves it: he rears back on his haunches and
works with two dextrous front paws. The great plume tail is the third limb and does all the jobs
Jimothy's arms do — it is his umbrella, his basket, his cushion, his banner and his parachute —
and because he is famously near-sighted, his face is always squinting slightly at the wrong part
of the scene.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square at the three-quarter camera, glossy black guard hairs beaded with
    rain, the double white stripe running clean from his forehead over his back, and the
    enormous tail carried high like a parade banner with the plume curling forward past his
    ears. Small amber eyes in a polite near-sighted squint, a faint contented smile, a wet
    gutter reflection under his paws — this is the existing idle.png and the whole sheet keys
    to it.

 2. SIT
    Sat back on his haunches with his back rounded and his front paws tucked against the white
    chest fur, half-lidded eyes, entirely off duty. The tail has folded down and wrapped
    forward around his feet like a stole, so he is sitting on and inside his own tail, warm
    and dry while the rain keeps coming down around him.

 3. EAT
    Sitting upright with a rain-softened takeaway box tipped open between his hind feet, fries
    ignored, holding one fat pale garden grub in both front paws like corn on the cob with his
    eyes shut in genuine bliss. The tail is curled around behind him as a windbreak so the
    rain cannot get into the box — considerate, and slightly gross, and completely him.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, chest almost touching the wet asphalt, whiskers
    forward and eyes locked up-screen. The tail is pressed flat and long behind him like a
    loaded spring, and his front paws are caught mid-drum in the little warning stamp a skunk
    does before anything happens — a soft blur on the forepaws sells it.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne and stretched, all four short legs spread wide, belly forward, mouth open
    in delight, near-sighted eyes as wide as they go. The tail streams straight out behind him
    as a long black-and-white comet banner that doubles his length across the frame — it must
    be painted whole, tip included, or the pose is nothing.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted with a splash ring of gutter water bursting off them, chest low,
    haunches still dropping, weight visibly absorbed. The tail has whipped forward and
    overshot past his own nose, plume fanned across his face, so he is momentarily half-buried
    in his own tail and squinting out through it.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on to the right, body low and stretched long, short legs at full extension, tail held
    out horizontal behind him with a lazy S-wave running through the plume. In profile the
    white stripe on the forehead and one amber eye carry the identity; his face wears a small
    determined scowl, because he is not fast and he knows it.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out and side-on, legs at full stretch and barely touching ground, ears pinned, eyes
    huge, tongue out the side of his mouth. The tail is flattened straight back into a white
    torpedo streak with hard speed lines behind it, and a soft pastel mint-green puff blooms
    at its base like a little booster — he is, unmistakably, jet propelled.

 9. COFFEE
    Reared up on his hind legs with a paper cup clamped in both front paws, coffee sloshing
    over the rim, steam curling off it, pupils shrunk to two dots. Every hair of the tail has
    stood on end and bottle-brushed it out to twice its normal size with jitter lines ticking
    off the plume, and a small fire-lick and speed streak flick out from under his hind feet,
    ready to go.

10. MAGNET
    Sitting up with both front paws thrown wide and open, mouth open in pure delight, while
    gold bottlecaps, fries and grubs spiral in toward him from every edge of the frame. The
    tail has curled forward into a big white scoop beside him and is already catching half of
    them — the plume is the basket, and it is winning.

11. UMBRELLA
    Sat up on his haunches with the whole tail arced forward over his head as a fat white
    parasol, rain sheeting off the plume in bright ribbons and not one drop reaching him. A
    cheap folded thrift-store umbrella is tucked unopened under one front paw, purely for
    show, and his squint is enormously smug about owning two roofs.

12. SHIELD
    The instant of the hit: he has flinched down flat with ears back, eyes squeezed shut and
    one front paw thrown up over his face. A blue-violet energy dome snaps around him, dented
    outward at the top where the bolt-upright tail plume shoves against it, rain streaking
    hard down the outside of the dome.

13. SCARED
    Front half dropped low with his back arched, both forepaws caught mid-stamp in a panicked
    drum roll, tail snapped bolt upright and fanned into a full warning flag. His head is
    twisted up and back but the near-sighted squint is aimed at the wrong patch of grey sky,
    one ear cocked, a single startled "!" floating above the plume.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left, low and scrambling, claws throwing up gutter water, mouth open in panic,
    wet fur plastered over eyes gone huge, with red speed streaks and "!!" behind him. The
    tail drags in a wobbling panicked wave rather than a proud banner, because for the first
    time in his life the thing behind him is not getting out of his way.

15. CHEER
    Reared up tall on his hind legs with both front paws flung to the sky, eyes squeezed shut
    in a huge grin, chest stripe blazing. The tail is launched straight up and fanned into a
    white firework, bursting pastel mint-green sparkles and tiny clover-shaped puffs out of
    the top like confetti — he has won, and the lane is his, and it is now extremely his.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with all four short legs sticking straight up in the air, tongue lolling
    to one side, one ear folded over, stars circling his head. The great tail has spread out
    flat beneath him like a fur fainting couch so he has landed in total comfort, rain falling
    gently on his upturned white belly.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled belly-down and splayed out like a dropped rug, spiral eyes, tongue out, stars
    wobbling around his head. The tail is bent above him into a lazy question mark, his back
    is dusted with swept-up leaves and wet litter from the sweeper's brushes, and two loose
    white tail-hairs drift down through the frame.

18. SPLASH
    Front half heaved up out of the water with both forepaws thrown skyward, mouth open in
    shock, eyes enormous, a tall white water burst crowning around him. Behind him the
    glorious plume has collapsed into a soaked rope a third of its size, floating limp on the
    surface trailing a few bubbles — the tail drowning is the whole joke and the whole
    heartbreak.

    SHEET NOTES
    • NEVER CROP THE TAIL. It is the largest shape on the sheet and it moves the most. Give
    every cell a full tail's length of spare magenta on the trailing side for leap, run-r,
    dash-run and flee, and a tail's length of headroom above crouch-adjacent verticals:
    scared, cheer and coffee all send it straight up.
    • SCALE HIM BY BODY, NOT BY TAIL. Match his body height to Jimothy's; if you fit the
    tail inside the same box as everyone else he shrinks to half the roster's size and looks
    like a different game. This means his cells are simply bigger than the other critters'
    cells — that is correct, cut them wide.
    • Four frames already exist and are good: idle, crouch, leap, land
    (assets/chars/skunk/). The fourteen new frames must match their camera, lighting and
    scale exactly. If you would rather repaint, repaint all eighteen in one pass — never mix
    an old scale with a new one on the same sheet.
    • run-l can be safely mirrored from run-r. His body is asymmetric front-to-back but the
    double stripe and the plume are symmetric left-to-right, and the only differences (the
    kink in the stripe, the eye highlight) do not read at sprite size. Skip painting it.
    • Near-black fur on a magenta ground is the one real hazard here. Keep a thin warm rim-
    light along the top of the black guard hairs in every frame so the silhouette survives
    against the dark road, and keep the white stripe crisp — the stripe and the plume are
    the only things a player sees at speed.
    • The spray gag appears in exactly three frames and only as soft pastel mint-green: a
    booster puff on dash-run, confetti sparkles on cheer, and the faintest hint on scared.
    Soft, clover-shaped, sparkly. Never a cloud, never brown, never a stink line. This is a
    general-audience game and he is a shy, polite animal who simply has the whole lane to
    himself.

BANANA SLUG      (files go to assets/chars/slug/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — No limbs at all, so the two eyestalks do every job an arm would do — pointing,
holding, flinching, cheering — and the muscular travelling wave rippling down his foot does
every job a leg would do. The slime trail is the third actor: its shape carries speed, panic and
joy, and props are never held, only stuck to the slime or balanced on the stalks.

 1. IDLE
    Laid out full length facing the camera with his front third reared up off the wet
    pavement, both eyestalks tall and splayed and the two little lip-feelers tasting the air.
    Banana-yellow gloss down his back, black leopard spots, a calm soft mouth, and a fresh
    silver slime trail unspooling behind him toward the bottom of the frame.

 2. SIT
    He has no haunches to sit on, so he settles: body coiled into a fat comma on a spreading
    cushion of his own slime, mantle relaxed, both eyestalks drooped to half mast and lazily
    crossed at the tips. Mouth a content little line — off duty, in a puddle, exactly where he
    wants to be.

 3. EAT
    Nose-deep in a fallen chanterelle, rasping a neat scalloped bite out of the cap, one
    eyestalk tipped down to watch the food while the other stays up on lookout. A soggy Pike
    Place fry lies beside him with one identical scallop already taken out of it, abandoned as
    too dry.

 4. CROUCH
    Gathered to spring: the whole body concertina'd to half its length and twice its height,
    ridged like a squeezed accordion, mantle flared, eyestalks pulled halfway back into his
    head and aimed up-screen. The slime under him is stretched tight and glassy, like a drawn
    rubber band.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne and stretched dead straight like a thrown banana, arced belly-forward, both
    eyestalks streaming back like ribbons, mouth open in undignified delight. A single long
    rope of slime still connects his tail to the ground below and is mid-snap, flinging dew
    droplets.

 6. LAND
    A belly-flop — the front third splatted wide and flat with a ring of slime squirting out
    sideways, the back half still arriving in a visible ripple down his foot. Both eyestalks
    have whipped forward past his own face from the impact, eyes crossed for a beat, grinning
    anyway.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on and travelling right, body stretched long and low with the muscular wave banding
    down his foot like rolling light, eyestalks laid back streamlined, jaw set with total
    determination. A glossy slime road unspools behind him and the motion arcs are honestly,
    comically tiny — this is his top speed.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same side-on sprint, flat out: body stretched thin, the foot waves compressed into a
    blur, eyestalks pinned straight back, eyes wide, mouth open. The trail behind him is a
    wide silver skid streak with orange friction streaks and a curl of steam, plus a rooster-
    tail of slime spraying off his tail.

 9. COFFEE
    Reared up tall on his back third and visibly vibrating — double-image blur on both
    eyestalks, pupils shrunk to pin dots, the front of his body glossed espresso-dark where he
    crawled straight through the spill rather than carrying it. A tipped takeaway cup steams
    pink behind him and his own slime trail glows hot with fire streaks.

10. MAGNET
    Reared up with his mouth open in a delighted O and both eyestalks splayed wide, because
    with him everything sticks: gold bottlecaps already glued along his back like sequins, a
    fry stuck flat to one eyestalk, more caps and snacks spiralling in on curved motion arcs.
    Slime glinting all over him.

11. UMBRELLA
    A fallen big-leaf maple held up like a two-pole tent on his two eyestalks, one under each
    side of the leaf, body settled underneath with his eyes half-lidded and smug. Rain sheets
    off the leaf edges, and one fat drip has got through onto his back, which he is plainly
    thrilled about.

12. SHIELD
    The hit lands: both eyestalks snap fully inside his head leaving two dimples, the body
    clenched into a tight tense hump, mantle flared, mouth a shocked little slot. A blue-
    violet energy dome flares around him with rain streaking off it and his maple leaf
    tumbling away out of the corner of the frame.

13. SCARED
    Coiled back tight with his front end craned up-screen, both eyestalks stretched to full
    periscope height and bent to point dead at the sky, pupils huge, one bead of slime-sweat
    wobbling on his mantle. The diving gull's shadow falls across the pavement and right over
    his back.

14. FLEE
    Absolute maximum slug velocity, which is nothing: body stretched to its longest, the foot
    waves frantic, eyestalks streaming back with a bold "!!" above them, mouth open in panic,
    slime flung off in a fine mist. The red speed streaks are so short they are the joke, and
    the trail behind him zigzags wildly.

15. CHEER
    Reared up as tall as he goes on the last third of his foot, both eyestalks flung skyward
    in a wide V with the little feelers up too, mouth open in a whoop, body arched proud in
    warm gold light. Behind him the slime trail curls up into a glittering silver victory
    streamer with a loop in it.

16. KO
    Flipped foot-up with his pale belly-foot to the sky, still gently rocking; all four
    tentacles flopped flat on the ground like dropped noodles, eyes reduced to little x's,
    mouth slack. Stars circle above him and a neat slime skid mark leads in from off-frame,
    showing exactly where he got clipped.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled in a loose S with both eyestalks crossed and tied in a soft overhand knot, a
    spiral in each eye, mouth slack in a woozy smile, his back striped with brush-bristle dust
    and street grit. Stars circle his head and the slime behind him is corkscrewed into a
    spiral where the sweeper spun him around.

18. SPLASH
    Bobbing in a burst of water, comically plumped and buoyant, his lower half already under a
    paler waterline, both eyestalks flung up and out in shock and his mouth an "oh!". A water
    crown and droplets burst all round him while his slime trail dissolves into pale milky
    curls behind — the actually sad part, and he knows it.

    SHEET NOTES
    • He is landscape where the rest of the roster is portrait — a long low body plus a
    trail. Paint all eighteen cells at one scale and keep his HEAD in roughly the same spot
    within each cell, or the engine's bottom-anchor will make him appear to slide between
    poses.
    • Crop hazard: the slime trail must END inside its cell and never run to the cut edge,
    or the cutter merges neighbouring frames into one blob. Give cheer and scared extra
    headroom — the raised eyestalks are the tallest thing on the sheet, and leap needs the
    widest cell.
    • Body length is his identity: keep it constant everywhere except crouch (deliberately
    concertina'd to half length) and leap / flee / dash-run (deliberately stretched). The
    black leopard-spot map and the darker mantle saddle over the front third stay in the
    same places in all eighteen frames.
    • Wet gloss is the whole read — one long specular highlight down the back in every
    single frame, including ko, dizzy and splash. A matte slug reads as a dead slug rather
    than a knocked-out one.
    • Asymmetry: his breathing hole sits on the RIGHT flank of the mantle only, so a
    mirrored run-l would put it on the wrong side. At 0.28s nobody will catch it, so run-l
    is genuinely optional here — paint it only if the sheet has room.
    • Bottom-anchored: foot and slime must touch the bottom of the cut in every frame except
    leap (airborne) and splash (half submerged). Suggested layout is 6 across x 3 down in
    the numbered order, white dividers, magenta #FF00FF ground.

RIVER OTTER      (files go to assets/chars/otter/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He has a raccoon's dexterous forepaws but none of a raccoon's legs: a long low spine,
stubby limbs and a thick rudder tail. So every raccoon standing pose becomes a rear up onto the
haunches with the tail braced behind as a third leg, and every raccoon run becomes either the
true humpback otter bound or a flat belly slide on wet pavement. His one constant prop is a
smooth green river stone he keeps jammed in the fur of his left armpit, the way real otters do,
and it appears in all eighteen frames.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four square facing the camera, long body relaxed into a lazy S curve, thick tail
    laid in a shallow arc to his right, head up and whiskers still dripping. Wet fur is peaked
    into little dark spikes along his spine and the smooth green river stone is tucked into
    the fur of his left armpit where he always keeps it.

 2. SIT
    Sat bolt upright on his haunches with the tail flat behind him doing the work of a third
    leg, both forepaws folded on his pale belly around the river stone, shoulders dropped and
    eyes half lidded. A puddle has quietly formed under him and he has made no move to leave
    it.

 3. EAT
    Flat on his back in a shallow puddle using his own chest as a table, the river stone
    balanced on his ribs as an anvil while both forepaws crack a mussel against it, hind feet
    up and loose in the air. Broken shell and a tipped paper tray of fried clams sit by his
    shoulder.

 4. CROUCH
    Coiled low with the spine compressed into a tight hump, elbows tucked under the chest,
    haunches loaded and hind feet planted wide on the shining road. Chin almost touching the
    ground, eyes locked forward up screen, tail flat and straight behind, stone still jammed
    in the armpit.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne and stretched to his whole ridiculous length like a thrown ribbon, forepaws
    reaching forward, hind legs and rudder tail streaming out behind. Belly toward the viewer,
    mouth open in a delighted hoot, a shower of water beads shaking loose off his fur.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted with the spine humping up behind them and the hind feet skidding in,
    whiskers thrown forward, head coming back up. A low crown of rainwater bursts around his
    forepaws and the tail whips upward to catch his balance.

 7. RUN-R
    Side on bounding gallop to the right in the true otter humpback rhythm, back arched high
    at the middle, fore and hind paws gathering underneath him, tail streaming level behind.
    Ears pinned flat, stone clamped in the left armpit, little puddle splashes kicking up
    behind each paw.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out belly slide to the right, forelegs tucked back along his flanks, hind feet still
    shoving, chin an inch off the road. A peeled wake of rainwater curls off both shoulders
    with white speed streaks behind, cheeks pushed back by the wind, eyes wide with glee.

 9. COFFEE
    Reared upright on hind legs with the tail braced behind as a tripod, a takeaway cup
    gripped in both forepaws and already half crushed, the river stone dropped and bouncing at
    his feet. Pink steam off the lid, whiskers standing straight out, pupils tiny, one hind
    foot drumming with fire streaks licking up off the wet pavement.

10. MAGNET
    Up on his haunches with his head back and mouth open, juggling three gold bottlecaps in a
    bright arc above his forepaws while more caps and a spray of fried clams come spiralling
    in toward his chest. The tail thumps the puddle behind him and sends up little rings.

11. UMBRELLA
    Sat upright holding a small open umbrella at a lazy tilt, so half his face still gets
    rained on and he could not care less, eyes lidded and smug, stone parked in his lap. Rain
    sheets off the umbrella rim in a bright curtain down one side.

12. SHIELD
    Dropped low and braced with both forepaws up in front of his muzzle, whiskers flattened
    back, eyes screwed shut, tail clamped hard along the ground. A blue violet energy dome
    flashes tight around him with rain and grit streaking off its surface, and the river stone
    hangs in the air by his ribs where he flinched it loose.

13. SCARED
    Reared into the bolt upright periscope stand otters do, tail as tripod, both forepaws
    clutched to his chest around the stone, neck stretched as tall as it goes. Eyes huge and
    rolled upward, mouth a small round O, every wet hair on his back standing in spikes.

14. FLEE
    Belly sliding hard to the left in flat panic, forepaws thrown out ahead, mouth wide open,
    tail thrashing behind. Red speed streaks and two exclamation marks over his head, dirty
    water fanning off both sides of his chest, and for once the river stone is tumbling loose
    behind him, abandoned.

15. CHEER
    Reared to full height on hind legs and tail, one forepaw punched at the sky holding the
    river stone overhead like a trophy, head thrown back, mouth open in a whooping chirp.
    Water flings off the tip of his tail in a bright arc and his belly fur is still gloriously
    soaked.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with all four short legs in the air and the tail folded under him, mouth
    open, tongue out the side, gold stars circling his head. The river stone has landed on his
    belly and simply sits there, riding out the count.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side in a loose comma with the tail curled up over his own nose, spiral
    eyes, tongue lolling, one forepaw patting weakly at the road for the stone that came to
    rest just out of reach. Stars orbit slowly overhead.

18. SPLASH
    Mid cannonball into the canal, knees tucked and forepaws hugging them, chin down, eyes
    squeezed shut in pure joy and mouth open in a happy shriek, stone clamped safe under his
    arm. A tall white water crown bursts up around him. This is the one death frame where the
    character is winning, and he must read as a critter who jumped in on purpose.

    SHEET NOTES
    • The tail is a third of his silhouette. In leap, run-r, dash-run and flee it runs far
    past the body, so give those frames extra horizontal room and never let the cut clip the
    tail tip.
    • He is bottom anchored, so keep the ground contact line consistent and keep the HEAD
    the same size in all eighteen frames. The belly slide and lying frames flatten him out
    and will read as a smaller animal unless the head holds scale.
    • Asymmetry warning: the river stone lives in the LEFT armpit or left forepaw in every
    frame. A mirrored run-l would teleport it to the other side, so paint the optional run-l
    for real rather than letting the engine flip run-r.
    • The lying frames (eat, ko, dizzy) need his body mass centred over the anchor point,
    otherwise he appears to slide off the tile rather than lie on it.
    • The splash water crown towers well above his head. Leave headroom above him in that
    painting so the burst is not sheared off at the crop line, and keep the wet fur spikes
    on his spine in every frame, he is never dry.

GREAT BLUE HERON      (files go to assets/chars/heron/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He has no hands, so the bill and the crook of the folded neck do all the carrying: a
cup balances on the bill tip, an umbrella handle hooks over the neck-S like a coat hook, and
food goes down the throat whole in one visible lump. Where Jimothy throws his arms about, the
heron uses wings — mantled forward as a cape, thrown wide as a shawl — and where Jimothy panics
with his whole body, the heron panics only from the ankles up: the feet stay planted and the
face never changes, because the stillness is the joke.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square on both stilt legs, dead still, neck folded into a shallow S so the
    head rides low over the shoulders and the yellow dagger bill points level at the camera.
    Shaggy slate chest plumes hang without stirring, the single thin black crest feather
    trails off the back of the skull, and the gold eye is fixed on the viewer and very
    slightly narrowed — nothing on him moves, and that is the pose.

 2. SIT
    Off duty: one leg has vanished entirely up into the belly feathers, the whole bird settled
    into a soft grey bell on a single stilt, neck folded flat so the head rests straight down
    on the shoulders and the bill lies along the chest. Eyes half-lidded, plumes puffed out
    over the tucked foot, a couple of loose feathers drifting — a wet lawn ornament having a
    moment to himself.

 3. EAT
    Bill tipped straight up at the sky with the last inch of a paper cone of fries
    disappearing into it, and a fat comedy lump already halfway down the long throat,
    stretching the feathers as it goes. Both feet planted, the spilled rest of the carton by
    his toes, and one gold eye rolled down to hold eye contact with the camera the entire time
    he swallows.

 4. CROUCH
    Coiled to strike: hocks folded back deep, body dropped low over the toes, neck compressed
    into a tight spring-loaded Z with the bill aimed forward like a nocked arrow. Wings still
    shut tight to the body, crest feather flattened back, gold eye wide and locked on the row
    ahead — all the energy is stored, none of it has leaked out yet.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne with both huge wings thrown open at the top of the downbeat, primaries
    fanned and rain-flecked, neck folded tight into the shoulders and the long legs streaming
    straight out behind him like trailing landing gear. Bill forward, chest plumes blown back,
    a scatter of loose down and drizzle in the air under him.

 6. LAND
    Wings cupped hard forward to brake, feathers curled and catching the air, toes splayed
    enormous and reaching down for the pavement with the knees folding to absorb it. Neck
    flung out forward for balance, bill level, one splash-ring of rainwater bursting up around
    the touching foot.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on to the right at a long heron stride, one leg fully extended forward and the other
    trailing straight back, wings half-cracked open as outriggers, neck folded neatly into
    place. He is moving fast and looks like he is merely walking somewhere on purpose — a
    slight tilt of the body is the only concession to speed.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out: legs at full scissor stretch, body low and level, wings held half-open and
    steady, white speed streaks tearing past his shins and pavement spray kicking up behind
    the back toe. The neck stays folded and the face stays utterly composed, which is the
    entire gag — the legs are a blur, the head glides along a perfectly straight line.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing wired, both feet planted, a takeaway cup balanced impossibly on the very tip of
    the upturned bill with pink steam curling off it. Crest feather standing bolt upright,
    chest plumes fanned electric, pupil shrunk to a pinprick inside a huge gold iris, and one
    stilt leg vibrating with a small motion-blur where it meets the ground.

10. MAGNET
    Feet rooted, bill cranked wide open at the sky like a delivery chute while fries, fish
    scraps and gold bottlecaps come spiralling in on curved motion-arcs from every side, one
    fry already in mid-drop down the gullet. Wings barely raised, expression barely changed —
    he has not taken a single step toward any of it and does not intend to.

11. UMBRELLA
    An open umbrella hangs off the crook of his folded neck by its hook — no hands needed, the
    neck-S is the coat hook — canopy tilted over his head, rain hammering the pavement in
    every direction but on him. Standing on one leg with the other tucked away, bill level,
    eye half-lidded and thoroughly smug beneath the dry patch.

12. SHIELD
    The instant of the hit: both wings mantled forward and up into a cloak over his face, a
    blue-violet energy dome flaring around him with rain streaking off it in sparks. One gold
    eye peers out through the gap between the primaries, and his feet have not moved a
    millimetre from where they were.

13. SCARED
    Full reed-alarm: body slimmed to a stick, every feather clamped flat, neck stretched dead
    vertical and the bill aimed straight up at the sky like a spear at whatever is circling.
    Eyes rolled up to the top of their sockets, one crest feather quivering, thin nervous
    motion-ticks either side of the head — the most dignified terror in Seattle.

HARBOR SEAL      (files go to assets/chars/seal/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He has flippers and no legs, so every pose Jimothy solves with hands or hind legs the
seal has to solve with his belly: he props on two stubby fore-flippers, hauls himself along in
an inchworm galumph, balances things on his nose instead of holding them, and rears only from
the ribs up while his back half stays a heavy sausage on the wet pavement. The comedy is that he
is magnificently unfit for a street-crossing game and completely unbothered about it — and on
exactly two frames, cheer and splash, his body is better at the job than anyone else's.

 1. IDLE
    Propped up harbor-seal fashion on the wet asphalt: body flat from the ribs back, chest
    lifted on two stubby fore-flippers, head high and tipped a fraction back, big black wet
    eyes on the camera, whiskers relaxed. A rain-sheen highlight runs the length of his spine
    and a dark damp smear of pavement sits under his belly — that smear is his signature and
    rides under him in every frame.

 2. SIT
    Fully off duty: the classic harbor-seal banana, head lifted and rear flippers lifted at
    the same time so he is balanced on his ribs like a rocking chair. Eyes half shut, mouth
    curved into a small satisfied line, one fore-flipper folded onto his belly — the pose of
    an animal who genuinely believes he pays rent here.

 3. EAT
    Nose-deep in a red-and-white paper basket of fish and chips pinned under one fore-flipper,
    head thrown back mid-gulp with a whole battered cod half-swallowed and its tail still

    Poured flat to the ground, everything shoved forward, spine compressed into a low hump

 5. LEAP

    tight to his sides, rear flippers trailing and fanned open, belly forward, mouth slightly

    touching his body or the spray mass.

 6. LAND
    Belly-first touchdown, chest low, shoulders taking the whole weight, fore-flippers splayed
    out sideways for balance, a visible ripple travelling backwards through his blubber.

    the impact.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on, caught mid-galumph travelling right: fore-flippers planted, spine humped up into
    an inchworm arc, rear flippers just whipping forward to catch up with him, head craned
    round so the camera still gets his face. A long wet drag streak on the pavement behind
    him.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same galumph gone flat out — the inchworm arc snapped straight at full extension,
    flippers blurred, eyes wide, mouth open. Orange-white speed streaks under the belly and a

 9. COFFEE
    Standing propped on his fore-flippers with a lidded takeaway cup balanced perfectly on the

10. MAGNET
    Reared up on his fore-flippers with his mouth wide open, catching a herring clean out of

11. UMBRELLA

12. SHIELD

    blown inside out and skidding away at the frame edge, rain streaking hard off the outside
    of the dome.

13. SCARED
    Reared as high as a seal can rear — chest and neck stretched straight up off the ground

14. FLEE
    Galumphing hard to the left in complete undignified terror, spine buckling in a big
    travelling wave, blubber wobbling a beat behind the motion, one rear flipper flailing

    streaks and two '!!' marks in the air behind him.

15. CHEER
    He claps — reared up tall on his belly, head thrown back, mouth wide open barking with

16. KO

17. DIZZY

18. SPLASH
    Taken by the spill and tumbling belly-up in white froth, water bursting around him, eyes

    rulings and generous gaps; his long horizontal silhouette plus the drag streak will
    overrun any fixed grid and the cutter will slice him in half.
    • The spray, the drag smear and the wet puddle under him are part of the sprite, not the
    background — keep them inside the crop and consistent in size, or he will look like he
    is floating on some frames and grounded on others.
    • Detached specks get eaten by the component cutter: droplets, bottlecaps, sparks, '!!'
    marks and the circling stars must touch his body or touch a connected effect mass,
    otherwise they vanish on the cut.
    • run-l is optional and safe to skip — his spot pattern reads fine mirrored, and the
    only asymmetric props (umbrella over the right shoulder, cup on the nose, chip basket)
    live on standing frames the engine never mirrors.
    • idle, crouch, leap and land already exist in assets/chars/seal/ — match their camera
    height, silver-grey spotted hide, cream belly and existing water-spray language exactly
    so the 14 new frames sit in the same set.

stand: reared bolt upright, balanced and quivering on a fanned tail fin, holding a leap he never
finished. Every travel pose becomes a flip rather than a run, and anything Jimothy holds in his
paws is instead wedged in the hook of the salmon's kype, stuck flat to his wet flank, or climbed
into.

 1. IDLE
    Reared bolt upright and balanced on his fanned tail fin like a leap he never finished,
    crimson body in a soft S, both pectoral fins held out for balance, olive-green hooked jaw
    level and one big round eye fixed on the viewer. Baked in: a wobble blur at the tail tip,
    a spreading puddle ring where fin meets asphalt, rain beading down the flank, and the
    snapped brass lure tangled in his dorsal.

 2. SIT
    Balance abandoned. He has slumped onto his side in a gutter puddle, curled into a comma,

 4. CROUCH
    Compressed into a tight loaded C, nose down, tail wound under him, fins flattened hard to

 5. LEAP

 7. RUN-R
    Mid-flip to the right, body arced into a working comma, humped back cresting, one pectoral

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same flip at flat out speed: body stretched long, three overlapping afterimages of the
    tail beat, a rooster-tail of spray thrown up behind him. Jaw clamped shut, eye wild, hot

 9. COFFEE
    Reared on his tail with a takeaway cup wedged in the crook of his hooked jaw, because a

10. MAGNET
    Reared up with his jaws wide in a full feeding gape while gold bottlecaps and fries spiral

11. UMBRELLA
    He has flipped a cheap black umbrella canopy-up, let it fill with rainwater and climbed

12. SHIELD

13. SCARED
    Bolt upright on his tail and rigid as a bowling pin, both pectoral fins clamped over the

14. FLEE

    the storm run-off pouring down past him toward the viewer while he fights UP it, because
    even fleeing he does not know another direction.

15. CHEER
    Top of the arc, the biggest leap on the sheet: body in a perfect backlit crescent above a
    great fan of thrown water, tail kicked, jaw open in a joyful gasp, every scale lit gold by
    the streetlight. The battered lure still hangs off his dorsal and catches the light too,
    worn like a medal. He got upstream.

16. KO
    Flat on his side on dry asphalt, tail bent up, one pectoral fin flopped over his eye, jaw
    slack with a single bubble escaping and a ring of small stars circling his head. The
    quietest frame in the game: paint a puddle two inches from his nose that he cannot reach,
    and let the rain fall everywhere except on him.

17. DIZZY
    Wrung into a loose corkscrew by the sweeper brush, tail wound around his own middle so the
    twist turns his face nearly to camera, both eyes spiralled, jaw hanging open, gills
    fluttering. Wet leaves and stiff bristles stuck all over the flank, a small tornado of
    litter still spinning around him, stars orbiting his head.

18. SPLASH
    His homecoming, and the one death on the roster that is a happy ending. He bursts up out
    of the storm water in a joy-roll, body twisted, an enormous white water crown exploding
    around him and streetlight refracting gold through the spray. Gills finally flared pink
    and working properly, eye wide with relief, already angled upstream toward the drain and
    the Sound beyond.

    SHEET NOTES
    • Identity in all 18: spawning colours are the costume. Crimson-red flank (#d4685e),
    pale peach belly (#f2b3a2), olive-green hooked jaw and humped back, plus one snapped
    brass spoon lure tangled in the dorsal fin trailing a short frayed leader. That lure is
    his silhouette anchor and his medal, so it stays on even in cheer and ko. Tangled around
    the fin, never through it. Nothing gory on this sheet.
    • Scale trap: nose-to-tail-tip must measure identical across all 18 frames. He coils in
    crouch and stretches in leap, and it is very easy to paint the coiled frames small and
    the airborne ones huge. Measure him against a fixed length line before inking.
    • Bounding box swings from tall-and-thin (idle, scared, coffee, umbrella) to wide-and-
    flat (sit, ko, dizzy). Sprites are bottom-anchored, so put real ground mass at the very
    bottom of the airborne frames (leap, cheer, splash): the puddle ring, the water crown,
    the thrown fan. Without it he will hover an inch above the road.
    • Asymmetry is severe on a fish. Eye, hooked jaw and the lure all read on ONE flank
    only, so mirroring run-r for run-l flips his eye to the wrong side and moves the lure
    across his back. Paint a real run-l for this character rather than letting the engine
    mirror.
    • Cutting hazard on magenta: his pale peach belly sits close to #FF00FF in hue and a
    border-flood cut can eat into it. Keep the belly warm and peachy rather than pink, give
    the whole silhouette a thin dark outline, and check the belly first on every cut sprite.
    • Crop hazards: the tail fin tip, the trailing frayed leader and the exclamation marks
    in flee are all thin and easy to clip. Leave a clear margin on all four sides of every
    frame.
    • The umbrella in frames 11 and 12 nearly doubles his width. Paint it half-furled and
    tucked around him so he still reads at the same on-screen size as the other sixteen
    frames.
    • Lay out with white divider lines and generous gaps, not an even grid. The coiled
    frames and the stretched frames have wildly different footprints and a fixed grid will
    slice a tail fin clean off.

    all that weight, fluke cocked up and back like a coiled spring, eyes forward and mouth set
    in a flat determined line. A ring of water squeezes out from under his belly where he
    presses down, and the dorsal fin is raked forward with the gather.

 5. LEAP
    A full porpoise, completely airborne and stretched long across the frame, back arched,
    white belly turned to the viewer, both flippers flared out wide like wings and the fluke
    trailing behind him. Sheets of gutter water still peel off his flanks, a burst spray ring
    hangs below where he left the road, and his mouth is open in absolute delight with his
    eyes squeezed into happy crescents.

 6. LAND
    Belly-first touchdown, flippers braced forward and splayed, chin down and jowls compressed
    against the road, fluke slapping down flat behind him. A fat crown of dirty street water
    throws out to both sides at the impact line, and he blinks once, eyes half shut, entirely
    pleased with the mess he has made.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on and travelling right in a long low galumphing arc, body barely clearing the
    asphalt, near flipper reaching forward to pull and the far one paddling behind, fluke
    kicked up mid-stroke. Full profile puts the notched dorsal fin and grey saddle patch right
    where you can read them, with spray fanning out under his belly and a wet skid line
    behind.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same galumph flat out: body stretched to its longest, fluke a blur, both flippers
    slapping the tarmac hard, a rooster tail of gutter water flung up behind him. Speed
    streaks rake past his flanks, his eyes are wide and his jaw is open in a huge grin, and
    the spreading wake he leaves would drench a small car.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing tall on both flippers, wired, with a takeaway cup balanced dead centre on the
    flat tip of one flipper because he cannot grip anything, lid off and pink steam curling
    up. Eyes pinned wide, pupils tiny, fire and speed streaks licking along under his belly,
    three empty cups tipped over in the puddle around him.

10. MAGNET
    Reared up on his flippers with his jaws thrown wide open in pure joy, eyes squeezed shut,
    gold bottlecaps, fries and little silver fish spiralling in toward his mouth from every
    edge of the frame. The pull ripples his own puddle outward in rings and a few caps glint
    hard against the black of his hide.

11. UMBRELLA
    A little striped café patio umbrella wedged upright behind his dorsal fin, covering maybe
    a third of him while rain hammers the rest of that enormous back. Half-lidded eyes, the
    faintest smug smile, flippers folded neatly in front — completely convinced he has solved
    the weather.

12. SHIELD
    The instant it eats a hit: chin tucked, both flippers crossed up in front of his face,
    whole body braced and shoved back a few inches, a blue-violet energy dome flaring white-
    hot at the point of impact around him. Rain streaks off the dome in bright arcs, one eye
    screwed shut, and the little umbrella is blown inside out and skidding away beside him.

13. SCARED
    Reared as high as he can get on trembling flippers, head craned back and up at the sky,
    eyes huge and perfectly round, mouth a tiny O in all that black bulk. The fluke is flat
    and rigid behind him, a sweat bead flies off his brow and his own puddle is shivering with
    concentric ripples.

14. FLEE
    Galumphing hard toward the left in a panic, mouth wide open, flippers flailing out of
    rhythm, fluke thrashing, dorsal fin leaning steeply into the turn. Red speed streaks and
    two fat exclamation marks trail behind him with a wall of flung water, and his eyes are
    white-rimmed and locked forward.

15. CHEER
    A leaning back-arched breach staged wide across the frame, front end thrown up off the
    road, both flippers flung high and open, jaws wide in a joyful open-mouthed shout. A crown
    of spray bursts under him where the fluke slapped down, gold bottlecaps confetti through
    the air, and the rain around him catches warm light.

16. KO
    Rolled onto his side with the white belly toward the viewer, flippers limp in the air,
    fluke flopped over, mouth hanging slack open and eyes shut into little upturned lines.
    Stars circle slowly around his head, the notched dorsal fin lies over sideways against the
    road, and a wide puddle spreads out from under him with rain rings landing in it.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled in a loose S on the wet road, spiral eyes, tongue lolling out one side of his
    jaw, dorsal fin bent right over like a wilted leaf. Stars orbit his head, both flippers
    are splayed uselessly out to the sides, and an arc of swept wet leaves and litter curves
    past him where the sweeper went through.

18. SPLASH
    He has hit the water and he is the only one in this game who is glad about it: flippers
    flung up, body sinking chest-deep in a huge white burst of water, spout going up off his
    head, eyes shut in bliss. Bubbles boil around him and a wave crown closes over the saddle
    patch, leaving just the notched dorsal fin and a small wistful smile above the surface as
    he goes home.

    SHEET NOTES
    • SCALE TRAP — every one of the 18 frames must be WIDER THAN TALL. The engine width-
    sizes any pose whose aspect is over 1.12 and height-sizes anything under it, so one tall
    frame (cheer, scared, coffee) makes him snap to a completely different size mid-
    animation. Stage all the rears and the breach as diagonals or leaning arcs, never as
    vertical columns.
    • Keep the same amount of orca in every frame measured SNOUT TO FLUKE, not by the
    bounding box — spray, the umbrella and the flung bottlecaps push the box around and will
    fool a size-match by eye.
    • The dorsal fin is the top of his silhouette in nearly every frame and the notch in its
    trailing edge is his ID, the way a hard hat is somebody else's. Leave a few pixels of
    magenta above the fin so the crop never shaves the notch off, and keep the notch on the
    SAME side in all 18.
    • ASYMMETRY — the fin notch, the grey saddle patch and the coffee-cup flipper are all
    one-sided, so paint the optional 19th frame run-l properly rather than let the engine
    mirror run-r. A mirrored orca reads as a different whale from the other side of the pod.
    • He is near-black (#14161a) on wet black asphalt, and the dark shop finishes darken him
    further. Every frame needs a bright rim light down his back and white spray or standing
    water breaking his outline, or he disappears into the road.
    • Bottom-anchored: the puddle and the point where his belly meets the ground are part of
    the crop. Keep that contact line at the very bottom edge and at the same height across
    all 18 or he will bob up and down between poses.
    • leap, dash-run, flee and ko will be by far the widest cels on the sheet. Give them
    their own rows with generous white divider lines and plenty of margin — do NOT lay this
    sheet out on an even grid, he is long enough to get sliced in half.
