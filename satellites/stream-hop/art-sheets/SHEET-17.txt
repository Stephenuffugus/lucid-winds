CITY PIGEON      (files go to assets/chars/pigeon/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He has wings instead of arms and a beak instead of hands, so every "holding" pose
becomes a coral foot pinning the object down while the beak does the work, and every "arms up"
pose becomes a wing throw. He also cannot sit on his haunches like a raccoon, so sitting is a
settled curb loaf with the feet tucked away under his chest, and lying on his back puts two
stiff coral feet straight up in the air, which is funnier than anything a raccoon could do.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square on wet asphalt, body level, chest out, head up and slightly cocked so
    one orange-ringed eye reads to camera; slate wings folded tight with the two black bars
    showing, green-and-violet neck sheen catching the streetlight, coral feet planted wide and
    unbothered. This is the pose he owns the crosswalk in.

 2. SIT
    Settled into a full curb loaf, breast down on the concrete, feet completely tucked out of
    sight under the feathers so he is just a warm grey brick with a head; feathers puffed a
    size larger, eye half-lidded, beak tipped down. A wet gleam along his back and a drying
    puddle beside him.

 3. EAT
    Head plunged into a tipped-over paper cone of fries, one coral foot clamped on the cone to
    stop it rolling, a single fry crosswise in his beak and his eye rolled up at the camera
    mid-theft. Crumbs, salt and one hopeful crust scattered on the pavement around him.

 4. CROUCH
    Body dropped low and horizontal, legs folded deep, feathers slicked flat to the skin,
    wings unlatched and just cracked open at the shoulders; head thrust forward, eye locked
    up-screen, tail pressed to the ground. All of him loaded like a spring, nothing loose.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, both wings hauled up to their widest arc with the primaries fanned, breast
    forward, coral feet trailing and curled beneath him, beak open in a delighted coo. Two
    loose down feathers hang in the air where he left the ground.

 6. LAND
    Wings swept back and cupped hard against the air to brake, tail fanned wide and angled
    down like a flap, both coral feet stabbing the pavement with the toes splayed; head low,
    eye forward, neck feathers blown backward by his own stop. A small burst of grit and
    rainwater kicks up under him.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on to the right in the flat-out pigeon scurry, wings clamped shut against the body,
    head thrust forward on a stretched neck mid-bob, one coral foot forward and one back at
    full stride. Body tipped slightly nose-down, tail up, all business, no flying involved.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same scurry but absolutely committed: neck at full extension, wings half-cocked out
    for balance, both feet blurred into a coral smear, crown feathers streaming back. Speed
    streaks trail off his tail and a rooster tail of puddle spray fans up behind him.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing wired over a takeaway cup on its side, one coral foot pinning the lid and his
    beak jammed through the sip hole with pink steam curling out around it; feathers stood on
    end, eye wide with a pin-point pupil. Orange fire streaks and vibration lines flicker
    around his feet.

10. MAGNET
    Standing tall with both wings flung open like a net and beak wide in an open-mouthed coo,
    gold bottlecaps, chip crusts and fries spiralling in toward his chest from every edge of
    the frame. Eye enormous and joyful, one foot lifted clean off the ground with the sheer
    greed of it.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing bone-dry beneath a blown-out umbrella somebody abandoned in the gutter, its
    handle wedged in a storm drain and its canopy tipped over him like a roof while rain
    hammers the asphalt all around his dry circle. He does not look up. Chest out, eye to
    camera, entirely smug.

12. SHIELD
    Braced low with both wings folded up over his head and crossed in front of his face, one
    eye squeezed shut and the other peeking, tail feathers splayed for balance; a blue-violet
    energy dome snaps around him with rain streaking off its curve. Loose feathers pop off his
    shoulders from the impact.

13. SCARED
    Frozen rigid with his neck periscoped straight up and his beak pointed at the sky, one
    foot still lifted mid-step, neck hackles ruffed out into a collar, both eyes huge and
    white-ringed. A gull's wing shadow falls across the pavement and up his chest.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left in blind panic, wings hammering the air but never actually lifting off,
    beak wide open, neck stretched flat out ahead of him, coral feet a blur. Red speed
    streaks, a scatter of shed feathers behind him and two "!!" marks over his head.

15. CHEER
    Standing at full height with both wings thrown up in a wide V, chest puffed and the
    iridescent neck inflated into a full courting balloon, beak open in a triumphant coo.
    Loose down and flecks of white crosswalk paint drift around him like confetti.

16. KO
    Flat on his back on the asphalt, both stiff coral feet pointing straight up at the sky,
    wings splayed to either side, beak hanging open, stars circling his head. A slow cloud of
    loose down still hangs in the air above him. Comic, never gory.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side with wings limp and head lolled back on a rubbery neck, spiral swirls
    where his eyes were, beak hanging open; stars orbit his crown while one coral foot
    twitches in the air. Sweeper bristles and a fan of loose feathers litter the ground around
    him.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked to the skin in gutter water, feathers plastered flat so he reads as half his own
    size, both wings flung up, beak open in shock, eyes bulging; a fat crown of water bursts
    around him. Ripples and a couple of floating feathers spread across the puddle at his
    feet.

    SHEET NOTES
    • Four frames already exist and are LIVE (idle, crouch, leap, land in
    assets/chars/pigeon/). Match their exact camera, scale and palette on the other
    fourteen: slate blue-grey body, pale grey coverts with two hard black wing bars, green-
    and-violet iridescent neck, orange-ringed eye, coral-red feet, dark tail band. Do not
    repaint those four unless the whole sheet is repainted together.
    • Identity marker: a dried fleck of white crosswalk paint across the base of his tail
    (he sleeps on fresh street lines). Keep it centred on the tail in every frame so the
    sheet stays mirror-safe.
    • Because that marker is centred and his plumage is symmetric, run-l can be mirrored
    from run-r. No nineteenth frame needed for this character.
    • Crop hazard: leap, cheer, land and shield run 1.5-1.8x the width of idle because of
    the open wings. Give those rows extra horizontal space and a clear divider line on both
    sides so a wingtip never touches a neighbour and gets read as one blob by the cutter.
    • Crop hazard: ko, dizzy and splash are horizontal bodies. Bottom-anchor them on the
    ground contact line (his back or side), not on where the feet would be, or he floats
    above the street in game.
    • Keep the head at the same height in every standing frame (idle, eat, coffee, magnet,
    umbrella, scared, cheer). sit is the one deliberate exception. He must not appear to
    grow or shrink between states.
    • He is a small bird next to a raccoon. Paint him at roughly the same on-screen height
    as Jimothy's frames so the engine's uniform scale does not turn him into a giant pigeon
    or a speck.
    • The coral feet and the orange eye ring are the only warm colours on an entirely grey
    animal. Keep them saturated. At gameplay size they are the whole read.

BALLARD CROW      (files go to assets/chars/crow/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — A crow has no hands, so the beak and one pinning foot do every job Jimothy's paws did:
it stands on the fry carton to eat, tucks its feet away into a loaf instead of sitting on
haunches, and mantles its wings over the snack pile rather than hugging it. Wings take over as
the arms for every big shape — thrown up in a V to cheer, clapped over the head to brace,
splayed flat for the KO — and the one thing that never changes pose is the gold-amber eye, which
finds the camera in all eighteen frames, including the two where it is upside down.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square on wet asphalt facing the camera, weight even, wings folded tight so
    the oil-slick blue-green sheen shows across the coverts, nape hackles slightly ruffled.
    Head level, beak straight at the viewer, gold-amber eye locked on you: calm, unhurried,
    and absolutely taking notes.

 2. SIT
    Sunk into a loaf on the curb, feet vanished up under the belly feathers so the body
    becomes one rounded black cushion, head pulled down into the shoulders until the neck
    disappears. Feathers puffed against the weather, rain beading and running off the back,
    eye half-lidded but still aimed at you.

 3. EAT
    Standing over a spilled carton of fries with one scaly foot planted flat on top of it,
    pinning it down the way crows actually do, head cranked low and the beak closed around a
    single fry mid-tug. Fries scattered on the wet ground around the carton, one gold eye
    rolled up to the camera, daring you to come closer.

 4. CROUCH
    Body dropped horizontal over deeply flexed legs, plumage slicked tight so the silhouette
    narrows to a loaded dart, tail angled down, wings cracked open a hand's width at the
    wrists. Head thrust forward and low, beak level, eye wide and fixed dead ahead: all
    spring, no lift yet.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne and climbing, wings beating down and forward in a strong half-spread with
    the primaries fanned, feet folded back under the tail. Chest thrown toward the camera,
    beak slightly open, one loose down feather trailing behind in the wake.

 6. LAND
    Wings cupped hard forward to brake with the tail fanned wide beneath as an airbrake, both
    feet thrown out ahead and toes spread to grab the ground. Head already coming back up and
    eyes forward on the next row, a small crown of water droplets kicked up under the feet.

 7. RUN-R
    Mid two-footed side hop to the right, both feet together and clear of the ground, body low
    and level, wings tucked but flicked half-open at the wrists for balance with the tail
    flared to steer. The head stays square to the camera even as the body travels sideways:
    cocky, and watching you the whole way across.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Cheating outright, a flat-out low skim a hand above the asphalt with wings blurred into a
    hard downbeat, feet trailing straight back, body stretched long and level. Wingtips clip a
    puddle and throw twin sheets of spray, motion streaks stacked behind, gold eye narrowed to
    a slit.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing over a stolen takeaway cup with the lid torn off, one foot clamped on the rim to
    hold it steady, beak dipped in and head tipping back mid-swallow. Pink steam curls off the
    cup, jitter lines shiver from every feather, hackles standing straight up and the pupil
    pinned to a dot inside the gold iris.

10. MAGNET
    Hunched low and mantling, wings spread forward and down into a greedy tent over an
    incoming heap of fries and gold bottlecaps, exactly how a crow hides food from other
    crows. Beak wide in a caw, caps and food streaking in from every side on bright spiral
    trails, both eyes blazing.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing dry and deeply smug in the one dry patch under a blown-out found umbrella, its
    bent ribs wedged at an angle against the curb. Feathers perfectly smooth, one foot lifted
    and tucked into the belly, rain hammering down all round the rim and running off in
    strings, head tilted at the camera: yes, this is mine now.

12. SHIELD
    Both wings clapped forward over the head and face like a shield made of feathers, head
    jammed down into the shoulders, feet braced and skidding on the wet road. A blue-violet
    energy dome flares around it with rain streaking off the curve, three loose black feathers
    knocked free and hanging in the air, and one gold eye glaring out through the gap in the
    primaries.

13. SCARED
    Crouched low with the head cranked straight up at the sky, beak wide open in an alarm caw,
    nape hackles flared into a spiky ruff. Wings half-lifted off the body ready to bail and
    tail clamped down, both eyes huge: a crow being bullied by a bird with worse manners.

14. FLEE
    A panicked half-flying scramble to the left, feet still pedalling on the asphalt while the
    wings beat out of sync and the body tips forward past its own balance. Beak open mid-caw,
    red speed streaks behind, two exclamation marks overhead and a thin trail of shed black
    feathers.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall with both wings thrown straight UP into a triumphant V, kept vertical rather
    than spread wide, chest pushed forward, head tipped all the way back, beak open in a caw
    you can hear from the sidewalk. One foot lifted off the ground, two feathers popping loose
    overhead, gold eye creased with joy.

16. KO
    Flat on its back on the wet asphalt, both feet straight up in the air with the toes curled
    in the great comic dead-bird pose, wings splayed limp to either side, beak open with the
    little pointed tongue out. Cartoon stars circle the head, and one gold eye is still open,
    still aimed straight at the camera, still keeping score.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled back on its tail with legs stuck out in front and both wings drooping like empty
    sleeves, head lolling onto one shoulder. Spiral gold eyes, beak hanging open, stars
    orbiting, and a slow drift of loose black feathers still settling around it from whatever
    just went past.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked through and suddenly half its own width, feathers plastered flat to a scrawny
    pencil neck and knobbly bare legs, the way a wet crow genuinely looks. Wings held out and
    up in a shocked shrug, beak wide open, a white burst of water exploding up around it and
    droplets flying, both gold eyes absolutely appalled.

    SHEET NOTES
    • NEAR-BLACK ON WET ASPHALT: this bird is drawn small on a dark road with only a soft
    ground shadow under it. Every one of the 18 frames needs a cool pale blue-grey rim light
    down the top and back edge, and the gold-amber eye kept at full saturation. The rim and
    the eye ARE the silhouette; without them the crow is a hole in the road. (The engine's
    procedural fallback strokes exactly this rim for the same reason.)
    • WINGSPAN CONTROLS SCALE, so keep it tight. The engine sizes any frame wider than
    1.12:1 by WIDTH (74px) and everything else by HEIGHT (59px). A full 2:1 wingspread
    therefore renders the bird's BODY about 37% smaller than in idle, and he must not change
    size between frames. Cap the spread at roughly 1.4x the body height in leap, land, dash-
    run and flee, and in cheer throw the wings UP in a vertical V rather than out sideways.
    • CUTTING: one pose per cell with white divider lines and generous gaps, never a fixed
    even grid. Spread wings and fanned tails are the exact case where a grid shaves a
    wingtip, and a crow missing half a primary reads as a broken bird, not a fast one. Lay
    it out in rows of three or four with the gaps wide enough that no wingtip touches a
    divider.
    • SCALE ANCHOR: four paintings already exist and are good (assets/chars/crow/idle.png,
    crouch.png, leap.png, land.png). Either keep those four and paint the other 14 to match
    their scale, lighting and camera exactly, or repaint all 18 in one sitting. Do not mix
    two sittings' scales on one character.
    • run-l is NOT needed. The crow carries no costume and no permanent prop, so mirroring
    run-r is free and safe. The only asymmetric props in this spec (the fry carton, the
    coffee cup, the umbrella) live in standing frames that are never mirrored.
    • DO NOT confuse the playable crow with the crow HAZARD. sprites/haz-crowdive.png is a
    different crow that dives at the player from above. Keep all 18 of these ground-based,
    camera-facing and eye-to-viewer so the two never read as the same bird in the same
    scene.
    • LOOSE BLACK FEATHERS are this character's version of Jimothy's dust puffs and speed
    streaks. Use them in shield, flee, cheer, ko and dizzy. They are free, they are on-
    brand, and a couple of hanging feathers read clearly even at 60px.
    • ENGINE FOLLOW-UP once the art lands: the crow's roster entry (index.html line 699)
    needs full:1 added alongside art:1, or the engine will keep folding all 14 new poses
    back onto idle/crouch/leap/land via POSE4 and none of this will ever appear.

SEAGULL      (files go to assets/chars/seagull/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He has no hands, so the bill does all the holding and the wings do all the arm work —
thrown up in a V for the cheer, clapped forward over his head for the shield, half-open for
balance in the runs. "Sitting" is a gull loaf, legs vanishing under the breast instead of
folding into a lap, and any prop he physically cannot grip is solved by the world instead: the
umbrella stands wedged upright on its own, the coffee goes down bill-first in one tip, and the
fry stays pinned under a webbed foot.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square on the wet pavement facing the viewer, weight even on both pale
    orange webbed feet, wings folded tight with the black primaries crossed over the tail,
    neck drawn in a little against the drizzle. One stolen french fry is clamped crosswise in
    the yellow bill and his dark eye is locked dead on camera with absolutely no guilt in it.

 2. SIT
    Dropped straight down into a loaf — legs gone entirely under him, white breast resting on
    the wet asphalt, head sunk into his shoulders, bill tucked, eye half-lidded and off duty.
    The fry lies on the ground an inch in front of his bill, saved for later and quietly
    guarded, with a shallow puddle reflecting his grey mantle back at him.

 3. EAT
    Home turf: a tipped-over red-and-white carton of fries at his feet, head thrown all the
    way back, bill wide open and a whole fry going down vertically the way gulls actually
    swallow, throat visibly bulging. Wings are cracked half-open for balance, three more fries
    are scattered on the pavement, and one eye is rolled down at the viewer mid-swallow —
    caught, and entirely unbothered.

 4. CROUCH
    Compressed low and forward exactly as the existing crouch painting has him, tarsi folded,
    breast nearly brushing the road, wings cracked open a few inches to unlock, tail cocked,
    neck thrust out and eye forward on the far kerb. The fry is still crosswise in the bill
    and a first flick of grit and water is kicking up under the webbed toes.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne with both wings thrown up into a wide V, primaries fanned and the black
    tips spread, feet trailing back with toes curled, white belly to camera and bill tilted up
    — delighted, weightless, still holding the fry. One or two loose down feathers shed behind
    him; keep this silhouette clean and readable small, because the engine also flies this
    frame across the screen as the diving-gull hazard.

 6. LAND
    Feet thrown forward with the webbed toes splayed wide, wings cupped forward to air-brake,
    tail fanned down like a flap, head coming back up as the weight settles. A white burst of
    puddle water and a ring of droplets explodes around the feet — and the fry is still in his
    bill, which is the whole joke.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on to the right in the ridiculous flat-out gull waddle-run: body held horizontal,
    neck stretched forward like an arrow, both wings half-open for balance, orange feet
    paddling in a blur under him. The fry trails horizontally from his bill and little flicks
    of rainwater come off his heels.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same waddle-run at absolute maximum, wings caught mid power-stroke so he is half
    running and half flying, feet skimming the wet asphalt, eye wide and pupil tiny. Three
    white speed streaks and a rooster-tail of spray are baked in behind him, and the fry is
    pinned flat back against his cheek by the wind.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing wired, the takeaway cup pinched by its rim in his bill and tipped fully upside
    down, lid popped, the whole thing going down in one go. Caffeine has landed: the feathers
    on his crown are standing straight up in a startled crest, pupil shrunk to a pinprick,
    pink steam curling off the cup, orange fire streaks and sparks painted under his feet —
    and one fry still pinned under his left foot even now.

10. MAGNET
    Wings thrown open to their full span, head back, bill wide in a triumphant scream at the
    sky, feathers fluffed with pure delight. The whole air around him is a spiral of incoming
    fries, gold bottlecaps and one tumbling carton, all arcing in toward his open bill on
    painted swirl-trails — the best day of his life, baked into the frame.

11. UMBRELLA
    A cheap black umbrella, open and standing on its own with its handle jammed into a storm-
    drain grate, and he is parked underneath it bone dry, wings folded, chin up, eye half-
    lidded and insufferably smug, one foot resting on the fry carton. Rain sheets off the rim
    in a curtain all around him and hammers the puddles just outside his little dry circle.

12. SHIELD
    Hunkered flat with both wings clapped forward over his head like arms, neck pulled down
    between the shoulders, one eye squeezed shut and the other cracked open to watch. A blue-
    violet energy dome flares around him with a bright hotspot where the hit landed, rain
    streaking off it, a couple of feathers blown loose — and the fry still dead level in his
    bill, unmoved.

13. SCARED
    Stretched bolt upright on both legs with his neck fully periscoped, bill open in a squawk,
    both eyes rolled up at whatever is circling above, wings half-lifted and shivering. This
    is the one frame where the fry has slipped — paint it mid-fall an inch below his bill,
    with a single sharp "!" beside his head, because only real fear makes him let go.

14. FLEE
    Full panic sprint to the left, body low and horizontal, neck stretched, both wings
    hammering, webbed feet a blur, water spraying up behind him. He has the entire red-and-
    white fry carton clamped in his bill this time — grabbed the whole thing on the way out —
    with red speed streaks and "!!" marks trailing off his tail.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on the ground with his chest thrown out, both wings straight up in a wide
    open V, primaries fanned, head back and bill wide in a triumphant scream at the rain. A
    single fry is flipping end over end in the air above him mid-toss, gold bottlecaps and
    chip fragments raining down like confetti, and a warm gold sunbreak painted behind his
    silhouette.

16. KO
    Flat on his back on the wet asphalt, both webbed feet sticking straight up, wings splayed
    out to either side, bill hanging open and eyes gone to dazed little dots with a ring of
    gold stars circling above his head. One loose down feather drifts down beside him and the
    fry lies on the road an inch from his open bill, uneaten.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side with one wing pinned under him and the other flopped across his face,
    legs splayed, spiral eyes, bill hanging open with his little pink tongue out and stars
    circling. A wet brush-swirl mark from the sweeper curls across the pavement beneath him
    and a whole hand of fries lies fanned out around his head like a dealt deck.

18. SPLASH
    Sitting chest-deep in the water — he can swim, he is simply deeply offended — head
    feathers plastered flat so his skull looks half its normal size, both wings thrown up out
    of the water, bill open in a betrayed squawk, eye enormous. A big white crown of water
    bursts around him and the fry carton is floating away upside down, fries scattering across
    the surface, which is the actual tragedy here.

    SHEET NOTES
    • ENGINE REUSE — READ FIRST: `chars/seagull/leap` is not just his hop frame, it is also
    the diving-gull HAZARD sprite (drawGullSprite, index.html ~1759). It is preloaded, drawn
    at about TILE*1.15, and mirrored horizontally when the dive comes in from the right.
    Keep leap a clean bird-in-flight silhouette that still reads at roughly 60px and
    survives a horizontal flip — do not restage it into something only legible at full size.
    • Because the engine mirrors sprites, keep every prop free of lettering: a plain red-
    and-white fry carton, no logo, no text, no numbers. Mirrored text is instant garbage.
    Same reason run-l can be skipped entirely — gull plumage is symmetric and a flipped
    run-r reads perfectly; spend that slot on a second fries pose instead if you want one.
    • The four frames already in the game (idle, crouch, leap, land) are the scale, palette
    and lighting lock for the fourteen new ones: slate mantle, black wingtips with white
    mirrors, yellow bill with the red gonys spot, pale orange webbed feet, dark eye. Match
    the bird's height in idle.png exactly. He should read as tall and narrow beside
    Jimothy's round silhouette, never smaller.
    • Wingspan is the crop hazard. cheer, magnet, leap and shield all throw the wings wide,
    and umbrella adds a rim overhead — those frames are far bigger than idle. Paint every
    frame in the same size box with the same ground line, and let the wings and effects run
    out toward the edges rather than shrinking the bird to fit them, because the engine
    scales by frame height and a taller cheer frame makes him visibly shrink at the exact
    moment the camera zooms in on him.
    • sit, ko, dizzy and splash all put him down on the deck. Keep his lowest point of
    contact right at the bottom edge of those frames with no empty magenta below it, or the
    bottom-anchoring will float him above the pavement.
    • The fry IS the costume — he has no clothes, so the stolen food is his identity and it
    belongs in nearly every frame. It only leaves him three times, deliberately: dropped
    mid-air in scared, lying out of reach in ko, and floating off in splash. Do not
    accidentally paint it back into those three; that gap is the emotional beat of the whole
    set.

OPOSSUM      (files go to assets/chars/opossum/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He is a low, long quadruped with a naked pink prehensile tail, and that tail is the
answer to every pose Jimothy solves with a spare paw: it coils under him for the seated frames,
hooks the umbrella handle overhead, and cradles the chowder tub and the thermos. He rears up on
his haunches for sit, eat, scared and cheer the way a real opossum does, so his standing
silhouette is shorter and longer-bodied than Jimothy's, and every prop hangs off a dingy orange
hi-vis night-crew vest that never comes off.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing the viewer, long pale body low to the wet street, pointed
    white face with a pink nose and black bead eyes, one thin ear nicked at the tip. The too-
    big orange hi-vis vest hangs open with its reflective bands catching streetlight, the
    laminated NIGHT CREW badge swinging on its lanyard, and the bare pink tail curled into a
    lazy S at his heels.

 2. SIT
    Sitting up on his haunches, forepaws folded on his belly, vest slumped open, eyelids at
    half mast like the shift is finally over. His tail is coiled beside him in a neat flat
    spiral, the way you loop a garden hose, and the dented steel thermos rests against his
    hip.

 3. EAT
    Sitting up with a to-go tub of cold clam chowder held steady by his tail wrapped twice
    around it, both forepaws working a little plastic fork, cheeks packed full. Eyes squeezed
    shut with contentment, a chowder smear on his chin, one oyster cracker balanced on the
    badge.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened right down on all fours with elbows out and haunches up, ears pinned flat, eyes
    locked dead forward past the camera. The tail lies straight out behind him along the
    pavement like a rudder and the vest bunches up over his shoulders.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, all four legs splayed wide, pale belly turned to the viewer, mouth open in
    a delighted little gape. The tail is whipped into a full curl overhead for balance, the
    vest has ridden up around his ribs and the badge flies out sideways on its lanyard.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted, spine bowed, hindquarters still dropping, whiskers thrown forward,
    eyes wide with the impact. His tail slaps flat on the road and kicks up a fan of gutter
    water, and the reflective bands buckle across his chest.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on bounding gallop to the right, body stretched long and low, forepaws reaching, hind
    feet gathered under him with the opposable rear thumb splayed. The tail streams straight
    out behind as a counterweight, the vest flaps open and the thermos swings forward on its
    cord.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat-out four-legged bound with legs at maximum stretch and still, visibly, not very fast.
    Orange fire streaks and speed lines rip under his belly, the thermos cap has popped off
    and a comet of coffee arcs behind him, eyes huge and tail rigid as a broom handle.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing bolt upright and utterly wired, tail holding the dented steel thermos while both
    forepaws clutch the screw-cap cup, pink steam curling off it. Pupils blown wide, whiskers
    vibrating in little visible motion arcs, reflective bands blazing, feet ringed with fire
    streaks.

10. MAGNET
    Standing with his tail curled into a scoop in front of him already heaped with gold
    bottlecaps, mouth open in pure delight. More caps and stray fries spiral inward from every
    edge of the frame on swirling motion trails, and his vest pockets are overflowing.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing dry and deeply smug under a small black umbrella held overhead by the prehensile
    tail alone, both forepaws free, one tucked in the vest pocket and the other cradling the
    thermos. Rain hammers down and sheets off the canopy on both sides, leaving a clean dry
    ring on the wet asphalt around his feet.

12. SHIELD
    The hit lands: he is crouched low with both forepaws crossed over his face, one black eye
    squinting through the gap, tail clamped tight around his own ankle. A blue-violet energy
    dome flares white at the point of impact and rain streaks off its curve in bright arcs.

13. SCARED
    Reared up on his haunches with his mouth cranked open in the full fifty-tooth opossum
    gape, drooling, hissing, eyes enormous and rolled up at the sky. Fur puffed to twice his
    width, forepaws clutched to his chest, tail stiff as a poker, badge flipped up over his
    face by the panic.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left with everything he has, legs blurred into a scribble, mouth open in a
    panicked wail, thermos and badge swinging wild off the flapping vest. Red speed streaks
    and two fat exclamation marks trail behind him, over a comically short skid mark that says
    he has covered about four feet.

15. CHEER
    Reared up tall on his haunches with both forepaws flung to the sky and his tail thrown
    into a triumphant question-mark curl, mouth wide in a fifty-tooth grin that is joyful this
    time. The reflective bands blaze in the light and a scatter of gold bottlecaps arcs up
    around him.

16. KO
    He is doing the bit and doing it beautifully: flopped on his side, legs stiff and curled
    in the air, tongue lolled out on the wet asphalt, tail limp, a small green stink squiggle
    rising off him. Stars circle his head for the engine's sake, and one black eye is cracked
    open just a slit, peeking straight at the viewer.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his back in a drift of swept wet leaves and brush bristles, spiral eyes,
    tongue flopped sideways, tail corkscrewed under him, vest twisted around backwards. Stars
    orbit his head, and the whole frame reads as the one time he genuinely was not faking it.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked to the skin with his fur plastered flat so he looks half his usual size, forepaws
    thrown up, mouth open in a shocked gape, tail hanging like a wet rope. A white burst of
    water explodes around his middle with droplets flung to the frame edges, and the hi-vis
    vest is a sodden dark orange rag.

    SHEET NOTES
    • The tail is the crop hazard. In leap, cheer and umbrella it swings well above his
    head; in run-r and dash-run it runs far off the back of the body. Give those frames
    generous margin and never trim to the fur. Also keep the tail touching the body
    silhouette at its base in every frame, or a connected-component cutter will read it as a
    stray blob and drop it.
    • He is asymmetric on purpose: the badge and lanyard sit on his LEFT chest, one ear is
    nicked, and the peeling reflective band is on the same side. Mirroring run-r for run-l
    would flip all three, so paint run-l as its own frame.
    • The detached effects (circling stars in ko and dizzy, swirling bottlecaps in magnet,
    the water burst in splash, speed streaks in dash-run and flee) are separate components
    from his body. Lay this sheet out with white divider lines rather than trusting
    connected components, or those elements get cut away.
    • Scale check: he stands lower and longer than Jimothy, so match him frame to frame on
    head height, not overall footprint. The reared-up frames (sit, eat, scared, cheer) must
    reach the same head height as the four-square frames (idle, crouch, land) or he will
    appear to grow and shrink between poses.
    • Umbrella and shield are the two widest frames on the sheet, the canopy plus its rain
    sheets and the full energy dome. Size the row gaps around them accordingly.
    • The vest is the identity. It is on in all eighteen frames, same faded orange, same two
    reflective bands with the same one peeling, even when soaked flat in splash or twisted
    backwards in dizzy.
