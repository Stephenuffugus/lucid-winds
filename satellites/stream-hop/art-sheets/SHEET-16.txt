JIMOTHY — SHEET 16
WAVE 1 — the six that only have ONE painting each

A single painting is wired to all four hop frames on these six. Every frame below is missing.
This wave REPLACES the withdrawn Sheet 14 — do not work from Sheet 14.

ONE CHARACTER PER IMAGE SHEET. Never combine. Magenta #FF00FF. Same camera and same scale
across all eighteen frames. Clear gaps or white divider lines between rows — never an even
grid. Suggested layout 6 columns x 3 rows in the order below. Full rules: THE ANIMATION BIBLE.

DECKHAND JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/deckhand/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He is a raccoon, so the body is Jimothy's body and every hero pose transfers one to
one — the real animation work is the costume. The navy cape and the coil of rope are cloth and
line, not stickers: the cape must hang wet and vertical at rest, snap flat behind him in the
air, and flog in a run, while the rope must have weight, sag, and a paw or shoulder actually
carrying it in all eighteen frames. The yellow hat is tied under the chin, which is the honest
solution for the frames where a hat would fly off — it slips onto its cord and stays with him.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera, weight settled back on his heels, the rope coil
    hanging straight down from his right paw and pulling that shoulder a little lower than the
    other. Cape hangs in wet vertical folds, one fat bead of rain gathering at the front of
    the yellow brim, face calm and patient like a man watching for a boat that is nine minutes
    late.

 2. SIT
    Sat upright on his haunches, the rope coil set down in front of him and both paws folded
    on top of it, cape puddled around his hips. Hat tipped back off his brow so the whole face
    reads, eyes half-lidded and content, looking somewhere past the camera at water we cannot
    see.

 3. EAT
    Still sitting, using the coil of rope as a table — an open paper boat of ferry-galley clam
    strips balanced on it, which is the joke. One knuckle shoves the hat brim up, the other
    paw lifts a strip halfway to a full cheek, tartar sauce on the whiskers.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours, haunches up and loaded, cape slid forward over his shoulders
    and bunched at the neck. Both paws are on the ground so the rope coil is clamped in his
    teeth, hat brim jammed down to the eyebrows, eyes locked forward.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, limbs spread wide and belly forward, cape snapped dead straight behind him
    like a signal flag. The rope has come loose in his paw and two open loops of it hang in
    the air behind him, hat held on by the taut chin cord, mouth open in delight.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted, body low, weight absorbed through the shoulders, the rope hitting the
    ground beside him in a slack heap with dust and rain kicking up off it. Cape flops forward
    over one shoulder and the hat brim has bounced down over one eye; the free paw is already
    pushing it back up. Whiskers forward.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on sprint to the right, body low, legs extended, cape streaming off to the left
    behind him. The rope coil is hooked over his far shoulder and swinging out of rhythm with
    his stride, one paw up clamping the hat down.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out to the right, legs at full stretch, cape horizontal, the rope coil bouncing hard
    off his hip and starting to unspool. Hat mashed flat to his skull by the wind with the
    chin cord snapped straight out behind, eyes wide, white speed streaks and rain torn
    sideways across the frame.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing, but vibrating — rigid, knees locked, fur bristled, holding a scorched ferry-
    galley paper cup at chest height with pink steam ripping off it. Gold-orange fire streaks
    curl up around his feet, the rope coil quivers on his shoulder, pupils gone to pinpricks
    under a trembling yellow brim.

10. MAGNET
    Standing with both arms open and mouth wide in delight, having thrown one long loop of
    rope out to the left — gold bottlecaps, coins and fries spiral in along that line toward
    him like beads running down a mooring rope. Hat pushed back, cape flung open, whole body
    leaning into the haul.

11. UMBRELLA
    He has lashed a black ferry-deck umbrella upright to his own coil of rope with a neat
    little hitch so it stands on its own and both paws stay free. He stands under it with arms
    folded, faintly smug, wearing a rain hat and a rain cape underneath it anyway, rain
    sheeting off the umbrella rim in a ring around him.

12. SHIELD
    Crouched and flinching at the instant of impact, one forearm across his face and the other
    holding the rope coil up in front of him like a buckler. A blue-violet energy dome closes
    around him with rain streaking off the outside of it and the rope fibres lit up along
    their edges inside; ears flat, one eye squeezed shut, hat brim pressed down inside the
    dome.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, hugging the rope coil to his chest like a life ring, head tilted back
    and up so the hat brim rides off his brow and the eyes go huge. Cape hem lifting, tail
    rigid, a hard gull-shaped shadow falling across his chest and the wet ground beside him.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting hard to the left, mouth open in flat panic, the rope half unspooled and whipping
    out behind him in loose flailing loops he has no time to gather. Hat blown back onto its
    chin cord and bouncing on his shoulders, cape flogging, red speed streaks and two sharp
    "!!" marks over his head.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall and thrown open, one arm flung to the sky, and at the top of the throw a
    loop of his rope hangs in the air above him as a perfect open circle, like a halo he tied
    himself. Mouth wide with joy, hat shoved back off the brow, cape thrown open, both feet
    planted proud.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with his legs in the air, wrapped shin to shoulder in three neat turns of
    his own rope like a badly parcelled package — undone by the one thing he is good at. The
    hat has fallen over his face leaving one dazed eye showing, mouth open, gold stars
    circling above the brim.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled and sitting up crooked, spiral eyes, tongue out, hat spun around backwards on its
    cord and the cape twisted up under his chin like a bib. The whole coil has come undone
    into a loose spiral on the wet ground around him, which for a deckhand is the actual
    injury; stars circling his head.

18. SPLASH
    Chest deep in dark water, fur plastered flat, arms up and mouth wide in shock, a white
    crown of water bursting outward around him. One paw still grips the rope and the line runs
    taut up and out of the top of the frame — he never let go of the tie-off — with the yellow
    hat floating brim-up beside him, still knotted to his throat.

    SHEET NOTES
    • Crop hazards above the head: the thrown lasso circle in cheer and the lashed umbrella
    in umbrella both extend well above his ears. Leave real headroom in those cells or the
    cutter will shave the top off the gag.
    • Crop hazards sideways: the trailing rope in flee, the thrown magnet line, and the taut
    line in splash all run out to the frame edge. Keep every rope end inside its own cell
    with a visible gap before the divider line, or the cutter will read two components and
    slice the rope off.
    • He is asymmetric — rope coil on his right shoulder, brass cape clasp offset at the
    throat. Mirroring run-r puts the coil and the clasp on the wrong side, so paint run-l as
    its own frame rather than letting the engine flip it.
    • Scale is measured ears-to-feet, not cape-spread. The cape flares wide in leap, dash-
    run and cheer and will read as a size jump if the body is painted smaller to fit it
    inside the cell — keep the raccoon the same height in all eighteen and let the cape
    crowd the gap instead.
    • Lock the hat yellow and the rope tan to one value each across the whole sheet. At
    190px on a rainy street those two colours are the entire silhouette read, and a frame
    painted a half-step darker will flicker in the hop cycle.
    • The hat is on a chin cord in every frame; that is what lets it slip in flee, ko, dizzy
    and splash without ever leaving him. Never paint it separated from the body — a loose
    hat somewhere in the cell becomes its own sprite when the sheet is cut.

MARKET DAY JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/market/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — Ordinary raccoon body, so every pose in Jimothy's pack works here anatomically. The
real problem is that both hands are already full, which is also the joke. Solve it by giving
each hand a permanent job: the brown paper grocery bag rides the LEFT arm hugged at the hip, and
the RIGHT paw stays free to grab, point, shield and cheer, usually with the baguette in it. The
green satchel hangs on a chest strap and never comes off, so it can swing, flap open or slap his
belly to sell motion without him ever setting anything down.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera, weight even, the grocery bag hugged against his left
    hip with baguette, carrot tops and celery fanning out of it, green satchel resting on his
    belly on its strap, a scallion clamped sideways in his teeth. Eyes cut just off-camera in
    a too-innocent sideways glance, the face of a raccoon who is not going to bring up the
    receipt.

 2. SIT
    Sitting back on his haunches, off duty: the bag set down leaning against his thigh with
    one paw laid flat on top of it out of pure habit, satchel slid into his lap with the
    clasps undone. Ears relaxed, eyes half-lidded, scallion still in his teeth like a
    toothpick, the contented slouch of someone who got away with it.

 3. EAT
    Sitting beside the slumped-open bag with the baguette out and visibly half gone, one
    broken end held up in his right paw, the other cheek packed full, crumbs raining down his
    belly into the open satchel. Eyes squeezed shut in bliss, carrot tops sticking out the
    corner of his mouth, not one flicker of guilt on his face.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours with the bag crushed to his chest by his left arm and the
    baguette standing straight up out of it like a mast, satchel pressed flat against the
    ground under his belly. Haunches up, eyes locked forward, scallion clenched hard in his
    teeth, the groceries already leaning back from the launch.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, belly forward, back legs kicked wide, the bag clutched against his chest
    and the baguette streaming out behind him like a rudder, satchel flown up and sideways on
    its taut strap. One carrot has escaped and hangs in the air beside his ear, and he is
    grinning far too wide to have noticed.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted, body low, weight absorbed through the shoulders, the bag slammed down
    beside him with a carrot and a celery stalk still floating an inch above the open top. The
    satchel has swung forward and thumped his chest, whiskers pushed forward, one eye
    scrunched from the impact.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on sprint to the right, body low and legs at full extension, the grocery bag tucked
    under his left arm like a stolen football with the top rolled shut, satchel streaming back
    off his hip. The baguette has moved into his teeth for the run and the scallion is parked
    behind one ear for later.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same sprint flat out: legs stretched to the limit, ears pinned, eyes wide and white-
    rimmed, baguette gripped crosswise in his teeth like a bit, satchel flying horizontal
    behind him. Speed streaks rip past his feet and a carrot and a celery stalk tumble in the
    air in his wake, already lost.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing wired and vibrating, fur lifted, pupils tiny, a takeaway cup jammed into the
    grocery bag right next to the baguette so it reads as one more thing he definitely paid
    for. Pink steam curls off the cup, orange fire streaks lick up under his back feet, and
    the scallion in his teeth is blurred with the shaking.

10. MAGNET
    Standing braced with the grocery bag held wide open in both arms like a net, mouth open in
    delight, gold bottlecaps and loose produce spiralling in on a swirling arc and dropping
    through the top. The satchel has flapped open on its own and is catching a couple of caps
    too, and the baguette leans out of the bag at a jaunty angle.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing under an open umbrella held high in his right paw, rain sheeting down all around
    him, and he has tilted the whole umbrella off to the left so the grocery bag stays dry and
    half his own shoulder is soaked. Smug, ears flat, water dripping off his chin, the
    baguette bone dry.

12. SHIELD
    Crouched and braced with the grocery bag thrown up over his head in both arms and his eyes
    screwed shut, a blue-violet energy dome flaring around him and rain streaking off its
    curve. A carrot has been knocked loose and hangs inside the dome, and the satchel has
    swung round behind his back from the force.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, the bag clutched flat to his chest with his left arm and the baguette
    held out in his right paw like a sword he has no intention of using, eyes huge and rolled
    straight up at the sky. Ears pinned back, the scallion drooping out of his slack open
    mouth.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting hard to the left with his mouth open in panic, the bag jammed under one arm and
    its contents spraying out behind him, a carrot, a celery stalk and one apple already
    airborne. Red speed streaks and two fat exclamation marks trail him, the satchel bouncing
    off his hip, the baguette gripped white-knuckled and unlost.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on his toes with the baguette thrown to the sky in his right paw like a
    torch, the grocery bag hoisted in his left and celery and carrot tops popping up out of
    the top. Mouth wide open in a joyful shout, eyes shut, satchel swung back off his hip, a
    few bottlecaps and stray leaves confettiing in the air around him.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with his legs in the air, the grocery bag tipped over beside his head and
    its groceries scattered in a sad little arc across the ground, the baguette laid across
    his chest like a fallen knight's sword. Mouth open, tongue out, stars circling his head,
    the satchel flopped open with a carrot resting on his belly.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side with spiral eyes and his tongue lolling, the emptied grocery bag
    upside down over one ear like a hat he has given up removing, the baguette still spinning
    on the ground beside him. Stars circle his head, the satchel hangs off his shoulder, and
    one lone carrot balances on his nose.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked through, arms up, water bursting white around his waist, and the paper bag is
    dissolving in his grip, sagging and tearing open with the groceries floating off
    downstream. The baguette bobs on the surface beside him like driftwood, the satchel hangs
    heavy and full of water, and his face is pure shock: not at the drowning, at the bag.

    SHEET NOTES
    • Silhouette is wider and taller than a bare raccoon because of the props. The baguette
    in cheer goes well above head height, and flying groceries break the body outline in
    leap, land, flee and magnet. Give every frame generous magenta margin and give the cheer
    row extra headroom so the baguette tip is not clipped.
    • He is asymmetric on purpose: satchel strap over the right shoulder, grocery bag always
    on the LEFT arm. Mirroring run-r would flip the bag to the wrong arm and the strap to
    the wrong shoulder, so paint the optional run-l by hand for this character rather than
    letting the engine mirror it.
    • Baguette length is a continuity prop. Full length in all seventeen other frames,
    visibly half eaten only in eat. Do not shorten it anywhere else.
    • The scallion in his teeth is the identity tell in the standing frames. It moves behind
    one ear for run-r and dash-run when the baguette takes the mouth, and hangs out of his
    slack mouth in scared and dizzy. It should never simply vanish.
    • Bottom-anchor on his hind feet, never on the grocery bag or the trailing satchel, or
    he will bob up and down between frames when the engine seats him on the row.
    • Head size is the scale check across all 18: keep the skull identical frame to frame.
    The bag is the thing that changes shape (full, crushed, open wide, tipped, dissolving),
    never him.
    • Lay out with white divider lines and clear gaps. Loose carrots, celery and bottlecaps
    float free of the body in several frames, so the cutter needs the dividers to know those
    pieces belong to that frame and not the next one.

HARD HAT JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/hardhat/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He is a raccoon on the same skeleton as hero Jimothy, so every pose transfers across
one for one — the honest work here is wardrobe physics, not anatomy. Give the hard hat a thin
chinstrap so it survives leap, land and splash, and treat the hi-vis sash as a loose loop that
swings, streams and slips off a shoulder rather than a decal painted flat on his chest.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera, weight even on all four paws, ringed tail hanging
    straight down. Orange hard hat level and scuffed, hi-vis sash running left shoulder to
    right hip with both reflective stripes catching the streetlight, face calm and faintly
    tired — a raccoon who has stood in this exact spot for thirty years.

 2. SIT
    Sat back on his haunches at the curb edge, hat pushed back off his forehead to show the
    sweat-flattened dent ring in his fur, one paw curled around the screw-top lid of a dented
    thermos with a curl of steam coming off it. Shoulders down, eyes soft, off duty for
    exactly ten minutes.

 3. EAT
    Sitting with a battered black lunch pail open beside him, holding half a wax-paper
    sandwich in both paws mid-bite with his cheeks full. Hat tipped back, thermos lid balanced
    on one knee, a crumb on his chin — the joke is that somebody at home still cuts it in half
    for him.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours, chest nearly on the asphalt, haunches loaded and up. Hat brim
    shoved down so his eyes glint out from under it, the sash swung forward and hanging loose
    off his chest, whiskers pinned back, ready.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, limbs thrown wide, belly forward, mouth open in a delighted whoop. The
    hard hat lifts a half inch off his head and is caught by its thin chinstrap, and the sash
    flaps straight out behind him like a torn flag.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted hard with the elbows bent, body low, tail up for balance, whiskers
    forward. The hat drops down over his brow on the impact and the sash swings across his
    chest; a small puff of road dust and three bits of loose gravel skitter out from under his
    palms.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on hop to the right, body long and low, back legs extended, one front paw reaching
    for ground. Hat pinned low over his eyes, sash streaming back off his shoulder with the
    reflective stripes smearing into a short light trail.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same side-on sprint but flat out — legs at full stretch, spine straight, ears flat,
    eyes wide. Hat brim pressed flat by the wind, sash horizontal like a banner, orange and
    white speed streaks painted behind him and sparks kicking off his back claws where they
    bite the asphalt.

 9. COFFEE
    Planted upright on his hind legs and visibly vibrating, holding a giant thermos-lid of
    coffee that is sloshing over the rim. His free paw is clamped on the hat brim to keep it
    on his shaking head, pupils two different sizes, pink steam off the cup and orange heat
    streaks licking up around his feet.

10. MAGNET
    Up on his hind legs holding the front of the hi-vis sash out with both paws to make a
    pouch, mouth open in delight, catching a spiral of gold bottlecaps and fries. The hat
    stays on and the ones he misses ring off the brim like hail off a roof, painted with
    little orange impact sparks.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing on his hind legs holding his orange STOP paddle flat over his head like a
    laughably small umbrella, rain sheeting past it and pouring off the hard hat brim in two
    proper gutter streams. Eyes half lidded, thoroughly smug: the paddle is decoration, the
    hat is doing all the work.

12. SHIELD
    The instant of the hit — he snaps the STOP paddle up flat in front of his chest and a
    blue-violet energy dome flares off its face, rain and grit streaking around the curve.
    Head turned away, one eye squeezed shut, hat knocked down over his brow, back foot
    skidding: braced, flinching, saved.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs with his head cranked back to the sky, both paws clamped down on the
    hard hat brim because three generations of instinct say the hat handles it. Eyes huge and
    white-ringed, mouth a small flat line, the sash slipped down off one shoulder.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left with his mouth open in panic, one paw jammed on the hat so it cannot fly
    off and the other pumping. The loose end of the sash streams behind him, his dented
    thermos tumbles and spills in his wake, red speed streaks and two painted '!!' marks over
    his shoulder.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on his hind legs, one arm thrown straight to the sky, the other paw a raised
    fist, mouth wide open in a whoop. The hat is knocked back on his head by the force of his
    own shout and the sash flies up off his chest, both reflective stripes lit — the road is
    still never finished, but this bit is.

16. KO
    Flat on his back, all four legs in the air, mouth open and tongue out, gold stars circling
    his head. The hard hat has tipped off and come to rest against his ear, still touching
    him, brim up and catching the light; the sash lies slack across his belly. Nothing worse
    than he has had before.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his side with spiral eyes and his tongue lolling, stars orbiting overhead. The
    hat has been spun clean around backwards so the brim sits over the back of his neck,
    sweeper grit and two stray broom bristles are stuck in his fur, and one stripe of the sash
    has unwound and trails off like a party streamer.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked through and going under, both arms up, mouth open in shock, fur plastered flat to a
    much smaller raccoon. White water bursts around him in a ring, the sash's reflective
    stripes glow hard in the dark water, and the hard hat — riding an inch above his head on a
    spout — is the only dry thing in the picture.

    SHEET NOTES
    • The hat and the sash are the whole identity: orange hard hat, and two reflective
    stripes running left shoulder to right hip with the small violet clip where the sash
    crosses. Same stripe count, same clip, every frame — that pattern is what reads at 40px
    on a phone.
    • CROP HAZARD: the hat brim is the widest and highest point of his silhouette. Leave
    8-10px of clear magenta above and to both sides of the brim in every frame, and the same
    around the raised paddle in umbrella, the raised arm in cheer and the outer water spray
    in splash.
    • CUTTER: the cut reads connected components, so nothing may float free on magenta. In
    ko the tipped-off hat must physically touch his ear; in magnet the bottlecap spiral must
    touch his paws or the brim; in splash the water burst must touch his fur. Anything
    detached gets dropped on the cut.
    • ASYMMETRY: the sash always runs over the LEFT shoulder to the RIGHT hip, so a mirrored
    run-r would flip the sash and the whole costume would read backwards. Paint a real run-l
    for this character rather than letting the engine mirror.
    • SCALE: the hat adds height that plain-fur skins do not have. Match his SHOULDER height
    to hero Jimothy, not his overall height, or he towers over the roster when they stand
    side by side.
    • Props come and go, the costume never does: the thermos appears in sit, eat and flee;
    the STOP paddle in umbrella and shield; the lunch pail only in eat. Keep them the same
    battered orange-and-white objects each time so they read as his, not as set dressing.
    • Rows separated by clear white divider lines with generous gaps. Never an even grid.

SCOUTMASTER JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/scout/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — Same raccoon body as hero Jimothy, so every pose in the reference pack transfers one-
to-one — the whole job here is the costume moving correctly: the navy patch vest is stiff and
swings as a unit from the shoulders, while the cream neckerchief is loose cloth that streams,
flops and soaks. Because the badges are asymmetric (square cream merit patch on HIS right chest,
small round brass badge low on HIS left hip, brass buttons down the placket) no frame of this
character can ever be mirrored — every side-on pose has to be painted for its own direction.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera, chest pushed out, chin level, paws neat at his sides —
    at attention rather than at rest, vest buttoned to the top and the neckerchief knot
    squared dead centre. Rain beading on his shoulders, badges catching a little streetlight.

 2. SIT
    Sitting upright on his haunches with his back straight and knees together, one paw
    smoothing the square merit patch flat against his chest, the other laid on his knee. Off
    duty and still sitting like he expects to be inspected, mouth in a small content line.

 3. EAT
    Sitting beside a sputtering little Sterno tin, toasting a marshmallow on a bent wire held
    with enormous procedural correctness, cheeks already packed full. Neckerchief tucked into
    the vest as a bib, dented tin mess-cup steaming on the wet ground beside him.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours on the wet asphalt, haunches up, elbows out, eyes locked up-
    street. The neckerchief tails hang forward past his chin and the brass hip badge glints —
    he is coiled like he is demonstrating correct technique to a troop that is not there.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne, limbs spread wide, belly forward, vest flapping open off his ribs and the
    cream neckerchief streaming straight back like a troop pennant. Mouth open, delighted,
    both badges flashing as he turns into the light.

 6. LAND
    Front paws planted wide on wet pavement, knees deep, tail up as counterweight, vest
    dropping back down against his chest and the neckerchief flopping over it. A thin ring of
    splash under his paws, whiskers forward, extremely satisfied with the landing.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on sprint to the RIGHT, body low, front and back legs at full extension, neckerchief
    streaming back to the left. His right flank is to camera so the square cream merit patch
    and the brass placket buttons read clearly along the near side.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat-out sprint, ears pinned, legs at full stretch, gold-white speed streaks painted
    behind him and the neckerchief horizontal in the slipstream. One corner of the merit patch
    has torn loose from its stitching and flutters — eyes wide, faintly appalled at his own
    speed.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing braced with a dented enamel camp percolator mug clutched in both paws, pink steam
    blasting up past his mask, fur standing on end, pupils shrunk to pinpricks. Little fire
    licks and speed streaks curl off his heels and the neckerchief vibrates like a plucked
    string.

10. MAGNET
    Standing with arms thrown wide and mouth open in delight as gold bottlecaps and snack
    scraps spiral in toward his chest, several already stuck flat to the navy vest like brand
    new merit badges. He is not catching loot, he is being awarded it, and he knows it.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing dead straight under an open, carefully hand-mended umbrella held in one paw at
    parade posture, rain hammering down and bouncing off the canopy all around him.
    Neckerchief bone-dry and perfectly knotted, one eyebrow up: he brought this, obviously.

12. SHIELD
    Crouched hard with one forearm slammed across his face and the other paw flung out flat, a
    blue-violet energy dome ringing him and rain streaking off its curve. Eyes screwed shut,
    teeth gritted, the vest and both badges lit cold by the dome's glow.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, both paws clutched over the badges on his chest, ears flat, eyes
    enormous, head tipped back to stare up-frame at whatever is lining up on him. His
    neckerchief knot has pulled crooked for the first time in his life — he is guarding the
    badges, not himself.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting toward camera-left with his mouth open in panic, red speed streaks and two
    painted '!!' marks behind him, vest flapping wide. One paw is clamped flat over his chest
    to stop the merit patch shaking loose while he runs.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on the spot, one arm shot to the sky in a crisp three-finger scout salute
    that has completely lost its composure into a whoop, other paw pressed to his chest. Mouth
    wide open, eyes shut with joy, gold sparks popping off the badges.

16. KO
    Flat on his back on the wet asphalt, legs in the air, arms flopped out, stars circling his
    head and his mouth hanging open. The neckerchief has flipped up over one eye and a single
    brass badge has popped off and spins on the ground beside his ear.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled and trying to sit up wrong, spiral eyes, tongue out, stars orbiting his head, the
    vest dragged half off one shoulder and the neckerchief spun round so the knot sits at the
    back of his neck. One paw is still gamely attempting a salute and missing his forehead by
    a mile.

18. SPLASH
    Soaked through with arms up and a white burst of water exploding around his waist, mouth
    open in shock, mask fur plastered flat and the vest gone black with wet. Two merit patches
    have peeled off and float away on the surface beside him, which is plainly the worse
    tragedy.

FIRST FROST JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/firstfrost/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — Same raccoon body as Jimothy, so every pose transfers directly — but this one stands
UPRIGHT on his hind legs in the existing painting, and one front paw is permanently occupied by
the takeaway cup. Every two-handed pose Jimothy solves with two paws (cheer, magnet, umbrella,
shield, splash) has to be re-solved here with one free paw plus the scarf and the jaw, and the
answer is always the same character truth: he protects the coffee first and himself second. The
scarf is his second limb — it streams, it catches, it wraps, it covers an eye — and the breath
fog is his signature effect, faint at rest and heavy under exertion.

 1. IDLE
    Standing upright and settled on his hind legs facing the camera, red knit scarf double-
    wrapped with the fringed tail hanging down his chest, ringed tail curled out to his left,
    free paw hanging relaxed, cup held up at shoulder height in the other paw with its pink
    steam curling straight up in the still cold air. Eyes slightly squinted against the chill,
    content, one faint white plume of breath at the muzzle — this is the existing painting and
    it is the master for scale and costume.

 2. SIT
    Sat back on his haunches with the ringed tail curled around his own feet like a lap
    blanket and the scarf loosened one turn so it pools in his lap. Both paws finally cradle
    the cup together, held up under his chin for the warmth, eyes half closed, steam and
    breath merging into one soft plume across his face — the only frame where he gets to hold
    it properly.

 3. EAT
    Sitting, the cup set down carefully upright beside his hip and still steaming, dunking a
    half-eaten old-fashioned donut into it with his free paw while the other paw catches
    crumbs against his scarf. Cheeks packed, eyes shut in total bliss, sugar already dusted
    down the red knit, breath fog puffing out around the donut.

 4. CROUCH
    Compressed low on all threes: hind legs folded, one front paw flat on the frosted
    pavement, and the cup paw held up and out to his side rock-steady in a barista carry so
    nothing tips. Head low, ears back, eyes locked forward, scarf bunched up around his jaw,
    one tight jet of breath escaping the corner of his mouth and a crackle of frost crystals
    under his palm.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne and stretched long, belly forward toward the camera, hind legs trailing,
    free paw thrown out ahead of him and the cup paw locked overhead with the cup dead level —
    not a drop moving, and he knows it. Mouth open in a delighted hup, scarf streaming
    straight back in one long red banner with a trail of breath fog strung along behind it.

 6. LAND
    Front paw and both hind feet touching down hard, knees folding to absorb it, body low, the
    cup swinging down to his chest with the coffee sloshed up into a wave crest at the lip
    that does not go over. Whiskers forward, eyes wide, scarf flopped up past his cheek, a
    puff of breath punched out of him and a small ring of frost dust kicked up off the wet
    street.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on to the right in a low fast lope, hind legs at full extension, tail level behind
    him, cup carried forward and perfectly level in his lead paw like a lantern. Scarf streams
    back to the left in a long horizontal banner, breath comes off in short beads over his
    shoulder, expression cold-eyed and businesslike — he has somewhere to be and it is thirty-
    four degrees.

 8. DASH-RUN
    The same rightward sprint gone flat out: body nearly horizontal, legs at full blurred
    stretch, cup thrust out ahead at arm's length, scarf snapped rigid straight behind him.
    Baked in are pale cold-vapour speed streaks instead of fire, a comet-tail of breath fog,
    and frost glitter kicked off the pavement; eyes huge, mouth open, coffee still level.

 9. COFFEE
    Standing but vibrating, hind feet planted and every hair up, the cup tipped to his mouth
    mid-swallow in one enormous gulp, ears bolt upright, eyes pinned wide. Baked effect: the
    pink steam has become a full plume blasting past his head and vapour rings bloom off his
    shoulders into the cold — he is visibly steaming, and the updraft is lifting the scarf off
    his chest.

10. MAGNET
    Standing, cup held high and safely out of traffic in one paw while the other pulls the end
    of his scarf out and open like an apron basket to catch the incoming haul. Gold bottlecaps
    and fries spiral in toward the scarf, two already ringing off the cup lid, mouth open and
    eyes bright on the loot, breath fog puffing with the laugh.

11. UMBRELLA
    Standing under a half-open moon-blue umbrella held in his free paw and angled into the
    weather, cup in the other, its steam rising and pooling under the canopy like a small warm
    room. Sleet bounces off the fabric and beads ice along the rim; chin up, eyes half-lidded,
    the smuggest raccoon in Seattle — dry, warm and caffeinated.

12. SHIELD
    Hunched and braced at the instant of the hit, knees bent, free paw flung up across his
    face, the cup pulled hard into his chest and hugged — he is shielding the coffee, not
    himself. A blue-violet energy dome flares around him with a bright impact ripple where it
    was struck, sleet streaking off it, eyes screwed shut, teeth gritted, scarf blown flat,
    one shocked plume of breath squeezed out. Nothing spilled.

13. SCARED
    Up on his toes, ears flat, head craned back and up at whatever is lining up above him,
    eyes enormous with the whites showing, free paw clutched to his chest and the cup paw
    extended out and away at arm's length still perfectly level. He is holding his breath, so
    instead of a plume there are two exclamation marks above his head drawn in frozen white
    vapour.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting left in full panic, mouth wide open, eyes rolled back over his shoulder, hind
    legs at full stretch, tail straight out and the scarf whipping across the frame, cup
    clutched against his chest. For the first and only time the coffee actually loses three or
    four dark drops behind him, which is how you know it is serious; pale blue-white panic
    streaks and frost spray come off his heels with a ragged trail of breath puffs.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on his hind legs, head thrown back, mouth wide open in a shout, one arm
    punched straight to the sky and the cup raised in the other in a full toast. Scarf flies
    up behind him in a big red arc, pink steam and breath fog burst upward together like a
    firework through a scatter of frost sparkles, and the first thin sunbeam of the morning
    catches him.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with his legs in the air, scarf splayed out on the wet pavement around
    him like a spilled ribbon, mouth open, tongue just showing, eyes gone to swooning half-
    moons with stars circling his head. The cup landed upright beside his ear, lid still on,
    still gently steaming — perfect, untouched, and he is never going to drink it; one last
    breath plume drifts off sideways.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his haunches and wobbling, spiral eyes, tongue out, ears at two different
    angles, the scarf wound completely around his head by the sweeper brushes so it covers one
    eye and the fringe hangs over his nose. The cup is still gripped but upside down, the last
    of the coffee draining into a sad dark puddle with its steam dying, stars circling above
    and wobble arcs either side of him.

18. SPLASH
    Chest-deep and going under, fur plastered flat, one paw flailing while the other holds the
    cup as high as it will reach above the water — saving it to the very last second. A cold
    blue crown of water bursts around him with cracked skim-ice at the rim, the scarf floats
    out on the surface in a wet red spiral, his eyes are wide and his mouth is a small shocked
    oh, and a single thread of rain is falling into the cup he just rescued.

    SHEET NOTES
    • The existing assets/skins/firstfrost/idle.png is the master — every one of the other
    17 frames must match its camera (front three-quarter high angle), its lighting and its
    size. He stands UPRIGHT on his hind legs at rest, unlike hero Jimothy who idles four-
    square; keep that upright baseline so the skin still reads as him.
    • Asymmetric costume: the cup is in his viewer-RIGHT paw and the scarf tail hangs down
    his viewer-LEFT chest. If the engine mirrors run-r the cup jumps paws and the scarf
    flips sides, and on an epic skin that will be noticed. Paint the optional 19th frame
    run-l for this character.
    • Two crop hazards above the head: the pink steam plume off the cup and the white breath
    plume off the muzzle. Both extend past his silhouette and both are the whole point of
    the character — leave headroom in every cell and make sure the cutter includes them
    rather than slicing them at the frame edge.
    • The scarf streams wide in leap, dash-run, flee and cheer. Those rows need extra
    horizontal room or a white divider line drawn between cells — a streaming scarf that
    touches the neighbouring painting will be read as one connected component and the two
    frames will cut as a single sprite.
    • Running gag to protect: the coffee never spills. Only flee (three or four flying
    drops) and dizzy (draining out upside down) break it. Everywhere else, including the
    leap, the land and the shield hit, it stays level. Do not add a spill anywhere it is not
    specified.
    • The ko frame has a second object on the ground (the upright cup beside his head) and
    the splash frame has water above his feet. Keep both bottom-anchored to the same ground
    line as every other frame so he does not appear to change size or float when the engine
    swaps frames.

GARAGE BAND JIMOTHY      (files go to assets/skins/garage/)
====================================================================================================
ANATOMY — He is a raccoon on the same skeleton as the hero, so every pose maps one-to-one off
Jimothy's twenty and no anatomy needs inventing — the honest work here is the kit, not the body.
The vest, the bandana, the three pins and the coiled amp cable have to behave like real cloth
and real rope in all eighteen (hanging open at rest, flaring in the air, trailing in a run,
plastered flat when he goes under), and his raccoon hands are a gift the fish and birds don't
have: he can hold a pick, a pizza box and a guitar case lid, so use them.

 1. IDLE
    Standing four-square facing camera, knuckles just brushing the wet pavement, sleeveless
    denim vest hanging open over his chest fur, purple bandana knotted at the throat, three
    enamel pins on his left lapel catching the streetlight and a coiled quarter-inch cable
    looped over the far shoulder. Ears up, brows slightly raised, patient and faintly hopeful
    — the face of a raccoon who unlocked the garage twenty minutes ago and nobody has turned
    up yet.

 2. SIT
    Sitting upright on his haunches with his back against a battered little practice amp, one
    paw resting on the knob panel, hind foot hooked through the coiled cable, tail curled
    around his side. Eyes half closed, ears drooped a touch, the guitar pick clamped in the
    corner of his mouth — off duty, humming something, still half listening for footsteps at
    the door.

 3. EAT
    Sitting beside an open pizza box balanced on the amp, holding a folded cold slice up in
    both paws with one long string of cheese still hanging, cheek already packed, eyes shut in
    pure contentment. The pick has been parked behind his ear so his hands are free, and a
    greasy paper plate sits by his foot.

 4. CROUCH
    Flattened low on all fours, haunches up, elbows out, vest bunched around his shoulders and
    the bandana shoved up under his chin, cable coil pulled tight against his back. Eyes
    locked dead forward, pick gripped in his teeth — the held breath of a count-in before the
    downbeat.

 5. LEAP
    Fully airborne and stretched, limbs spread wide, belly forward, the denim vest flared open
    off his ribs like two stiff wings and the bandana streaming back off his throat. Mouth
    open in a delighted whoop, eyes big, the amp cable half uncoiled and looping lazily behind
    him.

 6. LAND
    Front knuckles planted, body low and compressed, shoulders up around his ears, vest
    slapping back down against his ribs and one pin still swinging on its clasp. Whiskers
    forward, eyes forward, a small puff of grit kicked out under both paws — a drum hit
    arriving exactly on the beat.

 7. RUN-R
    Side-on sprint to the right, body long and low, legs at full extension, vest streaming
    back off his shoulders so all three pins are turned to the camera and the bandana flies
    out horizontal. The cable trails straight behind him in a shallow S, pick in his teeth,
    ears laid back.

 8. DASH-RUN
    Flat out and side-on, legs at maximum stretch, denim pressed flat against him, ears
    pinned, eyes wide with white showing all round. Baked in: warm amber amp-glow speed
    streaks off his flanks, a couple of orange sparks flicking off his heels and the cable
    snapping behind him like a whipcrack, with two faint sound-wave rings trailing in his
    wake.

 9. COFFEE
    Up on his hind legs and visibly vibrating, a paper cup from the corner place clutched in
    one paw, the other paw caught mid-windmill strum on an air guitar that isn't there, jaw
    locked around the pick. Pink steam curls off the cup, fire streaks lick up off both heels,
    eyes enormous — he can hear the entire song at once.

10. MAGNET
    Standing tall holding the empty pizza box open in front of him like a collection plate,
    mouth open in a laugh, one hind foot lifting off the ground with the pull. Gold bottlecaps
    and two spare guitar picks spiral in toward the box on a bright swirling ribbon, tugging
    his bandana sideways as they come.

11. UMBRELLA
    Hunkered under the raised lid of a battered sticker-covered hardshell guitar case, held
    over his head with both paws like a lean-to roof, rain hammering on it and sluicing off
    both edges in two sheets. One brow up, mouth in a smug little line, bone dry, cable still
    coiled neatly on his shoulder.

12. SHIELD
    Crouched behind the case lid with both paws braced against it, wincing hard with one eye
    screwed shut, pins rattling on the denim as the hit rings the case like a gong. A blue-
    violet energy dome flares around him with rain streaking off the curve, plus a single
    white shock ring and a thin feedback shimmer at the point of impact.

13. SCARED
    Up on his hind legs, both paws clutched to his chest over the vest, ears flat, eyes huge
    and fixed up-screen, whole body leaning back away from whatever is lining him up. The pick
    has dropped from his mouth and hangs in the air in front of his nose; the bandana has
    flopped half over one eye.

14. FLEE
    Sprinting toward the left, mouth wide open in panic, vest flapping, one paw clamped over
    the bandana knot so he doesn't lose it and the cable unspooled and whipping wildly behind
    him. Red panic streaks off his heels and two hard black "!!" marks over his head.

15. CHEER
    Standing tall on his hind legs, head thrown back, mouth open in a joyful whoop, one arm
    flung straight to the sky with the pick pinched glinting between two claws and the other
    fist punched against his chest. Vest thrown wide, bandana snapping, a warm amber amp-glow
    sunburst behind him with three drifting sound-wave rings and a scatter of gold sparks.

16. KO
    Flat on his back with all four legs in the air, vest splayed open around him like a burst
    cushion, bandana flopped over one eye, tongue just showing and his mouth open in a
    soundless ow. Yellow stars circle his head, the pick spins down through the air above his
    belly, and two loose pins wobble to a stop on the asphalt beside him — the rest stay on
    the denim.

17. DIZZY
    Sprawled on his rump with his legs out in front, wound from ankles to shoulders in his own
    amp cable like a maypole gone wrong, bandana spun round so the knot sits at the back of
    his neck. Spiral eyes, tongue out one side, ears pointing in two different directions,
    stars circling and one pin swinging on its clasp.

18. SPLASH
    Both arms up, soaked to the skin, fur plastered into spikes, the denim gone dark and heavy
    with water and the bandana clinging to his throat, pins dulled to grey. A white crown of
    water bursts up around his middle with droplets frozen mid-air, eyes shocked wide, mouth
    open — and one paw still holds the pick clear above the water like it's the only thing
    worth saving.

    SHEET NOTES
    • ASYMMETRY — paint run-l as well as run-r. The three pins live on his left lapel and
    the bandana knot sits off-centre at his throat; the engine's mirror would jump both to
    the wrong side and the player would notice on every sideways hop.
    • THE PINS ARE FIXED KIT — exactly three, same three, same order, all eighteen frames:
    pale eye button on top, amber sunburst under it, small orange dot below. They may swing
    on their clasps in land, dizzy and ko; they never change design and they never all
    vanish.
    • NEW KIT, MUST BE UNIVERSAL — the coiled quarter-inch amp cable over his shoulder is
    the one addition to the existing painting and it has to appear in all eighteen: coiled
    at rest, half uncoiled in leap, trailing in run and dash, whipping in flee, wrapped
    around him in dizzy. If it is dropped from any frame it reads as a continuity error, so
    decide it in frame one.
    • THE PICK IS THE THROUGH-LINE — tucked under the bandana knot or in his teeth at rest
    and in every hop frame, behind his ear when he eats, in his raised hand for cheer,
    falling through the air in scared and ko, held above the water in splash. It is small;
    keep it readable at sprite size.
    • SCALE HAZARD — the vest shoulders plus the cable coil make him broader than the other
    raccoons. Keep his head and body exactly the size of hero/idle; the costume must not
    inflate him or he will pop bigger than every other skin when the player swaps.
    • CROP HAZARD — cheer's raised arm and pick, umbrella and shield's guitar case lid,
    magnet's bottlecap swirl and flee's whipping cable all break well outside his standing
    silhouette. Give those five cells extra room and keep the white divider lines clear of
    them; on a 6x3 layout that is cells 9-15, so leave the widest gaps across the middle and
    bottom rows.
    • SCENERY IS RATIONED — the practice amp appears only in sit and eat, the pizza box only
    in eat and magnet, the guitar case lid only in umbrella and shield. Nothing else carries
    a set piece, and none of it may sit under his feet in a way that changes his ground
    line.
    • GROUND LINE — ko, dizzy and splash are the flat poses and are held 1.55s under a
    closing iris. Keep all three bottom-anchored on the same baseline as idle so the camera
    lean does not make him float.
