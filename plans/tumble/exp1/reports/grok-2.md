## Lane F. The Economy (coins + pocket finds) — entirely new design

### 1. Diagnosis (one paragraph)

The current Quarters system fails because it only pays for two rare perfect outcomes and nothing else. A player who sorts carefully but drops one ball gets zero. A player who plays three relaxed Loads a day can go a week without a single Quarter while Lint overflows. The emotional promise of laundry is that small lost things turn up; the current economy ignores that fantasy entirely. Real coins and pocket finds restore it: every Load can yield something, care still multiplies the yield, and a miss never steals what was already found.

### 2. The coins — eight new moments the player sees and hears

1. **Cuff shake** (when a pair is rolled into a ball)  
   Player rolls the pair; a soft metallic rattle comes from inside the cuff and a coin drops onto the table.  
   55 % penny, 30 % nickel, 12 % dime, 3 % quarter.  
   Clean Load raises quarter chance to 12 %. Only once per pair.

2. **Dryer door pocket** (first time the dryer door is opened each real day)  
   A small fabric pocket sewn inside the door flap holds one coin. Soft fabric swish + clink.  
   Always one coin: 40 % nickel, 40 % dime, 20 % quarter.  
   Feels like a daily discovery, not a reward for perfection.

3. **Basket bottom rattle** (when the Load ends and the basket is emptied)  
   Balls tumble out; two or three coins that had landed with earlier shots rattle free.  
   Distribution weighted by how many balls landed cleanly.  
   Spotless Load guarantees at least one dime.

4. **Sock-heel coin** (when a sock with high heelToe contrast is picked up)  
   A coin is wedged in the heel. Soft scrape as it is pulled free.  
   Mostly pennies and nickels. Higher chance on Heavy and Mountain Loads.

5. **Windowsill overnight** (first Load after the player has been away ≥12 hours)  
   One coin sits on the windowsill, slightly dewy. Soft ping when collected.  
   Fixed: one dime or quarter (70/30). Safety net for infrequent players.

6. **Lint-filter magnet** (end of every Load, after the trap is emptied)  
   A small magnet strip on the filter catches one extra coin that would have been missed.  
   Always a penny or nickel. Visual: coin clings, then drops into the jar.

7. **Reunion thank-you roll** (on every Reunion)  
   After the mate is found, a single quarter rolls slowly out of the Odd Bin and stops at the player’s feet.  
   Always a quarter. Makes the emotional moment also feel valuable.

8. **Idle table settle** (if the player leaves a Load unfinished for >90 seconds)  
   One penny quietly appears on the table edge with a tiny clink.  
   Only pennies. Prevents the “I stepped away and earned nothing” feeling.

### 3. How the money is counted — pick (b) again, different justification

**Coin jar that rolls into Quarters.**  
Coins drop into a clear glass jar on the shelf. Every 25 ¢ the jar performs a short animation and produces one Quarter. Dryers and packs still cost Quarters. Lint remains the soft currency for everything visible in the room. This keeps the laundromat ritual intact while making every small find satisfying. The jar itself becomes a visible progress meter the player checks between Loads.

### 4. The arithmetic (relaxed = 3 Regular Loads/day)

New expected yield per Regular Load: ~20–28 ¢ (cuff shakes + basket rattle + lint magnet + occasional heel coin).  
Clean Load ≈ +15 ¢. Spotless ≈ +22 ¢.  
Realistic relaxed day: ~70–85 ¢ → ~2.8–3.4 Quarters.

- First dryer (new prices below) ≈ 2–3 days.  
- First hero pack ≈ 3–4 days.  
- All dryers ≈ 18–22 days.  
- Everything Quarters-priced ≈ 30–35 days of relaxed play.

Ten Loads in one sitting: ~2.20–2.80 $ → 9–11 Quarters.  
One Load a week: still receives the windowsill overnight coin and the dryer-door pocket, so the jar never stays empty.

**First-day vs late-game**

## Lane B. Six completely new procedural pattern families

1. **Basketweave**  
   Looks like: small blocks of horizontal and vertical dashes alternating like woven fabric. Drawn from short thick dashes in a 2×2 or 3×3 repeating block.  
   Twin difference: block size or the direction that starts the weave.  
   Decoy: same weave but one block rotated 90°, or the dash length slightly longer in one direction.

2. **Cross-Stitch Grid**  
   Looks like: tiny X marks on a faint grid, like counted cross-stitch. Drawn from pairs of short diagonal lines forming X’s at regular intervals.  
   Twin difference: density of the X’s or the exact angle of the diagonals.  
   Decoy: same grid but every X is replaced by a small + , or the grid is offset by half a step.

3. **Ombré Stripe**  
   Looks like: horizontal stripes that quietly shift from one colour to its neighbour across the sock. Drawn from parallel bands whose colour interpolates.  
   Twin difference: the direction of the fade (top-to-bottom vs bottom-to-top) or the speed of the shift.  
   Decoy: same fade but the midpoint colour is shifted one step on the palette, or the stripes are slightly wavy.

4. **Speckled Tweed**  
   Looks like: a base colour densely flecked with two or three tiny contrasting dots, like tweed cloth. Drawn from small irregular dots of accent colours scattered over a solid field.  
   Twin difference: density of the flecks or which accent colour dominates.  
   Decoy: same tweed but the flecks are slightly larger, or one accent colour is swapped for its neighbour.

5. **Chevron Knit**  
   Looks like: wide chevron bands that look hand-knitted, with soft edges. Drawn from thick V-shaped bands stacked vertically.  
   Twin difference: the angle of the V or the width of each band.  
   Decoy: same chevron but mirrored (pointing the other way), or one band is thinner.

6. **Dot-and-Dash Morse**  
   Looks like: irregular sequences of short dashes and round dots that suggest a private code. Drawn from alternating short line segments and small circles in horizontal rows.  
   Twin difference: the exact sequence of dots and dashes, or the spacing between rows.  
   Decoy: same sequence but one dash is replaced by a dot, or the entire row is shifted by one unit.

All six remain readable at thumbnail size and supply clear near-miss decoys.

## Lane G. Paying without feeling cheap — new stance

**Straight answer.**  
Do not sell Quarters. Do not sell individual socks.  

Sell one thing only: a **Studio Note** for $3.99.  
It appears as a small sealed envelope on the cork board after the player has finished 15 Loads and owned the game for 10 days. Opening it gives:  
- one hero pack of the player’s choice (from any paid pack),  
- a unique “Studio Mug” that appears on the shelf,  
- a permanent +3 % Lint bonus.  

Presented as a quiet thank-you from the one-person studio, never as a shop. No other purchases. Every dryer, every pack, every decor item remains fully earnable by play.  

The “buy Quarters” button would cost more in one-star reviews than it would ever earn. Individual pack sales are safer but still risk the “this is a mobile game” reaction. The single Studio Note is the only purchase I would ship.

Two paid cozy games that handle this well:  
- **A Short Hike** — one price, complete experience, optional supporter-style extras only on some platforms and never required.  
- **Chicory: A Colorful Tale** — paid base, later optional paid expansion that adds pure content and is clearly separate.

## Lane J. What is wrong / what I would break — new observations

1. **The “no new slots” caution is too cautious.** Wallpaper, floor, table surface and curtains are the highest-value visual changes possible for code-small cost. I would break that caution for those four.  
2. **The free pack should be Bakery Shift.** It is warm, universal, and gives every player something to screenshot on day one that feels different from the existing Uncle Energy / Gas Station packs.  
3. **I would allow one more comfort that slightly helps visibility** (the Soft Cloth Patch that gives a 5 % softer twin glow for 20 s). It stays on the comfort side of the line because it does not change scoring or difficulty tiers; it only makes the existing Eyes pegs feel a little kinder after time invested.  
4. **Missed opportunity:** there is no “station that is just the sound of the room with the dryer off” — soft ambient of the laundry room itself. That would be the ultimate quiet station.  
5. **The brief is strong.** The only real risk is shipping too many new dryers or pattern families before the coin economy is felt by players. Prioritise the new coin moments and the first two hero packs.
