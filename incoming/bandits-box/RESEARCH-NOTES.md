# What the rest of the world already figured out

Research notes for Bandit's Box. Everything below is either shipped, in the backlog, or explicitly rejected.

---

## Japan — Bandai's ∞ (Mugen) line

The single most useful thing I found. Bandai launched ∞プチプチ (Mugen Puchi Puchi, "infinite bubble wrap") as a keychain in October 2007. Double-layer silicone, eight bubbles, designed in partnership with the actual bubble wrap manufacturer to get the feel right.

**The mechanic that made it:** every hundredth pop, it stops popping and does something ridiculous instead — a door chime, a barking dog, a fart. It's not a score. There's nothing to win. It's a joke that rewards you for staying, and it's the reason people kept the thing on their keys for years.

Bandai then spun the idea into a taxonomy, and this is the part worth stealing wholesale. **Each toy is one verb, repeated forever, with one small surprise:**

| Toy | The verb | The surprise |
|---|---|---|
| ∞プチプチ | pop | every 100th pop is a gag sound |
| ∞エダマメ | push a bean out of a pod | one of twelve random faces |
| ∞ペリペリ | rip open a package tab | none — the rip *is* the payoff |
| ∞チョコレート | snap a chocolate bar | three "flavours" |

Not one of them has levels, currency, or an unlock tree. **Shipped:** the surprise mechanic and Edamame. **Backlog:** PeriPeri (rip tab) and Chocolate (snap bar).

## China — 电子木鱼 (the electronic wooden fish)

A tap target that says "merit +1" when you tap it. That's the entire app. It hit **#2 on the Chinese iOS free chart**; one version passed a million registered users in two months. Someone logged 1.08 million taps in a single month.

Two things drove it:
1. **The counter is not a score.** No goal, no fail, no opponent. It just goes up. People found that soothing in a way a high score never is.
2. **The floating word is user-editable.** People changed it to "worries −999999". The joke is the product.

**Shipped, carefully.** There's now an optional tally, off by default, and you can rename the word that floats up. It counts and nothing else — no goals, no badges, no streaks. This is the closest the app gets to breaking its own no-scores rule, and it's opt-in for exactly that reason.

## The Western category — where the money is and where the anger is

The category leaders (JindoBlu's *Antistress*, and roughly forty clones) ship **50–56 toys** each. The recurring ones we don't have yet: kinetic sand slicing, squishy/stress ball, keyboard and switch banks, **reversible sequins**, balloon popping, light bulb smashing, hydraulic press, money gun, music box, mouse clicker, coin flip, sand pendulum, potter's wheel, dalgona cookie.

But the thing worth reading is the **reviews**, not the feature lists. The single most useful sentence I found in this entire research pass was a one-star review of a top-ten app:

> *"I didn't want to watch an ad every time I wanted to unlock a new satisfying fidget."*

Every major app in this category gates its toys behind rewarded video. A stress-relief app that interrupts you with an ad to relieve stress. **That is the whole opening.** Bandit's Box has no ads, no unlocks, and works offline. That needs to be said out loud on the store page, not buried.

**Shipped:** Sequins — the best of the missing toys and a perfect fit for the friction engine.

## The autism and sensory research — this changed my priorities

Consistent across every source, academic and practitioner:

1. **Personalisation is the key UI element**, not a nice-to-have. Sensory tolerance varies enormously person to person, so one tuned default serves nobody. → **Shipped:** four themes (night, paper, faded, bold), plus the existing loudness, softer-highs, grip, quiet, calm-motion and buzz controls.
2. **Predictability.** Consistent navigation, no UI moving after updates, label every icon, no mystery-meat navigation. → the picker grid labels every toy in words, and toy order is now fixed.
3. **Avoid coercive gamification.** Named explicitly as a thing to avoid. → this is why the tally is opt-in and has no goals.
4. **No autoplay, no flashing, toggleable animation/sound/haptics.** → already in place.
5. **Redundant representation** — don't tie information to one channel. → every sound has a ripple; quiet mode keeps the full experience visually.

**Shipped since this doc was written**
- ~~PeriPeri~~ — rip strip, speed-tied tear sound, fresh package slides in
- ~~Chocolate snap~~ — three flavours with genuinely different crack timbres
- ~~Switch wall~~ — 25 switches, drag a run with your thumb, multi-touch
- ~~Stress ball~~ — every finger makes its own dent, depth is real pressure

**Next up**
1. **Balloon** — inflate on a held press with the tension rising as a note, then pop. The only toy where anticipation is the point.
2. **Kinetic sand slicing** — distinct from our sand tray; this is cutting, not drawing
3. **Coin flip / sand pendulum** — both nearly free given the physics already written
4. **Per-toy favourites** — 21 toys is enough that the picker should float the three you actually use
5. **Keyboard clicks** — a mechanical keyboard panel; very high in every competitor's list

**Explicitly rejected**
- Ads, rewarded video, unlock trees, currency — the entire category does this and it's our differentiator not to
- Leaderboards, streaks, daily rewards — coercive gamification, named in the autism literature as harmful
- Levels or progression gates on toys — a fidget toy you have to earn is not a fidget toy
