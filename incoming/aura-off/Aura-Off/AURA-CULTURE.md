# AURA OFF — Volume II: The Culture Dossier

**Compiled 28 August 2026.** Companion to `AURA-BIBLE.md`. Same evidence tiers apply: **[V1]** documented by named press, **[V2]** verified but not attested inside a battle, **[V3]** our invention, **[?]** unresolved.

This volume covers what the bible didn't: the language, the people, the map, the backlash, the defence, and the money.

## 1. THE GLOSSARY

Exact terms as used, with sourced meanings. This is the vocabulary the game should speak — and the vocabulary it should avoid.

### 1.1 Core terms

| Term | Language | Meaning | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **aura** | universal | Perceived coolness, presence, confidence, style, charisma. **Explicitly not spiritual** — sources go out of their way to say so. | [V1] |
| **aura farming** | EN | Cultivating effortless charisma through repetitive "cool" actions. Popularized 2024 via anime and celebrity discourse. | [V1] |
| **farmear aura** / **farmar aura** | ES / PT | The Spanish and Portuguese forms. *Farmear* is gamer slang — repeat actions to accumulate resources, XP, or points to level up. | [V1] |
| **batalla de aura** / **aura battle** / **aura off** | ES / EN | The face-to-face competition. "Aura off" is a play on "show off." | [V1] |
| **aura points** / **puntos de aura** | both | The imaginary currency. | [V1] |
| **anak coki** | Indonesian | The boat dancer. Dikha's affectionate nickname after going viral. | [V1] |
| **Togak Luan** / **Tukang Tari** | Malay Riau | The formal name of the boat-dancer role. | [V1] |
| **clip farming** | EN | Distinct from aura farming: **deliberately** provocative or exaggerated acts to manufacture engagement. Aura farming relies on *seeming* effortless; clip farming is overt and premeditated. | [V1] |

### 1.2 The scoring language — this is our HUD

Documented as common on TikTok and Instagram:

- **"+1000 de aura"** — the standard callout
- **"+10.000 de aura"** — the big one
- **"+100 aura"**
- **"aura 100%"**
- **"perdió aura"** — lost aura, for a public failure
- **"aura infinita"** — infinite aura. **This is the actual prize in Argentina.** No cash. Just the title.

> **Design ruling:** the game's score popups should use this exact register. `+1000 AURA` is correct and native. Invented units are not.

### 1.3 Adjacent slang [V2]

Verified terms, present in the culture, **not** verified as in-battle gestures: *sigma*, *beta*, *rizz*, *mewing*, *looksmaxxing* (softmaxxing vs hardmaxxing), *NPC*, *goofy ahh*, *brain rot*, *Fanum tax* (taking a bite of a friend's food, named for YouTuber Fanum), *gyatt*.

**Excluded from the game:** *gyatt* (inappropriate for audience). **Handle as comedy only:** sigma, mewing, looksmaxxing — see the bible's content-safety note.

### 1.4 On "six-seven" meaning nothing

Worth internalizing because it shapes the game's tone. Dictionary.com made **"6 7" its 2025 Word of the Year while conceding it has no fixed meaning.** Excélsior reports kids use it to mean "más o menos" (so-so), or just when joking around. A 15-year-old told the Washington Post it's ironic — people are making fun of the fact that it isn't funny.

> **Design ruling:** the game must not explain the joke. Moves get flavour text about *how to perform them*, never about *what they mean*. Meaning is the one thing this culture refuses to supply.

## 4. THE FEEDBACK LOOP

Yahoo's account of the mechanism, and it's the sharpest structural insight available: **the street feeds the networks, and the networks bring more people to the next competition.** Each battle generates fresh video, which recruits the next crowd.

Euronews adds that organizers deliberately design the battles to be **fast-paced, echoing the short high-impact videos that spread the trend** — competitors perform for just a few seconds at a time.

> **Two design rulings.**
> 1. **Exchanges must be short.** Seconds, not a routine. Our ~1.5–2.2s clips are right; do not lengthen them.
> 2. **The loop is a mechanic waiting to be built.** Winning should generate a "clip" that recruits a bigger crowd for the next fight — literally more crowd figures on screen, and a higher meter ceiling. That models the real propagation and gives the campaign a visible sense of scale.

## 5. THE NEGATIVE FILE

Every documented ban, incident, and criticism. This is Act 3's source material — and it should be handled with care rather than played for laughs.

### 5.1 Costa Rica — banned in schools [V1]

The **Ministerio de Educación Pública (MEP)** prohibited the competitions in schools. Minister **José Leonardo Sánchez Hernández** stated the position precisely, and the nuance matters:

Farmear aura is, in principle, a playful activity among young people. It stops being that when it is used to **humiliate, ridicule, harass, or discriminate** against another person; when it **endangers the integrity** of participants; when it creates **dangerous crowding**; or when it **interrupts lessons**.

He was explicit that the measure **is not intended to persecute or criminalize the expression** — it applies only where school coexistence or student safety is at risk.

> That is a genuinely well-drawn line, and it's the honest read on this culture: the same format is either a kid finding somewhere to belong or a kid getting publicly humiliated, and which one depends entirely on the room.

### 5.2 Honduras — banned in public spaces [V1]
Mayor **Samuel Romero** of **La Libertad**. His remark: if it were up to him, compulsory military service would exist.

### 5.3 Mexico — first legal obstacle [V1]
The municipal government of **Ignacio de la Llave, Veracruz** issued an official statement cancelling a "Batalla de aura" scheduled for its Parque Municipal. Triggered national debate and mockery.

### 5.4 Spain — violence [V1]
A **Barcelona** battle began normally, then escalated until gestures gave way to shoving and blows. Coverage stressed this **departs from the normal dynamic**, where confrontation stays limited to attitude and staging.

### 5.5 The broader criticism [V1]
- Battles spread amid a **Mexican policy debate on regulating social media and phone use in schools.**
- They are a target of mockery and criticism, **especially from older audiences.**
- A widely-shared TikTok critique asks whether this is innocent fun or **normalizing kids measuring their worth by how many likes and shouts they get in a plaza** — framed as social pressure disguised as a trend, potentially damaging younger kids' self-esteem.

### 5.6 The child-safety thread — read this one carefully
- Competitors in **Torreón were as young as six.**
- Dikha, 11, became the target of **documented online scams and impersonators** exploiting his fame. Australian ABC reported warnings about it.
- CNA reported Dikha **crying from exhaustion** when woken to compete, with his father encouraging him to get up. His father's words: as a parent, he wants his children to be more successful than he was.

> ⛔ **Standing rule for this project.** We are building a game whose core audience overlaps with real children currently being filmed, judged, and monetized by adults. **The game must never reward humiliating an opponent.** No move that mocks a specific person. No "roast" mechanic. Our BAIT category is *self*-directed clowning — falling over, noodle legs, absurd poses — never punching at the opponent. Lasso is the single exception and it's a rope gag, not a mockery.
>
> This isn't legal caution. It's the actual dividing line the Costa Rican education ministry drew, and they drew it correctly.

## 6. THE POSITIVE FILE

The defence is substantive and it should shape the game's heart.

**María Eugenia Tovar**, Venezuelan psychologist [V1]: she stresses the importance of communication through social media and through memes. All this digital communication is **real** for adolescents and genuinely has an impact on them — to the point that it can also **prompt in-person encounters.**

**Frederick Luis Aldama**, UT Austin [V1]: the appeal is tied to a **long history of the plaza, the courtyard, the gathering in parks.** These are collective, public, person-to-person. They provide a **free, nonviolent space** for young people to seek human connection among peers, and may reflect a **desire to disconnect from technology and be present.**

**AFP's framing** [V1]: these are in-person gatherings of young people that are **increasingly rare in the digital era.**

**Uvitinho**, the Suzano organizer [V1]: for him farmar aura goes beyond the internet's definition — it's about **expressing your personality and your tastes.**

**Kaled Rosales**, the winner [V1]: more than for the prize, he does it to have a good time and take his mind off things at home.

> **This is the game's thesis.** Not "get famous." Not "beat everyone." It's: *the phones went away, everyone came outside, and for twenty minutes a public square belonged to teenagers.* Every environment should be a real public space. The crowd should feel like people, not a meter. The ending should be about the room, not the ranking.

> A fifteen-to-twenty-second clip of a child dancing generated **six and a half million dollars** for a rural regency in Sumatra, and turned a centuries-old tradition into an international destination.
>
> That is the single best story in this entire project, it is entirely true, and **nobody has built a game about it.** Act 5 shouldn't just be the hardest fight. It should be the reveal — the player finally learns where all of this came from, and it isn't TikTok.

## 8. NEW MOVE MATERIAL

### 8.1 Verified additions

**The closing grimace** [V1] — AFP's account of a battle's end: two kids jump and gesture, stare fixedly into each other's eyes, and one throws **a final, decisive grimace** — *una mueca final contundente*. That ends it. Everyone applauds.

> **Build this.** A finisher move, unlocked late, usable only when the meter is close. Pure face, zero body. It's the documented way battles actually end.

**Costume and prop work** [V1] — beyond the sandal-lasso: some competitors compete **dressed as a frog**, using **a giant rubber clog** during the battle.

> Extends the Fit Check into a real system: absurd costume as an opening statement. And the giant clog validates prop moves as a category, not a one-off.

**The boat dance, physically described** [V1] — AFP's description of how athletes imitated Dikha: **swirling their hands, then swinging their arms back and forth.** That's the cleanest movement description available anywhere in the corpus. It should drive the River Prow keyframes.

**The ambience** [V1] — Infobae notes battles are surrounded by **video game music**, **football references**, and **digital aesthetics**. Everyone films on their phones.

### 8.2 Format details worth building

- Competitors **register in advance** [V1]
- **Elimination rounds** [V1]
- Competitors perform for **only a few seconds** per turn [V1]
- Judged by **jury and public**, or public alone by volume of **cheers, applause, and laughter** [V1]
- Prizes range from **3,000 pesos** to **R$250** to **nothing but the title** [V1]
- Rosales **studied his opponents' moves in order to counterattack** [V1] — study is part of the sport, which validates our unlock-by-defeat system

## 10. WHAT CHANGED IN THE DESIGN

Everything above, condensed into decisions:

1. **Score popups use native language.** `+1000 AURA`, `+10.000`, `AURA INFINITA` for a flawless win, `PERDIÓ AURA` on a whiff. Not invented units.
2. **Add "The Grimace"** — a face-only finisher, unlocked late, usable only when the meter is close. It's how battles actually end.
3. **Fit Check becomes a real system** — costume as opening statement. Frog suit. Giant clog. Absurdity is a documented strategy.
4. **Add the clip loop** — winning grows the crowd for the next fight. The street feeds the networks; the networks fill the street.
5. **No move may mock the opponent.** BAIT is self-directed clowning only. This is the line the Costa Rican ministry drew and it's the right one.
6. **Rebalance the roster toward women.** Alejandra Bastilla took second in Cochabamba; AFP photographed a young woman competing in CDMX. Currently 6 of 20. Raise it.
7. **Act 5 is a reveal, not just a difficulty spike.** The player learns the whole thing started with an eleven-year-old on a 40-metre canoe, and that it made his home region $6.5 million.
8. **The ending is about the room, not the ranking.** The winner's own words: he does it to have a good time and take his mind off things at home.
