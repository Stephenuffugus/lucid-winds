You are helping redesign the borders and unlockables in Hues, a phone game by Sky Wolf Studio. I am attaching three screenshots: a border during play, the border shop, and the shared result card.

WHAT HUES IS
A target color appears. The player slides a saturation pad and a hue strip until their swatch matches it, then locks it in. Every guess is scored on real color difference. Modes: one Daily Hue a day (five colors, the same for everyone, a streak, a share card of color squares), Endless (three lives, rising levels, four difficulties, coins), and Versus (pass the phone). The look is quiet on purpose: near black, chalk white, one coral warning color and one brass accent, so the colors being matched are the only loud things on screen. It sells for $0.99 on Google Play: one purchase, no ads, nothing else to buy.

WHAT EXISTS TODAY
1. Borders are the main unlockable. A border frames the two swatches (Target and Yours) on every play screen, and frames the shared result card.
2. There are 120 borders: 8 drawn in code (Hairline, Bevel, Frost, Brutalist, Gold Leaf, Neon, Deco, Prism) and 112 picture frames in 16 families (Clay Crew, Crayon Box, Sticker, Pressed Garden, Foil, Trophy, Medals, Stained Glass, Embroidery, Patchwork, Neon, Woodblock, Moss, Season Stickers, Clockwork, Fable).
3. A picture frame is landscape, about 1.39 to 1, with an empty center window. In play it is stretched over the swatch and its corner decorations spill outward. On the share card it is sliced so the corners stay fixed while the rails stretch.
4. Every border is bought with coins, from 250 coins up to 12,000 for the showpiece (Golden Chameleon). Coins come from playing and from three daily goals. Nothing is earned only by skill yet.
5. Planned but never built: "alive" borders whose critters blink, an earned trophy lane (streaks, stages, versus wins), swatch backs, coin skins and win bursts.
6. The shop is one long grid of tiles.

WHAT IS WRONG
The owner's words: "a serious overhaul on the borders and unlockables". What we have seen:
1. Some picture frames cover part of both swatches during play. The frog frame in the first screenshot covers the top of the very colors the player is trying to match. A border must never cover the color.
2. All 120 borders are bought the same way, so none of them means anything. Nothing marks a long streak, a perfect match or a hard win.
3. The families do not share one look, and some fight the quiet style of the game.
4. One long grid is hard to browse.

WHAT I WANT FROM YOU, IN THIS ORDER

PART A, THE PLAN (answer this first, no pictures yet)
1. Your verdict on the current borders: which families to keep, redraw or retire, and why.
2. A new unlockable structure: what kinds of unlockables Hues should have (borders, and anything else that suits a color game), how each one is earned (coins, earned only trophies for streaks, perfect matches, levels, versus wins, the Daily), how many of each, and the prices. Work for it, but fun.
3. How the shop should be organized: its sections, what a player sees first, owned versus not owned.
4. Rules every border must follow so it never hurts the game: the center window stays clear, decorations only on the outer rails and corners, nothing ever covers a swatch, readable at phone size, calm next to the colors.
5. You are good at this, so take creative freedom: suggest anything we have not thought of.

Answer format for Part A: a short verdict, then a table of the new unlockables (name, type, how it is earned, price or goal), then the shop layout, then the border rules, then your extra ideas.

PART B, THE ART (only after the plan is agreed, one family per message)
1. Each border sheet: 8 frames in a 4 by 2 grid, each frame landscape, about 460 by 330 pixels, with an empty center window of about 1.39 to 1.
2. The background AND the empty center window are one flat solid magenta, #FF00FF: no gradient, no texture, no shadow on it.
3. No magenta, hot pink or pink purple anywhere in the frame art, so the magenta can be removed cleanly. If a family has to be pink, use flat pure green #00FF00 as the background instead and say so.
4. Crisp edges, no soft glow into the magenta, no text, light from the upper left.
5. End each reply with an assembly note for every frame: its name, a short description, which unlock or price it belongs to, and for an animated border, which frames make the loop and how fast.
