# JIMOTHY SHEET 14L — Quiet hazards — tension without a single extra object

⛔ THE OTHER FUN SHEET, and the one I would fight for. These are small, cheap and mostly
NOT lethal, and they do more for how the game feels than another truck ever will.

The best design idea in the whole pack is cell 4: a harmless thing that reads as dangerous.
It teaches players to actually READ a lane instead of pattern matching on shape, and it
costs one small sprite. Cell 1 is the second best: a shadow that arrives before the thing
that casts it is the cheapest telegraph in games.

## The nine cells

1. **Gull shadow on the ground** — one soft dark ellipse. Arrives a beat BEFORE the dive.
   Almost no pixels, enormous fairness win.
2. **Gull mid snatch, holding a bottlecap** — a hazard that ROBS you instead of killing
   you. Tension with no punishment. We have nothing like this yet.
3. **Scattering pigeons** — burst outward when you land near. Pure reaction, zero danger,
   makes the world feel alive.
4. **A plastic bag blowing across the lane** — looks exactly like a hazard, is completely
   harmless. The fake out. Makes every real hazard read better by contrast.
5. **Wind gust as a debris streak** — leaves and grit. Shoves you one column sideways.
   A whole mechanic for one sprite.
6. **A rat running AWAY from you** — the opposite of every other rat. Funny, harmless.
7. **Puddle splash ring** — feedback for landing in shallow water. Cosmetic only.
8. **A dropped hot dog with a gull eyeing it** — scenery that tells a joke.
9. **Fog bank edge** — one soft gradient strip. Hides the far side of a lane, so you have
   to commit. Adds real difficulty and REMOVES visual detail, which is the trick.

## Format
3 x 3 grid, **magenta #FF00FF** background, **white divider lines** between cells.
Side on, same camera and the same ground line as the existing vehicles, so they drop
straight into a lane. One sheet per image. Note a rough width per item ("about as wide as
a sedan" is fine) so the hitbox can be set honestly.
