READ THIS FIRST (Stephen, about a minute)

What the game is: Bubblenaut is the alien bubble trap platformer Jessie asked for. You are a little alien on specimen duty: blow bubbles to trap the runaway critters, pop them, and catch the treasure that falls out. Five worlds of five rooms, one kind of critter and one treasure per world. Today everything is drawn by the code as simple shapes: a coloured blob with eyes for you, coloured balls with eyes for the critters, rounded bars for platforms, and a flat colour with star dots behind. These pictures replace all of that with real characters and real places.

NAME: Bubblenaut is still a working title and the name is your call. Nothing in these pictures has words in it, so the art does not wait on the name, but the Play listing will.

Concept line: a pocket sized alien zookeeper chasing escaped critters through five glowing worlds, everything chunky, glossy and easy to read at a glance on a phone.

What is in here: 2 pastes, 21 pictures.
Paste 1: the characters, the critters, the treasures and the building blocks, each cut out on plain grey. 13 pictures.
Paste 2: the five world backgrounds, the title screen art and the store art (Play banner, app icon). 8 pictures.

The five worlds: Moss Moon (the Hopper, the Sunfruit), Crystal Caverns (the Skitter, the Geode), Rust Belt (the Drone, the Star Cog), Frost Ring (the Slick, the Frost Pearl), Magma Core (the Cinder, the Ember Opal).


THE STYLE IS YOUR CALL. Three options:

A. Glossy vinyl toy (my pick). Every character looks like a chunky collectible vinyl figure: rounded, soft studio light, glossy highlights, candy colours glowing on dark backgrounds. Why I pick it: big simple shapes still read at the tiny size they show in play, and it keeps the bright on midnight colours the game already has.

B. Retro arcade cabinet art. Airbrushed 1980s cabinet side art: chrome highlights, bold outlines, star fields, saturated gradients. A loving nod to the old bubble arcades without copying any of their characters.

C. Cosmic sticker sheet. Flat bold shapes with a thick white sticker outline and almost no shading. The most consistent from picture to picture and the cleanest to cut out, but the plainest of the three.

The pastes below have A built in. To use B or C, in both pastes replace the line that starts with "Style for every picture:" with one of these:

B: Style for every picture: 1980s airbrushed arcade cabinet art, chrome highlights, bold dark outlines, saturated colour gradients, a little glow, crisp and polished.

C: Style for every picture: a flat vector sticker, bold simple shapes, flat colours with at most one small shine, a thick clean white outline around every object, no texture.


HOW TO USE IT

1. Pick the style.
2. Copy everything from START PASTE 1 down to END PASTE 1 and send it to Astra as one message.
3. When it is done, send me the pictures (the zip, or the images) and say "Bubblenaut paste 1". I cut them out, size them and wire them in, and I show you the game with them in before anything goes live.
4. Then Paste 2 the same way.

About animation: each character comes as four poses in one picture, so it stays the same character. The motion between poses (the squash on landing, the bob, the lean) is done by the code. If you later want full frame by frame animation, that is a second paste per character once the style is proven.

picture-09 to 13 blocks per world: assets/tiles/<world>-{ledge,block,block-top,deco}.png. The thin platform ('=') draws today as a 12 px bar at y + 3 of its 36 px tile (roundRect in render); the solid block ('#') fills the tile. The ledge must repeat horizontally and the block in both directions: make them seamless in post (crop to the flat middle, mirror or offset fill), Astra will not hand back a true tile. Decorations sit on the top face of a solid block with open air above it; place them by a seeded random per room so a room looks the same every visit.
Bubbles and pops stay drawn by code (drawBubble): a picture on grey cannot carry see through glass. Give the code bubble a better shine instead, and use pop-burst.png as the pop flash.
