# Meadow Weave — Art Pack

> Turn the hex, set it down, let meadow meet meadow — close a region and it comes alive and presses a feral seed into your pouch.

**Genre:** Dorfromantik-style hex tile-laying (single-file HTML5, 540×960 portrait — canvas board + DOM menu screens). An axial hex grid grows one tile at a time: each pointy-top hex has six edges, each edge one of **five lands** — Meadow / Pond / Forest / Field / Orchard — you **Rotate** the next tile and tap a glowing legal slot to **place** it. Every matched edge scores (+2; a perfect placement against 3+ neighbors bonuses and refunds a tile to the bag). Grow a same-land matched cluster to a size milestone (3/6/9…) or fully enclose one and the region **comes alive** — a Living Diorama particle burst at the region's heart — and **presses a feral seed** into your pouch (bigger or enclosed regions press rarer seeds; rarity 3+ seeds get a gold rim). Four modes: **Endless Weave** (52-tile bag), **Daily Meadow** (42, date-seeded, streak), **Quest Weave** (46 + two region-size quests), **Zen Grove** (infinite, no earn). No clock, no losing — the run simply ends when the bag runs out. Colorblind support is first-class: an **Edge symbols** toggle (on by default) overlays a glyph per land (`"` meadow, `~` pond, `^` forest, `|` field, `*` orchard) so edges never read by hue alone.

_The game already ships and plays procedurally (`satellites/meadow-weave/index.html`) — this art is an optional visual upgrade **and** the cosmetics library for the in-game wardrobe economy. Grid geometry, hitboxes, scoring and the five-land edge model are IDENTICAL across every look below; only the skin changes._

## Pick a look

⚖️ One honest constraint before the options: in this game the **landscape IS the mechanic** — the five lands are hard-coded into play and the menu copy ("meadow meets meadow, pond meets pond") is code-owned. So the theme is free to change the **material and the setting** (what the tiles are made of, whose table they sit on) but not the landscape fiction itself. The three directions below run cozy → polished → boldest material departure; all kid-friendly.

### 1. Pressed Herbarium (cozy, craft-table)
*A keepsake craft table at midnight: the hexes are layered pressed-paper landforms — cut cardstock meadows, tissue ponds, dried-fern forests — assembled scrapbook-style under a warm desk lamp, with real pressed seeds waxed into the pouch pages.* Warmest and most native to the "press a feral seed" fiction, and it literally matches the starter tile set's in-code name (*Papercut*). **Why not:** it keeps the game fully inside the cozy-botanical house lane the Director wants freedom from, and soft paper edges read the quietest of the three on a portal card.

### 2. Lantern Atlas ⭐ RECOMMENDED (polished storybook-explorer)
*A cartographer's night desk: every hex is a hand-inked, gouache-washed atlas plate that glows as if a warm lantern burns beneath the vellum — luminous landforms with engraved line textures and thin gilded seams, laid on a deep midnight table scattered with map tools. When a region comes alive, the map itself stirs: fish leap out of the inked pond, grain swirls off the field.* This leaves the garden-craft fiction for a map-room explorer fiction while keeping the landscapes (which the mechanic owns anyway). It sits native on the game's locked midnight palette (`#0d100c` table, `#c8a84b` gold, `#e8dcc8` cream), its engraved per-land textures ARE the colorblind answer (texture + value before hue), and the wardrobe's four in-code tile sets — Papercut, Stained Glass, Embroidered, Moonlit Ink — read naturally as four plate treatments from the same atlas. **Why not:** slightly more rendering discipline needed than Herbarium (ink lines must stay crisp at a 34px hex).

### 3. Aurora Glassworks (bold, premium, least botanical)
*Leaded stained-glass jewel hexes on a dark slate table, each land a different backlit glass — rippled blue pond glass, deep bottle-green forest, honey-amber field — with solder-line seams that flare when edges match.* The furthest material departure and the most premium; it hands the *Stained Glass* and *Frostglass* cosmetic names their look for free. **Why not:** backlit glass trades away warmth, five glowing hues risk leaning on color exactly where the colorblind rule forbids it, and full-glass boards get visually loud as the map grows past 40 tiles.

**Recommendation: Lantern Atlas.** It escapes the forced-cozy craft lane without fighting the code-owned landscape copy, is the safest fit for the locked midnight palette (lowest integration risk), and its engraving-first texture language is the strongest colorblind play of the three. Borrow Herbarium's warmth for the feral-seed pouch chips, and let Glassworks be exactly what the *Stained Glass* / *Frostglass* cosmetics look like inside the atlas. **All sheets below bake Lantern Atlas in.**

## Cosmetics economy

Every Meadow Weave cosmetic is earned by **PLAYING** — no loot boxes, no randomized purchases, nothing that touches the grid, tile draws, scoring or seed rarity. Every unlock is a **KNOWN threshold shown right on the locked wardrobe card** (the card displays its `desc` string until earned). Two cosmetic families and four earn faucets, all already wired in `meadowweave_save` (`PROG` + `TILESETS[]` / `BACKDROPS[]`, unlock check `ownedC()` → `metric(kind)`):

- **Tile sets** (`PROG.tileset`): *Papercut* (starter, free) → **Stained Glass** (close **20** regions, `PROG.totalRegions`) → **Embroidered** (best score **1200**, `PROG.bestScore`) → **Moonlit Ink** (**7-day** Daily streak, `PROG.streak`).
- **Table backdrops** (`PROG.backdrop`): *Night Table* (starter, free) → **Firefly Field** (weave **6** maps, `PROG.runs`) → **Harvest Dusk** (best score **800**, `PROG.bestScore`) → **Frostglass** (close **60** regions, `PROG.totalRegions`).

Faucets: **regions closed** (the core loop), **best score** (mastery), **runs finished** (showing up), and the **Daily streak** (the lapsed-friendly return loop). All four persist in `localStorage` (`meadowweave_save`), so gating is free and offline-safe; the equipped choice lives in `PROG.tileset` / `PROG.backdrop`, and `PROG.owned{}` allows an optional manual grant without touching earn logic. The **feral pouch** (`PROG.pouch`, capped at 200, rarity 1-4) is a keepsake gallery — seeds are trophies, never a currency. Sunbeams stay the earn/collection currency only (`_sbCapEarn`: **30/day** cap; per-run grant is `min(12, ⌊score/500⌋ + ferals)`, **+2** on the first Daily clear of the day; **Zen earns none**). Skins are **purely visual** — they never change tile edges, legal slots, region logic or Sunbeam payout, and every item stays fully earnable free by playing.
