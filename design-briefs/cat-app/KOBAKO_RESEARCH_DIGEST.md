### RESEARCH: Japanese cat culture (maneki-neko, cat shrines and islands, folklore, art, modern cat economy and naming) for a cat trading-card app

FINDINGS:
- [verified] MANEKI-NEKO PAW: 右手 (migite, right paw) raised = 金運上昇 (kin'un jōshō, money luck). 左手 (hidari-te, left paw) = 千客万来・商売繁盛 (senkyaku-banrai / shōbai-hanjō, invites people and customers). Both paws raised is read either as 'money AND people' or as greedy/surrender, depending on who you ask.
  (source: All About 暮らしの歳時記 https://allabout.co.jp/gm/gc/486335/ ; corroborated by 縁起物百科事典 https://engimono.net/luckycharm/manekineko-guide/ and English Wikipedia 'Maneki-neko')
- [likely] PAW HEIGHT is a second, separate axis: paw raised 耳より高く (higher than the ears) = 遠くの福を招く (invites fortune from far away); 耳よりも低い (lower than the ears) = 近くの福を招く (invites nearby fortune).
  (source: All About https://allabout.co.jp/gm/gc/486335/)
- [verified] MANEKI-NEKO COLOUR MAP (the mainstream Japanese consumer mapping): 白 shiro white = 開運招福・商売繁盛 (general luck, business); 黒 kuro black = 魔除け・厄除け・家内安全 (wards evil, household safety); 赤 aka red = 病除け・無病息災 (wards illness); 金/黄 kin/ki gold-yellow = 金運上昇 (wealth); ピンク pink = 恋愛成就 (love); 青 ao blue = 学業成就・就職成功・交通安全 (study, job, road safety); 緑 midori green = 家内安全・学力向上 (family safety, academic ability); 紫 murasaki purple = 健康長寿 (health and long life).
  (source: All About https://allabout.co.jp/gm/gc/486335/ ; a narrower version (white/gold/black/red/pink only) at 縁起物百科事典 https://engimono.net/luckycharm/manekineko-guide/)
- [verified] Japanese sources explicitly state the colour meanings are NOT canonical — 縁起物百科事典 notes interpretations 「地域や販売者によって異なります」 (vary by region and by seller). There is no single authority. A product may pick one mapping as long as it is internally consistent.
  (source: https://engimono.net/luckycharm/manekineko-guide/)
- [verified] GOTOKUJI'S OWN CAT BREAKS THE COMMERCIAL RULES. The temple states: 「豪徳寺の招福猫児は小判を持っておらず、右手をあげています。」 — Gotokuji's cat holds NO koban coin and raises its RIGHT paw. Doctrine: 「招福猫児は、人を招いて『縁』をもたらしてくれますが、福そのものを与えてくれるわけではありません。」 (it brings you the connection, not the fortune itself).
  (source: Gotokuji official site 大谿山 豪徳寺 https://gotokuji.jp/manekineko/)
- [verified] GOTOKUJI LEGEND: 井伊直孝 (Ii Naotaka, 1590–1659), 2nd lord of the Hikone domain, was beckoned in from the temple gate by a cat while out hawking, sheltered from a sudden thunderstorm, and in gratitude endowed the poor temple. Gotokuji was restored with his support in 寛永10年 = 1633. The temple was later renamed from his posthumous name. Gotokuji calls the figure 招福猫児 (manekineko), written with unusual kanji, not the standard 招き猫.
  (source: Gotokuji official https://gotokuji.jp/manekineko/ ; Tets Kimura (Adjunct Lecturer in Creative Arts, Flinders University), The Conversation, 2023 https://theconversation.com/what-is-the-story-of-maneki-neko-the-japanese-beckoning-cat-203906)
- [likely] GOTOKUJI'S RETURN LOOP is the real mechanic: visitors buy a manekineko, take it home with a wish, and RETURN it to the 招福猫児奉納所 (dedication rack) when the wish is granted. Prices ¥500–¥10,000 by size; 豆 (mame, bean-size, cased) ¥1,000; お守り ¥1,000; 御朱印帳 ¥2,500. NHK's Buratamori staff counted over 15,000 returned cats on the racks (TV-source, treat the count as approximate).
  (source: Gotokuji shop page https://gotokuji.jp/shop/ ; count reported at https://serorino-hitorigoto.com/goutokuji-manekineko-goshuin/ citing NHK ブラタモリ)
- [likely] IMADO SHRINE (今戸神社, Imado-jinja, Asakusa, Taito-ku) rival claim: an impoverished old woman gave up her cat; the cat appeared in her dream saying 「自分の姿を人形にしたら福徳を授かる」 (make a doll of me and you will receive fortune); she made them in 今戸焼 (Imado-yaki, local earthenware, fired in Imado since the 16th century) and sold them at Sensoji, and they became a hit. Imado is a Shinto shrine (founded 1063); Gotokuji is a Sōtō Zen Buddhist temple. Do not mix the words.
  (source: SPOT https://travel.spot-app.jp/tokyo_manekineko_chiptaso/ ; Imado Shrine official https://imadojinja1063.crayonsite.net/ (confirms 1063 founding and 縁結び matchmaking focus, but does NOT itself claim the maneki-neko origin on its site))
- [likely] JISHOIN (自性院, Nishi-Ochiai, Shinjuku-ku, Shingon Buzan sect): in 文明9年 = 1477, at the Battle of Egota-Numabukuro, a black cat appeared before the lost warlord 太田道灌 (Ōta Dōkan) and led him to the temple, saving him. Dōkan kept the cat; after its death a stone 猫地蔵 (neko-jizō) was donated. The secret cat-Jizō statue is unveiled to the public ONCE A YEAR, on 節分 (Setsubun, Feb 3).
  (source: ja.wikipedia 自性院 (新宿区) https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/自性院_(新宿区) ; 太田道灌 official site https://doukan.jp/about/episode3 ; https://nyanmaga.com/自性院/)
- [verified] EARLIEST DOCUMENTED MANEKI-NEKO is late, not ancient: the 丸〆猫 (marushime-neko), an Imado-ware figurine, recorded in 武江年表 (Bukō Nenpyō, a chronology of Edo) under 嘉永5年 = 1852. Utagawa Hiroshige's ukiyo-e 「浄るり町繁華の図」 (Jōruri-machi hanka no zu), also 1852, shows a stall of them. Mass production by plaster mould came in the Meiji era (1868–1912); the big-head, wide-eye 'cartoon' maneki-neko is a 1950s Aichi Prefecture redesign.
  (source: Tets Kimura, The Conversation https://theconversation.com/what-is-the-story-of-maneki-neko-the-japanese-beckoning-cat-203906 ; en.wikipedia Maneki-neko; https://travel.spot-app.jp/tokyo_manekineko_chiptaso/)
- [likely] THE BELL CAME BEFORE THE COIN. Early maneki-neko had no koban; they wore a red 首輪 (kubiwa, collar) with a 鈴 (suzu, bell), copied from real Edo-period practice — 奥女中 (okujochū, ladies of the inner palace) belled their cats so they would not be lost. The bell later mutated into the gold coin.
  (source: 和composition WARGO https://wargo.jp/blogs/neko-column/manekineko_explanation ; corroborated by 中川政七商店 https://story.nakagawa-masashichi.jp/craft_post/121878)
- [likely] KOBAN DENOMINATION INFLATION: the coin's written value escalated over time — 千両 (sen-ryō) → 万両 → 百万両 → and today 千万両 (senman-ryō, ten million ryō) is the standard. Modest older cats carry 千両. Tied to the proverb 猫に小判 (neko ni koban, 'gold coins to a cat' = pearls before swine).
  (source: 招き猫の秘密 https://www.manekineko-secret.com/knowledge106 ; https://wargo.jp/blogs/neko-column/manekineko_explanation)
- [verified] THE BECKONING GESTURE IS PALM-DOWN. The Japanese beckon by holding the hand up palm DOWN and folding the fingers toward themselves — that is what the cat's paw is doing. Western-market maneki-neko are frequently manufactured palm-UP, which is a different (and in Japan slightly rude) gesture.
  (source: en.wikipedia Maneki-neko https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maneki-neko)
- [verified] 招き猫の日 (Maneki-neko no Hi, Beckoning Cat Day) = SEPTEMBER 29, established 1995 by the 日本招猫倶楽部 (Nihon Shōbyō Club). The pun: 9 = くる (kuru, 'comes') + 29 = ふく (fuku, 'fortune') → 来る福 kuru-fuku, 'fortune comes'. The 来る福招き猫まつり festival has run in Seto (Aichi) since 1996 and also in Ise (Mie) and Shimabara (Nagasaki). Seto began mass-producing ceramic maneki-neko in the 1880s.
  (source: ja.wikipedia 来る福招き猫まつり ; https://www.makemyday.jp/note/manekineko-day-0929/ ; 招き猫ミュージアム https://www.luckycat.ne.jp/)
- [verified] CAT DAY (猫の日, Neko no Hi) = FEBRUARY 22, established 1987 by the 猫の日実行委員会 (Cat Day Executive Committee, formed autumn 1986 by cat-loving academics and cultural figures) together with ペットフード工業会 (now 一般社団法人ペットフード協会, the Pet Food Association of Japan). Pun: 2-2-2 = にゃん・にゃん・にゃん (nyan nyan nyan). The date was chosen by PUBLIC BALLOT — 8,953 submissions were received and Feb 22 was the most popular (reported at roughly 30% of the vote). Stated purpose: 「猫と一緒に暮らせる幸せに感謝し、猫とともにこの喜びをかみしめる記念日を」.
  (source: ja.wikipedia 猫の日 https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/猫の日 ; 雑学ネタ帳 https://zatsuneta.com/archives/102221.html)
- [verified] Feb 22 is now a genuine national commercial tentpole, not a niche. 2025 examples: Cainz 「にゃん祭り2025」 (Feb 1–Mar 3, all stores + online); FamilyMart 「ファミリ～にゃ～ト大作戦!」 21 original products across ~16,200 stores from Feb 18; 7-Eleven cat-design QUO Pay prize draw Feb 17–23; ねこの日おめでとう NyART FESTIVAL at Tokyu Plaza Harajuku Harakado; ねこまつり at Yushima (20th edition). Separately, 8 August is 世界猫の日 (World Cat Day) — a DIFFERENT day, do not conflate.
  (source: Cainz press release https://www.cainz.co.jp/news/11475/ ; https://www.nyankoinfo.com/japan-cat-day/ ; https://dogcalin.com/2025-nekonohi/ ; ja.wikipedia 猫の日)

- [likely] JAPAN NOW KEEPS MORE CATS THAN DOGS. Pet Food Association of Japan 2025 全国犬猫飼育実態調査: cats 8.847 million (884.7万頭, down from 915.5万), dogs 6.820 million (682.0万頭, up from 679.6万) — a gap of ~2.03 million. The widely-reported 'cats overtook dogs in 2017' used the OLD estimation formula; under the formula revised in 2021, cats had already overtaken dogs by 2014.
  (source: Pet Food Association 2025 survey as reported by https://midori-ikimono.com/2025/12/22/petfoodkyokai2025/ and https://www.hpc-news.co.jp/media/market/a1947 ; formula-revision caveat from https://kashikapedia.com/pet-trend/)
- [verified] TASHIROJIMA (田代島, Ishinomaki, Miyagi): ~1,000 residents in the 1950s, ~80 by 2015 with 83% elderly (designated a 限界集落 'terminal village'); several hundred cats, at least 150 permanently in one village. Cats were kept to protect silkworms from mice in the late Edo period. 猫神社 (Neko-jinja, Cat Shrine) enshrines a cat killed accidentally by a rock dropped by fishermen laying fixed nets. DOGS ARE BANNED on the island. Manga-Island cat-shaped lodges built in 2000 (Shotaro Ishinomori connection). Fishermen read the cats' behaviour to predict weather and catch.
  (source: en.wikipedia Tashirojima https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashirojima ; Japanese Government Online 'Cat Island' https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/202111/202111_05_en.html)
- [likely] AOSHIMA (青島, Ehime) is DYING, not thriving. Human population ~900 postwar, ~700 in the 1960s, 13 in 2017, and as of December 2024 only FOUR residents. Cats ~130 (2018) down to ~80. A sterilisation programme was run to control the population. A cat-app that romanticises 'cat islands' without this context is repeating a stale 2015 listicle.
  (source: en.wikipedia Aoshima, Ehime and Cat islands in Japan https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_islands_in_Japan ; Tokyo Weekender https://www.tokyoweekender.com/japan-life/news-and-opinion/cat-island-wont-survive-much-longer/)
- [verified] 猫島 (nekojima) is the general term. Wikipedia's list names, all for rodent control: Aoshima (Ehime), Tashirojima (Miyagi), Ainoshima ×2 (Fukuoka, Shingū and Kitakyushu), Manabeshima (Okayama), Enoshima (Kanagawa), Sanagishima (Kagawa), Iwaishima (Yamaguchi), Okishima (Shiga — a freshwater-lake island), Muzukijima (Ehime), Genkaishima (Fukuoka). Cats mattered because mice chewed fishing nets and ate grain and silkworms.
  (source: en.wikipedia Cat islands in Japan https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_islands_in_Japan)
- [unverified] NANZENJI (南禅寺) IS NOT A CAT TEMPLE — I could find no cat association. The Kyoto cat temple is にゃんにゃん寺 Nyan Nyan Ji (opened 2016 by painter Toru Kaya, with a resident cat 'abbot'), and there is 称念寺 (Shōnenji, 'neko-dera'). The famous cat-rescue temple is 御誕生寺 Gotanjōji (Sōtō Zen, Echizen, Fukui), which shelters dozens of abandoned cats. Do not put Nanzenji in copy.
  (source: Searched 南禅寺 猫 with no result; Nyan Nyan Ji via Tokyo Weekender https://www.tokyoweekender.com/art_and_culture/cat-temple-kyoto-nyan-nyan-ji/ ; Gotanjōji via Fukui City official tourism https://fuku-iro.jp/spot/detail_10201.html)
- [likely] 猫返し神社 (Neko-gaeshi Jinja, 'cat-return shrine') = 阿豆佐味天神社・立川水天宮 (Azusami-Tenjinsha, Tachikawa, Tokyo). Its 蚕影神社 (Kokage-jinja) sub-shrine was built to enshrine cats as protectors of silkworms from mice; instead of komainu it has a cat statue called ただいま猫 ('I'm home cat'). It got its nickname after jazz pianist 山下洋輔 (Yamashita Yōsuke) wrote in a magazine that his lost cat came home after he prayed there. People pray here to get a missing cat back.
  (source: トラベルjp https://www.travel.co.jp/guide/article/5438/ ; https://cat-spot.com/azusamiten/)
- [verified] NEKOMATA (猫又): a cat yōkai with a FORKED/split tail. Two types — mountain-dwelling beasts and household cats grown old. Earliest references: 明月記 (Meigetsuki, diary of Fujiwara no Teika), entry for 2 August 天福元年 = 1233, on a 'nekomata' harming people in Nara; and 徒然草 (Tsurezuregusa, Yoshida Kenkō, c.1330): 'in the deep mountains there is a thing called nekomata that devours people.' Mid-Edo, 伊勢貞丈 (Ise Sadatake) wrote that cats several years old split their tails and become nekomata; the common folk figure is 10+ years.
  (source: en.wikipedia Nekomata and Bakeneko ; ja.wikipedia 猫又 https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/猫又 ; https://www.konekono-heya.com/myth/nekomata.html)
- [verified] BAKENEKO (化け猫) has ONE tail; nekomata has two. Attributed powers: shapeshifting into humans, speaking, cursing, possessing people, animating the dead, and famously DANCING WITH A NAPKIN ON ITS HEAD. Transformation ages vary by region: 12 years (Ibaraki, Nagano), 13 years (Kunigami, Okinawa), 7+ years (Yamagata district, Hiroshima). The lamp-oil-licking omen is recorded in the Edo encyclopaedia 和漢三才図会 (Wakan Sansai Zue) — a real behaviour, since cheap lamp oil was sardine oil.
  (source: en.wikipedia Bakeneko https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakeneko)
- [verified] WHY BOBTAILS: in the Edo period there was a folk belief that cats with long snake-like tails could bewitch people, and tails were cut; Wikipedia states this is the speculated reason so many Japanese cats now have short tails. 踊場 (Odoriba, 'dancing place') in Totsuka, Yokohama is named for a dancing-cat legend, and a memorial tower was erected there in 元文2年 = 1737 to pacify the cat's spirit.
  (source: en.wikipedia Bakeneko https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakeneko)
- [verified] NABESHIMA BAKENEKO DISTURBANCE (鍋島の化け猫騒動): retainer Ryūzōji Matashichirō was executed by daimyo Nabeshima Mitsushige; his mother killed herself; her cat licked her blood, became a bakeneko, entered the castle and tormented Mitsushige nightly until retainer Komori Hanzaemon killed it. Dramatised as 花嵯峨野猫魔碑史 during the Kaei era (1848–54) at the Nakamura-za; the Saga domain's complaint only spread it further.
  (source: en.wikipedia Bakeneko https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakeneko)
- [likely] KASHA (火車, 'burning chariot', also 化車): a cat yōkai that steals corpses, usually appearing with thunder and dark clouds at funerals to snatch the body from the coffin, either to eat or to animate. Counter-measures recorded: making noise with bells, gongs and drums during the wake, and laying a razor or iron scissors on the coffin lid (cat-spirits fear cold iron).
  (source: en.wikipedia Kasha (folklore) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasha_(folklore) ; yokai.com https://yokai.com/kasha/)
- [likely] NAGASAKI BOBTAILS: nearly 80% of Nagasaki's cats are bobtails — roughly twice the rate anywhere else in Japan. Local names: おまがり猫 omagari-neko ('bent-tail cats') and かぎ猫 kagi-neko ('hook cats'), tail forms including hooked tip, curved, and bun-shaped. There is an 尾曲がり猫神社 Omagari Neko Shrine and a 長崎猫本会/Nagasaki Cat Society. Origin theory: descent from Dutch East Indies ships during Nagasaki's era as Japan's only foreign port (17th–19th c.), then a genetic mutation fixed in relative isolation. Quoted: Kazuya Hideshima (shrine worker, Nagasaki Cat Society) and Soshin Yamamoto (veterinarian/cat specialist). I could NOT find the underlying survey or its date for the 80% figure.
  (source: Associated Press, 27–28 May 2025, e.g. https://www.wcax.com/2025/05/28/japan-rare-bobtail-cats-are-considered-good-luck-nagasaki-is-filled-with-them/)
- [verified] MALE CALICO (三毛猫のオス, mike-neko no osu) ≈ 1 in 30,000 births — because the orange and black colour genes both sit on the X chromosome, a male calico requires an XXY karyotype (Klinefelter). In the Edo period male calicos were believed to be guardians of safe voyages: 'a ship carrying a male calico will not sink in a storm.' A male calico named タケシ (Takeshi) sailed with Japan's First Antarctic Research Expedition. The three colours are read as white = invites fortune, black = wards evil, orange = health — which is why the calico became the model for the maneki-neko.
  (source: ja.wikipedia 三毛猫 ; News Postseven https://www.news-postseven.com/archives/20180930_770158.html ; Nihon Pet https://www.nihonpet.co.jp/cat/mikeneko.html)
- [likely] 1602 CAT-LIBERATION DECREE: in mid-8th-month of 慶長7年 (1602) a notice board was raised at Ichijō-dōri in Kyoto reading 「一、洛中猫の綱を解き、放ち飼いにすべき事／一、同猫売買の禁止の事」 — untie all cats in the capital and let them roam free, and ban the buying and selling of cats. Issued by the Kyoto Shoshidai on Tokugawa Ieyasu's order; recorded in the courtier 西洞院時慶's diary 時慶記 (Tokikeiki). Cats had become so expensive that strays were being caught and sold to the rich, leaving the city to the rats.
  (source: WARGO https://cat-lover.wargo.jp/user_data/cat009.php ; News Postseven https://news-postseven.com/archives/20190911_1447212.html ; ja.wikipedia 日本猫)
- [verified] KUNIYOSHI'S CAT-TOKAIDO: 「其のまま地口 猫飼好五十三疋」 (Sono-mama Jiguchi: Myōkaikō Gojūsan-biki), 1848 (嘉永元年), triptych, published with Ibasen of Nihonbashi. - [verified] KUNIYOSHI'S CAT CALLIGRAPHY: 「猫の当て字」 (Neko no Ateji), c. 天保12–14年 = 1841–43, signed 一勇斎国芳戯画, publisher 伊場屋仙三郎 (Ibaya Sensaburō). - [verified] HIROSHIGE'S CAT: 「浅草田甫酉の町詣」 (Asakusa tanbo Torinomachi mōde, 'Asakusa Ricefields and Torinomachi Festival'), No. 101 of 名所江戸百景 (One Hundred Famous Views of Edo), 11th month of 1857. DESIGN IMPLICATIONS:
- COAT VOCABULARY IS THE SECOND COLLECTION AXIS, AND IT IS FREE. The vision model already returns `coat`. Constrain it to the JSPCA's 15 formal categories plus hachiware / kutsushita / kagi-shippo, and print BOTH the Japanese term and the English on the card in the DM Mono data row (e.g. `COAT — KIJITORA / キジトラ / brown mackerel tabby`). That gives ~18 fixed slots to complete with no extra API cost, no invented lore, and a genuinely educational payload. It also fixes a real product problem: 'tabby' is one western word covering kijitora and sabatora, which Japanese owners treat as two different cats.
- PORT GOTOKUJI'S RETURN LOOP AS THE RETENTION MECHANIC. The temple's actual loop is: take a cat home WITH A WISH → return it to the rack when the wish comes true. Add a `wish` field at the naming step (18 chars, same constraint as the name). Later, the user can mark it FULFILLED; the card gains a permanent gold seal and moves to a 'FULFILLED' shelf in the collection strip. This is pure localStorage, needs no server, no feed, no comments — and it is the only thing in the current design that gives a user a reason to reopen a one-shot photo scanner three weeks later. It is also doctrinally correct: Gotokuji itself says the cat brings the connection, not the fortune.
- MAKE THE RAISED PAW A USER CHOICE WITH REAL SEMANTICS, NOT DECORATION. Render a raised paw on the card and let the namer pick: RIGHT = 金運 kin'un (money), LEFT = 千客万来 senkyaku-banrai (people/customers). Second toggle: paw ABOVE the ear line = distant fortune, BELOW = near fortune. Four combinations, all documented Japanese convention, zero invented lore, and it turns the naming screen from one text field into a small ritual. Draw the paw palm-DOWN with fingers folded toward the viewer — the palm-up western wave is the single most common maneki-neko manufacturing error.
- MOVE FOIL OFF THE RARITY ROLL AND ONTO THE COAT. Right now foil intensity scales with the rarity tier, which is a western TCG convention. The Japanese colour system is more interesting because colour carries MEANING: gold coat → gold foil (金運, wealth); black coat → MATTE, an anti-foil that absorbs light (黒 = 魔除け, wards evil — black is lucky in Japan, so matte should read as premium, not as a downgrade); white → pearl (開運招福); orange/chatora → warm copper (無病息災, health); calico → all three at once, since mike's three colours are exactly white+black+orange and that is the documented reason the calico became the maneki-neko model. The finish is then earned by what the cat actually is, which the owner can verify, instead of by a hidden dice roll.
- ADD MIKE-O AS A VERIFIABLE ULTRA-RARE. Male calico ≈ 1 in 30,000 (XXY). If the model returns calico and the user marks the cat male, fire a distinct tier — 三毛雄 MIKE-O — with the Edo sailors' line on the card ('a ship carrying one will not sink in a storm'). This is the strongest card in the whole deck design-wise, because the rarity is real-world and checkable rather than app-invented, so the owner feels they discovered something true about their own cat rather than won a lottery.
- REWRITE THE `title` FIELD AS A JIGUCHI PUN ENGINE, NOT AN ADJECTIVE. Kuniyoshi's 猫飼好五十三疋 (1848) turned the 55 Tokaido stations into 55 cat sound-puns (日本橋 → 二本だし, 品川 → 白かを, 草津 → こたつ). That IS the trading-card epithet mechanic, invented 178 years ago. Two moves: (a) instruct the vision model to produce a pun/near-homophone epithet rather than a descriptive one; (b) ship the 55 Tokaido stations as a named collection track — each station slot unlocks when a scanned cat's pun fits it. The result is a collection target that is finite, culturally anchored, and impossible for a competitor to copy without doing the same reading.
- SHIP THE ZUKAN (図鑑) SHELL, NOT A CAROUSEL. The Japanese collection UI convention is a field-guide index with GREYED SILHOUETTES for uncollected entries; the horizontal strip of what you already own is a western app-store convention. Show the 18 coat slots and the 55 station slots as empty silhouettes from first launch. Empty slots are the engine — Neko Atsume hit 10M downloads by Dec 2015 against a 50k target largely because the rare-cat conditions were never documented and players had to hunt.
- PUT ONOMATOPOEIA IN THE STAT ROW. The six invented stats map cleanly onto real Japanese mimetics: FLOOF → もふもふ mofu-mofu, LOAF → ゴロゴロ goro-goro (or ふみふみ fumi-fumi for kneading), VOID → the cat you cannot find, MENACE → スリスリ suri-suri (the rub that precedes the ambush), ZOOMIES → 運動会 undōkai / the 3am dash. Print the Japanese mimetic in small DM Mono under each stat bar. These are 擬態語 gitaigo — they encode state, not sound — which is exactly the deadpan-clinical-about-an-absurd-animal register the app already has, and it is a register no western cat app is using.
- TWO CALENDAR TENTPOLES, HARD-CODED. 2 February 22 (猫の日, established 1987, ni-nyan-nyan-nyan) is a genuine national commercial event — FamilyMart ran 21 cat SKUs across ~16,200 stores in 2025, 7-Eleven ran a prize draw, Cainz ran a month-long festival. Cards minted on Feb 22 get a permanent ニャンニャンニャン stamp. Second, smaller: 29 September (招き猫の日, 9=くる + 29=ふく = 来る福, 'fortune comes', set by the Nihon Shōbyō Club in 1995) — that day's cards get the 来る福 seal. Both are date-checks against the device clock: about eight lines of code for the only two days a year the app has a reason to push.
- AGE FIELD → NEKOMATA OVERLAY. Folk tradition says a cat past roughly 10 years (regionally 7, 12 or 13) splits its tail and becomes a 猫又. Add an optional age at naming; cats 10+ get a forked-tail motif and the NEKOMATA marker, played completely straight. This rewards exactly the cats whose owners are most emotionally invested and most likely to export the PNG — and it costs one number and one SVG path.
- GIVE THE PNG EXPORT A GOSHUIN GRAMMAR, NOT A HOLO-CARD GRAMMAR. The Japanese proof-of-visit artefact is the 御朱印: a dated, hand-inked entry with a red vermilion seal (朱印) stamped over it. Adding a dated red seal block to the 1080×1350 export reads as RECORD rather than MEME, which matches the '3am veterinary field unit' voice far better than a holographic tilt does, and it visually differentiates the export in a feed full of western holo cards. The tilt effect stays in-app; the exported still gets the seal.
- OFFER A KAGI-SHIPPO (HOOK TAIL) TRAIT. Ask the vision model for tail shape as a separate field. A bent or bobbed tail is folk-lucky (it hooks fortune in), and Nagasaki — where the AP reports nearly 80% of cats are bobtails, roughly double anywhere else — has its own 尾曲がり猫神社 and cat society. It is a single-bit trait a vision model can genuinely read from a photo, unlike most 'rarity' inputs, so it makes the rarity feel observed rather than rolled.
- DESIGN THE MONETISATION AROUND THE KOMPU GACHA BAN NOW, NOT LATER. Since 1 July 2012 it has been illegal in Japan (景品表示法, カード合わせ) to charge for randomness AND give a reward for completing a set. The zukan and the 55-station track are set-completion mechanics. Keep every completion reward free and every paid element deterministic, or the Japanese market is closed the moment the app monetises. If randomness ever becomes paid, JOGA guidance also expects per-item probabilities to be published and expected spend to a rare capped at 100× a pull or ¥50,000.
- NAME THE TIERS IN A MATERIAL REGISTER, NOT A FANTASY ONE. ALLEY / HOUSE / SHOW / FOIL / MYTHIC mixes three vocabularies (habitat, judging, print finish, RPG). Japanese collectible ladders are concrete and material — 上/特上/極, or metals. If the app is going to lean Japanese, a coherent alternative is a fortune ladder tied to what the cat brings rather than how rare it is: 野良 NORA (stray) → 家 IE (house) → 招福 SHŌFUKU (fortune-inviting) → 金 KIN (gold) → 又 MATA (nekomata). Whatever is chosen, the five names should come from ONE system.
- LET THE RARE CONDITIONS GO UNDOCUMENTED. Neko Atsume never told players which toys drew which rare cats, and the community built the wiki — that was the growth engine, not a UX failure. Ship male-calico, kagi-shippo, Feb 22, Sept 29 and the 10-year nekomata trigger with NO in-app explanation. The PNG export is already the sharing mechanism; a stamp nobody can explain is the strongest possible reason to post one.

TRAPS:
- Getting the paw backwards. RIGHT = money, LEFT = people/customers. Western merchandise reverses this constantly. Worse: western-made maneki-neko are usually moulded palm-UP, because a palm-up curl is the western 'come here'. The Japanese beckon is palm DOWN with the fingers folding toward the beckoner — that is literally what the cat's paw is doing. A palm-up cat is the instant tell that nobody checked.
- Treating black as the sinister tier. In Japan 黒 = 魔除け・厄除け (wards off evil, protects the household) and black cats are LUCKY. Shipping a black cat as the 'VOID / cursed / spooky' rarity imports a western superstition into a Japanese-flavoured product and inverts the meaning. Same trap in reverse for gold: 金 is not just 'shiny high tier', it specifically means 金運, money luck.
- Presenting the maneki-neko as ancient or sacred. The earliest documentation is 1852 (武江年表); mass production is Meiji-era plaster moulding; the familiar big-head cartoon shape is a 1950s Aichi redesign. It is a cheerful shopkeeper's charm, sold at Gotokuji from ¥500. A reverent 'ancient Japanese sacred cat spirit' framing is both wrong and heavier than Japanese people themselves treat it — and Gotokuji's own doctrine actively deflates it: the cat brings you the connection, not the fortune.
- Saying 'shrine' and 'temple' interchangeably. 豪徳寺 Gotokuji is a Sōtō Zen BUDDHIST TEMPLE; 今戸神社 Imado-jinja is a SHINTO SHRINE; 自性院 Jishoin is a Shingon Buzan temple. Mixing them in one sentence is the single fastest way to look like you read one listicle.
- Putting NANZENJI in the copy. I could find no cat association with 南禅寺 at all. The Kyoto cat temple people mean is にゃんにゃん寺 Nyan Nyan Ji (opened 2016), and the famous cat-rescue temple is 御誕生寺 Gotanjōji in Fukui. Shipping 'Nanzenji, the cat temple' would be an immediate credibility hit with any Japanese reader.
- Using bakeneko / nekomata / kasha as giggling mascots. These are corpse-and-vengeance figures. The kasha (火車) leaps onto coffins at funerals to steal the body; mourners banged gongs and laid iron scissors on the lid to stop it. The Nabeshima cat licked a suicide's blood before haunting the daimyo nightly. They work beautifully as a rare tier played dead straight — which the app's clinical voice is already built for — and terribly as a winking sticker.
- Naming Hello Kitty as a cat reference. Sanrio's own position is that she is a personification of a cat: a little girl, Kitty White, from outside London, who herself owns a pet cat. Invoking her in marketing invites both a trademark problem and a 'they didn't check' reaction from exactly the audience you want.
- Claiming cat cafes as a Japanese invention. The first was 猫花園 in Taipei, 1998. Japan's first was 猫の時間 in Osaka, 2004. Japan scaled the format; it did not originate it.
- Building the set-completion reward into a paid gacha. Complete-gacha has been illegal in Japan since 1 July 2012 under the 景品表示法 as prohibited 'card matching'. A collection app with paid randomness and a completion prize is the textbook violation.
- Machine-translating the epithets into Japanese. The whole Kuniyoshi tradition is 地口 jiguchi — sound puns. A pun does not survive translation. A Japanese build needs epithets written as Japanese puns from scratch, which means the prompt has to change per locale, not the output.
- Recycling the 2015 'cat island paradise' listicle. Aoshima had FOUR human residents as of December 2024 and its cat population has fallen from ~130 to ~80 under a sterilisation programme; Tashirojima is a designated 限界集落 with 83% elderly residents. These are depopulation stories with cats in them. Treating them as a whimsical travel backdrop reads as tone-deaf to anyone who has followed the coverage.
- Defaulting to breed as the identity axis. Anicom's 2025 insured-kitten data is 14.2% Scottish Fold, 11.2% mixed, 10.7% Munchkin — and the 日本猫 does not appear as a named breed at all; it is hidden inside 混血猫. Japanese cat owners identify their cat by COAT PATTERN (kijitora, sabatora, hachiware, mike, sabi), not by pedigree. A breed-first card is the wrong primary field for this audience.
- Cute-voicing the Japanese copy on the assumption that 'Japanese = kawaii'. The two most successful Japanese cat properties do the opposite: Neko Atsume has almost no voice at all and lets the cats' names and left-behind mementos carry it; Chi's Sweet Home ran in a seinen magazine and works because it observes real cat behaviour closely. The app's existing 'deadpan clinical about an obviously ridiculous animal, never mean about the cat' register is already closer to the Japanese norm than a kawaii rewrite would be. Do not soften it.
- Saying the art is hand-drawn or traditional. The maneki-neko itself has been mould-mass-produced since the Meiji era, and the app's cards are generated. Studio rule already covers this, and it applies doubly to anything wearing Japanese craft signifiers.

- [verified] WHITE CAT DEAFNESS — real numbers. Across the literature: white cats with two blue eyes 64.9–85% deaf; one blue eye 39.1–40%; no blue eyes 16.7–22%. - [verified] Mechanism: the dominant White (W) gene suppresses melanocyte migration. - [verified] VISION MODEL COLOUR PERCEPTION — the specific weakness that will hurt this app. ColorBench (Liang et al., University of Maryland, arXiv 2504.10514, 32 models, 11 tasks): the strongest proprietary models reached only ~54% overall vs human ~80%+. DESIGN IMPLICATIONS:
- TYPE THE CARD ON THREE INDEPENDENT AXES, not one string. Axis 1 GROUND (kiji / saba / cha / kuro / shiro / sabi / mike / buchi). Axis 2 GEOMETRY (mackerel / classic / spotted / ticked / solid). Axis 3 WHITE GRADE 0-5 (none / mitted / bicolour / harlequin / van / solid white). This mirrors the Japanese folk system on axis 1 and the western pedigree system on axis 2, which is exactly the reconciliation nobody has built. It also multiplies the card space from ~15 names to hundreds of legitimate combinations without inventing anything.
- SHIP THE JAPANESE WORD-ORDER RULE AS A FREE STAT. キジ白 means tabby-dominant, 白キジ means white-dominant; JSPCA prints both as distinct entries. Compute white coverage percentage from the image, flip the name order at 50%, and print the coverage number in the data line. One real linguistic rule, zero invented content, and it makes two photos of the same cat produce visibly different cards.
- CALIBRATE THE FIVE RARITY TIERS TO REAL JAPANESE POPULATION DATA AND PRINT THE NUMBER ON THE CARD. ALLEY = kijitora / kuro-shiro / cha-tora (ranks 1-3, Iris Ohyama 2019, n=1,702). HOUSE = kuro / cha-shiro / mike / shiro / kiji-shiro. SHOW = saba-tora / grey / sabi. FOIL = classic tabby (tab allele absent from surveyed Japanese populations), dilute calico, kagi-shippo, kinme-ginme odd-eye. MYTHIC = male mike, with "1 in 3,000 calicos" printed as fact. Rarity that is citable beats rarity that is a dice roll — and it is exactly the mechanic the botanical game already knows how to run.
- ADD A HARD GENETIC VALIDATOR BETWEEN THE MODEL AND THE CARD. Three rules that are non-negotiable and cheap: (a) a red/orange cat can never return "solid" — orange is epistatic to non-agouti, so force a tabby subtype; (b) mike and sabi force the sex field to ♀ with the 1-in-3,000 male override as a separate rare event; (c) a solid-white result must not silently claim an eye colour if the eyes are shut. This turns published genetics into deterministic post-processing and kills the three most embarrassing possible outputs.
- GATE EVERY FIELD ON MODEL CONFIDENCE AND RENDER LOW-CONFIDENCE FIELDS AS 判定不能 / UNRESOLVED RATHER THAN A GUESS. ColorBench measured GPT-4o at 46.2% robustness to colour transformation, so a warm indoor bulb will flip saba/kiji. Specifically gate: saba-vs-kiji, tortie-vs-calico (needs white in frame), smoke-vs-solid, ticked-vs-solid. An UNRESOLVED field on a card that reads as a 3am veterinary field unit is completely on-brand and buys the whole app its credibility.
- TURN THE MODEL'S WEAKNESS INTO THE RETENTION LOOP. When confidence is low, do not degrade — request a second frame with a specific instruction ("turn a light on", "get the paws in frame", "we need the belly"). Each re-shoot is another scan animation, another card. This is the addiction engine and it is honest.
- MAKE POSTURE A SECOND, INDEPENDENT COLLECTION AXIS. The coat is fixed per cat; the pose is not. Verified pose set to ship: 香箱座り kōbako-zuwari (loaf), スフィンクス座り, エジプト座り, 尻尾巻き座り, スコ座り, 横座り, おじさん座り, アンモニャイト, へそ天, ごめん寝, まぶしい寝, おててないない, ツチノコ, ミーアキャット, 猫鍋. One cat, fifteen cards, and the user has to catch the cat doing each one. That is the hook the director asked for, and every term on that list is attested.
- GIVE THE TAIL ITS OWN SLOT: 長尾 / かぎしっぽ / 短尾 / お団子しっぽ. It is genuinely regional (Nagasaki >80% kinked vs ~40% national), it has real genetics behind it (HES7 c.5T>C, dominant), and it has the single best lore line in the whole subject — Edo townspeople preferring bent tails because a long-tailed cat becomes a nekomata.
- GIVE THE EYES THEIR OWN SLOT: 銅色/カッパー, アンバー, ゴールド, ヘーゼル (榛色), グリーン, ブルー, and 金目銀目 kinme-ginme for odd eyes. Odd-eye is the single best FOIL trigger — visually unmistakable, genuinely rare, and culturally the most auspicious cat in Japan. Detect it only when both eyes are open and lit; otherwise return UNRESOLVED rather than guessing.
- BILINGUAL CARD TYPOGRAPHY, DECIDED NOW NOT LATER. Romaji as the card name (KIJI-TORA), kanji/kana as a type glyph (キジトラ), English in the data line (BROWN MACKEREL TABBY). Bricolage Grotesque has no Japanese glyphs — pick and self-host a real JP display face (a Noto Sans JP or M PLUS weight) before any kanji ships, or the canvas export will render tofu boxes at 1080x1350.
- GROUND THE FLAVOUR LINE IN ETYMOLOGY, NOT ADJECTIVES. Every one of these words is a picture: kiji is a female pheasant's plumage, saba is mackerel skin, sabi is rusted iron, mugiwara is straw, kōbako is a lacquered incense box, hachiware was originally a cracked skull that merchants rewrote as the kanji for widening prosperity. The deadpan clinical voice plus a real etymology is a better line than anything invented, and it is unfakeable by a competitor who did not do this research.
- BUILD 22 FEBRUARY (猫の日, from 2-2-2 = nyan-nyan-nyan, established 1987) AND 8 AUGUST INTO THE CALENDAR NOW. Annual event cards, a foil variant, a maneki-neko frame. The studio already runs seasonal systems in the botanical game — this is the same machinery with a fixed, culturally real date rather than an invented one.

TRAPS:
- Assuming サバトラ = "mackerel tabby". It does not. サバ is the fish's SKIN COLOUR (grey-silver); the English "mackerel" refers to the STRIPE GEOMETRY. All three Japanese tora coats are mackerel-patterned. A Japanese user will spot this mapping error immediately and it will read as machine-translated research.
- Naming a card HACHIWARE and drawing a blue-and-white cartoon cat face. ハチワレ is one of the three leads in ちいかわ, currently one of Japan's biggest character IPs. The word is generic public vocabulary and safe to use; the visual is not. Photograph-derived art only, no cartoon face.
- Writing 鉢割れ, or making a joke about a cracked skull. That reading was deliberately abandoned as inauspicious and rewritten as 八割れ so it would mean 末広がり, widening prosperity. Getting this backwards is a specific, avoidable insult.
- Describing the Japanese Bobtail tail as recessive. A large number of Japanese pet-media pages say 劣性遺伝子; CFA's own breed page and the HES7 paper (Xu et al., Sci Rep 2016) both say dominant with complete penetrance in heterozygotes. Copying the blogs demonstrates you never opened a primary source.
- Making "deaf white cat" a MENACE or VOID stat joke. White cats with two blue eyes run 64.9-85% congenital deafness. Deadpan clinical about a ridiculous animal is the brief; deadpan clinical about a disability is a different thing entirely. Same rule for the male calico: it is a chromosomal disorder (XXY, 16/25 in Centerwall & Benirschke 1975) with near-universal sterility, not a loot drop to be farmed.
- Repeating the "coat colour determines personality" claim. Japanese pet media runs this constantly (毛色と性格) and it is not established science. An app that ships a personality stat is fine — an app that asserts the coat CAUSES it is making a claim it cannot defend, and cat people will litigate it.
- Machine-translating the deadpan English copy into Japanese. Japanese cat vernacular is warm and onomatopoeic (ゴロゴロ, もふもふ, にゃん, 〜にゃ). A literal rendering of clinical English deadpan reads as a cold veterinary diagnosis sheet, not as a joke. If the app goes bilingual, the Japanese copy needs writing, not translating.
- Presenting dialect as national vocabulary. ヨモギネコ is Tōhoku (Aomori/Akita/Iwate), 熊猫 is Chūbu, 安猫 is Kagoshima, さんけ for 三毛 is regional. Also: 熊猫 means PANDA in Chinese — never let that string reach any CJK-shared surface.
- Getting the maneki-neko paw backwards. Right paw = 金運 money. Left paw = customers and people. It is the single most recognisable detail in the entire subject and it is a coin flip if you do not check.
- Letting the rarity system punish black cats. A vision model recovers almost nothing from a black cat photo, and black cats are already disadvantaged in adoption. If low-detail photos map to low rarity, the app has quietly built a system that rates black cats as boring. Give kuro its own dedicated high-tier treatment (烏猫 karasu-neko is a real and beautiful term for exactly this cat).
- Inventing Japanese. Two specific landmines already in the brief: "kin-gin-yougan" is not a term I could verify (the real everyday term is 金目銀目 kinme-ginme; 金銀妖瞳 kingin-yōdō exists but is literary/subculture), and "nekozuwari" is not the short form of 香箱座り (that is 箱座り hako-zuwari). "Purrito" has no attested Japanese equivalent at all — do not coin one.
- Claiming any classification accuracy figure. There is no published benchmark for cat coat PATTERN classification by any model. The breed numbers that exist (92% GCViT on 12 breeds) are a closed-set problem and do not transfer. If the app makes an accuracy claim, it has to have measured it in-house first.

### RESEARCH: Cat personality and cognition science (Japan-weighted) as an evidence base for a photo-to-trading-card cat app

FINDINGS:
- [verified] The 'Feline Five' (Litchfield CA, Quinton G, Tindle H, Chiera B, Kikillus KH, Roetman P, 2017, PLoS ONE 12(8):e0183455) rated 2,802 pet cats — 1,687 South Australia, 1,115 New Zealand — via owner survey on 52 trait adjectives, 7-point Likert ('not at all' to 'very much so'), 4 items dropped in data reduction leaving 48. The five factors, exactly as named: Neuroticism, Extraversion, Dominance, Impulsiveness, Agreeableness.
  (source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0183455)
- [verified] Feline Five highest-loading items per factor (not the full 48-item list): Neuroticism = insecure, anxious, fearful of people, suspicious, shy. Extraversion = active, vigilant, curious, inquisitive, inventive, smart, decisive, persevering. Dominance = bullying, dominant, aggressive to other cats. Impulsiveness = impulsive, erratic, reckless. Agreeableness = affectionate, friendly to people, gentle.
  (source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0183455)
- [verified] Feline Five explains only 41.53% cumulative variance (Neuroticism 18.24%, Extraversion 12.03%, Dominance 6.97%, Impulsiveness 6.51%, Agreeableness 3.67%). Cronbach's alpha: Neuroticism .90, Extraversion .80, Dominance .80, Agreeableness .78, Impulsiveness .72. So even the best-validated cat personality model leaves ~58% of variance unexplained, and Agreeableness — the trait owners care most about — is the weakest factor.
  (source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0183455)
- [verified] Litchfield et al. state their own limitations: respondents were overwhelmingly female; they never asked raters how long they had known the cat, which they call a threat to validity. The data came out of the Cat Tracker citizen-science project (Dr Philip Roetman, University of South Australia), which also GPS-tracked 441 cats.
  (source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0183455 ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6262432/)
- [verified] Fe-BARQ (Feline Behavioral Assessment & Research Questionnaire; Duffy DL, de Moura RTD, Serpell JA, 2017, Behavioural Processes) is a 100-item owner-report instrument, 10–15 min to complete, measuring 23 behavioural factors plus 14 miscellaneous behaviours. The 23 factors: General activity/Playfulness, Sociability with people, Directed vocalization, Purring, Attention-seeking, Sociability with cats, Stranger-directed aggression, Owner-directed aggression/Touch sensitivity, Resistance to restraint, Familiar cat aggression, Dog aggression, Fear of unfamiliar dogs/cats, Fear of novelty, Separation-related behavior, Trainability, Predatory behavior, Prey interest, Sleeping location preferences, Excessive/compulsive self-grooming, Other compulsive behaviors, Inappropriate elimination, Elimination preferences, Crepuscular activity.
  (source: https://vetapps.vet.upenn.edu/febarq/about.cfm)
- [likely] Fe-BARQ's 23 factors were extracted by exploratory factor analysis of 2,608 responses to 149 behavioural items; 17 of the 23 showed adequate-to-high internal reliability (i.e. 6 did not).
  (source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28232232/)
- [verified] JAPAN — Saito A, Shinozuka K, Ito Y, Hasegawa T (2019), 'Domestic cats (Felis catus) discriminate their names from other words', Scientific Reports 9:5394. Four experiments, habituation-dishabituation: Exp1 n=16 household; Exp2 n=34 (24 household, 10 café); Exp3 n=29 (20 household, 9 café); Exp4 n=33 (30 household + 3 lab). Five coded responses (ear moving, head moving, vocalizing, tail moving, displacement) scored 0–3 by blind raters. Household cats significantly dishabituated to their own name vs general nouns, whether spoken by owner or by a stranger.
  (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6449508/)
- [verified] JAPAN — the crucial null in Saito 2019 that popular coverage omits: CAT CAFÉ cats could NOT discriminate their own name from other cohabiting cats' names (p = 0.732; only 3 of 9 café cats increased responding). Household cats could (p = 0.005, n=6 habituated). Saito et al. also note cats responded with ORIENTING behaviour (ear/head) not COMMUNICATIVE behaviour (vocalizing/tail), and they explicitly do not claim cats attach referential meaning to a name — the name is likely a learned discriminative stimulus associated with reward.
  (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6449508/)
- [verified] JAPAN — Takagi S, Chijiiwa H, Arahori M, Saito A, Fujita K, Kuroshima H (2021), 'Socio-spatial cognition in cats: Mentally mapping owner's location from voice', PLoS ONE 16(11):e0257611. Exp1 n=50 (27 café, 23 household), Exp2 n=45 (all café), Exp3 n=47 (24 café, 23 household). Two speakers ≥4 m apart, one outside the room; habituation of 5 calls from Speaker 1 then a test call from Speaker 1 or 2, 15 s observation. Eight blind raters scored surprise 0–4.
  (source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0257611)
- [verified] JAPAN — Takagi 2021 result detail: only the OWNER'S VOICE produced the teleportation surprise. Exp1 significant location × sound interaction p = .017; SamesoundDifflocation (owner's voice suddenly relocated) elicited most surprise vs DiffsoundDifflocation p = .021 and vs SamesoundSamelocation p < .001. Exp2 (cat meows) reversed the prediction — DiffsoundDifflocation was most surprising. Exp3 (electronic sounds) showed NO location × sound interaction (p = .830). The effect is specific to the owner's voice, not to sound generally.
  (source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0257611)
- [verified] JAPAN — Takagi S, Saito A, Arahori M, Chijiiwa H, Koyasu H, Nagasawa M, Kikusui T, Fujita K, Kuroshima H (2022), 'Cats learn the names of their friend cats in their daily lives', Scientific Reports 12:6155. Exp1 n=48 (19 household, 29 café); Exp2 n=26 household cats. Expectancy-violation: model cat's name played 4× then a face shown for 7 s, congruent vs incongruent. Affiliations span Kyoto University (Psychology + Wildlife Research Center), Azabu University, Sophia University, Anicom Speciality Medical Institute.
  (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9007945/)
- [verified] JAPAN — Takagi 2022 result detail, again with a null the headlines dropped: HOUSEHOLD cats looked longer in the incongruent (wrong face for the name) condition — violation index t(13)=2.522, p=0.025; living-environment × congruency interaction p=0.009. - [likely] JAPAN — Takagi S, Arahori M, Chijiiwa H, Tsuzuki M, Hataji Y, Fujita K (2016), 'There's no ball without noise: cats' prediction of an object from noise', Animal Cognition 19(5):1043-1047, Kyoto University. DESIGN IMPLICATIONS:
- Rename the six stats to map onto something defensible, and split them into two visually distinct bands on the card. Band A = MORPHOLOGY (honestly readable from a photo): CHONK (body condition), FLOOF (coat length/density), and a breed/coat readout. Band B = DISPOSITION (a prior, not a measurement): map VOID → Neuroticism, MENACE → Dominance, ZOOMIES → Impulsiveness/Extraversion, LOAF → inverse Extraversion, borrowing the Litchfield Feline Five factor names. Print the band label on the card. The card then says what it knows and what it guesses, in its own layout, without a single apologetic paragraph.
- Make the photo produce a CONFIDENCE number and let the card visibly earn its stats. Litchfield's five factors explain only 41.53% of variance from a 52-item owner questionnaire; a single JPEG has strictly less information than that. Ship the first card at low confidence with wide stat bars (ranges, not points), and let the user tighten them by answering a short owner quiz. This is also the retention engine: the card is incomplete on mint, which is exactly the hook the studio already runs in Lucid Winds.
- Build the owner quiz from real instruments so the tightened stats are actually valid. Ten to fourteen items drawn from the Fe-BARQ factor set (General activity/Playfulness, Sociability with people, Attention-seeking, Directed vocalization, Purring, Fear of novelty, Stranger-directed aggression, Trainability, Predatory behavior, Crepuscular activity) or the Helsinki CPBQ seven traits (Fearfulness, Activity/Playfulness, Aggression toward humans, Sociability toward humans, Sociability toward cats, Excessive grooming, Litterbox issues). Fe-BARQ is 100 items at 10-15 minutes; a 12-item subset at 60 seconds is the mobile-shaped version and it is defensible because the item wording is borrowed, not invented.
- Add a second card face: ATTACHMENT STYLE, as a four-way typology using Vitale et al.'s exact ethogram — SECURE, INSECURE-AMBIVALENT, INSECURE-AVOIDANT, INSECURE-DISORGANIZED. Derive it from a 3-question in-app version of the Secure Base Test framing (what does your cat do when you leave, when you come back, when you pick them up). Base rate is 64.3% secure so it will be a warm result for most users, and the six-week training study showed the classification does not change — so it is a stable, permanent card attribute, unlike a rarity roll. This is the single highest-value science-to-mechanic conversion available.
- Do the pain check, and make it the reason a vet would recommend the app. The Feline Grimace Scale is five action units — ear position, orbital tightening, muzzle tension, whiskers change, head position — scored 0-2, validated on 110 still images with ICC 0.89. If the vision model reads flattened ears plus orbital tightening plus muzzle tension, do NOT print MENACE 91. Suppress the card, print a quiet clinical line in the app's own deadpan register, and offer a re-shoot. CatsMe! already has 380k users on exactly this and reports ~38.7% of submitted cats flagged for pain, so the trigger will fire often enough to matter.
- Use BREED as the legitimate photo-to-personality bridge and say so on the card back. Salonen et al. (n=5,726, 40 breeds) found significant breed differences in all ten behaviour traits with heritability 0.40-0.53. So: vision model reads breed/type → breed supplies a weak disposition prior → the card back shows the prior as a small line ('Breed baseline: high activity') separate from the cat's own stats. That is a real inference chain a curious user can follow, and it makes the card's numbers feel derived rather than rolled.
- Handle coat colour by inverting it into a joke the app is in on. Stelow 2016 found a small owner-reported aggression association for tortie/calico/black-and-white/grey-and-white; Delgado 2012 found the much larger effect is that HUMANS hold colour stereotypes cats do not have. So render a 'FIELD BIAS' stamp on the card — the app names the stereotype the user is about to apply ('tortitude: reported, not demonstrated') instead of encoding it as a stat. The deadpan clinical voice already in the spec is the perfect vehicle for this and it turns a liability into a signature.
- Ship SLOW BLINK as an interactive, not a stat. Humphrey et al. 2020 showed cats produce more eye-narrowing after an owner slow-blinks, and that slow-blinking makes even an unfamiliar human more approachable. Give the app a 'SLOW BLINK' button that runs a 10-second guided prompt with a timing bar and then invites a second photo. It is the only mechanic in the whole product where the app makes something real happen between the user and their actual cat, and the second photo is a free extra capture.
- Build the collection around COHABITANT PAIRS, not just individuals, and gate it on friends-only. Takagi 2022 showed household cats — but not café cats — link a companion cat's name to its face, and that the effect strengthens with time living together and with family size. Behnke 2021 found 72% of cats were Avoidant toward their cohabitant cat while only 17% were Secure. So: a two-cat household unlocks a BOND card with its own tier, and the bond is more often frosty than warm, which is funnier and truer than a friendship meter. This works entirely inside the existing PNG-export-only social model.
- Use NAME RECOGNITION as the naming step's payoff. Saito 2019 established name discrimination in household cats, and specifically that it survives a stranger's voice but collapses in cat cafés. When the user types the 18-character name, the app can note that this string is now (per Saito 2019, n=16-34 per experiment) a discriminable auditory stimulus for their cat and that cats reply with ear and head orientation, not with a vocalisation — so 'ignoring you' is the documented response, not a failure. That is a joke, a citation and a name-entry screen in one.
- Store a stat DIFF over time, because the science says the trait tightens rather than jumps. The Developmental Psychobiology longitudinal work found individual differences become more repeatable between 6 and 12 months. Photograph the same cat again and show the delta as 'consolidation', not as 'your cat changed'. This gives a legitimate reason to re-photograph a cat the user already carded, which the current one-shot flow does not have.
- Localise for Japan at the mechanic level, not the sticker level. Concretely: (a) offer Japanese default names from the real Anicom frequency list — ムギ, ベル, ラテ, 琥珀, モモ, ソラ — rather than translated English names; (b) present the four attachment styles as a shindan (診断) result screen, the format a Japanese audience already reads fluently from ketsueki-gata and MBTI; (c) size the export PNG for LINE sharing as well as 1080x1350, since LINE is the sharing rail in Japan; (d) keep the six-stat card, since Japan's own cat AI apps (CatsMe!, Anicom, RABO) all ship clinical single-number outputs and none ship a collectible card — the gap is real.
- Add ONE mythology tier that is not cute. Japanese cat folklore carries nekomata (猫又, the split-tailed cat that has lived too long) and bakeneko (化け猫) alongside maneki-neko. A top rarity tier that reads as an old, slightly wrong cat rather than a sparkly one fits the '3am veterinary field unit' voice far better than FOIL/MYTHIC does, and gives senior cats — currently doomed to low CHONK and low ZOOMIES — a reason to be rare. Age should be a rarity ladder, not a penalty.
- Keep the server proxy work on the critical path and put a rate-limited, cached vision call behind it, because the card must be deterministic per photo. Lucid Winds already treats determinism-from-hash as a design law; do the same here — hash the normalised image bytes plus the model output and mint from the hash, so a re-upload of the same photo returns the same card. Without this, users will re-roll the same cat until they get MYTHIC and the whole rarity economy is worthless within a day.

TRAPS:
- Claiming or implying the app MEASURES personality from a photo. No validated instrument in the field takes an image as input — Feline Five, Fe-BARQ and CPBQ all need an owner who knows the cat; the Feline Temperament Profile needs a live 10-phase interaction; the Secure Base Test needs a 6-minute separation-and-reunion protocol. Todorov & Porter 2014 showed different photos of the SAME human produce different trait impressions. Sell it as a dossier the owner co-signs, never as a reading.
- Printing a personality stat on a cat that is actually in pain. The Feline Grimace Scale's five action units (flattened/rotated ears, orbital tightening, muzzle tension, whisker change, head position) overlap almost exactly with what a naive model would score as MENACE or VOID. CatsMe! flags ~38.7% of submitted cats. Shipping without a pain-suppression gate means the product will, at scale, turn a hurt cat into a funny high-stat card.
- Baking coat colour into stats. Delgado 2012 is unambiguous that the colour-personality link is mostly a HUMAN stereotype, and it demonstrably harms black cats' adoption prospects. Stelow 2016's tortie/calico aggression association is small, owner-reported, and plausibly contaminated by the very expectation being measured. Encoding 'tortitude' as +MENACE hard-codes a bias with real-world consequences for shelter animals.
- Repeating the distorted pop-science versions of the Japanese studies. The four biggest distortions: (1) 'cats know their names' — café cats could NOT distinguish their name from other cats' names (p=0.732), and Saito et al. explicitly do not claim referential understanding; (2) 'cats know where you are' — the effect held for the OWNER'S VOICE only, and vanished with cat meows and with electronic sounds; (3) 'cats understand physics' — Takagi 2016 showed longer looking at incongruent rattle-vs-object outcomes, nothing more; (4) 'cats beat babies at word learning' — Snopes has fact-checked that framing of Takagi 2024. Citing the press release instead of the paper will be caught by exactly the audience most likely to evangelise the app.
- Treating the Kikusui/Nagasawa oxytocin work as cat science. The Science 2015 oxytocin-gaze positive loop is a DOG finding, and wolves did not show it. The cat oxytocin evidence is thin and inconsistent: Hattori et al. 2024 (n=30) found increased gaze in MALES only, and Koyasu et al. 2022 is a self-declared pilot with 15 cats and 4 males. Marketing 'the love hormone loop' for cats would be borrowing a dog result and would be visibly wrong to anyone in the field.
- Assuming a Japanese audience wants kawaii. Japan's own shipped cat AI is clinical — CatsMe! returns a pain grade, Anicom patents emotion-from-video, RABO's Catlog is a health telemetry collar. The '3am veterinary field unit' register is closer to Japanese market convention than a pastel cat game would be. Conversely, do not weeb it up: gratuitous kanji, mismatched kanji readings on a name (the same sound can carry entirely different meanings depending on characters), random 'nya', or maneki-neko slapped on as decoration will read as a foreigner's costume.
- Anthropomorphising the flavour text into invalid emotions. Bouma et al. 2023 (n>1,800) found that owners who anthropomorphise are LESS accurate at reading cats from photos, and that owners who frame the cat as a child attribute the most ecologically invalid emotions — jealousy, compassion, revenge. A card that says the cat feels jealous or is plotting revenge is not just wrong, it actively trains the user toward the worst-performing interpretive style. Chijiiwa 2021 is the corrective: cats showed no avoidance of a person who refused to help their owner. Cats do not keep score.
- Claiming a 'cat translator' or emotion decoder as a feature extension. MeowTalk's ~90% lab figure drops to roughly 70% in the creator's own real-world estimate, and cats have no shared vocal lexicon — meows are individually negotiated with one specific human. Any universal decoding claim is unfoundable and would attach the app to a category that specialists already treat as a punchline.
- Shipping a public feed or leaderboard. Beyond the studio's existing position, the science makes ranking actively hostile: high VOID/Neuroticism, low Agreeableness and Insecure-Avoidant attachment are the results a leaderboard would render as losing. Finka 2019 already links owner neuroticism to worse cat outcomes; a public ranking would land hardest on exactly the owners least able to absorb it. PNG export plus friends-only is the right ceiling and should be stated as a design position, not a backlog item.
- Letting the model score kittens, seniors and cats in carriers on the same scale. Attachment classification differs between kittens and adults in base rate; personality repeatability is still consolidating at 6-12 months; and a cat photographed at the vet is in a state, not a trait. Detect context (carrier, exam table, cone, post-anaesthetic) and refuse the card rather than mint a MENACE 98 from a terrified animal.
- Overstating the replication strength of the attachment numbers. The famous 64.3% secure figure comes from one lab (Vitale/Udell, Oregon State) on 70 classifiable kittens plus 38 adults; Behnke's 2021 thesis from the same lab got only 47% secure across 36 cats, with 53% independent inter-rater agreement on exact style. Quote the 64.3% if it is useful, but do not present it as a settled constant, and do not let the app's base rates be tuned to it as if it were.

- [verified] The core loop has FOUR player actions and nothing else: (1) buy food and goodies with fish, (2) place them in the yard, (3) close the app, (4) reopen later, photograph whoever showed up, and collect the fish they left. Hit-Point's stated design goal was 「まったり」/ "loosely enjoyable", with 'healing' (癒し) as the theme — explicitly for people who cannot own a cat.
  (source: https://www.meta.com/blog/neko-atsume-interview/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Atsume)
- [verified] Academically, Neko Atsume is the canonical "progress while gone" idle game. Cutting, Gundry & Cairns, 'Busy doing nothing? They concluded idle-game engagement does not fit existing engagement models and is better described as a HABIT that players acquire and maintain.

DESIGN IMPLICATIONS:
- MAKE THE APP PLAYABLE WHILE CLOSED. Right now the entire loop is synchronous: open app, shoot cat, get card, done — there is no reason to reopen. Steal Neko Atsume's actual structure: after a scan, the cat is 'settling in' and something resolves on a real-world clock you cannot skip. Concretely: a scanned cat generates ONE overnight field observation per real day (a new stat reading, a behavioural note, a second candid frame) that is only viewable on a later open. The reason to reopen must be authored before the reason to open is polished.
- BUILD THE FIELD LOG (Catbook) AS A SEPARATE, ALWAYS-GROWING SURFACE FROM THE CARD. The card is the shareable artifact; the log is the retention object. Per cat: date first scanned, scan count, every stat reading over time with drift, every title it has ever held, every observation line. Neko Atsume's Catbook tracked visits and goodies-used and that dossier is what people were completing, not the yard. A card you generate once is a screenshot. A dossier that thickens is a habit.
- SHIP MEMENTOS, AND MAKE THEM DETERMINISTIC — this is where you beat Neko Atsume outright. Neko Atsume gated its emotional payoff on a hidden random roll that could take 100+ visits; the documented completionist spent 3 months on ONE cat and got 'a strange item'. Do the opposite: at scan 3, 7, 15, 30 of the SAME cat, that cat awards a named memento — a foil sticker, a paw-print stamp on the card border, a second flavour line, a 'seen in winter coat' variant frame. Show the counter. Never roll for the payoff of loyalty.
- SEPARATE THE TWO RARITIES AND NEVER LET MONEY TOUCH EITHER. Neko Atsume's rare cats were gated on item KNOWLEDGE (put out the heating stove and the right food), not on a random paid draw — that is why guide sites and subreddits existed. Split yours the same way: (a) TRAIT rarity, honestly derived from the photo, non-negotiable and never purchasable; (b) CONDITION rarity, earned by the player doing something — scanning at night, in snow, mid-yawn, mid-zoomie, a second cat in frame, the same cat in a different season. Condition rarity is a knowledge game with a public answer key. That is what makes a wiki, and a wiki is what makes a community.
- CURRENCY MUST HAVE A TERMINAL SINK OR DO NOT SHIP CURRENCY. The single clearest cause of Neko Atsume's decay, from Japanese players' own words, was 'niboshi atsume' — everything bought, cats all collected, currency accumulating into a void. If you add a currency, either (1) make it consumable-only with a permanent sink (frame styles, film stocks, lens filters that get used up), or (2) skip it entirely and let the collection itself be the economy. v1.0 has no currency. That is currently a feature. Defend it.
- PHOTO EXPORT IS THE DISTRIBUTION CHANNEL — TREAT IT AS THE PRIMARY ENGINEERING SURFACE, NOT AN EXPORT BUTTON. Neko Atsume grew from 4M to 10M downloads in seven months on screenshots alone, and the academic engagement model isolates 'social media sociability' as its own dimension of play. So: 1080x1350 is right, but also ship a 1080x1920 story crop and a 1200x630 link crop; put a tiny non-ugly wordmark and a short claim code on the card; make export one tap from the reveal, not from a menu; and make the exported PNG readable at thumbnail size (title and rarity legible at 200px wide). The card must survive being reposted with the app name cropped off.
- GIVE THE PLAYER SOMETHING TO TOUCH. The number-one repeated request in Neko Atsume 2's App Store reviews is 'let me pet the cats', and Hit-Point built an entire VR product around exactly that. You have no 3D cat — but you have their real cat's photograph. Make the card physical: press-and-hold on the card and it purrs (haptics + a low loop), the holo tilt already responds to gyro, and a long press could reveal the 'belly' side of the card. One tactile verb, on the object they already own emotionally.
- USE THE FELINE FIVE AS THE HONEST SPINE UNDER YOUR JOKE STATS. CHONK/LOAF/VOID/MENACE/ZOOMIES/FLOOF is a good voice but it is six arbitrary axes. Map them privately onto Litchfield's five validated factors (Neuroticism, Extraversion, Dominance, Impulsiveness, Agreeableness) so the readings are internally consistent, then present the joke names on the card. Payoff: you can ship a genuinely useful 'what your cat's profile suggests' panel with a real citation, which is the difference between a meme generator and a thing people keep on their phone.
- BUILD ANTI-DETERIORATION, NOT DETERIORATION. Tamagotchi's hook was irreversible death and it worked once, in 1996, then collapsed. Neko Atsume 2 added item durability in 2024 and enthusiasts called it a straight downgrade to a healing game. Rule for this app: nothing the player owns may ever degrade, expire, break, or require maintenance. Cards do not rot. Streaks do not punish. The only direction is accumulation. This is non-negotiable in a category whose entire promise is 'healing'.
- ADOPT THE CAT CAFE SCRIPT FOR THE SCAN SEQUENCE. Pay for time → wash hands → surrender control → wait → be chosen. Your scan beam already does the ritual; what is missing is 'be chosen'. Make the reveal read as the CAT deciding what to disclose, not the app grading a photo — the field unit proposes, the cat confirms. Concretely: the reveal should occasionally withhold one stat ('SUBJECT DECLINED') and release it on the next scan. Withholding is more feline than delivering, and it manufactures a second visit.
- STEAL DOKO DEMO ISSYO'S WORD MECHANIC, NOT ITS CHARACTER. The player already types a name (18 chars). Let them also file 2-3 short field notes on their own cat ('hates the vacuum', 'lives in the sink'). Feed those notes back into later generated flavour lines and observations, deadpan-clinical, slightly wrong. Toro's whole appeal was hearing your own words recombined by something that did not fully understand them. This costs one text field and a prompt change and it is the single highest-affection-per-byte mechanic in this entire report.
- REFUSE PAID RANDOMNESS OUTRIGHT AND SAY SO ON THE STORE PAGE. Zendle & Cairns (N=7,422) found loot-box spend associated with problem-gambling severity more strongly than other IAP spend. Japan banned the set-completion variant in 2012. Your product photographs a real animal a person loves — a paid random draw sitting next to that is both an ethics failure and a brand failure. Ship a 'no loot boxes, no random purchases, no ads' line in the listing; Neko Atsume 2's 4.7-star reviews show players reward exactly this and say so unprompted.
- IF YOU EVER MONETISE, MONETISE THE ARTIFACT, NOT THE ODDS. Legitimate SKUs: extra card frames and film stocks, a physical printed card, a PDF binder sheet, unlimited scan history, higher-res export. All deterministic, all 'I paid and I received the named thing'. If you ever DO ship any random element, apply tenjou (天井): publish the rate, guarantee the outcome by the Nth attempt, show the counter on screen. That is the one gacha mechanic that is unambiguously player-protective and it is free to implement.
- GIVE THE ENDGAME A LAST PAGE THAT IS NOT A CLIFF. The documented Neko Atsume completionist's motivation 'completely dissipated' the moment the book was full. Design the terminal state deliberately: a printable/exportable full-collection binder page, a 'complete' seal with the date, and one thing that only exists after completion (an annual re-scan that shows the same cat aging across years). Aging is the one axis a real-cat app has that no virtual-pet game has — the cat in the photo is genuinely getting older, and a card from 2026 next to a card from 2029 is a keepsake no gacha can produce.
- SEED A KNOWABLE SECRET SET. Neko Atsume's rare cats existed as a discoverable, documentable puzzle ('heating stove + any food except Thrifty Bitz'), which is why guides, subreddits and wikis formed and why the pun names — Hermeowne, Joe DiMeowgio, Chairman Meow — became memes. Ship 15-25 named CONDITIONS with real, discoverable triggers and let the community write the guide. Do not document them in-app. The externally-written guide is free retention infrastructure.
- SIZE THE AMBITION HONESTLY AGAINST THE TEAM. Neko Atsume was two people and reached 30M downloads; Battle Cats is one game from a Kyoto studio that has held >1M Japanese MAU every month since June 2021 on $700M lifetime revenue. Both won on ONE mechanic executed for a decade, not on feature count. Neko Gacha shipped 150+ cats, 6-star rarities, gacha and party RPG and is culturally invisible. For a single-file vanilla JS app the correct move is one verb (scan), one artifact (card), one clock (daily), one book (log) — and then years of content on top of that unchanged spine.

TRAPS:
- Assuming 'Japanese cat personality research' exists as the director imagines it. The five-factor cat personality work (Feline Five) is AUSTRALIAN/NEW ZEALAND (Litchfield et al. 2017, N=2,802). Japan's contribution is cognition — name recognition, spatial mapping from voice, incidental memory — from Kyoto/Sophia/Azabu. Citing 'Japanese research' next to a personality typology is a factual error a Japanese player or journalist will catch instantly, and it would be the one thing they quote.
- Shipping a paid random draw because 'gacha is Japanese and Japan loves it'. Japan criminalised the set-completion variant in 2012 under consumer-protection law, and the rate transparency people credit Japan with is voluntary — I fetched PONOS's own English gacha page for The Battle Cats and it publishes no percentages at all. Copying gacha 'because Japan' means copying the least defensible half of it.
- Adding a set-completion prize behind paid randomness. 'Collect all five VOID-type cats to unlock the Mythic frame' is textbook kompu gacha shape. Free, it is fine. The instant any of those five come from a purchased random source, it is the exact structure Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency banned on 18 May 2012.
- Building deterioration into a healing product. Neko Atsume 2 added item durability in 2024 and it is the single most criticised change in the sequel. Streaks that reset, cards that fade, cats that 'leave if you neglect them' — every one of these imports Tamagotchi's 1996 hook into a 2026 genre that is explicitly sold as 癒し (healing). It will read as punishing people for loving their cat.
- Cute-voicing the cat. The current 'veterinary field unit at 3am' register is correct and rare; the entire market — Cats & Soup, Purrfect Tale, Duet Cats, most of the Japanese ranking charts — is saturated with pastel baby-talk. Drifting cute is drifting into the most crowded, lowest-ARPU part of the category. Deadpan clinical about an absurd animal is the defensible position.
- Pun names in Japanese, or kanji/kana as decoration. 'Hermeowne' and 'Chairman Meow' work in English because the pun mechanism is English. Japanese cat-name humour uses different machinery (にゃん substitutions, onomatopoeia, 2/22 = nyan-nyan-nyan). A western team writing Japanese cat puns, or setting 猫 in a display font because it looks cool, produces the exact weeb-adjacent register that alienates both audiences. Take the STRUCTURE (named, punny, memeable rare tiers) and write it natively in English.
- Misrepresenting the art as anything other than generated. This applies doubly here: the card art is derived from a photo of a real animal a person loves. Any copy implying an artist drew their cat is a lie the user can disprove by looking at it. State plainly that the card is generated from their photo.
- Treating the sequel's 'visit other players' yards' idea as validated. Both Neko Atsume 2 (2024) and Tamagotchi Uni (2023) bolted a visiting/social layer onto a private care loop, and Neko Atsume 2 reviewers said it was 'not much different from sharing screenshots' while players complained about grinding the same yards for a stamp. The studio has already correctly ruled out public feeds and stranger surfaces. Do not quietly reintroduce them as 'visiting'.
- Mistaking install volume for a business. Cats & Soup has 50-60M downloads and Sensor Tower panel revenue in the low tens of thousands of dollars per month per platform-region. The cozy cat category reaches enormous numbers of people who will not pay. Plan the economics around the artifact (prints, frames, unlimited history) and around near-zero server cost, not around a monetised loop.
- Shipping the reveal animation and calling it the product. The scan beam and holo tilt are a one-time delight; the Cutting/Gundry/Cairns work found engagement in idle games is a HABIT built on CHECKING FREQUENCY. Polishing the first thirty seconds while leaving no reason for day two is precisely how a cat app becomes a 2016-style fad that peaks in seven months.
- Random-rolling the reward for loyalty. Neko Atsume's mementos were the emotional core and they were a hidden dice roll — the documented completionist waited over three months for one cat and got an item they found underwhelming. Repeating that in 2026 with a user's OWN cat as the subject converts affection into resentment.
- Letting the API key ship in the client. The known blocker is a real one: a Claude vision call from a single-file client with an embedded key is a key on a billboard. This is a launch blocker, not a v1.1 item, and it interacts with everything above — a server proxy is also where daily observation generation, deterministic memento counters, and abuse limits have to live.
- Making rarity purchasable in any form. The card claims to describe a real animal. The moment rarity can be bought, every card in circulation becomes unfalsifiable and the exported PNG — which is the entire distribution strategy — loses the credibility that makes people repost it.

DESIGN IMPLICATIONS:
- SPLIT BREED FROM COAT INTO TWO SEPARATE FIELDS, AND NEVER PUT A PATTERN IN THE BREED SLOT. 'Tortoiseshell isn't a breed' is the single most-repeated one-star review in this entire category, and it is the one Cat Scanner has never fixed in seven years. The card should state COAT with total confidence (tortoiseshell, tuxedo, calico, mackerel tabby, void) because that IS knowable from a photo, and treat BREED as a low-confidence field that defaults to an honest, dignified 'Domestic Shorthair — no pedigree detected'. Over 90% of cats are random-bred; make that the celebrated default rather than a failure state. This alone removes the loudest complaint in the market and costs nothing.
- SCANNING MUST BE UNLIMITED, FREE, AND UNGATED — NO ENERGY, NO CANS, NO AD-PER-SCAN, EVER. Both direct competitors died on this exact mechanic in public and in living memory: CatchCat went from viral to 2.97 stars on '1 ad to 1 can', and MeowTalk went from 20M downloads to 1.53 stars on $7.49/week plus an ad before every capture. The moment of scanning IS the product. Monetise the artifact after it exists (a physical print, a foil finish, a card back, a year-book) and never the act of looking at your own cat.
- MAKE THE CARD DETERMINISTIC PER CAT, AND MAKE RE-SCANNING ADD RATHER THAN REROLL. 'One day I went and tried it again but this time I got different results!!' is a named complaint against Cat Scanner, and CatchCat users are angry they get 'MANY of the same cat' with no way to curate. Derive the card's identity from a stable per-cat key (owner + cat profile + image feature hash) so the same cat always resolves to the same card, and let a re-scan mint a dated VARIANT of that card — a new pose, a seasonal print — that stacks into the cat's own set. This turns the duplicate problem into the collection mechanic.
- BUILD FOR THE INDOOR CAT SOMEONE ALREADY OWNS, NOT FOR STRANGERS' STREET CATS. CatchCat's fatal design constraint is that it needs an outdoor cat in your street, which excludes Australia, most of the US and UK suburbs, and the entire indoor-cat majority. The unit of collection should be 'my cat, over time' rather than 'a cat I found'. That also sidesteps the whole stranger-social surface the brief already rules out.
- USE TIME AS THE COLLECTION AXIS, BACKED BY REAL SEASONAL DATA. Catlog's 16-billion-point dataset gives verifiable, dated hooks for re-scanning: adult cats gain about 7% body weight July to September, feeding frequency drops in the same window, sleep peaks in winter, and daily sleep rises about 18 minutes per year of age. A monthly or seasonal re-scan that mints a 'Summer Coat' or 'Winter Loaf' variant is therefore not an invented gacha excuse — it is a real, citable biological rhythm, and it gives the app a retention loop that is honest.
- GROUND EVERY STAT IN SOMETHING DEFENSIBLE AND SHOW THE SOURCE. CHONK can cite the seasonal 7% summer weight swing; VOID and LOAF can cite the measured domestic sleep profile (high 01:00–04:00 and 09:00–15:00, low when the owner is awake); ZOOMIES can cite that 0–2 year olds are overwhelmingly the most active age class. A single small 'FIELD NOTE' line on the card back, sourced to real research, is the cheapest possible separation from the 'AI slop' verdict that Hacker News handed CatchCat, and it fits the deadpan veterinary-field-unit voice exactly.
- ADD AN OWNER-ANSWERED PERSONALITY AXIS THE PHOTO CANNOT FAKE, MODELLED ON THE FELINE FIVE. A short quiz mapping to Neuroticism, Extraversion, Dominance, Impulsiveness and Agreeableness (Litchfield et al. 2017, n=2,802) gives the card a second input channel, makes two identical-looking tuxedos produce genuinely different cards, and defends against the 'it just guessed from the picture' criticism. It is also the honest way to deliver a 'personality profile' without pretending vision can read temperament.
- LET USERS DELETE, RENAME, REORDER AND PIN CARDS FROM DAY ONE. Curation is explicitly requested in CatchCat's reviews and its absence is cited in multiple one-stars. A collection you cannot prune is a burden, not a keepsake.
- TREAT THE COLLECTION AS A MEMORIAL AND NEVER LOSE OR PAYWALL AN OLD CARD. The cruelest review in this whole research set is a MeowTalk user who lost their deceased cat's recordings and was charged for the privilege. Every cat app eventually becomes a memorial. That means: local-first persistence (finish the localStorage swap and add export), no card ever locked behind a lapsed subscription, and a genuinely good 'in memoriam' card treatment rather than an awkward silence.
- SHIP A PHYSICAL OUTPUT, BECAUSE THAT IS THE PROVEN NON-PREDATORY MODEL. Doconoko — the highest-rated real-cat app in Japan at 4.72 and ad-free for a decade — makes its money on printed calendars and photo cubes, not subscriptions. The 1080x1350 PNG export is the right instinct; the next step is a real printed card or a year-end set, which is a one-off purchase a cat lover will make happily and which cannot rot into a $30/month grievance.
- GIVE AWAY ONE PIECE OF REAL UTILITY: A PRINTABLE LOST-CAT POSTER GENERATED FROM THE CARD. Doconoko's most-praised feature auto-builds a lost-pet flyer from the stored profile for printing at a convenience store. A card app already holds the exact assets needed — a good photo, a name, a coat description, distinguishing marks. Shipping this free turns a toy into something people defend, and it is the single highest-goodwill feature available for near-zero engineering cost.
- DO NOT BUILD BREED COLLECTING OR A BREED LEADERBOARD — IT IS TAKEN. Cat Scanner already ships 'just like in Pokémon Go' breed catching, virtual treats, challenges, leaderboards and a community feed at 1M+ installs. Competing there means fighting an incumbent on its own ground with its own worst bug. Compete on the individual cat instead: the depth of one cat's set, not the breadth of a breed index.
- SHIP iOS, AND CLAIM THE NAME ON BOTH STORES IMMEDIATELY. CatchCat is Android-only with no iOS date, and MeowTalk has vanished from the iOS App Store entirely while a Turkish shell company now owns its name in iOS search. The iOS half of the viral cat-card market is currently empty AND the naming space is being actively squatted. Register on both stores before any launch noise.
- KEEP THE HOLD ON PUBLIC FEEDS AND STRANGER VOTING — THE RESEARCH VINDICATES IT. Cat Scanner's chat has a bullying and moderation problem serious enough to appear in its App Store reviews; Pet Parade's vote-and-coin economy generates 'you ruined it' one-stars; Petzbe's gentle pets-only network is at roughly 1,500 ratings after eight years. PNG export as the sharing mechanism is the correct call and should be stated as a design principle, not a v1 shortcut.
- DESIGN FOR MULTI-CAT HOUSEHOLDS AS THE POWER USER. Catlog makes the sixth cat free; CatsMe leads its store copy with 'I have three, I can't keep track of who's how'. The horizontal collection strip should be built for one to five cats deep with clear per-cat grouping, not for an undifferentiated pile.
- MAKE RARITY A PROPERTY OF THE CARD, NEVER A VERDICT ON THE CAT. ALLEY through MYTHIC must read as print rarity — how unusual this particular rendering is — and never as a judgement of the animal's worth or pedigree. A rescue moggy graded 'ALLEY' by an app is a review waiting to happen. Consider naming the tiers after print/archive language rather than value language so the ladder cannot be misread as a ranking of cats.
- THE DEADPAN CLINICAL VOICE IS THE MOAT, SO WRITE IT BY HAND. The entire competitive set now reads as machine-generated — Hacker News's verdict on CatchCat was literally 'AI Slop' and its own users called the text 'horrible'. Hand-written epithets, flavour lines and field notes are the cheapest available differentiator in a category where every competitor shipped model output raw. Curate a bank of lines the way the botanical game curates its haiku banks.

TRAPS:
- Sprinkling Japanese honorifics as decoration. RABO writes 猫様 ('honoured cat') sincerely and consistently as house style across a decade of research reports; a western app dropping '-sama' or '猫様' into English copy reads as cosplay and will be spotted instantly. Borrow the RESPECTFUL POSTURE — treating the cat as the senior party in the relationship — not the vocabulary.
- Maneki-neko, torii gates, sakura petals and pastel kawaii UI as shorthand for 'Japan'. Japan's own best cat products are restrained and clean: Doconoko is a quiet white photo app, Catlog is clinical data design. The kawaii-coded cat aesthetic westerners reach for is a 2015 Neko Atsume memory, not what Japanese cat owners currently use.
- Calling tortoiseshell, calico, tuxedo, tabby or 'void' a BREED. This is the number one one-star complaint in the whole category and it lands hardest on the most passionate segment: a reviewer scanned four purebred Ragamuffins including retired Grand Champions and was told '95% tuxedo'. Getting this wrong signals to serious cat people that you did not do the homework.
- Letting a rarity tier imply the cat is worth less. 'ALLEY' aimed at someone's rescue cat is a real risk. Rarity must describe the artifact, never the animal, and the copy has to make that unmissable on first run.
- Drifting into implied health claims. A 'health' or 'condition' stat pulls you into the Feline Grimace Scale / Sylvester / CatsMe space, which is genuinely regulated-adjacent, genuinely scientific, and commercially dead (9, 15 and 54 ratings respectively). Worse, a wrong reassurance about a sick cat is the one failure mode that would end the studio's reputation. Keep every stat unmistakably a game stat.
- Energy currencies, scan limits, or ads gating the capture. Both direct competitors executed this and both are publicly burning for it right now. There is no version of this that the audience will not recognise on sight.
- Weekly subscriptions. MeowTalk's move to $7.49/week is the most-cited single cause of its collapse from 20M downloads to 1.53 stars. In a category this scarred, any subscription at all needs a very strong justification, and a weekly one is disqualifying.
- Assuming a public feed makes it social. Cat Scanner's community chat produced bullying complaints in its App Store reviews; Pet Parade's voting economy produced 'you ruined it'. Stranger-to-stranger surfaces in pet apps reliably generate moderation cost and one-star reviews without generating retention.
- Assuming Japanese cat owners want breed identification. Most Japanese housecats are 雑種 (mixed), the breed question barely registers, and the money there goes to Catlog's ¥980/month health monitoring and to Doconoko's community. A breed-ID pitch localised into Japanese would land on an audience that does not have that question.
- Reading gacha mechanics as harmless fun in a Japanese launch. Japanese users parse gacha patterns fluently and hold strong views about them; a wholesome cat keepsake that suddenly reveals pull rates and pity timers would be read as bait, not delight.
- Treating Japan as a cheap localisation target. Doconoko has run for ten years with Hobonichi editorial staff producing daily programming (放送局). Machine-translated Japanese in a cat app aimed at the most cat-literate audience on earth will be identified within one screenshot.
- Picking a name that is already squatted or clone-adjacent. The cat scanner and cat translator name spaces are saturated with near-identical titles from shell companies, and MeowTalk lost its own name on iOS to one. Search both stores and secure the listing before any public mention.
- Being flippant about a dead cat. The most painful review found in this entire research pass is a user who lost their deceased cat's recordings and was charged for it. Deadpan clinical humour is the right voice for a living cat and completely wrong the moment the collection becomes a memorial — the app needs to know the difference and handle it with care.
- Publishing borrowed Catlog research as if it were the studio's own. The findings are excellent and citable, but RABO earned them across 16 billion data points; every use needs visible attribution, both because it is right and because uncredited use of a named Japanese company's research would be noticed by exactly the audience being courted.

### RESEARCH: What actually makes a photo-collection app addictive and viral — evidence base for a cat trading-card app (reinforcement schedules, streaks, collection-completion psychology, share-image virality, identity-result content, the single-subject problem, daily ritual, and the ethical lines)

FINDINGS:
- [likely] Variable-ratio (VR) reinforcement — reward after an unpredictable NUMBER of responses — produces the highest steady response rate and the greatest resistance to extinction of the four basic schedules. Established by Ferster & Skinner, 'Schedules of Reinforcement' (1957); the classic pigeon work is 1956-57.
  (source: https://www.simplypsychology.org/schedules-of-reinforcement.html (secondary summary of Ferster & Skinner 1957))
- [verified] Pokémon GO's documented base shiny rate is 1 in 512 for virtually all eligible wild Pokémon; 1 in 256 for Shadow Pokémon from Grunts, 1 in 64 for non-5-star raids and hatched eggs, 1 in 20 for 5-star raids, 1 in 25 on Community Days, 1 in 10 on Raid/Research Days. These are crowd-sourced estimates from The Silph Road, not Niantic-published. This is the canonical example of a VARIANT of an otherwise identical, endlessly repeatable subject carrying the variable-ratio load.
  (source: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Shiny_Pok%C3%A9mon_(GO) (page last updated 18 Mar 2026))
- [verified] Near-misses in gambling are experienced as LESS pleasant than full misses but INCREASE desire to continue playing, and they recruit striatal and insula circuitry that also responds to actual monetary wins. Critically, the motivational effect was restricted to trials where the subject had personal control over the wager. Insula activity correlated with both subjective desire and trait gambling susceptibility.
  (source: Clark, L., Lawrence, A.J., Astley-Jones, F., & Gray, N. (2009). Gambling near-misses enhance motivation to gamble and recruit win-related brain circuitry. Neuron, 61(3), 481–490. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19217383/)
- [verified] Goal-gradient in a real café reward program ('buy ten coffees, get one free'): across 949 completed/redeemed 10-stamp cards covering nearly ten thousand coffee purchases, mean inter-purchase time fell by 0.7 days (t = 2.6, p < .05) from the first to the last interval — a 20% acceleration. DESIGN IMPLICATIONS:
- Put the variable ratio on the CARD PRINT, never on the cat. Every scan of the same cat mints a new numbered print — #001, #002 — and the randomised layer is the artifact: foil treatment, frame, border ornament, epithet variant, and a serial number. Set the top-tier foil roll around 1-in-500 to 1-in-512 (Pokémon GO's documented base shiny rate is 1 in 512) so it is a real event, and boost it on named event days the way GO boosts Community Day to 1-in-25. The cat's identity stays constant and un-graded; the collectible varies. This gives you Skinner's strongest schedule without ever telling a user their pet rolled badly.
- Solve the single-subject problem the way Untappd and Strava already solved it: reward REPETITION of the fixed subject on its own dedicated track. Ship a 'Century Club' analogue (100 scans of one cat = a named, permanent badge on that cat's page) and a Local Legend analogue (a rolling 90-day 'most-documented' title per cat that must be re-earned, so the counter can never be finished and abandoned). Both are documented live mechanics on apps whose users also only have one route or one favourite beer.
- Never show an empty collection. On first mint, hand the user a 12-slot case with 2 slots already filled (starter/gift cards) rather than a 10-slot empty case. Kivetz et al.'s field experiment: identical 10 purchases completed in 12.7 days with the 2 pre-stamped bonus slots versus 15.6 days without — 20% faster, medians 10 vs 15 days. Display progress as proportion-of-original-distance remaining, not raw count, because that is the functional form their goal-distance model fits.
- Plan for the post-reward reset. The same paper shows effort rates DROP to a lower level immediately after the first reward is earned, then re-accelerate toward the second. So the moment a set completes, the next set must already be open AND already endowed with a head start. A completion screen that ends in a full stop is a churn event you built on purpose.
- Copy Duolingo's forgiveness stack, not just its counter. Ship: streak freeze with TWO held at once (doubling freezes raised their DAU +0.38%), a weekend-equivalent amulet (theirs produced +4% week-later return and 5% fewer streak losses; their DAU normally sags 5-10% on weekends), and an explicit pause. Weight milestone celebration heavily at days 2, 3, 5 and 7 — their published math is that 2-to-3 days is a 50% increase in streak length while 200-to-201 is 0.5% — then re-anchor long streaks to a new unit (weeks, seasons, 'field tours') so increments keep meaning something.
- Cap daily scans at roughly 3 and make that a feature, not a limit. Duolingo's published finding is that bingeing learners abandon the app at a much higher rate than paced learners. A cat owner with 900 photos will burn the entire novelty of the app in one sitting on day one if you let them. Frame the cap as film in a camera: 'THREE PLATES REMAINING TODAY' fits the 3am veterinary field unit voice exactly.
- Treat the exported PNG as the whole growth engine and design it to Wordle's rules. It must be legible with zero context, must be about the OWNER's cat rather than a spoiler of the app, and must carry the minimum viable identifier: cat name, epithet, rarity, the six stats, date, small wordmark plus a short URL. No QR code, no 'sent from', no auto-post, no aggressive watermark. Wordle's grid carried no branding at all and still produced ~100,000 public shares on a ~2M-player day, roughly a 5% public-share rate — that is the number to instrument against.
- Add a daily shared prompt so exported cards are COMPARABLE. Wardle's own stated reason the grids spread is that everyone was solving the same puzzle, not a random one per player. A single global daily assignment — 'FIELD ORDER 0412: DOCUMENT THE LOAF' — makes two friends' cards sit next to each other meaningfully, which is what converts a private artifact into a social one. It also creates the once-a-day ritual without an appointment window.
- Notification discipline: exactly one per day, at a time the USER picks, and nothing in the app may ever be missable. Zagal et al.'s explicit test is that Playing by Appointment 'is nullified if completing appointments is not required for progression'. If the daily prompt expires, or if the streak gates content, you have shipped the dark pattern. BeReal's randomly-timed 2-minute window is the version to refuse.
- Make the EPITHET the shareable payload, not the stats. Forer's participants rated an identical generic profile 4.30 out of 5 for personal accuracy — the mechanism of personality-quiz virality is felt specificity, not truth. So invest writing effort in a large bank of concrete, oddly specific, non-negative epithets ('DEPUTY WARDEN OF THE LINEN CUPBOARD') and let the numeric stats be supporting evidence. The epithet is the sentence a person repeats aloud.
- Tune the reveal for AWE, and structurally exclude sadness. Berger & Milkman: +1SD of awe raises share odds by 30%, anger by 34%, anxiety by 21%, while sadness LOWERS them by 16%. The scan-beam-then-card-slam already targets awe. This also means there must be no 'disappointing result' beat anywhere in the loop — a low-tier reveal is a sadness event and it is both cruel and anti-viral.
- Make set completion effort-gated, never luck-gated, and never paid. Japan banned kompu gacha effective 1 July 2012 precisely because completing a randomised SET to unlock a rare prize was ruled illegal under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations. Sets should complete on documentable actions ('five distinct poses', 'one scan in each season'), so the collection is always finishable by a patient person with one cat and no money.
- Build the longitudinal record as the primary repeat-value mechanic. Same cat, re-scanned over months, produces a time series on the card back — a CHONK curve, a stat drift chart, a strip of every print in date order. This is the Letterboxd diary / Vivino vintage pattern: the subject is fixed, the RECORD accrues. It is also the single feature a one-cat owner cannot exhaust.
- Write the rarity rule into the product and into the UI: rarity is a property of the PHOTOGRAPH and the PRINT, not of the animal. Every cat must be able to reach MYTHIC given the right light, pose and moment. State it plainly on the result screen ('RARITY GRADES THE PLATE, NOT THE SPECIMEN'). This is what makes 'my cat got ALLEY' structurally impossible to read as a verdict on the cat.
- Audit the six stats against veterinary shame. CHONK maps directly onto feline obesity, which is a genuine welfare issue and a genuine source of owner guilt; a high CHONK score presented as a stat is an accusation with a number on it. Either reframe it as a physical-presence measure with no healthy/unhealthy axis, or replace it. The Feline Five (Litchfield et al. 2017, n=2,802: Neuroticism, Extraversion, Dominance, Impulsiveness, Agreeableness) gives you a real, citable, non-judgemental scaffold to map the joke stats onto.
- Ship the bereavement path before launch, not after the first support email. Thanatosensitivity (Massimi & Charise, CHI 2009) exists precisely for this. Concretely: a cat can be marked IN MEMORIAM; its streak freezes permanently rather than breaking; its collection slot is preserved and never prompts; all notifications for that cat cease immediately and irreversibly; the export gets a quiet date-range rather than a black band. Over a 15-year cat lifespan and a large userbase this WILL happen, and getting it wrong once is the kind of thing people screenshot.
- Refuse contact upload, invite-gating and auto-posting outright. Zagal et al. name these as IMPERSONATION / FRIEND SPAM and SOCIAL PYRAMID SCHEMES. The whole distribution model is: render PNG, hand it to the OS share sheet, done. Locket reached roughly 80 million downloads with no feed, no comments and a hard friend cap — private-by-default is not a growth handicap.
- Handle the near-miss honestly or not at all. Clark et al. (2009) show near-misses raise the urge to continue while feeling WORSE, and only when the player believes they had control. So never taunt after a roll ('SO CLOSE TO FOIL!'). The legitimate version is pre-hoc and informational: a plate-quality meter that tells the user what the photo scored on lighting and framing BEFORE the roll, so the retry is a skill decision rather than a lever pull.
- Work with zero account and zero server-held data by default. Local storage plus explicit export; the API proxy holds only the key, not the library. Wardle's stated thesis — people have an appetite for things that transparently do not want anything from them — is the same posture as the studio's existing 'deliberately no public feed' decision, and it is the strongest available differentiator against every other AI pet-scanner.

TRAPS:
- Adding a cat blood-type or star-sign reading because 'Japan loves that'. Ketsuekigata is pseudoscience (Nawata 2014: <0.3% of personality variance across >10,000 respondents) AND a live harassment category in Japan with its own word, bura-hara. A Japanese audience will read it as either dated or tacky, and it hands you nothing the epithet system does not already do better.
- Shipping any 'collect the full set to unlock the rare card' mechanic that touches money or randomised drops. That is kompu gacha, banned in Japan since 1 July 2012 under the Premiums and Representations Act. It is the exact mechanic a western team reaches for on instinct.
- Letting rarity read as a grade on the animal. 'The AI says my cat is common' is the single sentence that kills this app in a cat-lover community, and it is one screenshot away at all times. Rarity must attach to the print and the photograph, and the copy must say so.
- Any stat a vet could read as a diagnosis. CHONK is the obvious one — feline obesity is a real welfare problem and owners of overweight cats already feel judged. MENACE is the second one: for an owner managing a genuinely aggressive or fearful cat, a high MENACE score is not a joke.
- Cross-user leaderboards or 'rarest cats today'. One loud top-of-board result flattens the experience for everyone else, and it converts a private affection into a competition the user's cat cannot win. Keep all comparison inside a single user's own library, or friends-only and unranked.
- An unforgiving streak. The Snapchat literature documents adolescents feeling obligated, distressed at breakage, and anxious about losing signal — and here the streak is attached to a living animal the user loves. A broken streak will feel like a failure of care, not a failure of engagement. Freezes, pauses and a repair path are not optional.
- Notifications written in the cat's voice ('Mittens misses you'). It is impersonation of a real being the user is bonded to, and it is catastrophic the first time it fires for a cat that has died. Notifications should stay in the deadpan field-unit register and speak about the app, never as the animal.
- Designing on the Zeigarnik effect. 'Leave the collection incomplete so they remember it' does not replicate — the 2025 Ghibellini & Meier meta-analysis found no memory advantage for unfinished tasks. What IS robust is the resumption tendency (Ovsiankina), so build a visible, obviously-resumable half-finished object rather than relying on nagging incompleteness to work by itself.
- Assuming Japanese cat culture means Neko Atsume's art direction. The transferable lesson from Neko Atsume is structural, not visual: no fail state, nothing withers, nothing punishes you for closing the app, and the reward for returning is discovery rather than avoided loss. Copying the pastel style while keeping western retention pressure gets you the worst of both.
- Cute-voiced copy. The existing 'deadpan clinical about an obviously ridiculous animal' voice is the differentiator and it is fragile — one 'purrfect' or one cat-pun epithet collapses it into the same register as every other cat app. The joke works because the app refuses to acknowledge that it is a joke.
- Over-branding the exported PNG. Wordle's grid carried literally no branding and still became the growth engine; a card plastered with a QR code, a download badge and an app-store banner reads as an ad and people stop posting it. The card must be something a person is proud to post because of the cat, with the attribution small enough to survive that.
- Setting retention targets from vendor benchmark tables. The 2025-2026 numbers are irreconcilable across sources — GameAnalytics reports 75% of games below 3% D28 and median D7 in the low single digits, while aggregator blogs quote casual-game D7 of 11-13%. Instrument your own cohorts from day one and compare the app to itself; quoting an external benchmark in a planning doc will just produce a wrong goal.
- Treating the model's output as an authority claim. If the UI reads as 'the AI has assessed your cat', every result becomes a judgement and negative-sounding results become injuries. Frame the model as a field instrument producing a PLATE — fallible, atmospheric, about the photograph — not as an oracle producing a verdict about the animal.

DESIGN IMPLICATIONS:
- DEFAULT TO claude-haiku-4-5 AT 768 px LONG EDGE, NOT 1024. Haiku is standard-resolution tier (1568 px cap) so nothing is lost by not going higher, and 768 px costs 588 visual tokens vs 1036 at 1024 px — $25.88 vs $30.36 per 10,000 scans, and a ~40% smaller upload over 4G. Run a blind A/B of 768 vs 1024 vs 512 on 50 real cat photos before locking it; 512 px (266 tokens, $22.66/10k) may well be enough for breed/coat/vibe and would be a 4x reduction in image tokens. Reserve claude-sonnet-5 for a paid 'Deep Scan' tier at $60-91/10k, and treat claude-opus-5 at $152/10k as economically out of reach for a free consumer loop.
- MAKE THE CARD CONTENT-ADDRESSED, NOT MODEL-DETERMINED. You cannot get determinism from sampling parameters — temperature/top_p/top_k are 400 errors on Opus 5 / Opus 4.8 / Fable 5 and non-default values are rejected on Sonnet 5. So: (1) canonicalise the image BEFORE hashing — decode via createImageBitmap with imageOrientation 'from-image', downscale to a fixed 256x256, drop to a fixed colour space, and SHA-256 the raw RGBA pixel buffer, never the file bytes; (2) key a Firestore `scans/{pixelHash}` doc on that hash and return the stored card on any re-scan, so the model is called exactly once per distinct photo; (3) derive every cosmetic variable that does not need semantics — foil shine seed, tilt phase, holographic hue offset, tiebreak jitter on stats — deterministically from the same hash, exactly as the botanical game derives plant traits from a SHA-256. The model supplies judgement; the hash supplies identity. Note the pixel-hash still breaks under crop/rotate/filter — that is acceptable (a cropped photo is arguably a different card) but must be a deliberate product decision, and a 64-bit dHash/aHash perceptual fallback is the upgrade path if 'my cat got a different card' becomes a support issue.
- CLAMP AND VALIDATE EVERY FIELD SERVER-SIDE — THE SCHEMA CANNOT DO IT. Structured outputs guarantee CHONK is an integer and present, but minimum/maximum are unsupported JSON Schema features that return 400, so nothing stops the model returning CHONK: 4200. In the Cloud Function, after parsing: clamp all six stats to 0-100, whitelist rarity against the five-value enum, truncate title and flavor to hard character limits, and reject-and-retry-once on failure. Also branch on stop_reason === 'refusal' BEFORE touching content[0].text, and map it to a friendly 'we could not read that photo' rather than a stack trace.
- THE CAT GATE BELONGS IN THE CLAUDE CALL, NOT ON-DEVICE. An on-device MediaPipe gate costs 7.7 MB of first load (45 KB bundle + 3.12 MB gz wasm + 4.56 MB tflite) — about 12 seconds on 4G — to save $0.003. That is a terrible trade for a first-run experience. Keep `is_cat` as a field in the graded JSON. What DOES belong on-device is coat-colour extraction: getImageData on a 64x64 downscale plus median-cut in plain ES5 is zero KB, sub-10 ms, and gives you an instant palette to tint the scan beam and the card frame while the network call is in flight. That turns dead waiting time into apparent responsiveness at no download cost. If a client-side gate is ever wanted, gate it behind a returning-user flag and lazy-load MediaPipe on second visit, never on first paint.
- BUILD THE PROXY AS onCall WITH APP CHECK AND A TRANSACTIONAL PER-UID DAILY QUOTA. Shape:

const {onCall, HttpsError} = require('firebase-functions/v2/https');
const {defineSecret} = require('firebase-functions/params');
const admin = require('firebase-admin'); admin.initializeApp();
const ANTHROPIC_KEY = defineSecret('ANTHROPIC_API_KEY');

exports.gradeCat = onCall({
  region: 'us-central1', secrets: [ANTHROPIC_KEY],
  enforceAppCheck: true, memory: '512MiB', timeoutSeconds: 60, maxInstances: 20,
}, async (request) => {
  if (!request.auth) throw new HttpsError('unauthenticated', 'sign in first');
  const uid = request.auth.uid;
  const {b64, mediaType, pixelHash} = request.data || {};
  if (!/^image\/(jpeg|png|webp)$/.test(mediaType)) throw new HttpsError('invalid-argument','bad type');
  if (typeof b64 !== 'string' || b64.length > 1_400_000) throw new HttpsError('invalid-argument','too large'); // ~1MB binary
  if (!/^[0-9a-f]{64}$/.test(pixelHash||'')) throw new HttpsError('invalid-argument','bad hash');

  const db = admin.firestore();
  const cached = await db.doc('scans/'+pixelHash).get();
  if (cached.exists) return cached.data().card;            // determinism + free

  const day = new Date().toISOString().slice(0,10);
  const qref = db.doc(`quota/${uid}/days/${day}`);
  await db.runTransaction(async t => {                      // atomic, not client-enforced
    const s = await t.get(qref);
    const n = s.exists ? s.data().n : 0;
    if (n >= 20) throw new HttpsError('resource-exhausted','daily scan limit');
    t.set(qref, {n: n+1, updated: admin.firestore.FieldValue.serverTimestamp()}, {merge:true});
  });

  const res = await fetch('https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages', {
    method: 'POST',
    headers: {'x-api-key': ANTHROPIC_KEY.value(), 'anthropic-version': '2023-06-01', 'content-type': 'application/json'},
    body: JSON.stringify({
      model: 'claude-haiku-4-5', max_tokens: 700,
      system: GRADING_SYSTEM_PROMPT,
      output_config: {format: {type: 'json_schema', schema: CARD_SCHEMA}},
      messages: [{role:'user', content: [
        {type:'image', source:{type:'base64', media_type: mediaType, data: b64}},
        {type:'text', text:'Grade this specimen.'}
      ]}]
    })
  });
  if (res.status === 429) throw new HttpsError('resource-exhausted','busy, try again');
  if (!res.ok) throw new HttpsError('internal','grader unavailable');   // fetch() does NOT throw on 4xx/5xx
  const msg = await res.json();
  if (msg.stop_reason === 'refusal') throw new HttpsError('invalid-argument','cannot grade that image');
  const card = clampCard(JSON.parse(msg.content.find(b=>b.type==='text').text));
  await db.doc('scans/'+pixelHash).set({card, uid, created: admin.firestore.FieldValue.serverTimestamp()});
  return card;
});

Key points: the key lives only in Secret Manager via defineSecret and is read with .value() at runtime; enforceAppCheck blocks scripted abuse from outside the app; request.auth.uid is already verified by the callable protocol (do NOT hand-roll verifyIdToken); the quota increment is inside runTransaction so two tabs cannot both pass the check; the cache lookup happens BEFORE the quota decrement so a re-scan is free; and !res.ok is checked explicitly because fetch() resolves on 4xx/5xx. Set maxInstances deliberately — it is your real spend brake against the $500/month tier cap.
- EXPORT AT A FIXED 1080x1350 BACKING STORE, JPEG OR PNG ONLY, AFTER AN EXPLICIT PER-FACE FONT AWAIT. Do not multiply the export canvas by devicePixelRatio — 1080x1350 is 1.46 Mpx and safe, but DPR 3 gives 13.1 Mpx against mobile Safari's 16.78 Mpx area cap, which fails silently with a blank canvas rather than an exception. Gate the draw on `await Promise.all([document.fonts.load('800 100px "Bricolage Grotesque"'), document.fonts.load('400 100px "DM Mono"'), document.fonts.load('500 100px "DM Mono"')])` — one load() call per weight actually used, because document.fonts.ready never waits for canvas-only text, and a 16px load does not load the 800 face. Then `canvas.toBlob(cb, 'image/jpeg', 0.92)`; never request image/webp, which is unsupported in Safari and iOS Safari and silently returns PNG. Render in a Worker via transferControlToOffscreen where available (Safari/iOS 16.4+, Chrome Android 69+) with a main-thread fallback.
- STORE PHOTOS IN INDEXEDDB AS BLOBS, ONE RECORD PER CARD, BEHIND A WEB LOCK. localStorage is 5 MiB and holds roughly two base64 photos — use it only for a lightweight index/pointer and settings, never image data. Put each card in its own IndexedDB record with the Blob stored directly (not a data URL, which costs 33% and forces a string decode), keyed by pixelHash. Wrap every write in `navigator.locks.request('cards-write', async () => {...})` (Safari/iOS 15.4+, Chrome Android 69+) and mutate single records rather than reading and rewriting a whole collection object — the read-once/write-wholesale pattern is exactly what let two tabs destroy 4,000 stars in the portal. Broadcast a 'card-added' message on a BroadcastChannel so the other tab re-reads instead of overwriting. Call navigator.storage.persist() on first successful save, and show the real number from navigator.storage.estimate() (Safari/iOS 17+) in a settings row rather than guessing a limit.
- MAKE HOME-SCREEN INSTALL A FIRST-CLASS, EARLY PROMPT ON iOS — IT IS DATA PROTECTION, NOT A NICE-TO-HAVE. WebKit deletes localStorage, IndexedDB, service worker registrations and caches after seven days of Safari use without interaction with the site; home-screen web apps keep their own usage counter and are the documented exception. A collector who scans five cats and does not return for eight days loses everything in Safari but keeps everything if installed. Ship the minimum installable manifest (HTTPS, name/short_name, 192 and 512 PNG icons, start_url, display:standalone, prefer_related_applications:false), and because beforeinstallprompt does not exist on iOS, draw your own Share-menu instruction card — triggered after the second successful scan, when the user has something to lose.
- WRITE THE SERVICE WORKER SO IT CANNOT HANG A NAVIGATION. Do not call event.respondWith() on navigation requests at all unless you can guarantee settlement: an unsettled promise passed to respondWith blocks the paint forever with no error and no timeout. Safest shape: `if (e.request.mode === 'navigate') return;` (let the network handle it), and for same-origin GET assets only, race the cache/network promise against a hard `new Promise(r => setTimeout(() => r(fetch(e.request)), 3000))` fallback. Version every cache name, delete non-matching caches in activate, and remember caches.keys() is origin-wide so a shared origin with the botanical game will collide. Add a kill switch: a fetch of /sw-kill.txt that, if present, calls registration.unregister() and reloads.
- BUDGET AND INSTRUMENT AGAINST THE TIER SPEND CAP, NOT THE PER-SCAN PRICE. Start tier pauses all API usage at $500/month, which is ~164,000 Haiku scans. Set a per-uid daily cap (20 free scans is a defensible starting point at $0.06/user/day) enforced transactionally in Firestore, set maxInstances on the function as a second brake, and log every scan's usage.input_tokens/output_tokens to a daily rollup doc so the actual cost per scan is measured rather than assumed. Move to Build tier before launch. The Batch API's 50% discount is unusable here — a scan is interactive and batches take up to 24 hours.

TRAPS:
- Putting the Anthropic API key in the single HTML file, or in a Firebase 'config' object, or in a functions/.env committed to the repo. The key must live in Secret Manager via defineSecret and be read with .value() at runtime. Every client-side key on a public static host is a key that gets scraped and billed to you inside a day.
- Assuming temperature: 0 exists. It is a 400 error on Claude Opus 5, Opus 4.8, Opus 4.7 and Fable 5, and non-default values are rejected on Sonnet 5. A team that ships the determinism story as 'we set temperature to zero' will discover at integration time that the parameter no longer exists on the frontier models, and that even where it does exist Anthropic's own docs say it never guaranteed identical output.
- Hashing the uploaded FILE BYTES to dedupe scans. The same photo re-shared through Messages, WhatsApp, or a different camera roll export has different bytes and hashes differently, so the user pays twice and gets a different card for the same cat. Hash the canonicalised decoded pixel buffer (fixed size, fixed orientation, fixed colour space), not the file.
- Calling canvas.toBlob(cb, 'image/webp') to shrink the export or the upload. Safari and iOS Safari do not support WebP canvas encoding and, per the HTML spec, silently return PNG instead. The team tests on a Pixel, sees 180 KB, and ships 900 KB PNGs to every iPhone with no error anywhere.
- Multiplying the export canvas by devicePixelRatio for crispness. At DPR 3 a 1080x1350 card becomes 13.1 Mpx against mobile Safari's hard 16.78 Mpx area cap — it works on an iPhone 15 and produces a silent blank canvas on an older or memory-pressured device, with no exception to catch.
- Using document.fonts.ready as the gate before ctx.fillText. It only waits for fonts the layout engine decided to load, and canvas-only text never participates in layout — so on a cold load the export renders in the system fallback font and the whole 'veterinary field unit' typographic identity silently disappears. It also fails weight-specifically: awaiting a 400-weight load does not load Bricolage Grotesque 800.
- Storing cat photos in localStorage. 5 MiB per origin is about two base64 photos before QuotaExceededError. The bug does not appear in testing with one cat.
- Reading the whole collection out of storage, mutating it in memory, and writing it back wholesale. With two tabs open, the second write erases everything the first tab added. Use one IndexedDB record per card, a Web Lock around writes, and a BroadcastChannel to tell the other tab to re-read.
- Shipping an on-device model to gate cat-vs-not-cat. MediaPipe is 7.7 MB of first load (~12 s on 4G) and transformers.js's default detector, Xenova/detr-resnet-50, is 43 MB even quantized. Both to save $0.003 per scan on a check the vision call already performs for free as an is_cat field.
- Expecting an on-device model to identify breeds. COCO-80 has exactly one relevant label, 'cat'. ImageNet-1k has five (tabby, tiger cat, Persian, Siamese, Egyptian Mau) plus a set of big cats that will confidently mislabel a large tabby as a lynx. Neither is a breed classifier, and shipping one as if it were will produce a stream of 'my cat is not a Siamese' complaints.
- Enforcing the daily scan quota in client-side JavaScript, or with a plain read-then-write in the Cloud Function. Two rapid taps or two open tabs both pass a non-transactional check. The counter must be incremented inside a Firestore runTransaction, and the Firestore rules must not let the client write its own quota doc.
- Treating a policy refusal as an exception. A declined request returns HTTP 200 with stop_reason 'refusal' and possibly an empty content array. Code that does content[0].text crashes; code that checks res.ok alone never notices. Related: fetch() does not reject on 4xx/5xx at all, so a bare fetch().catch() around the Anthropic call is not error handling.
- Calling event.respondWith() on navigation requests in the service worker. Any code path where that promise never settles — a stalled fetch on a captive-portal Wi-Fi, an await on a cache that never resolves — produces a permanent black screen with no error, no timeout, and no way for the user to recover except clearing site data.
- Budgeting on per-scan cost and missing the $500/month Start-tier spend cap, which pauses ALL API usage until the next calendar month. A modest viral moment (~164,000 Haiku scans) takes the app offline mid-launch, and the tier upgrade is not instant.
- Trying to enforce stat ranges in the JSON schema. minimum/maximum/minLength are unsupported JSON Schema features that return a 400 — the schema guarantees the field exists and is an integer, nothing more. Clamp server-side or ship a card with CHONK 4200.
- Adding cache_control to a ~900-token system prompt on claude-haiku-4-5 and assuming it caches. Haiku 4.5's minimum cacheable prefix is 4096 tokens; below that nothing caches, no error is raised, and cache_creation_input_tokens quietly returns 0. The minimums are not monotonic across generations (512 on Opus 5, 4096 on Haiku 4.5), so a value that works on one model silently no-ops on another.
- Building any face-matching, 'find cats that look like yours', or owner-identification feature. Anthropic's Usage Policy prohibits facial recognition and biometric categorization of people, and Claude refuses to name people in images. The same clause means a photo containing a person needs a defined handling path, not an unconsidered one. And because the app is consumer-facing, the policy requires disclosing that the user is interacting with AI — which the deadpan clinical voice can carry, but it has to actually be said.

### RESEARCH: What a vision model can honestly read from one cat photo, and the ethics of grading someone's pet — accuracy limits, body condition, pain signals, rarity design, and hard legal lines for the Sky Wolf Studio cat card app

FINDINGS:
- [verified] The Oxford-IIIT Pet dataset is 7,349 images across 37 breeds: 25 dog breeds and only 12 cat breeds (Abyssinian, Bengal, Birman, Bombay, British Shorthair, Egyptian Mau, Maine Coon, Persian, Ragdoll, Russian Blue, Siamese, Sphynx), ~200 images per class split 50 train / 50 val / 100 test.
  (source: Parkhi, Vedaldi, Zisserman, Jawahar, 'Cats and Dogs', CVPR 2012, Tab. 1. doi:10.1109/CVPR.2012.6248092 — PDF read via https://web.archive.org/web/20260501172535/https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/publications/2012/parkhi12a/parkhi12a.pdf)
- [verified] The canonical cat-breed benchmark deliberately DELETED the cat most people own. Verbatim from the dataset construction: images were dropped when the breed label could not be fixed, 'for instance because the pet was a cross breed'. There is no Domestic Shorthair, moggy, or mixed class anywhere in the 37. Every published accuracy number on this benchmark is measured on a closed world of pedigree cats.
  (source: Parkhi et al. 2012, Sect. 2.1 'Dataset collection'. Same PDF as above.)
- [verified] Original 2012 results: cat-vs-dog family classification 95.37%; 12-class CAT breed 66.07% (with ground-truth segmentation) / 63.48% (automatic); 25-class DOG breed 59.18% / 55.68%; full 37-class flat classification 59.21% / 54.03%.
  (source: Parkhi et al. 2012, Tab. 4 rows 8-9 and Sects. 4.1-4.3.)
- [verified] Cat breeds look easier than dog breeds but are actually harder relative to the task. Cat breed accuracy (66.07% on 12 classes, chance 8.33%) is only 7.9x chance; dog breed accuracy (59.18% on 25 classes, chance 4.0%) is 14.8x chance. Dog breeds carry far more discriminative signal — skull shape, ear carriage, body proportion, size — while domestic cats are morphologically near-uniform and differ mainly in coat, which is exactly the trait that varies freely within random-bred cats.
  (source: Computed from Parkhi et al. 2012 Tab. 4 against the paper's own stated chance baselines (Sect. 2.1 evaluation protocol). Arithmetic is mine; the underlying accuracies and class counts are the paper's.)
- [verified] On the closed 37-class set, model capability is no longer the bottleneck — it is near-saturated. GPipe reaches 95.9%, EfficientNet-B6 95.4%, ResNet-152 94.5%. Zero-shot CLIP with no pet-specific training reaches 85.4% (RN50) rising to 93.4% (RN50x64).
  (source: Tan & Le, 'EfficientNet', ICML 2019, arXiv:1905.11946, Tab. 6; Radford et al., 'Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision', arXiv:2103.00020, appendix per-dataset zero-shot table.)
- [verified] UK: an estimated 89-92% of domestic cats are non-pedigree Domestic Shorthair, Domestic Longhair or Domestic Semi-longhair. Roughly nine in ten scans in a UK-facing app have no correct breed answer.
  (source: Irving McGrath et al., 'More Than a Moggy; A Population Genetics Analysis of the United Kingdom's Non-Pedigree Cats', Genes 2021;12(10):1619. doi:10.3390/genes12101619)
- [verified] A moggy is not a mix of breeds — calling it one is a category error, not just imprecision. SNV-array analysis of 1,344 UK cats (PCA + fastSTRUCTURE) confirmed DSH/DLH/DSLH cats are RANDOM-BRED, 'rather than admixed, mix breed, or crossbred'. Their linkage disequilibrium decays rapidly, unlike pedigree cats. Any 'your cat is 40% Maine Coon' style output is therefore scientifically false, not merely uncertain.
  (source: Irving McGrath et al., Genes 2021;12(10):1619.)
- [verified] Globally, random-bred cats form 'a single worldwide population' with isolation by distance, based on >1,000 genotyped random-bred cats. Heterozygosity declines with distance from the Fertile Crescent progenitor range. Geography is a real, readable axis in cat populations; breed is not.
  (source: Nilson et al., 'Genetics of randomly bred cats support the cradle of cat domestication being in the Near East', Heredity 2022;129(6):346-355. doi:10.1038/s41437-022-00568-4)
- [verified] JAPAN, hard primary numbers: among 274,324 cats newly insured 2023-04-01 to 2024-03-31, 日本猫 (Japanese domestic cat) is rank 1 at 28.8% (79,099) and 混血猫 (mixed) rank 2 at 13.6% (37,284) — 42.4% non-pedigree. Scottish Fold is rank 3 at 7.8% (21,417) and Munchkin rank 4 at 7.6% (20,741). Japan's pet cat market is dramatically more pedigree-heavy than the UK's.
  (source: アニコム損害保険『家庭どうぶつ白書2025』(Anicom, White Paper on Household Animals 2025), chart 1-4. https://www.anicom-page.com/hakusho/book/pdf/book_202512_1.pdf)

Newly acquired Japanese kittens come overwhelmingly through the pedigree pet-shop pipeline. A Japan launch aimed at young cats meets a very different population than one aimed at all cats.

Scottish Fold's folded ear is caused by a dominant TRPV4 variant that also causes osteochondrodysplasia, a painful degenerative joint disorder; the veterinary literature states 'All Scottish Fold cats are believed to be affected'. Munchkin's short legs are chondrodysplasia. Any rarity system that gilds these is rewarding a genetic disease.

  (source: Graff et al., 'Inter-evaluator bias and applicability of feline body condition score from visual assessment', Front Vet Sci 2025;12:1604557. doi:10.3389/fvets.2025.1604557)
- [verified] The practical implication of that 1.8% figure: a photo CAN reliably separate ideal from obese. So a CHONK stat derived from apparent body fat is not a joke that happens to correlate with health — it is a low-precision but genuinely functional obesity screen wearing a joke's clothes.

DESIGN IMPLICATIONS:
- KILL THE BREED FIELD AS A CLAIM. Split the model output into COAT (colour, pattern, length, white-spotting — genuinely readable) and an optional BREED SIGNAL that only fires above a hard confidence floor and is phrased as resemblance ('carries Siamese points', 'Maine Coon build'), never identity. In the ~90% case the correct card line is a proud, top-tier named archetype for random-bred cats. 'DOMESTIC SHORTHAIR' must render as a legitimate headline category with its own art treatment, never as an 'unknown' or fallback state. Ban any percentage-mix output entirely — the genetics say a moggy is random-bred, not admixed, so '40% Maine Coon' is false, not merely uncertain.
- DECOUPLE CHONK FROM BODY FAT AT THE SCHEMA LEVEL, NOT THE COPY LEVEL. Photo-based BCS separates ideal from obese with only 1.8% misclassification, so if CHONK is computed from apparent body condition it IS an obesity screen no matter what you call it. Instead derive CHONK from pose, silhouette, frame occupancy, loaf compression and floof — an explicitly non-diagnostic 'presence' measure. Enforce two invariants in code: CHONK has a floor (never reads as a deficit), and high CHONK is always the joke's hero. Then run a validation pass: sample 200 cards, have someone score BCS independently, and check the correlation. If CHONK correlates with BCS, the stat is broken and must be re-derived — that is a shippable acceptance test, not a vibe.
- STRIP PAIN-ADJACENT VOCABULARY FROM THE MODEL OUTPUT SCHEMA, AND NEVER IMPLY AN ALL-CLEAR. The published evidence is that general chatbots scoring the Feline Grimace Scale UNDERESTIMATE pain and their limits of agreement straddle the analgesia threshold. So: (a) blocklist 'tired', 'sad', 'squinting', 'uncomfortable', 'lethargic', 'in pain', 'unwell' and ear/whisker/orbital descriptors from every generated field; (b) because the FGS action units are exactly ear position, orbital tightening, muzzle tension, whiskers and head position, any stat that reads those is accidentally a pain score — audit VOID and MENACE prompts specifically for this; (c) carry a permanent, always-on, never-triggered footer pointing to a vet, so that the ABSENCE of a warning is never information. A silent scanner reads as an all-clear.
- MAKE RARITY STRUCTURALLY INCAPABLE OF RANKING PEDIGREE. Set breed's contribution to rarity to exactly zero. Derive FOIL and MYTHIC from photo-moment properties the cat cannot lose at — pose novelty, coat-pattern combinatorics, lighting, first-of-its-kind within the user's OWN collection, streak and time-of-day. Hard-code an exclusion list so Scottish Fold, Munchkin, Persian and other brachycephalic or chondrodysplastic conformations can never map to a higher tier: Japan's #3 and #4 cats are a TRPV4 joint disorder and chondrodysplasia, and 32% of successful 'funny pet videos' already feature agony-breeding traits. Rarity must be about the photograph, never the animal's genome.
- BUILD THE RARITY FLOOR BEFORE THE CEILING. Since no cat may be the bad one, ALLEY must be designed first and designed to be lovable — give it the best copy, its own visual identity, and make it the most common outcome on purpose. Rarity then reads as 'which flavour of special', not 'how good is my cat'. Ship no global leaderboard, no cross-user ranking, no rarity percentile against strangers: the moment rarity is comparable between strangers, somebody's cat is last. Rarity should be comparable only against the user's own collection and against fixed archetypes.
- GENERIC FLATTERY WILL NOT LAND — SPECIFICITY IS A REQUIREMENT, NOT A POLISH ITEM. Andersen & Nordvik found people REJECT profiles that deviate far from reality; the Barnum effect does not save you. Every card must contain at least one detail only that photo could have produced (a specific ear notch, the exact loaf geometry, the object it is sitting on, the direction of gaze). Budget model tokens for one concrete observed detail per card and treat a card with no photo-specific detail as a failed generation worth retrying.
- TREAT COAT-COLOUR STATS AS FORMALISED HUMAN STEREOTYPE AND OWN IT IN THE VOICE. Owners already believe grey cats are aloof, orange cats are friendly and tortoiseshells are stubborn, so colour-driven MENACE and VOID will feel uncannily accurate — because they encode the owner's prior, not the cat. This is fine for a card game IF the deadpan clinical voice is doing the joke, and dangerous if it reads as measurement. Keep the register at 'the field unit is reporting what it sees', never 'the instrument has determined'.
- MAKE HUMAN FACES A HARD STOP, NOT A JOKE. Every other non-cat input should be a delightful, distinct refusal — dog, other animal, bread loaf, plush toy, photo-of-a-photo, no animal at all — with bespoke copy for each; these are retention gold. But a human face gets no card, no stats, no score, no export. Detect it as early in the pipeline as possible, do not persist the image, and do not render output. Under Illinois BIPA a face-geometry scan derived from a photo carries a private right of action at $1,000 per negligent and $5,000 per intentional or reckless violation plus fees, with written notice and written release required BEFORE collection. 'It was a joke' is not a defence, and the photograph exclusion does not cover the geometry your pipeline computes.
- PUT THE DISCLAIMER ON THE PNG, BECAUSE THE PNG IS THE PRODUCT. Export is explicitly the sharing mechanism, which means the card travels without the app's context. Bake a small permanent mark into the 1080x1350 canvas — 'novelty card, not a health assessment' — in the data-font register so it reads as part of the field-unit aesthetic rather than legal boilerplate. Anything not on the canvas does not exist downstream.
- IF YOU EVER WANT A MORE HONEST READ, ASK FOR MORE PHOTOS AND SHOW THE DISAGREEMENT. Majority voting across evaluators was the single most accurate method in the photo-BCS study. Multi-photo scanning maps naturally onto the card-collecting loop: two or three angles produce a more confident card, and MODEL DISAGREEMENT becomes a first-class mechanic — an 'unstable read' card that is rarer and more interesting than a confident one. This turns the model's genuine uncertainty into content instead of hiding it behind false precision.
- LOCALISE JAPAN ON POPULATION REALITY, NOT ON CAT-COUNTRY VIBES. 日本猫 is the single largest category of insured Japanese cats at 28.8% and must be a first-class, high-status card type with its own art — not folded into a generic 'domestic'. But among 0-year-olds it collapses to 0.8% while Scottish Fold and Munchkin dominate, so a young-cat-skewed launch meets an almost entirely different population. Cat Day, 22 February, is a real dated national hook co-founded by the Japan Pet Food Association and is the obvious annual event anchor. Cats have outnumbered dogs in Japan since around 2017 and the gap is still widening.
- MINE JAPANESE CAT COGNITION, NOT JAPANESE CAT PERSONALITY. The famous five-factor cat personality model is Australian/NZ. What Japan actually leads on is cognition: cats learn the names and faces of housemate cats, map their owner's location from voice alone, and cross-modally match voice to face. That is a far richer seam for stat names, flavour lines and future mechanics — a stat about whether a cat knows where you are is both original and true, whereas a Feline-Five knockoff is neither.

TRAPS:
- Shipping CHONK as a body-fat read and assuming the joke insulates you. Photo-based scoring separates ideal from obese at 1.8% misclassification, 24% of cat owners already underestimate their cat's condition, and — the killer — owners of heavy/obese cats are significantly MORE attached to them. A funny stat that lands as a diagnosis is not attacking the cat's weight, it is attacking the relationship the whole app depends on.
- Using humour to soften the weight message. This feels like exactly the right move for a deadpan clinical voice, and veterinary communication research specifically flags it as double-edged: vets report weight conversations turning adversarial and name humour as having both benefits and challenges. The 'field unit at 3am' register is perfect until the subject becomes the animal's actual body, at which point it becomes a stranger making a fat joke about a family member.
- Assuming a model output of 'domestic shorthair' is a failure state to be engineered away. In the UK it is the correct answer for 89-92% of cats. A team that treats it as a bug will spend its effort making the model guess a breed, which means making the model confidently wrong nine times out of ten. Worse: the canonical benchmark everyone cites deliberately deleted crossbreed cats from the dataset, so the published accuracy numbers were never measured on the users' actual cats.
- Outputting a breed percentage mix. 'Your cat is 35% Maine Coon' is not a hedge — population genetics shows UK random-bred cats are random-bred, explicitly 'rather than admixed, mix breed, or crossbred'. The output is not imprecise, it is a category error, and cat genetics people will say so publicly and correctly.
- Making Scottish Fold or Munchkin a high rarity tier for the Japan market. They are Japan's #3 and #4 cats at 7.8% and 7.6% of insured cats, so the commercial pull is obvious — and the fold is caused by a dominant TRPV4 variant that also produces a painful degenerative joint disorder, while the short legs are chondrodysplasia. Gilding them with FOIL or MYTHIC puts a studio that also runs a botanical game on the wrong side of a live and well-organised welfare argument.
- Treating the 'Feline Five' as Japanese research in a pitch, a store listing, or marketing copy. It is Litchfield et al. 2017, South Australia and New Zealand. This is a trivially checkable error that would undercut the exact claim the director wants to make about building on Japanese work — and Japan's real contribution, cat cognition out of Kyoto and Azabu, is genuinely more interesting anyway.
- Reading Japan's cat obsession as a pedigree obsession, or as its opposite. Both readings are wrong. All-ages insured cats run 42.4% non-pedigree with 日本猫 at #1, but newly acquired 0-year-olds are overwhelmingly pedigree with 日本猫 at 0.8%. Ship one breed model tuned to either half and you misread the market.
- Believing the Barnum effect will carry weak stats. Product folklore says people accept any plausible personality profile; the actual experiment found respondents identified and REJECTED random profiles that deviated far from their real scores. Generic cat-flavoured flattery will read as broken, not magical. The app has to actually see something in the photo.
- Assuming BIPA's photograph exclusion protects you. The statute excludes photographs and physical descriptions like hair colour and eye colour — but the regulated item is a 'scan of hand or face geometry', which is precisely what a vision pipeline computes FROM the photograph. With a private right of action at $1,000 negligent and $5,000 reckless per violation plus attorneys' fees, and written notice plus written release required before collection, a cute 'HUMAN DETECTED: rarity ALLEY' easter egg is a class-action generator.
- Letting the absence of a health warning function as reassurance. If an app scans your cat's face and says nothing about its health, owners will infer their cat is fine — and the published evidence is that general LLMs scoring feline pain from faces are biased toward UNDERESTIMATING it. Silence from a scanner is not neutral; it is an implied all-clear that the underlying model has not earned.
- Building stats that accidentally read the Feline Grimace Scale action units. The five validated units are ear position, orbital tightening, muzzle tension, whiskers change and head position. Those are exactly the features a model will reach for when asked to score VOID, MENACE or mood. Without an explicit blocklist, the app will produce an unvalidated, biased pain score and print it on a shareable card.
- Optimising for the funniest possible cat photo without noticing what that selects for. In an analysis of 162 humorous pet videos, 82% showed stress reactions, 52% injury risk, 30% suspected pain and 32% agony-breeding traits — and 93.8% were commercially successful. A rarity system that rewards the most extreme, most viral-looking photo is, statistically, a system that rewards distressed and unhealthily-bred animals.
- Adding a global leaderboard or public rarity percentile later, after the private version proves out. The 'no cat is the bad one' guarantee is architectural, not cosmetic: the instant rarity becomes comparable between strangers, somebody's cat is objectively last and the affectionate frame collapses into a judgement. The current decision to omit public feeds and comments is the load-bearing ethical choice in the whole product, and it will come under the most commercial pressure precisely when the app starts working.
- Quoting a US non-pedigree percentage in a deck. I could not verify one. The defensible numbers are UK 89-92%, Japan 42.4% among insured cats, and 'a single worldwide random-bred population' globally. Likewise, every 'the average owner takes N photos of their pet per week' figure I could trace was unverifiable PR polling — do not build the pitch narrative on it.
