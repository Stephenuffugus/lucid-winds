# Petal Alchemy — Revamp Brief

**Revives:** Infinite Craft (Neal Agarwal, 2024) — drag one element onto another, discover a third, keep combining outward into an endless emergent tree of stuff

## The essence (protect this)

The pure "what happens if I…" itch: two things touch, a new thing is born with a name and a little icon, and you *have* to try the next combination. It's discovery as its own reward — no score, no timer, just the compulsion to find the next thing and the delight when a weird pairing produces something perfect. Protect the drag-two-together verb, the instant birth of a named new element, the "first to discover" spark, and the sprawling sense that there's always one more thing out there.

## Signature upgrade

THE TRUE BLOOM — most combinations make a new *element* you collect, but a rare, hard-won branch of the tree produces a **True Plant**: a real, hand-drawn, one-of-one Lucid Winds bloom that **mints straight into your Greenhouse** and becomes a **breeding parent**. Infinite Craft's discoveries evaporate the moment you close the tab; ours flow into the game you're already playing. This is the definitive hook because it gives the endless "just one more combo" loop a *destination* — you're not just making funny words, you're alchemizing your way toward living plants you keep, cross-breed, and show off. It fuses the mechanic (combine to discover), the reskin (elements are gorgeous hand-drawn nature-nodes, not text bubbles), and the economy (True Blooms mint + feed breeding, discoveries pay dew) into one act. An Infinite Craft where the tree you grow is a garden you own.

## Dated friction to kill

- LLM lag and nonsense: Infinite Craft calls a model per combine — seconds of waiting, and results that are often gibberish or inconsistent ("Sand + Sand = Sandcastle" one time, something else the next). Ours is a hand-authored deterministic recipe map: instant, always sensible, always charming, same result every time so discoveries are shareable and comparable.
- Total aimlessness: with zero goals many players combine for two minutes and quit. Ours adds a soft spine — a Discovery Log with silhouette "seeds to find," category collections, and daily targets — direction for those who want it, ignorable for pure sandboxers.
- Nothing persists or pays: the original keeps a list and that's it. Ours mints True Blooms, pays dew per new discovery, and tracks a real collection.
- Canvas clutter: dragging on an infinite messy plane gets unwieldy fast. Ours pairs a tidy searchable Shelf of everything you own with a clean combine-tray, plus pin/favorite and a "combine again" repeat so you're never re-hunting the same two nodes.
- Unreadable at a glance: tiny text bubbles blur on a phone. Ours are bold hand-drawn node icons with names, readable at thumbnail, colorblind-friendly by silhouette.
- Dead-end frustration: hitting a combo that does nothing, repeatedly, with no hint. Ours gives a gentle "these two don't take… yet" shimmer and an optional dew-bought hint that nudges toward a productive pairing.

## Game-feel spec

- Combine: drag one node onto another; they lean together, a soft alchemical glow pulses at the seam, and the new node blooms open with a papercut unfold + a rising two-note chime. A brand-new discovery gets a brighter flare, a "NEW" sprig, and a floating dew reward.
- True Bloom moment: when a combine yields a real plant, everything slows for ~0.7s (the slow-mo the portal already loves), petals drift, the plant SVG draws itself stroke by stroke, and a warm chord resolves as it files into your Greenhouse — the game's peak payload.
- Shelf: your discovered elements live on a scrollable, searchable, category-tabbed shelf; long-press to pin favorites to a quick tray; a subtle counter shows "X of Y in this branch found."
- Combine-again: after any combine, a repeat button re-runs the same pair, so grinding out a chain of the same reaction is one tap, not two drags.
- Dead pairing: a non-recipe combine gives a soft grey shimmer and a tiny "not yet" wilt — never a buzzer, never a punish.
- Discovery cadence: a gentle progress ribbon and the occasional milestone burst ("50 discovered!", "first Mythic element!") keep the sandbox feeling like it's going somewhere without imposing a fail state.

## Onboarding & difficulty

You start with four base elements — Seed, Rain, Sun, Soil — and one glowing hint arrow dragging Seed onto Rain to make Sprout. That single guided combine teaches the entire verb in seconds; the new Sprout immediately invites "what do I combine *this* with?" and you're off, no tutorial screen, no text wall. Difficulty is depth, not pressure: early combos are obvious and forgiving (Sprout + Sun = Leaf), but the tree branches into botany, weather, seasons, creatures, and finally the rare multi-step chains that yield True Blooms and Mythic nodes — reaching those takes real experimentation and memory. The hard-to-master ceiling is *efficiency and completion*: discovering everything, finding the shortest path to a target in Daily Recipe, and unlocking the deepest True Blooms that need long deliberate chains. Easy to make your first flower; a long, cozy road to a full compendium.

## Modes

- Free Alchemy (main) — the endless sandbox: combine anything, discover everything, mint every True Bloom you can reach, at your own pace. Where the collection lives.
- Daily Recipe — a single target element revealed each day ("make a Moonbell") that everyone races to build from the base four; scored by step count, pays a guaranteed Sunbeam + a daily bloom scaled by streak. The retention beat.
- Bloom Hunt (quests) — themed discovery challenges ("find all four Seasons," "reach a Mythic," "grow the whole Rose branch") with dew + cosmetic rewards; turns the sandbox into gentle goals.
- Zen Garden Lab — no counters, no targets, just the shelf and the tray for relaxed tinkering; earns no Sunbeams, purely for play.

## Theme & identity

Fully botanical and on-brand — this is the most Lucid Winds mechanic in the whole audit. Every node is a hand-drawn papercut nature-icon: the base four (Seed 🌰, Rain 🌧️, Sun ☀️, Soil 🌱) build into Sprout, Root, Leaf, Bud, Bloom, then into real plant families, then branch sideways into weather, the four seasons, dew, pollen, and finally the 85 companions and Mythic curiosities (a combine chain can *discover* a Toad or a Beholder). True Bloom nodes render through the actual plant SVG engine and mint via hashToTraits, so a discovered flower is a genuine one-of-one. Palette is the midnight garden — deep near-black lab table, sage and gold node frames, luminous dew accents, cream labels. It reads native because the discovery tree literally grows the same plants and creatures the rest of the game is made of.

## Retention hook

Daily Recipe is the spine: one shared target a day, a guaranteed Sunbeam, and a unique daily bloom whose rarity climbs with your login streak. Free Alchemy feeds two collections continuously — the element Compendium (every discovery logged, categories to complete) and the Greenhouse itself (True Blooms mint in, feeding breeding). Dew drips in per new discovery, funding hints and cosmetic early-unlocks. Cosmetics are earned at known thresholds of elements-discovered / True-Blooms-minted / daily-streak: node-frame skins (brass, pressed-flower, stained-glass), element-art themes, and Discovery-Log parchment/binding styles. Sunbeams follow the portal standard (30/day/game, 12/run cap). The compounding pull: the streak protects your daily bloom, every session deepens the tree and mints real plants, and the compendium + Greenhouse show off how far you've alchemized.

## Why ours wins

Infinite Craft is a brilliant toy you close and forget; Petal Alchemy is that same irresistible "what if I combine these" loop, but the things you discover are beautiful hand-drawn plants and creatures you *keep* — minted into your greenhouse, bred into new ones, logged in a compendium — with none of the LLM lag or nonsense. The tree you grow is a garden you own.

## Build notes

Single-file vanilla ES5 canvas/DOM, no frameworks — fits the portal satellite pattern. Heavy reuse: the plant SVG renderer + hashToTraits mint True Blooms (a discovered plant node carries a deterministic hash so it renders + breeds like any other), the 85-companion art set supplies creature nodes, the 4-season palette and getHaiku can flavor discoveries, and Sunbeams follow the standard. Genuinely new code is modest: the drag-combine UI, the shelf/search/pin system, the discovery-log + milestone tracking, and the date-seeded Daily Recipe pathfinder (BFS over the recipe graph for par step count). **The real cost is content, not engineering:** a hand-authored recipe map — element A + element B → element C, plus each element's name, icon, category, and rarity. Start at ~150 combos across a coherent botany→weather→seasons→creatures tree (enough for hours of discovery and a dozen True Blooms) and grow it over time like the haiku banks; the graph is data, so extending it is adding rows, not code. Keep node art as lightweight papercut SVG so it's cheap and colorblind-readable. Deterministic recipes make discoveries shareable and Daily Recipe fair. ⛔ One of four "your run mints a plant" concepts (with Season Sway / Cipher Bloom / First Sprout) — don't ship them back-to-back.
