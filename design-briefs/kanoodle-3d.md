# Kanoodle, virtually (Stephen 7/17 — Penny's puzzle)

**The ask:** "a 3D model of each piece you can rotate and build in 3D and the flat version. once each piece is made then players can fit them together to make a pyramid or a flat rectangle and there are so many combinations."

## What Kanoodle actually is
12 polysphere pieces (each 3-5 connected balls in a fixed shape, distinct colors). Two boards: a 5x11 flat rectangle (55 cells) and a 4-layer square pyramid (30 balls). Puzzles seed a few pieces in place, you fit the rest. Thousands of published challenges.

## Build shape (recommended: two phases)
**Phase 1 — FLAT Kanoodle (a normal satellite, 2D, very buildable).**
The 5x11 board with the real 12 pieces. Tap a piece, tap rotate/flip (8 orientations), drag onto the grid, snap when legal. Seeded challenge ladder (easy seeds 8 pieces, expert seeds 3) generated by solving the board backward: place all 12 by solver, remove N, verify unique-ish solvability with the same solver — the exact machine-proof pattern we use everywhere. Daily board too. This alone is a great game and ships fast.

**Phase 2 — the PYRAMID in 3D.**
Real 3D needs rotatable stacked-sphere rendering. NO frameworks per house rules, so: painter's-algorithm canvas 3D (project ball centers, sort by depth, draw circles with radial shading) — spheres are the one shape this is EASY for. One finger orbits the pyramid, tap a rest position to drop the selected piece; pieces sit on a 30-socket lattice so placement is discrete (no free 3D dragging). Piece inspector: the current piece floats in a corner and can be spun the same way.

## Solver
Exact-cover (dancing links or simple DFS over 12 pieces x orientations x anchors) — flat board is tiny; pyramid lattice is 30 cells, also tiny. Bake proofs per challenge.
